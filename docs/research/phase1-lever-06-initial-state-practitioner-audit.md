# PáircVision Phase 1 — Lever 06: INITIAL STATE — Practitioner Audit

**Type:** Practitioner and coaching-intelligence audit. Research only: no production code, drills, sessions, UI or product design.
**Companion to:** [STARTING POSITIONS scientific audit](./phase1-lever-06-starting-positions.md). Its provisional verdict was *YES BUT NARROWED → INITIAL STATE*.
**Frozen context:** [SPACE](./phase1-lever-01-space-final-gate.md) · [NUMBERS](./phase1-lever-02-numbers-final-gate.md) · [TIME](./phase1-lever-03-time-final-gate.md) · [DIRECTION](./phase1-lever-04-direction-final-gate.md) · [SCORING](./phase1-lever-05-scoring-final-gate.md)
**Status:** v1 practitioner audit, September 2026. See the [INITIAL STATE Final Evidence & Falsification Gate](./phase1-lever-06-initial-state-final-gate.md). **Where this audit and the final gate differ, the final gate governs PáircVision V1.**

> **Central practitioner question.** When good coaches deliberately manufacture the beginning of an activity, what part of the game problem are they supplying, and what part are they still asking players to perceive and solve?
>
> **Secondary question.** Are coaches using initial states to teach players **where to be**, or to repeatedly expose them to a **game problem** from which they must make decisions?
>
> **Stance.** This audit tries to **falsify** the scientific audit's verdict. Practitioner popularity is never scientific evidence.

## 0. Method, access and labels

### 0.1 How the audit was done

- **Searches (September 2026)** covered:
  - constraints-led and ecological coaches (USA Hockey, AIK / Mark O'Sullivan, Transforming Basketball / Alex Sarama);
  - governing-body material (FA Boot Room, USA Hockey, GAA Tobar);
  - rugby practitioner-research (McKay & O'Connor, 2018);
  - tactical periodisation (Frade / Mourinho);
  - AFL stoppage coaching;
  - Gaelic coaching and kickout commentary (Colm Nally, FRC; Armagh coach Conleith Gilligan; high-performance Gaelic coach studies);
  - basketball transition and advantage-start material;
  - "structure vs freedom" discussion.
- **Access.**
  - Proxy refusals continued (T&F, Human Kinetics, SAGE, the FA and GAA PDFs).
  - **Nothing was read in full text.**
  - Material is **SEARCH-INDEX ONLY (SIO)** or **ABSTRACT ONLY (AO)**. Quotes are as indexed and should be checked against the original.
- **Anonymous drill websites are not treated as practitioner authority.** Where one is cited, it is labelled **COACHING CONVENTION**.

### 0.2 Labels

- **Statement type:** DIRECT STATEMENT · REASONABLE INFERENCE · PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION.
- **Claim classification:** PRACTITIONER CONSENSUS · COMMON PRACTICE · CONTESTED · WEAKLY SUPPORTED · UNSUPPORTED · CONTRADICTED · NOT FOUND.

### 0.3 Honest headline

1. **Practitioners do think in "where in the game are we?" terms.**
   - The clearest case is rugby: McKay & O'Connor chose practice starts from the **frequency of possession sources in matches**.
   - Tactical periodisation's "four moments", FA transition guidance and AFL "stoppage" structures point the same way.
2. **The "supply the problem vs supply the answer" distinction appears explicitly** in constraints-led practitioner language:
   - "repeat the process of finding the solution rather than … giving the solution" (USA Hockey);
   - "illusion of competence … training action without authentic perception" (Sarama, on neutral "check-ball" starts).
3. **Structured rehearsal has credible defenders** (tactical periodisation's game model; AFL stoppage set-ups; Gaelic kickout structure). **No consensus.**
4. **The pretend-turnover concern** (coach-called transition removes perception of transition) is **largely unaddressed**.
   - The most-cited practitioner case (McKay) **constructed** transition-possession starts.
   - This is a **contradiction** to record, not resolve.
5. **Kinnerk:** nothing attributable on starting configurations.

## 2. Why coaches choose a start

| Start | Stated rationale | Classification | Source (statement type) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unstructured possession sources: turnover, kick receipt, quick tap | Match analysis showed most possessions (~56 %) came from **attacking transition**, which traditional practice ignored | **Match preparation / realism by frequency** | P1 (DIRECT, AO) |
| Advantage start (defender passes, then closes out) instead of check-ball | Players "almost never" catch the ball against a set, neutral defender. Check-ball builds an "illusion of competence" | **Realism / perception-action / decision-making** | P2 (DIRECT, SIO) |
| Continuous small-area games with constant transitions | "Hundreds of transitions" per game; players meet situations "they would rarely see in a traditional drill" | **Problem exposure / realism** | P3 (DIRECT, SIO) |
| Transition in every practice | Transition = reaction to "the first few moments" of winning or losing the ball; include it in all practices | **Realism / problem exposure** | P4 (DIRECT, SIO) |
| Four "moments" (organisation, transitions) | Every exercise must relate to the **game model** | **Tactical rehearsal (team identity)** | P6 (secondary, SIO) |
| Stoppages / dead-ball set plays | "Predictable structures"; training is where to practise set plays | **Tactical rehearsal** | P7 (DIRECT, SIO) |
| Score-time scenarios ("up by four, three minutes to go") | Mimic match problems, opposition characteristics, time management | **Match preparation / adaptability** | P9 (DIRECT via research, AO) |
| Kickouts / restarts | "Structure over kickouts" in modern football | **Tactical rehearsal** | P8 (DIRECT, SIO) |
| Freeze and Fix, then back to the game | Game-first guided discovery | **Decision-making / problem exposure** | P10 (governing body, SIO) |

**Not found with a stated rationale:**
- loose-ball starts;
- set-defence starts;
- recovery-run starts;
- "ball entering an area" starts.

These are common in session materials, but their reasons are **not documented** by credible practitioners. We do not infer them.

## 3. Supply the problem vs supply the answer (priority)

| Practitioner | Language | Which side |
|---|---|---|
| **USA Hockey** (P3) | Coaches want "to repeat the process of finding the solution rather than repeat the process of giving the solution" | **Explicit: supply the problem** |
| **Sarama** (P2) | Neutral check-ball starts give "action without authentic perception", producing "choreographed jab-steps and rehearsed dribble combos". Advantage starts vary the defender's closeout ("hot", "cold", "normal") so the attacker must **read** it | **Explicit: supply a realistic problem with variable information** |
| **Constraints-led coaching** (Sport NZ resource; CLA writers; SIO) | "The objective becomes repeating the search for solutions rather than repeating a single solution" | Explicit (general) |
| **O'Sullivan / AIK** (P5) | Shift "away from implementing predetermined 'optimal' techniques or patterns, towards … an adaptive, interactive performer" | Explicit (general) |
| **Tactical periodisation** (P6) | Game model = "pre-determined behaviours" giving the team identity across four moments | **Supplies structure deliberately** (answer-oriented at team level) |
| **AFL stoppage set-ups** (P7) | "Predictable structures" at dead-ball situations | **Supplies the answer at restarts** |
| **Nally** (P8) | Structure over kickouts, defence and attack, but "patches of chaos" are desirable | **Both** |

**Do experienced coaches explicitly distinguish placing players in the solution from creating a situation where they must find it?**

- **Yes, in constraints-led circles** (DIRECT: USA Hockey, Sarama, AIK).
- **Tactical-periodisation and set-play coaches deliberately supply structure.** They are not confused about this. They are pursuing **collective coordination to a shared model**, which is a different aim (§19).

## 4. "Coach solved it already"

**Criticism of over-prescription (found):**
- Sarama: "illusion of competence"; many NBA teams use "very scripted drills"; repetitive drills transfer poorly (practitioner claim, SIO).
- AIK: move away from predetermined patterns.
- CLA resources: over-prescriptive coaching "may be detrimental to learning" (Sport NZ; SIO).

**Defence of structured positioning (found):**
- Tactical periodisation: pre-determined behaviours as the game model.
- AFL stoppage set-ups.
- Gaelic structure over kickouts (Nally).
- Pattern-play literature: patterns rehearsed and then run "under simulated pressure" (secondary; SIO).

**Width, support, recovery, overload:**
- **No credible practitioner source** was found discussing the specific cases:
  - starting players wide to teach width;
  - pre-placing support to teach support;
  - placing everyone goal-side to teach recovery;
  - handing an obvious 4v3 to teach overload recognition.
- **Status: NOT FOUND.** The scientific audit's logical point stands as PÁIRCVISION reasoning.

## 5. Initial state as "where in the game are we?"

**Candidate:** *INITIAL STATE determines which moment or picture of the game an episode begins from, before subsequent play becomes self-organising.*

| Practitioner framing | Matches the candidate? |
|---|---|
| McKay: **possession sources** (kick receipt, turnover, quick tap vs set piece) | **Strongly.** The start is chosen by *which moment* and *how often it occurs* |
| Tactical periodisation: **four moments of the game** | Yes (moment-based) |
| FA: transition = "first few moments" after winning or losing the ball | Yes |
| Sarama: **advantage vs neutral** start; closeout state | Yes. It is about the **state of the defence** at the start, not only positions |
| AFL: **stoppages** as distinct starting moments | Yes (restart moments) |
| Gaelic: kickouts; score-time scenarios | Yes (restart moment; game-state information) |

**Verdict.** Practitioners **do** think in these terms, especially "possession source" and "moment of the game".

**The practitioner framing adds a dimension the scientific audit under-weighted: FREQUENCY.**
- McKay sampled starts **in proportion to how often they occur in matches**.
- That is a **sampling** logic, not a positioning logic.

## 6. Transition

| Design | Practitioner support |
|---|---|
| Coach-triggered turnover ("turnover!" → possession given) | Used by McKay (constructed unstructured-possession starts, DIRECT). Session writers use manufactured turnovers so sessions aren't "too clean" (SIO; attribution UNR) |
| Transitions arising within continuous play | USA Hockey (continual transitions in small-area games, DIRECT); FA (include transition in all practices, DIRECT) |
| Recognising that transition has occurred | "Players must be coached to read the triggers that the ball is about to be won back or lost" (SIO; attribution UNR) |

**Candidate PáircVision concern:** *a pretend turnover may practise responding after transition while removing the need to perceive that transition has occurred.*

- **Support:**
  - Indirect. Sarama's "action without authentic perception" argument about neutral starts applies by analogy.
  - USA Hockey and the FA favour **continuous** transitions.
- **Contradiction:**
  - McKay's constructed turnover starts are the most-cited success case. Two tries in the 2011 Grand Final **resembled** rehearsed scenarios (anecdotal, not evidence of learning).
- **Silence:** no practitioner explicitly separates **perceiving** transition from **responding** to it.

**Status: PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS; practitioner field CONTESTED / largely silent.**

## 7. Restarts are different

- **AFL** (P7): set plays revolve around dead-ball situations; "predictable structures"; training is the place to rehearse them. **DIRECT** (community manuals).
- **Gaelic** (P8, P11): structure over kickouts is now normal. Kick vs hand-pass choices discussed by an inter-county coach (Gilligan). Rationale for **how** kickouts are practised: **NOT FOUND**.
- **Reasoning:** is rehearsing a kickout picture different from manufacturing a neat open-play picture?
  - Practitioners imply yes (set plays = predictable structures).
  - **No practitioner explicitly argues it** in representativeness terms.
  - **PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION, consistent with P7.**
- **Current Gaelic rules.** Kickout rules changed in 2025–26 (kickout beyond the 40 m arc; three players in each half, per secondary sources). **The Official Guide text remains unread** (SCORING gate research debt). Practitioner commentary on 2026 kickout **practice design** was not found.

## 9. Pattern play / phase play

**Strongest case for structured rehearsal (practitioner reasoning):**
1. **Shared model.** Eleven, fifteen or thirty players must coordinate. Rehearsal builds a shared picture of where teammates will be (tactical periodisation).
2. **Fixed-start situations.** Restarts **are** fixed in competition (AFL stoppages, kickouts, lineouts).
3. **Efficiency.** Walkthroughs let coaches install team structure with low physical load (convention; rationale inferred, **REASONABLE INFERENCE**).
4. **Match-specific preparation** against a known opponent structure (HP Gaelic coaches mimic opposition; P9).

**Strongest criticism:**
1. Choreography without perception ("illusion of competence", Sarama).
2. Predetermined patterns vs adaptive performers (AIK).
3. Over-prescription limits search (CLA writers).

**Where rehearsal may genuinely be appropriate (PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION):**
- **competition-prescribed starts**;
- **team-coordination structures** that must be shared;
- **brief exposure** before live play.
- **Not** as a substitute for perceiving and creating open-play pictures.
- **No anti-pattern-play ideology.**

## 11. Body orientation

| Practice | Supplied or discovered? |
|---|---|
| "Half-turn / open body" receiving taught as technique (Coaches' Voice, FIFA Training Centre; SIO) | **Supplied** (instructional) |
| Advantage starts with closing-out defenders (Sarama) | **Discovered.** The attacker's orientation must adapt to the closeout |

## 12. Pressure starts

- Sarama's closeout constraints ("hot", "cold", "normal") manipulate the **defender's approach state** (DIRECT).
- USA Hockey / FA: realism and transition (DIRECT).
- **What practitioners think they manipulate:**
  - **realism**;
  - **perception** (reading the defender);
  - **decision-making**.
- Explicit "time" language is rare.
- **Comparison with the frozen TIME gate:** the manipulation *produces* usable-time differences (TIME T1, T7). Practitioners frame it as **defender state and realism**, not time.
- **PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION:** "defender state at the start" (closing, set, recovering) is an INITIAL-STATE variable whose **effect** is TIME.

## 23. Failure modes (practitioner-described or strongly defensible)

| Failure mode | Basis | Proven learning harm? |
|---|---|---|

## 24. Volunteer coach translation (U12 Gaelic)

> **Two very different things can look the same on the pitch.**
>
> **"I'm putting them in the right place."** You stand the corner-forward out wide and the wing-back ready to support. It looks great. But you did the thinking. This is fine when the real game always starts that way, like your kickout set-up, or when the whole team needs to know a shared plan. Just don't mistake it for them learning to find those spots.
>
> **"I'm starting the game at a moment that gives them a problem."** Start from a real kind of moment: a kickout, a turnover, a breaking ball, their player running at yours. Then let them sort it out. They have to notice what's on, and where to go.
>
> **Pick moments that happen a lot in matches.** Turnovers and breaking ball happen all the time in our game. Practise starting from those more than from neat, tidy set-ups.
>
> **Watch out for "pretend" moments.** Shouting "turnover!" is handy, but in a match nobody shouts. Sometimes let turnovers happen for real in the game, so they have to spot them.

## 26. Final practitioner verdict

### **STRENGTHENS INITIAL STATE** (moderately, and narrows it further)

**Why:**
- Credible practitioners independently organise practice around **which moment of the game** play starts from:
  - possession sources (McKay);
  - four moments (tactical periodisation);
  - transition moments (FA);
  - stoppages (AFL).
- They manipulate **the opposition's state at the start** (Sarama's closeouts).
- Neither is reducible to "where players stand".

**Why only moderately:**
- No practitioner claim is learning evidence.
- Many starts (advantage chains, overload starts) are just **NUMBERS / TIME methods**.
- Score-time scenarios belong to **INFORMATION**.
- The pretend-transition problem is unaddressed.

### What does INITIAL STATE allow a coach to manipulate that SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION and SCORING do not already describe?

1. **Which game moment an episode samples, and how often.** Possession source: kickout, turnover, breaking ball, set attack, set defence. Frequency-weighted to match reality or deliberately skewed.
2. **The state of the opposition and the ball at t0.** Defence set, recovering or closing out; ball loose, won or restarted. Ideally **varied**, so it must be read.
3. **Whether the start is supplied by the coach or emerges from play.** Called turnover vs natural turnover; placed vs self-organised positions. This determines whether **recognising the moment** is part of the task.
4. **Whether the start is competition-prescribed** (restarts, where rehearsal is representative) **or constructed** (open play).

"Where players stand" alone is **not** enough, and is **not** the core. It is the method; its effects are recorded under SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION.

**Carry to the final gate:**
- whether "INITIAL STATE" should be reframed as **EPISODE START / GAME-MOMENT SAMPLING**;
- whether "supplied vs emergent start" is a lever property or a cross-lever principle (alongside NUMBERS N6, DIRECTION DIR-4, SCORING SC-2).

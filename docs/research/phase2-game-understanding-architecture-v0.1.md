## 0. Answer

**Is there now a coherent, defensible PáircVision architecture for describing recurring Gaelic football problems and what players do within them?**

**Yes — as a descriptive architecture, provisionally.**
- It held across three structurally different problems (reactive open play; planned-then-emergent restart; continuous settled attack).
- The third test required **no new layer**.
- It separates object types that traditional principles-of-play lists mix together (§3).
- It is **coherent and defensible as a way of describing and reading problems**.

**It is not:**
- validated science;
- a proven aid to coaching or learning;
- a coaching philosophy (§22).

**Most of its content is unmeasured in current-rules Gaelic football.**

**Verdict: A — coherent enough to proceed** to the next research stage (§23). **"Proceed" does not mean build product.**

**Next step: the reliability pilot** (§18, §24).

## 2. Nothing is frozen automatically

**Before any element is frozen, it requires:**
1. **Reliability:** independent observers agree on the observable features it depends on (§18).
2. **Rule verification:** the official 2026 rule text is checked for every rule-created relationship used, including Limerick's underage rules for local use.
3. **Current-rules content:** at least descriptive measurement (club or county video coding) of the picture features in the audited problems under the current rules.
4. **Coverage:** audits of the unaudited core problems (§4.3) to show the taxonomy holds beyond three cases.
5. **Independent practitioner review:** Gaelic coaches (not the research author) check that states, problems and resources match what they meet. That review gathers practitioner knowledge; it is not evidence.
6. **No learning claim** may be attached until Coach Intervention and learning / retention / transfer research exist (Phase 1 §8).

## 3. Core objects — a taxonomy

**Purpose:** stop treating fundamentally different concepts as peers. Traditional lists put "delay", "tackle", "zones A→B→C" and "width" side by side (landscape audit §13).

| Object | Definition (V0.1) | Examples | Where it came from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **CONTEXT** | Conditions framing everything: age/format/ruleset (county); score, time, hooter | U12 13-a-side; adult 2026 rules; one point down with 3 minutes left | Age map; game structure audit §19 |
| **RULE CONSTRAINT** | A rule that creates or forbids relationships | 4v3; goalkeeper pass-back; arc (kickout minimum and two-point line); kickout 13 m; solo-and-go limits; U12 two-skills limit | Game structure audit §1; age map |
| **ENTRY EVENT** (trigger) | A discrete occurrence that moves play into a state | Regain; possession lost; own/opposition kickout; throw-in; foul (solo-and-go); mark; line-break; score/wide | Audit 03 §2 |
| **GAME STATE** | The configuration currently facing players: **ball status** (ours / theirs / contested-loose) × **opponent organisation** (settled / unsettled) | "Our ball, their defence unsettled"; "ball loose after a contest" | Game structure audit §4; Audit 03 |
| **RECURRING PROBLEM** | What the team must solve within a state | "Use a temporary advantage before the defence recovers"; "progress against an organised defence"; "win or secure a contested ball" | Audits 01–03 |
| **PLANNED STRUCTURE** | What a team pre-sets where the problem allows it: positions, numbers, roles, cover | Kickout set-up; settled-attack shape; rest defence | Audit 02 §8; Audit 03 §13 |
| **PICTURE** | Observable relationships at a moment: pressure, defenders goal-side, free players within range, space, local numbers, location vs rule lines | "Carrier under loose pressure; 4 defenders goal-side vs 3 of ours ahead; teammate free wide" | Audits 01–03 |
| **PICTURE FEATURE** (condition) | A property of the picture, not a separate object | **Overload**, isolation (1v1), pressure level, displaced defender | Audit 03 §7 (overload = state feature) |
| **RESOURCE / RELATIONSHIP** | Something players create or use to change the picture | **Support** (jobs), **width**, **depth**, **spacing**, **mobility** | Audit 03 §7 |
| **AIM** | What an action tries to achieve | **Penetration/progression**, **security**, **scoring**, **advantage used / kept** | Audit 03 §7 |
| **DECISION** | A choice among options, **coupled** on-ball and off-ball | Carrier: progress now / manipulate / secure. Off-ball: create or close options. | Audits 01, 03 |
| **ACTION** | What a player does | Carry, kick pass, hand pass, run beyond, hold width, shoot, jump to contest | All |
| **TECHNICAL EXECUTION** | How well an action is performed | Kick accuracy and range, catch, hand pass under pressure, tackle, pickup | All; GPOI |
| **PICTURE CHANGE** | The difference between successive pictures, judged through a **named construct** | Advantage used / kept / lost; outcome-ladder level; territory / bypass / disruption / value | Audits 01–03 |

**Cleanups relative to the brief's candidate list:**
- **"STATE/CONDITION" is split.** A **game state** (whole-team configuration) is not the same kind of thing as a **picture feature** (overload, pressure). Overload is a feature of the picture, not a state.
- **CONTEXT** is added, to hold age/format/rules and score/time. Late game under the hooter is context, not a problem.
- **Technical execution** stays separate from **action**. The action is what was attempted; execution is how well.

**Every term was tested against the three audits.** Tick = needed; ✓* = needed with a caveat.

| Layer | P5 (Audit 01) | Kickout (Audit 02) | P7 (Audit 03) | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Context | ✓ (age/rules; score/time) | ✓ (U14 county variants) | ✓ (arc; hooter; U12) | **Keep (added)** |
| Optional planned structure | Rarely (regains are unplanned) | ✓ (essential) | ✓ (attacking shape) | **Keep, optional** |
| Entry event | ✓ (regain) | ✓ (kickout) | Absent (state emerges) | **Keep, optional** (states can emerge without one) |
| Game state | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | **Keep** |
| Recurring problem | ✓ | ✓ (chain) | ✓ | **Keep** |
| Picture over time | ✓ | ✓ (sequence) | ✓ (continuous) | **Keep** |
| Information available | ✓ (decays) | ✓ (accumulates) | ✓ (continual) | **Keep, renamed** (see below) |
| On-ball + off-ball decisions | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | **Keep, as one coupled layer** |
| Possible solutions | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (plural) | **Keep, renamed "Options → actions"** |
| Technical execution | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | **Keep** |
| How the picture changed | ✓ | ✓ (ladder) | ✓* (construct contested) | **Keep; must name its construct** |
| Next state / problem | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | **Keep** |

**Renames:**
- **"Information available" → "Information available to players".** It is a **player-side subset** of the picture, timed and perspective-dependent. It is **not observable as perceived** (Audits 01 §17, 02 §13). Keeping it distinct from "picture" (observer-side) prevents the error of treating what a coach saw as what a player saw.
- **"Possible solutions" → "Options → actions".** Options are *created* (largely off-ball) and *chosen* (on-ball). "Solutions" implied a right answer.

**Removed:** nothing. **Added:** CONTEXT, which had been handled ad hoc in each audit.

```
CONTEXT (age · format · county rules · score/time)
│
├─ [ENTRY EVENT] ──► GAME STATE ──► RECURRING PROBLEM
│                                      │
│                    [PLANNED STRUCTURE — where the problem allows it]
│                                      │
│         ┌──────────── loop over time ┴──────────────────────────────┐
│         │ PICTUREₜ (observable relationships)                        │
│         │   └─ information available to playersₜ (not observable)   │
│         │ COUPLED DECISIONS (off-ball creates/closes options;        │
│         │                    on-ball chooses)                        │
│         │ OPTIONS → ACTIONS                                          │
│         │ TECHNICAL EXECUTION                                        │
│         │ PICTUREₜ₊₁  → how did the picture change? (named construct)│
│         └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
│                                      │
└──────────────────────────────► EXIT → NEXT GAME STATE / PROBLEM
```

**Status: PV-SYNTHESIS.** Each layer is conceptually necessary in at least one audit. **No layer's content is measured in the current Gaelic game.**

## 6. States vs triggers

**Formalised (from Audit 03):** *the event that creates a problem is not necessarily the problem.*

| State | Entry routes (examples) |
|---|---|
| S1 our ball / opponent unsettled | Regain (open play); won contested kickout vs a disorganised opposition; line-break or switch during settled attack; quick solo-and-go after being fouled; won break |
| S3 their ball / we are unsettled | Mirror of S1: loss; lost kickout contest; our line broken; their quick restart |
| S5 contested / loose | Kickout contest (planned); tackle spill; block; rebound (unplanned) |

**Implications for how the eventual map should be structured:**
1. **Organise by state and problem, not by event.** Events are **routes in**. The same problem (R1) is reached from several events, and its core behaviour does not depend on which.
2. **Events still matter as context.** A regain in contact, a won break and a line-break may produce *different typical pictures* (e.g. where support is), but they enter the *same* problem. Their pictures differ; their problem does not.
3. **This removes duplicates** (P16 ≡ R1; P13 = entry to R6) and prevents an inventory of events masquerading as problems.
4. **Practice design implication (flag only, Phase 1 not reopened):** GAME MOMENT's "how the follow-up moment arises" (Candidate 10) becomes a choice of **entry route** into a state. Different routes may preserve or remove different information (GI-2).

## 7. Information through time

| Problem | Information profile | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **R1 (P5)** | Present at entry; **decays** as the defence recovers. The opportunity lasts while relationships stay unfavourable (no fixed window). | CROSS-SPORT (imbalance; transition-duration data); Gaelic pre-2025 durations |
| **R5 planned (kickout)** | Partly planned before the kick; **accumulates** in flight; **revealed** at contact (break direction) | CROSS-SPORT (fly-ball control); practitioner |
| **R2 (P7)** | **Continually changes**; no discrete start; many small picture changes | CROSS-SPORT (disruption over ~3 s windows) |

**Does "picture over time" contain all three? Yes.**
- A sequence of pictures with information attached to each moment accommodates decay, accumulation, revelation and continuous change.
- **Condition:** the architecture must not default to "the picture at the start". For R2 there is no start.
- **What it does not provide:** time scales (how long pictures last, how fast defences recover). Those are **UNKNOWN** for Gaelic football.

## 8. Planned structure and adaptation

**Phase 1 Decision Allocation is preserved:** *"What am I telling them, and what am I leaving them to solve?"*

**Placement in Game Understanding:**
- **Planned structure is an optional layer that sits *before* the picture loop.** It is legitimate where the relevant decision can be made before the information for later decisions exists. Examples: kickout positions, numbers, roles and cover (Audit 02); settled-attack shape (Audit 03); rest defence.
- **The boundary is set by when information exists, not by a preference for autonomy.** Examples: the break direction (kickout); whether an advantage exists (R1); whether a defender has shifted (R2).
- **No direction of preference.** Neither more planning nor more autonomy is better in general (Phase 1 DA-1 to DA-3: legitimacy is conditional on the objective).
- **Observation consequence:** judging whether a player's position was "correct" **requires knowing the plan** (Audit 02 §9). Without the plan, observation is ambiguous.

## 9. On-ball and off-ball are one problem

| Finding | Status |
|---|---|
| The carrier chooses among options that **teammates have created or not** | PV-SYNTHESIS; CROSS-SPORT (distributed situation awareness framing) |
| **Support without receiving** (occupying or dragging a defender; holding width) | CROSS-SPORT (basketball spacing; handball second-line movement; soccer team-level disruption) + PRACTITIONER. **Gaelic-unmeasured.** Only movement-based forms transfer; screens may be illegal. |
| **Movement that creates space** (moving away from the ball) | PRACTITIONER; CROSS-SPORT |
| **Security behind** (recycle option; 4v3 rest defence) | PRACTITIONER; RULE LOGIC; CROSS-SPORT (rest defence) |
| **Options ahead** (depth; runs beyond) | PRACTITIONER; CROSS-SPORT |
| **Movement can worsen the picture** (crowding the carrier; everyone ahead; nobody back) | PRACTITIONER; PV-SYNTHESIS |
| The closest supporter is **not necessarily** the best support | PV-SYNTHESIS (tension with cross-sport local-numbers evidence noted) |

**Formalised:**
- The decision layer is **one coupled layer**. Off-ball decisions determine the option set; on-ball decisions select from it.
- "The carrier made a bad choice" and "the team gave the carrier no good choice" are **different diagnoses of the same observed action.**

## 10. Execution is its own layer

| Possible combination | Example |
|---|---|
| Good decision, failed execution | Right switch, overhit kick; correct break position, fumbled pickup |
| Poor decision, successful outcome | Forced pass that happened to find a runner; poor kickout position, lucky break |
| Good decision, good execution, poor outcome | Correct position, aerial duel lost to a better opponent |
| Poor decision, failed execution | Both errors present |

**For coaches observing** (conservative):
- **Execution failure is often visible**: the attempted action did not come off.
- **Decision quality is sometimes judgeable**, if a clearly better option was visible from the observer's position, and the plan is known.
- **Frequently indeterminate:** e.g. a tackle lost while carrying (misjudged cover, or lost a winnable duel?).
- **Perception is never directly observable.**
- **The GPOI separation** (control / decision / execution) is the Gaelic-used precedent. It is a **research instrument**, not yet a sideline tool.

## 11. Outcome ≠ understanding — what a coach can legitimately infer

**Combining:**
- Phase 1: **practice behaviour ≠ learning** (§8; CF-1 v2);
- Audit 01: outcome doesn't identify decision quality;
- Audit 02: won/lost hides sequence quality;
- Audit 03: "progress" depends on the construct.

| Level | What it is | Can a coach infer it from observation? |
|---|---|---|
| L0 Outcome | Score, won/lost kickout, turnover | **Yes**, but it says **little** about anything below |
| L1 Actions and execution | What was attempted; did it come off | **Yes** (usually) |
| L2 Picture change | Advantage used / kept / lost; ladder level; construct-specific progress | **Yes, with a named construct and some judgment** |
| L3 Picture-appropriateness of the choice | Given the picture and plan, was it a reasonable option? | **Sometimes**, cautiously. Needs the visible alternatives and the plan; hindsight risk. |
| L4 What the player perceived / understood | — | **No** (needs player report, e.g. video recall) |
| L5 Learning (retention, transfer) | Change across time and contexts | **No** from single observations. Needs repeated observation over time in varied contexts (Phase 1 §8). |

**Rule:** coaches can routinely infer L0–L2, L3 only cautiously, and never L4–L5 from a single observation.

## 12. "How did the picture change?" — cross-problem stress test

| Problem | What "change" means | Construct |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Advantage used before recovery / kept / lost | Relationship to defenders |
| R5 (kickout) | First contact → possession → secure possession → attacking advantage | Outcome ladder |
| R2 | Territory / opponents bypassed / defensive disruption / scoring value; **these may disagree** | Contested; must be named |

**Heuristic under test:** *judge an action partly by what it did to the next picture.*

**Survives as a DEFENSIBLE COACHING HEURISTIC**, not as scientific fact, **with four conditions:**
1. **Name the construct.** "Better" must mean something observable: a defender left the lane, a teammate became free within range, possession secured, a shot from a valuable location.
2. **Allow for delayed effects.** An action may prepare the *next* action (the soccer "hockey assist" finding, cross-sport). Judge over a short sequence, not one frame.
3. **Guard against hindsight.** A worse next picture can follow a good choice (execution, opponent quality).
4. **It is not decision quality.** Picture change is L2 evidence. It informs, but does not settle, L3.

**Result: SURVIVES** (PV-SYNTHESIS, consistent with all three audits).
- The principles are **retained**, not dismissed. They supply shared vocabulary.
- They are **placed** at the resource / aim / feature levels rather than at the top.
- **Evidence status: conceptual.** No study compares coaching organised by principles vs by problems. **No claim is made that problem-first organisation teaches better.**

## 14. Linked game map (conceptual; not software; transitions largely unresearched)

```
                ┌───────────────── R5 contest / loose ball (S5) ─────────────────┐
                │  planned: own/opp kickout, throw-in   unplanned: spill, block    │
                └──────┬──────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                 won + opp unset │                      │ lost
                                 ▼                      ▼
   regain ─────────►  S1 → R1 use advantage      S3 → R6 defend while unsettled ◄──── loss / quick restart
                         │         │                   │            │
            defence recovers│    shot range            │ reorganised│  regain
                         ▼         ▼                   ▼            │
      line-break ◄──  S2 → R2 progress ──► R4 shot    S4 → R7 defend organised attack
      (back to S1)       │    │                              │
                  pressed│    │ loss ─────────────────► S3   └─► R8 regain by pressure ──► S1 / S2
                         ▼
                   S2p → R3 escape press ──► S2 / S1 (escaped) or S3 (lost)

   Score / wide ──► restart ──► R5 (planned)
```

**Status:**
- The **structure** is PV-SYNTHESIS.
- Individual transitions are **supported by logic and scattered associations**. Example: "won kickout → turnover" was the most common pre-2025 outcome (sub-elite), showing that S5 → S1/S2 is often followed by → S3.
- **No transition probabilities are known** for the current game.

## 15. Age and rules as context

**Integrated from the Age × Rules verdict (B):**
- Core recurring problems survive; important problems and relationships need **variants**.
- Architecture treatment: **AGE / FORMAT / CURRENT RULES is a CONTEXT input**, not a separate philosophy per age.

| Context | Architectural consequence |
|---|---|
| **U8 / U10** (5v5, 6v6) | **Likely outside** the U12–Adult model. Adult numerical relationships are arithmetically impossible, and states and problems are not established at these formats. |
| **U12** (≤13-a-side, reduced pitch, two skills per possession, no marks, no 4v3 / arc found) | Same states and most problems. Remove rule-created relationships (4v3, arc, marks). The carrier's options are constrained (skill limit). R5 planned is a **different restart**. |
| **U13 / U14** | **County-variable** transition. Dublin applies all FRC rules at U13–U16; Laois a subset at U13; the national "down to U14" statement's status is unclear. |
| **Adult / U14+ where FRC applies** | Full rule-created relationships |
| **Limerick** | **UNRESOLVED.** Must be established before any local testing. |

## 18. Reliability gate

**Gate:** before PáircVision turns the architecture into coaching guidance or product behaviour, test whether **independent observers** can **reliably identify** the proposed observable features from **representative footage**.

**What the pilot must establish** (definition only; not run here):

| Element | Requirement |
|---|---|
| **Footage** | Representative of intended use: wide-angle club and/or county footage (not broadcast only, which misses pre-kick structure); U14+ under FRC rules and U12 formats; clips of S1 (after regains), S2 (settled attack) and S5 (kickout contests) |
| **Coders** | At least two independent coders per clip. Ideally a mix of analyst and ordinary coach, to test whether agreement survives outside expert analysts. Blind to each other's codes and to match outcome where feasible. |
| **Coding manual** | Written operational definitions (with anchors) for each candidate feature, fixed before coding |
| **Features to test** (from the audits) | State (settled/unsettled); pressure on the carrier (none/loose/tight); defenders goal-side vs attackers ahead; free teammate within range (y/n); support jobs present (security / forward / width / occupy); first action type and direction; kickout: length band, contested (y/n), first touch, break direction, numbers within radius at contact, cover present, second possession; picture change (improved / kept / worsened, with a named construct); decision vs execution attribution |
| **Agreement statistics** | Chance-corrected agreement for categories (e.g. Cohen's/Fleiss' κ) and ICC for counts. **Thresholds set in advance.** Conventional benchmarks (e.g. κ ≥ 0.6 as "substantial") are conventions, not validation. |
| **Decisions the pilot must produce** | For each feature: **keep** (reliable), **revise** (redefine and retest) or **drop** (unreliable, e.g. inferential terms) |
| **Secondary questions** | Does live sideline coding agree with video coding? Does agreement differ between analysts and ordinary coaches? Which a-priori risky categories (Audit 02 §13.1) fail? |
| **Explicit non-goals** | Not validation of usefulness, not evidence of learning, not a test of whether features predict outcomes |

## 19. The simple coaching layer

**Attempt** (ordinary language; no internal taxonomy exposed):

1. **"What's the picture right now — are they organised or scrambling, and is our player under pressure?"**
2. **"What have I told them, and what am I leaving them to work out?"** (the Phase 1 question, unchanged)
3. **"Who is making options for the player on the ball — ahead, beside, behind, or by pulling a defender away?"**
4. **"Was that the choice or the skill?"**
5. **"Afterwards — was the picture better, the same or worse?"**

**30-second test (a parent unexpectedly coaching U12):**

| Question | Understandable in 30 s? | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Yes** ("organised or scrambling" is plain) | "Picture" may need one sentence of explanation |
| 2 | Yes | Abstract for a first-timer; relevant only once they plan anything |
| 3 | **Yes** | "Pulling a defender away" may need an example |
| 4 | **Yes** | Coaches may over-attribute to "skill" or to "choice"; that needs care, not more words |
| 5 | Yes | "Better" is undefined. Acceptable at this layer, but coaches may equate it with "went forward" (Audit 03 warns against this). |

**Result:**
- Questions **1, 3, 4 and 5** pass the 30-second test.
- Question 2 is the Phase 1 question and already the only coach-facing question there.
- Compressing to **four** (1, 3, 4, 5, with 2 kept for planners) is defensible.
- **These are not product copy.** They are a test of whether the architecture *can* be said simply. **Deep framework. Simple coaching.**

## 20. Reality check 1 — "The ball carrier has no support"

**Drill-library reading:** "Support is the problem → pick a support drill."

**V0.1 reading — questions to answer before "support" is diagnosed:**

| Question | Why it matters | Architecture layer |
|---|---|---|
| **Which state was it?** Regain vs unsettled defence (S1), settled attack (S2), pressed in own half (S2p), after a kickout break (S5)? | Support means different jobs in each (Audits 01, 02, 03) | State / problem |
| **What was the picture?** Pressure on the carrier; defenders goal-side; where teammates were | "No support" may mean "support was there but covered" | Picture |
| **Which support job was missing?** Security, forward option, width, occupying a defender? | "Support" is a set of jobs | Resource |
| **Was there no option, or an option not taken?** | Off-ball failure vs carrier's decision (or perception) | Coupled decisions |
| **Did supporters create the problem?** (crowding; everyone ahead; nobody back) | Movement can worsen the picture | Coupled decisions |
| **Was it planned structure?** (roles left nobody in the space; 4v3 obligations) | Structure, not players | Planned structure / constraint |
| **Was it a rule or age effect?** (U12 two-skills limit forces early release; the goalkeeper unavailable under adult rules) | Context | Context / constraint |
| **Was it execution?** (carrier couldn't reach the option; hand pass failed) | Not a support problem | Execution |
| **Where did the observation come from?** (a directionless grid or a real game) | Practice may remove the information (Phase 1 GI-2) | Phase 1 link |

**Reality check result:**
- The same sentence can describe **at least six different problems**.
- V0.1 **does not tell the coach the answer**. It tells them **what to look at before choosing a remedy**.
- That is an improvement in *diagnosis*. It says nothing about which remedy works, which is **Coach Intervention / learning territory, not researched**.

## 21. Reality check 2 — "They keep it in a grid but panic when pressed in their own half"

**V0.1 decomposition:**

| Layer | Content |
|---|---|
| **Context** | Age and rules. Adult/U14+: 4v3, and the goalkeeper can't be used to recycle. U12: no 4v3 found; goalkeeper rule unknown; two-skills limit. |
| **State / problem** | S2p → **R3 keep and move the ball under a high press**. The grid is **not** this state: no direction, no press trigger, no rule-created numbers. |
| **Entry routes** | Own kickout secured (R5 exit), regain deep (S1 that became pressed), settled possession pushed back |
| **Picture** | Pressure on the carrier; pressers vs available options; space behind or beside the press; local numbers |
| **Information available to players** | Press trigger and direction, free teammate beyond the press, which defender is coming. **Much of it is absent in a grid** (GI-2). |
| **Coupled decisions** | Off-ball: create escape options (security, width, depth beyond the press). On-ball: secure vs play through vs go long. |
| **Options → actions** | Short release, switch, carry past the first presser, long kick. **Several are legitimate.** |
| **Execution** | Kick range and accuracy under pressure; hand pass in contact |
| **Picture change** | Escaped (→ S2 or S1), held (still S2p), lost (→ S3, high-danger location) |
| **Planned structure** | Build-up roles and shape may legitimately be planned; the escape itself adapts to the press |

**Does V0.1 genuinely improve understanding?**
- **Yes, as description and diagnosis.** It shows why the grid transfer fails:
  - the grid lacks the **state**, the **information** and the **rule-created relationships** of the real problem;
  - "panic" may be **perception** (not observable), **decision** (sometimes observable), **options not created** (off-ball, observable) or **execution** (visible).
- **No.** It cannot say which fix works. That remains UNKNOWN.

## 22. Philosophy status

**Phase 1 Pass 2 verdict:** C, a practice-design framework, not a coaching philosophy. Three components were identified:
- **WHAT** to coach → Game Understanding;
- **HOW** to construct practice → Phase 1;
- **HOW** to coach within practice → Coach Intervention;
- plus learning/progression across all three.

**Does Phase 2 change it?**

### **C — still frameworks/tools rather than a philosophy.**

**Why not B:**
- Phase 2 adds a V0.1 **description of WHAT exists in the game**. It does not add positions on:
  - **what is worth developing first**;
  - **how coaches should intervene**;
  - **how learning is sequenced**.
- As in Pass 1, a coach who talks constantly and one who never speaks could both use V0.1.

**What has emerged** (noted, not upgraded): a **consistent epistemic stance** across Phases 1 and 2:
- conditional legitimacy;
- judge relative to the picture;
- outcome ≠ decision ≠ learning;
- name your construct;
- rules and age as context.

That stance is a **precondition** for a philosophy. It is not one.

**What would move this to B:** Coach Intervention research plus an explicit, evidence-labelled position on what to develop and in what order.

## 24. Next step (exactly one)

### **The reliability pilot** (as defined in §18).

**Why this, and not the alternatives:**
- **It is the binding constraint.** Every downstream use depends on coaches being able to see what the architecture names. That includes Coach Intervention, practice translation and even further audits.
- If key features cannot be reliably observed, the architecture must change *before* more is built on it.
- **Coach Intervention research** is the natural next *content* step (the philosophy gap, §22). Starting it before observability is known risks building intervention guidance on unobservable features.
- **Practice-design translation** is premature for the same reason. Phase 1 is ready, but the targets are not yet observable.
- **Another Game Understanding audit** (e.g. out-of-possession R6/R8) would widen coverage but not resolve the binding constraint. **Recommended as the follow-on**, not the next step.
- **Technical-skill integration:** the layer exists, but content should follow once observation is established.

**Not performed here.**

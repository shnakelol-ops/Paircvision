# PáircVision Phase 1 — Lever 02 NUMBERS: Final Evidence & Falsification Gate

**Parent document:** [`phase1-lever-02-numbers.md`](./phase1-lever-02-numbers.md) (v1 evidence review)
**Evidence standard inherited from:** [`phase1-lever-01-space-final-gate.md`](./phase1-lever-01-space-final-gate.md)
**Type:** Verification and falsification gate. Research only: no drills, sessions, UI or production code.
**Status:** Gate v1, September 2026. **Where this gate and the v1 NUMBERS review differ, this gate wins.**

> **Rule of this document.** "Produced / appeared" describes behaviour *during* a constrained game. "Learned" is reserved for evidence from retention (after a delay), transfer (to an unconstrained game or reference game) or competition.
>
> **Four separations that must never collapse:**
> - more decisions ≠ faster decisions ≠ better decisions ≠ learned decisions
> - more support options ≠ better support decisions
> - more successful possession ≠ better attacking play
> - finding an artificial spare ≠ recognising a natural spare; compact defending under an underload ≠ learned defensive organisation

## 0. Method and access limits

- **Scope.** A targeted attack on the v1 conclusions. It looks specifically for **contradictory** evidence, checks the author lists and headline figures used in v1, and adds evidence missed there.
- **Access.** The network proxy blocked full text on PubMed/PMC, Frontiers, SAGE, Taylor & Francis, Ovid/JSCR, ResearchGate, MDPI and university repositories. **All verification is abstract-level or index-level** (search-engine snapshots of publisher pages, abstracts and repository metadata).
  - Where a claim rests on a figure reported in an abstract, it says **(abstract-level)**.
  - Where the author list or attribution comes only from a search index, it says **(attribution via index)**.
  - Unresolved items are marked **†**.
- **Labels (unchanged):**
  - **RESEARCH EVIDENCE**: peer-reviewed.
  - **ESTABLISHED PRACTICE**: governing-body or common coaching use.
  - **PRACTITIONER OPINION**: coaching media, industry or league statistics.
  - **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS**: our inference.

  Confidence is H / M / L / VL.

## 2. The eight emerging propositions: falsification verdicts

Scale: **SUPPORTED · PARTIALLY SUPPORTED · PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC · PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS · CONTRADICTED**

### P1. Numbers change the game even when pitch dimensions stay fixed → **SUPPORTED**

- **For:**
  - Fixed-area studies show changed load, technical actions and team shape: Castellano et al. (2016); Bonney, Ball, Berry & Larkin (2020), AFL 5v5/5v6/6v6/7v7; Nunes et al. (2020), 4v2–4v6 in 30×25 m; Coutinho et al. (2022), 11v11–11v14 on a fixed pitch.
  - Natural experiment: an 11v10 dismissal on an unchanged pitch changes possession, passing, shots and work-rate.
  - Even at constant ApP, fewer players raises intensity (Hill-Haas et al., 2009).
- **Against:** none found.
- **Caveat:** at fixed dimensions, numbers and ApP change together. P1 is about "the game changes", not "numbers alone cause it".

### P2. Numerical superiority is not the same as spatial superiority → **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

- **For:**
  - AFL 2019 tracking: numerical advantage raised inside-50 scoring likelihood, but **more total players inside 50 lowered scoring probability regardless of numerical advantage** (PLoS ONE 2021; abstract-level).
  - Silva et al. (2015): reaching the same ApP by changing **numbers** produced more free space around each player. Changing **dimensions** produced more advantageous local numerical relations. Numbers and space act differently.
  - Futsal 5v4 and handball 7v6: superiority converts modestly and carries counter-risk (confounded).
- **Against / limits:** most direct evidence comes from **one** match-analysis study (AFL) plus SSG interaction studies. No study has manipulated "numbers-up but congested" vs "numbers-up with space" in training.
- **Verdict wording:** *Being numbers-up does not guarantee usable space; congestion can cancel it. This is shown in AFL match data and consistent with SSG interaction studies. It is not yet tested in training design.*

### P3. Local numerical relationships matter more to the immediate decision than total team numbers → **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED; the "more than" part is a PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS**

- **For:**
  - Local numerical dominance relates to scoring opportunities (Vilar et al., 2013).
  - Dyadic decisions are governed by local interpersonal distances (Passos et al., 2008).
  - AFL advantage effects were zone- and phase-specific.
- **Contradicting / complicating evidence (searched for specifically):**
  1. **Total numbers matter a lot on their own.** An 11v10 dismissal changes whole-team possession, passes and shots, and lowers the sanctioned team's scoring, even though each local situation varies (Ridder et al., 1994; Bar-Eli et al., 2006; IJPAS 2016).
  2. **Total density can override local advantage:** more bodies inside 50 ↓ scoring *regardless of advantage* (AFL 2021).
  3. **Pressure, not headcount, may be what matters.** In futsal, goals clustered where defensive pressure was lower (index-level summary of futsal zone analyses †). "Local numbers" and "local pressure/space" are confounded in every study.
- **No study compares** the predictive power of local vs total numbers for individual decisions.
- **Verdict wording:** *Local numbers around the ball are an important part of the picture, but so are total numbers, total congestion and defensive pressure. Whether local numbers matter "more" is untested.*

### P4. GIVEN, RECOGNISED, CREATED and EXPLOITED advantages are different game problems → **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS** (a useful conceptual model; partial research grounding)

- **Grounding that exists:**
  - Research distinguishes **experimentally imposed** superiority (SSG manipulations) from **naturally emerging** local superiority (match analyses: Vilar et al., 2013; AFL 2021).
  - "Creating numerical superiority" is named as a principle of invasion games (e.g., Silva et al., 2014, introduction).
  - Match studies describe **created** overloads, e.g., LGFA kickout "Overload" strategies on long kickouts (McColgan et al., 2026) and counter-attacks against unbalanced defences (Tenga et al., 2010).
  - Exploitation under time limits has been manipulated (Rochael & Praça, 2024).
- **Not grounded:** no study treats **recognition** and **creation** as separately measured, separately trainable capacities. No study shows that the four behave as distinct problems in learning (e.g., one improving without the others).
- **Status:** keep it as a **PáircVision organising model for observation**, explicitly labelled as such. **Do not present it as a developmental sequence** (see §3).

### P5. Permanent floaters change the nature of the problem rather than simply make it easier → **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**

- **For:**
  - Floaters shift which tactical principles are used, not just how often possession succeeds. Outside or sideline floaters → ↓ penetration and concentration, ↑ width/length use and defensive unity (Roca & Teoldo, 2017; Carvalho et al., 2021).
  - Floater rules change team length and width, and differently by age (Praça et al., 2022).
  - Floater position (central / wide / inner zone) changes performance (Paul et al., 2025 †).
  - Outer floaters change dispersion and shape (Castellano et al., 2016).
- **Not evidenced:** effects on **scanning**, **transition** after losing the ball, or **progression in metres**. These are unstudied.
- **Verdict wording:** *Floaters change which solutions the game rewards. In the studies available, attackers used more width and possession and fewer penetrating actions, and defenders compacted more. Effects on scanning and transition are unknown.*

### P6. Defensive underloads can produce defensive behaviours such as compactness without demonstrating defensive learning → **SUPPORTED**

- **For:**
  - Compactness, synchronisation and defensive unity are *produced* acutely under inferiority (Silva et al., 2014; Gonçalves et al., 2016; Coutinho et al., 2022; Roca & Teoldo, 2017).
  - **No study was found that tests subsequent equal-number defending** after underload training.
- **Nearest evidence:** Pizarro et al. (2020) (n = 8, no control group, published commentary). Defensive actions improved after attack-focused sessions in which defenders were outnumbered. This is **weak, confounded evidence** that some defensive improvement *may* accompany such training. It does not show transfer to equal-number defending.

### P7. Numerical superiority does not guarantee successful exploitation → **SUPPORTED**

- **For (by factor):**

| Factor | Evidence | Label | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Real matches | NHL 5v4 power play ≈20–25% conversion; handball 7v6 ≈42–53% effective with ≈16% goal-to-goal counter shots; futsal 5v4+GK no better than 4-player attack (confounded) | PRACTITIONER OPINION (league stats) + RESEARCH EVIDENCE (observational) | M |
| **Magnitude** | Graded, not guaranteed, effects on load, spread and exploration (§3) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (A) | L–M |
| **Location** | AFL advantage effects differ by zone; teams are often outnumbered in their forward 50 | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | M |
| **Space / density** | More bodies inside 50 ↓ scoring despite advantage | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | M |
| **Timing / time pressure** | Time pressure lowered decision and execution quality in 5v4 / 4v4 counter-attack SSGs (Rochael & Praça, 2024) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (A) | L–M |
| **Defensive organisation** | Counter-attacks vs *imbalanced* defences more effective than vs organised ones (Tenga et al., 2010) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | M |
| **Duration of advantage** | **No direct study** | — | VL |

### P8. Success during an artificial overload does not show that players can recognise, create or exploit natural overloads after it is removed → **SUPPORTED**

- **No study** trains with artificial overloads, removes them, and measures recognition, creation or exploitation of natural overloads in balanced games or competition.
- Práxedes et al. (2018) measured pass decision-making and execution before and after a superiority programme, but not the detection of natural overloads.
- **Symmetry caveat:** absence of evidence is not evidence of absence. The honest status is **UNKNOWN**, not "it doesn't work".

## 3. The central question: does an overload teach an overload?

These are **not** assumed to be a developmental sequence.

| Transfer path | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Practising a **GIVEN** overload → better **exploitation of a given** overload *during* practice | Acute: superiority ↑ passes and progression opportunities. Práxedes et al. (2018): average-skill U12 pass decisions and execution improved over 14 superiority sessions | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** (one dataset, average-skill children, non-counterbalanced) |
| GIVEN → improved **RECOGNITION** of natural overloads | None | **UNKNOWN** |
| GIVEN → improved **CREATION** of overloads | None | **UNKNOWN** |
| Practising 3v2 → improved exploitation of *naturally occurring* 3v2 | None | **UNKNOWN** |
| **Temporary / changing** overloads → broader solution search | Canton et al. (2019): ↑ long-term exploratory breadth in U23, not U15 (acute, within session) | **Acute only**; learning **UNKNOWN** |
| RECOGNITION → CREATION (or the reverse) | None | **UNKNOWN** |
| Created overloads in competition → outcomes | LGFA kickouts: overload strategies associated with winning long kickouts (descriptive). AFL: advantage ↑ scoring likelihood | **Match association only**; not training evidence |

**Bottom line.** Research shows that **given** overloads *produce* more passing, spreading and easier possession. It shows, once, that they can accompany improved pass decisions in average-skill children. **Whether an overload teaches players to recognise, create or exploit overloads in the real game is unknown.**

## 4. Magnitude of overload

| Question | Evidence | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Are effects linear? | Studies with graded conditions: Nunes et al. (2020), 4v2 → 4v6; Coutinho et al. (2022), 11v11 → 11v14; Torrents et al. (2016), 4v3 / 4v5 / 4v7; Gonçalves et al. (2016), low vs high inferiority. Effects generally **increase with the imbalance**. Torrents reports the reduction in exploration was **"especially clear"** for 7v4, suggesting larger effects at the extreme. | **Graded; possibly non-linear at the extremes. Shape not established.** (RESEARCH EVIDENCE, L–M) |
| When does an overload stop being a decision problem and just make possession easy? | Large superiority (e.g., 7v4) → **least exploration** (Torrents et al., 2016); superiority → ↓ physiological load (Rumpf et al., 2026) | Consistent with "large overloads simplify the problem". **No threshold is supported by evidence.** |
| Does +1 preserve defender choice better than +2/+3? | Not measured (no study codes defender decision options) | **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS** |
| Does extreme underloading produce passivity or learned helplessness? | High inferiority ↓ physical output and exploration (Rumpf et al., 2026 review, abstract-level). But across six studies inferior players had **higher** HR and RPE than superior players. | "Passivity" in *some* conditions is plausible. **"Learned helplessness" has no evidence and must not be claimed.** |
| Is there an optimal magnitude? | No learning study compares magnitudes | **No evidence. Do not state one.** |

## 5. Floaters / neutrals: deep check

| Floater condition | What the evidence shows | Label | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Inside the playing area** | Changes tactical behaviour (Moniz et al., 2020, *J Hum Kinet* 71:167–177 †, direction unverified); load higher than outside floaters, lower than regular players | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | L |
| **Outside the boundary (sideline / end)** | ↓ penetration (−40%), ↓ offensive coverage, ↓ concentration, ↑ defensive unity (Carvalho et al., 2021). More width and length use in the opponent's half, and defensive compaction (Roca & Teoldo, 2017). Lowest floater load. | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (abstract-level; one research network) | L–M |
| **Fixed to a zone / position** | Central vs wide vs inner-zone floaters perform differently (Paul et al., 2025 †) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | L |
| **Free-moving** | Implied in inside-floater designs; no separate comparison | — | VL |
| **Always available** (permanent) | All studies above are permanent-floater designs | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (A) | L–M |
| **Temporarily activated** | **No study found** | — | VL |
| **Touch-restricted** | Outside floaters are commonly 1-touch and inside floaters 2-touch in these designs, so touch limits are **confounded** with position | — | VL |
| **Removed** (with vs without) | Floaters off → more penetration, concentration, 1v1s (Roca & Teoldo, 2017) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | L–M |
| **One vs two floaters; floater rules by age** | Floaters ↑ width; the two-floater format ↓ length (U13/U14; Praça et al., 2022) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | L–M |

**Verifying the "≈40%" claim.** Verified at **abstract level**: −40.12% in the *count of penetration-principle actions* coded by FUT-SAT (Carvalho et al., 2021). **Allowed wording:** *"In one study, teams made about 40% fewer penetrating actions when outside floaters were used."* **Not allowed:** "40% less forward progress", "40% fewer line-breaks" or "floaters make teams 40% less attacking".

**Reliance.** **No study measured reliance or dependency on neutrals**, nor behaviour after floaters were removed. PáircVision should say only:

> *"Neutral players change which solutions the game rewards. Teams tend to use them repeatedly as a safe option and attempt fewer penetrating actions. Whether players become reliant on them is not known."*

## 9. Gaelic football and hurling

**Recheck result.** Repeated searches found **no study manipulating player numbers or overloads in Gaelic football practice**. The claim stands.

**Hurling (verified, abstract-level).** Malone & Collins (2017, *JSCR* 31(6):1493–1499): 4-a-side produced higher relative intensity than 5- or 6-a-side, regardless of game design or goal type. **Physical and physiological only.**

**New GAA match evidence (descriptive, not causal, not training).**
- McColgan, Bradley, Earle, Gaul & Martin (2026), *Applied Sciences* 16(7):3277: 89 games and 2,172 kickouts, 2019–2023 TG4 All-Ireland Senior LGF Championship. Four offensive and three defensive kickout strategies were identified. Some findings (≈0.42 net points per retained kickout; a 11+ player zonal press and a "Flat 4" as most effective) are attributed via index to this study and/or its companion (*IJPAS* 2025, 25(3):405–418). **Which figure belongs to which paper is unresolved †.**
- The "Overload" strategy was frequently used, and appeared most successful, for **long kickouts where the kicking team created a numerical advantage in an area, mainly the wings**.
- This is **created**, **local** overload evidence in a Gaelic code. It is associational, from female inter-county players.

**Context only (never causal):**
- Black card / sin bin (10 minutes at 14 players). GAA-reported scoring rises after 2014 had several simultaneous changes.

- Goalkeeper involvement in open play.

## A. What PáircVision can responsibly teach coaches today

1. **Changing numbers changes the game, even when the cones don't move.** Fewer players means more touches and more running. An extra attacker makes keeping the ball easier and makes the defending team squeeze together.
2. **Numbers and space work together.** The same numbers in a tight or a large area are different games. The same area per player reached in different ways is also a different game.
3. **Being numbers-up doesn't guarantee a score.** It depends on where the extra player is, how crowded the area is, how organised the defence is and how quickly you act.
4. **Big overloads make the problem easier and narrower.** Teams find fewer different solutions when they are well up in numbers.
5. **Floaters change what the game rewards.** In the available studies, teams used width and possession more and attempted fewer penetrating actions. Floaters themselves work less hard.
6. **Outnumbered teams usually squeeze together and often work harder physically.** That doesn't mean they are learning to defend.
7. **Small formats increase involvement**, especially for quieter or less dominant children. Involvement is not the same as learning.
8. **Seeing players use a spare player in a practice game shows the game is producing it.** It doesn't show they will spot or create a spare player in a match.

## B. Present only as a coaching option or hypothesis

1. GIVEN / RECOGNISED / CREATED / EXPLOITED as a way to **think about and observe** overloads (a PáircVision model, not research-proven categories and not a sequence).
2. Overloads that **come and go** (transition, late defender, changing every minute) may be more match-like than permanent ones. There is acute support for broader solution search in adults only; learning is unknown.
3. A **+1** overload may keep more of a real decision for both sides than +2 or +3 (a hypothesis; no threshold evidence).
4. Removing floaters, or making them temporary, as a way to check whether players still penetrate (an observation idea, untested).
5. Superiority may help **average-ability younger players** improve passing decisions (one small dataset).
6. Created kickout overloads (LGFA evidence) as a Gaelic-specific example of numbers used deliberately (descriptive association).

## C. What PáircVision must NOT claim

1. ❌ "Overloads teach players to recognise, create or exploit overloads in matches." **UNKNOWN.**
2. ❌ "3v2 practice improves players' use of real 3v2s."
3. ❌ "Underloads teach defending" or "improve defending with equal numbers."
4. ❌ "Underloads cause learned helplessness." There is no evidence.
5. ❌ "Players become reliant on floaters." This was not measured.
6. ❌ "Floaters make teams 40% less attacking" or cut "forward progress by 40%". The figure is a count of coded penetration actions in one study.
7. ❌ Any "optimal" overload size or threshold.
8. ❌ "Local numbers matter more than total numbers." Untested; total numbers and congestion also matter.
9. ❌ "Numerical inferiority makes players lazy / passive" as a general rule. Outnumbered players often work *harder* physically, and passivity is plausible only in extreme underloads.
10. ❌ Any Gaelic football or hurling evidence on tactical or learning effects of numbers in *training*. None exists.
11. ❌ Causal claims from the black card, sin-bin data, the 2025 rules or goalkeeper use.
12. ❌ "Numerical superiority creates space" as a universal. It can congest.
13. ❌ More decisions, faster decisions or more possession presented as better decisions or better attacking play.

## D. Important contradictions

1. **Work rate under inferiority.** Six SSG studies show *higher* internal load for outnumbered players, and the ten-man match case shows *more* running. The 2026 review also reports *reduced* output under **high** inferiority. **Likely resolution:** moderate inferiority → more effort; extreme inferiority → retreat and protect. Plausible, not proven.
2. **Exploration.** A permanent large advantage → ↓ exploration (Torrents et al., 2016). Changing imbalances → ↑ exploration in adults (Canton et al., 2019). **Partly resolved:** the difference appears to be *permanent vs temporary* imbalance, and age.
3. **Superiority and space.** In SSGs superiority expands the team; in AFL matches more bodies in the scoring zone cut scoring.
4. **Floaters.** More possession-type actions, fewer penetrating ones. Whether that is "better" depends on the target.
5. **Local vs total numbers.** Local dominance predicts chances (Vilar et al., 2013), yet whole-team dismissals shift everything, and congestion overrides advantage.
6. **Práxedes.** Improvement after superiority but not after equality. This could reflect superiority's value, or order, ceiling or timing effects.
7. **Real overloads convert modestly** (power play, handball 7v6, futsal 5v4+GK), while practice overloads usually make possession easy. Practice overloads may be **easier than real ones**.

## E. Remaining research gaps

1. A retention and transfer test of GIVEN → RECOGNISE / CREATE / EXPLOIT in balanced reference games and competition.
2. Permanent vs temporary vs transition overloads compared for **learning**.
3. Magnitude (+1 / +2 / +3) compared for learning, with defender-choice measures.
4. Equal-number defending after underload training, with a control group.
5. Floaters: temporary vs permanent; touch limits separated from floater position; behaviour **after** floaters are removed; effects on scanning and transition.
6. Duration of natural advantages in matches (how long overloads last) in any code, especially Gaelic.
7. Gaelic football and hurling: *any* numbers-manipulation training study; natural overload mapping around kickouts, puckouts, breaks and turnovers (building on the LGFA work).
8. Local vs total numbers vs local pressure as predictors of decisions.
9. Publication of the NCT06092294 RCT results.
10. Low-cost coach-observable indicators of natural overload recognition and use.

## F. Plain-language NUMBERS explanation for a volunteer Gaelic coach

> **Changing the numbers changes the game.**
>
> - **Fewer players a side:** everyone gets more ball and more running. It's great for getting quieter players involved.
> - **Give the attack an extra player** (say 4v3): they'll keep the ball more easily and spread out, and the defenders will squeeze together. That's useful, but it's *you* giving them the spare player.
> - **A big overload** (say 6v3): it gets easy. Players stop looking for different ways to play, because anything works.
> - **Floaters or neutrals:** passing gets easier, but teams tend to go sideways more and forward less. Watch whether your players still try to break lines.
> - **Being short a player:** players usually squeeze in and often work harder. That's not the same as learning how to defend when numbers are even.
>
> **More players isn't always better.** Pile too many into the scoring area and you can crowd yourselves out, even with a spare man.
>
> **The big question.** If your players use the spare man in a 3v2, that shows the game is working. It doesn't yet show they'll *spot* a spare man, or *make* one, in a real match.
>
> To find out:
> 1. Go back to even numbers.
> 2. Look again next week.
> 3. In matches, ask: when we had more players near the ball, did we see it, and did we use it before it disappeared?
>
> *Honest note:* nearly all of this comes from soccer and Aussie Rules. In Gaelic games, research so far covers mainly fitness, plus some kickout analysis in ladies' football showing that teams deliberately crowd an area to win long kickouts.

## G. Recommended frozen NUMBERS v1 principles

1. **N1 (Numbers change the game).** Changing numbers changes involvement, intensity, team shape and the solutions that work, even on a fixed pitch. *SUPPORTED.*
2. **N2 (Numbers are not space).** Numbers and space interact. Area per player alone cannot describe the game, and being numbers-up is not the same as having usable space. *SUPPORTED / PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.*
3. **N3 (Advantage ≠ exploitation).** Numerical superiority raises the chance of success but does not guarantee it. Magnitude, location, congestion, timing and defensive organisation all matter. *SUPPORTED.*
4. **N4 (Bigger overloads simplify).** Larger overloads make the problem easier and narrow the solutions players search for. There is no evidence-based "right" size. *PARTIALLY SUPPORTED; no threshold.*
5. **N5 (Floaters change the problem).** Neutral players change which solutions are rewarded (in the evidence: more width and possession, fewer penetrating actions) and have unrepresentative loads. Reliance is unknown. *PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.*
6. **N6 (Produced ≠ learned).** Behaviour produced by an overload or underload (spare-player use, compact defending) is not evidence that players can recognise, create, exploit or defend overloads once numbers are even. *SUPPORTED; transfer UNKNOWN.*
7. **N7 (Observation model, not a sequence).** GIVEN, RECOGNISED, CREATED and EXPLOITED advantages are a PáircVision lens for observing play. They are not proven distinct skills or a learning order. *PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS.*
8. **N8 (GAA honesty).** For Gaelic games, NUMBERS principles are borrowed from other codes unless they are marked GAA-supported (hurling intensity; LGFA kickout overloads, descriptive only). *Evidence status rule.*

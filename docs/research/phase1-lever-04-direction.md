# PáircVision Phase 1 — Lever 04: DIRECTION — Scientific Audit

**Type:** Scientific evidence audit. Research only: no drills, sessions, UI, product features or production code.
**Frozen context:** [SPACE gate](./phase1-lever-01-space-final-gate.md) · [NUMBERS gate](./phase1-lever-02-numbers-final-gate.md) · [TIME gate](./phase1-lever-03-time-final-gate.md)
**Status:** v1 audit, September 2026. A separate Final Evidence & Falsification Gate is expected to follow.
**Companion:** [DIRECTION practitioner & coaching intelligence audit](./phase1-lever-04-direction-practitioner-audit.md).

> **Central question.** What actually happens when a coach gives a practice a meaningful direction or destination? How does adding, removing, reversing or manipulating direction change what players perceive, where they move, what decisions they have, and what they may ultimately learn?
>
> **Starting stance: adversarial.** We do **not** assume directional practice beats non-directional possession. We tried to falsify that belief.
>
> **Standing principles carried forward:**
> - Practice behaviour ≠ learning ≠ transfer to competition.
> - A constraint has no fixed difficulty independent of the player (frozen cross-lever finding).
> - Knowing that difficulty varies does **not** tell us how to set it for each player.

---

## 0. Method, access and evidence markers

- Targeted searches for comparisons of possession and directional games, multiple targets, scoring modes, directional rules (offside, pass-count rules), transition, pass value / progression analytics, "through / around / over", GAA passing and territory evidence, and learning / transfer studies.
- **Access.** The network proxy blocked full text (Frontiers, T&F, SAGE, Elsevier, PMC, ResearchGate, university repositories). **No FULL TEXT was accessed.**
  - **AO** = abstract only (publisher or PubMed abstract via index).
  - **SIO** = search-index only.
  - **UNR** = unresolved author, figure or detail.
- **Labels:** SUPPORTED · PARTIALLY SUPPORTED · CONTRADICTED · UNKNOWN · PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC · PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS. Confidence H / M / L / VL.
- **Honest summary.**
  - Almost all evidence is **soccer / futsal / basketball, acute and within the practice game**.
  - One 2026 narrative review directly compares possession and scoring formats (14 studies).
  - **No study** tests whether directional practice improves the perception or exploitation of directional opportunities **after the constraint is removed**, or in competition.
  - **No Gaelic football or hurling study** manipulates direction in practice.

## 1. Defining DIRECTION

### 1.1 What "direction" actually covers

| Manipulation | What it changes | Most appropriate home |
|---|---|---|
| Attacking / defending a fixed goal | Creates a **scoring objective** and a **thing to protect** | **DIRECTION (core)** |
| End zones, target players, target gates, line goals | Changes the *form* of the objective | **DIRECTION (core)** |
| Multiple or opposite targets; scoring either way | Changes the *number and location* of objectives | **DIRECTION (core)** |
| Non-directional possession | Removes the objective; success = keeping the ball | **DIRECTION (the "zero" condition)** |
| Line-breaking, territorial progression as a scoring rule | Redefines **what counts as progress** | DIRECTION (core) |
| Direction reversing after turnover | Objective flips at transition | **DIRECTION × TIME** |
| Direction changing on a coach cue | Objective changes on an external signal | **DIRECTION × INFORMATION** |
| "Forward only", "no back pass", "must cross line in X passes", "must use wide player", "must switch before scoring" | Removes or mandates actions | **RULES** (cross-link to DIRECTION) |
| Inside / outside, central / wide, channels | Sub-areas of space | **SPACE** (zones / channels, frozen) |
| Through / around / over | Describes attacking *solutions* | **Observation language** (see §6), not a manipulation |
| Forward / backward / lateral movement; player vs ball direction | Behaviour, not a manipulation | **Emergent outcome** |
| Offside | A rule that constrains depth relative to direction | **RULES** (cross-link) |

### 1.2 One lever or several?

**PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS (evidence-consistent):** DIRECTION is coherent only if defined narrowly: **the structure of objectives**, meaning where scoring and protection happen, how many objectives there are, and what counts as progress.
- Directional *restrictions* belong to RULES.
- Channels and zones belong to SPACE.
- Cue-based switching involves INFORMATION.
- Movement direction is an outcome.

Defined this way, DIRECTION is closer to one lever than TIME was. The evidence below still shows **different target forms produce different behaviours**.

## 2. Directional vs non-directional practice

### 2.1 The best direct comparison

**Rumpf, Jäger, Lloyd & Lochmann (2026)**, narrative mini-review, *Frontiers in Sports and Active Living* (doi:10.3389/fspor.2026.1784555). **14 studies, 221 male players** (5 adult studies, n = 90; 9 youth studies, n = 131). AO / SIO.

| Outcome | Possession vs scoring games | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Ball touches | Possession **+38.7% total, +60.4% individual** | RESEARCH EVIDENCE |
| Passes | Possession **+99.0%**; passes per player **+55.8%** | RESEARCH EVIDENCE |
| Heart rate | Possession mean HR **+4.35%**, max **+6.43%**, time >90% HRmax **+39.7%** | RESEARCH EVIDENCE |
| Running | Possession total distance **+12.6%**, relative distance +8.43%, speed +14.2% | RESEARCH EVIDENCE |
| Perception | Possession RPE **+24.2%**, mental challenge **+18.4%** | RESEARCH EVIDENCE |
| Enjoyment | Possession **−6.45%**, especially vs games with goalkeepers | RESEARCH EVIDENCE |
| Tactical | "Limited evidence": more players involved for extended periods, exploring more space, with decreasing distance to the nearest opponent | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (thin) |

**Coaching surprise.** Adding goals **reduces** touches and passes. Possession games are **more** technically involving and more physically and mentally demanding, not less.

### 2.2 Directionality manipulated directly

**Coutinho, Gonçalves, Kelly, Santos, Figueiredo, Soares & Travassos (2024)**, *IJSSC* 19(6):2438–2448 (AO). 18 youth players (≈13.6 y); **NO direction vs MULTI direction vs ONE direction.**

| Condition | Observed (acute) |
|---|---|
| **NO direction** (possession) | **Highest spatial exploration index** |
| **MULTI direction** | Highest game pace, total distance and jogging distance; greater distance to nearest teammate |
| **ONE direction** | **Better passing decision-making**; greater distance to nearest opponent |

Authors' interpretation: NO direction for spatial exploration, MULTI for load and pace, ONE for ball control and passing actions. Small sample, acute.

### 2.3 Other relevant comparisons

- **Possession vs small goals, 3v3 youth** (*J Sports Sci Med*, 2025; SPACE gate): possession games gave higher HR and passing success. Small-goal games changed shooting and decision patterns with field size. RESEARCH EVIDENCE (L).
- **Representative SSCG vs maintaining-ball-possession games (MBPG) vs progression-to-target games** (*Front Psychol*, 2019, 10:1554; U15 / U17 non-elite, n = 20):
  - rule and configuration changes altered team tactical behaviour differently by age;
  - **younger players had more difficulty in MBPG in the smaller configuration**;
  - **more rule manipulations reduced exploratory behaviour**.

  AO; authors UNR.
- **Basketball RCT (8 weeks), baskets vs possession games** (*J Sports Sci Med*, 2024; n = 50, ≈16.7 y): basket games improved **neuromuscular** outcomes more. **Physical learning only.** AO.
- **Positional games vs official matches** (pro soccer, descriptive): physical profiles differ. SIO; not central.

## 3. Attacking the possession-grid assumption

**PáircVision's concern (hypothesis):** players can become good at keeping the ball in a box without becoming good at escaping pressure, advancing territory or attacking a goal.

| Question | Evidence | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Do non-directional possession games improve useful match behaviour? | **No study tests match transfer.** An 8-week rondo RCT (U13, n = 24) improved **Torrance creativity and problem-solving test scores**, which are generic, non-sport tests (*Biol Sport*, 2025) | **UNKNOWN** for match behaviour; the rondo outcome is **far-transfer on a paper test** and must not be read as game learning |
| Do possession games transfer to directional match play? | No test found | **UNKNOWN** |
| Do possession games improve support, scanning, decisions or retention? | Acutely they give **more touches and passes** and **more spatial exploration**. No learning data | **Acute: supported. Learning: UNKNOWN** |
| Do directional games produce better transfer? | No test found | **UNKNOWN** |
| Do directional games reduce technical involvement? | **Yes, acutely**: fewer touches and passes (Rumpf et al., 2026) | **SUPPORTED (acute)** |
| Are possession games sometimes more appropriate? | They are more involving and more demanding, with more spatial exploration. Younger players found small possession games **harder** (*Front Psychol*, 2019) | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED:** depends on objective and player |
| Artificial rules vs free play, tested in a free reference match | U14 / U16, n = 32; four sessions; pre/post test in a **non-constrained 8-a-side match**. **Average outcomes barely changed**; individual responses differed widely (Gonzalez-Artetxe et al., 2023, *Biol Sport*; same programme also in *PESP*, 2022, 27(5)). Rule type UNR | Rare reference-game design; **no clear average transfer effect** |

**What a non-directional possession game appears to train well (acute evidence only):**
- more touches and passes;
- more spatial exploration;
- higher physical and mental demand.

It trains these for the **possession problem**: keeping the ball under pressure, with no objective to protect or attack.

**What changes when a destination is introduced (acute):**
- fewer touches and passes;
- players (in one study) made better passing decisions relative to the target;
- the defence gains something to protect;
- the problem becomes **"keep the ball *in order to* reach somewhere"**.

**Neither is shown to transfer better.**

## 4. Progression is not the same as forward movement

| Idea | Evidence | Label | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passes can be valuable by **disrupting the defence**, whatever their direction | **D-Def** measures change in the opponent's centroid, line positions, surface area and spread in the 3 s after a pass (Goes, Kempe, Meerhoff & Lemmink, 2019, *Big Data*). Extended to passing sequences: the "hockey assist" several passes before a goal (*Entropy*, 2021) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (analytics, pro soccer) | M |
| Pass value via **space control gained** | Rein, Raabe & Memmert (2017) (SPACE gate) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | M |
| Passing *path* (not just location) predicts shot outcome; quick, smooth sequences without duels help | Cao (2024), *Sci Rep* 14:9572 (34,938 shots) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (ML model) | L–M |
| Successful teams prefer possession play; game control most important | Kempe, Vogelbein, Memmert & Nopp (2014), *Int J Sports Sci* 4(6A):35–41 (Bundesliga / World Cup, 676 games) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (association) | L–M |
| Experienced youth players produce longer sequences with more circulation; inexperienced play faster, individual sequences | Almeida, Ferreira & Volossovitch (2013), *J Hum Kinet* 36:97–106 | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | L–M |
| **Contradicting / complicating:** counter-attacks and fast attacks more efficient vs unorganised defences | TIME gate (Tenga et al., 2010; Sarmento et al., 2018; J Hum Kinet efficiency data) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | M |
| Gaelic football: more hand passing, backward passes and passes to goalkeepers over time; longer possessions associated with scoring | GAA 2023 report (Tobin et al.); TIME gate | GAA descriptive + RESEARCH EVIDENCE | M |
| "Use the ball to improve the picture" | Consistent with disruption and space-control metrics | **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS / coaching language**, *underlying idea partially supported* | — |

**Verdict.** The idea that **progress ≠ forward movement** is **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED**. Pro soccer analytics value passes by how they change the defence or space control, not only by direction. But direct, rapid progression is also effective against disorganised defences. **Neither is inherently better. Context decides.**

## 5. Through / Around / Over

| Question | Finding |
|---|---|
| Established research terminology? | **No.** No peer-reviewed invasion-sport literature was found using through / around / over as a validated taxonomy. Research uses related constructs: penetrative passes (FUT-SAT "penetration"), width / switching, direct vs possession play, long balls |
| Coaching taxonomy? | **Yes, in rugby coaching media.** Rugby Coach Weekly's 1-3-3-1 framework: a wide channel "to play *around*", middle forwards "to play *through*", and field spread "for a kicking game to play *over*" (PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC) |
| Sport-specific? | Soccer and rugby coaching language widely; basketball has little "over"; ice hockey uses "dump and chase" (over); AFL and Gaelic have strong "over" via long kicking |
| Useful PáircVision synthesis? | **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS:** a cross-sport *observation* vocabulary |

**Information that plausibly makes each solution available (PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS; mechanism-consistent with SPACE / NUMBERS):**
- **THROUGH:** interior gaps between defenders, a defender committed or out of position, defensive-line spacing. Related to penetration and D-Def disruption.
- **AROUND:** unguarded width; defence compressed centrally. Multiple lateral targets increase lateral-corridor time (Travassos et al., 2014).
- **OVER:** space behind a high line or pressing block, and a receiver able to contest.

**Gaelic relevance:**
- The hand-pass : kick-pass ratio has shifted strongly towards hand passing (≈1:1.6 in the 1980s → 2.5:1 in 2014–16, IJPAS 2017; 3.2:1 in 2023, GAA report).
- The 2025 rules (2-point arc, kickouts beyond the 40 m arc, players required in each half) change the value of "over" and of long kicking.
- **No study** evaluates through / around / over outcomes in GAA.

## 6. One target vs multiple targets

| Study | Manipulation | Observed (acute) | Access |
|---|---|---|---|
| Travassos, Gonçalves, Marcelino et al. (2014), *Hum Mov Sci* 38:241–250 | 1 vs 3 targets | ↑ distance between teams; more time in lateral corridors and defensive sectors | AO |
| Futsal U9–U17 (*Hum Mov Sci*, 2018; UNR authors) | 1 regular vs 2 small targets | 2 targets → higher team dispersion and **more regular** positioning; 1 target → more irregular positioning; **U9 most sensitive** | AO |
| Four baskets, youth basketball (*PLoS ONE*, 2019, 14(8):e0221773) | 2 vs 4 baskets | ↑ field goals and dribble-drives per player, ↑ physical demand, ↑ collective dispersion. Authors: favoured **individual behaviours** and "might impair team playing" | AO |
| Female national players, 8v8 (*Sensors*, 2025, 25(13):4035) | 1 vs 2 vs 3 small goals | Physical differences; **trend towards better decision-making with 1 goal** and longer possession vs 3; no technical differences | AO |
| Prepubescent 4v4 (*Sports*, 2016, 4(4):53) | Goal number and placement (including infield goals) | Formats associated with different turns, dribbles, shots, overlaps | AO |
| Castellano et al. (2016), *J Hum Kinet* 51:153–163 | Small goals vs GK vs floaters | Dispersion and shape changed | AO |

**Do multiple targets…**
- increase scanning? **UNKNOWN** (not measured).
- create more options / switching / width? **SUPPORTED (acute)**: more lateral occupation and dispersion.
- change defensive positioning? **SUPPORTED (acute)**: defence spreads to cover targets.
- reduce predictability? **Not measured.** Two targets produced *more regular* positioning in futsal.
- increase decision complexity? **Not measured directly.** One study showed a *trend towards better decisions with one goal*.
- create artificial behaviour? **Plausible.** Basketball four-basket games favoured individual play.
- **Learning: UNKNOWN.**

## 8. Directional restrictions may destroy good decisions

| Restriction | Measured evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Offside** (restricts depth relative to direction) | Rumpf, Jäger, Uthoff & Lochmann (2026), *Front Sports Act Living*: offside changed depth positioning, length-to-width ratio and spatial exploration, and lowered total and low-speed distance. Authors: offside "restricts tactical behaviour" | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (AO) |
| **Must make N passes before shooting** | Led to more passes and possession maintenance (systematic review summary, *Chaos Solitons Fractals*, 2020; UNR primary) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (SIO) |
| 2-touch | More direct attacking style (same review) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (SIO) |
| Forward-only / no back pass / must cross a line in X actions / must use the wide player / must switch before scoring / cannot return to a previous zone | **No study found** | **Logical / practitioner concern** |
| More rule manipulations in general | ↓ exploratory team behaviour (*Front Psychol*, 2019) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (AO) |
| Positional zone restrictions (SPACE gate) | ↓ teammate co-adaptation (Gonçalves et al., 2017) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE |

**Can a directional restriction make a behaviour compulsory while making a legitimate solution illegal?**
- **Logically yes.** "No back pass" forbids the recycle that would draw a press; "must use the wide player" forbids central penetration when it is on.
- **Directly measured: no.** No study codes "correct option forbidden by the rule". This parallels the TIME-gate finding on touch limits.
- **Making a behaviour compulsory does not teach when it is appropriate.** No evidence exists either way (**UNKNOWN**), and the SPACE-gate principle against systematically punishing good decisions applies.

## 9. Direction and defensive behaviour

| Finding | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Targets give defence something to protect; defenders position closer to targets; more goals → defend around targets, attackers use wider channels | Travassos et al. (2014); index summary of target studies | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (acute), M |
| Scoring mode changes regain type and location and defensive shape | Almeida et al. (2016) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE, L–M |
| Proximity to goal changes attacker–defender dyads (defenders tighter nearer goal) | Headrick et al. (2012) (SPACE gate) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE, M |
| In possession games, the defence has no goal to protect; defending = regaining the ball; distance to nearest opponent decreases | Rumpf et al. (2026) (thin tactical evidence) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (thin) |
| Offside changes defensive-line depth | Rumpf et al. (2026, offside study) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE |
| Goal-side positioning, forcing play wide, screening, protecting depth coded as outcomes of direction | **Not directly studied** in SSG comparisons | UNKNOWN |

**Does a defence behave fundamentally differently when something meaningful is behind it?** **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED.** Target-protection and depth effects are measured, but goal-side positioning, screening and forcing play in a direction are not coded. Defending a neutral box is, by construction, a **ball-regain problem without depth protection** (**PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS**, logically strong).

## 10. Direction and information

- Goals specify **where danger and opportunity are**:
  - Proximity to goal changes dyad behaviour (Headrick et al., 2012).
  - Elite players **scan less near the opponent's goal and in wide areas** (Jordet et al., 2020), so field position relative to direction changes information-gathering behaviour.
  - Target number changes positioning regularity (futsal, 2018).
  - RESEARCH EVIDENCE, L–M.
- **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS.** Direction acts partly by making certain information *meaningful* (what counts as progress, where to protect, whether a player faces the objective), not only by pulling movement towards a destination.
- **Distinction from the future INFORMATION lever.** DIRECTION sets the *objective structure*. INFORMATION concerns *what signals are available and how they are presented* (cues, instructions, feedback, visual conditions). Direction-changing-on-cue sits at their intersection.

| Finding | Evidence | Label | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Younger (U15 vs U17) teams struggled more in small possession games | *Front Psychol* (2019) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | L |

- Younger players' difficulty in small possession games is **one** acute data point, not a prescription.

## 16. Assumptions tested

| Assumption | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Directional games are more representative than possession games | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (structurally) / empirically UNTESTED** | Matches have direction, so structurally yes. No representativeness study compares possession vs directional SSGs with matches |
| More representative automatically means more learning | **UNKNOWN** | No direct test. Linear vs nonlinear pedagogy results are mixed (Woods et al., 2025) |
| Non-directional possession does not transfer | **UNKNOWN** | Never tested either way |
| Direction teaches players to progress | **UNKNOWN** | Acute effects only |
| Forward play is inherently better than backward / lateral | **CONTRADICTED** (as universal) | Disruption metrics and possession associations; counter-attacks also effective: context decides |
| End zones teach penetration | **UNKNOWN** | Line goals change defending (Almeida et al., 2016); no penetration-learning data |
| Multiple targets improve scanning | **UNKNOWN** | Scanning not measured; targets ↑ dispersion and lateral play |
| Goals automatically improve decision-making | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (acute, one study)**; "automatically" unsupported; learning UNKNOWN | One direction ↑ passing decisions (Coutinho et al., 2024); 1 vs 3 goals trend (*Sensors*, 2025) |
| Reversing direction after turnover teaches transition | **UNKNOWN** | Not studied |
| "Through / around / over" is scientifically established | **CONTRADICTED** (not established) → **PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC** | Coaching taxonomy only |
| Forcing forward play teaches when to play forward | **UNKNOWN**; logically doubtful | No study; compulsory ≠ appropriate |
| A game with direction is inherently more game-like | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** (structure) | But possession games may be closer on other properties (involvement, load); "inherently" unsupported |
| A player moving toward goal is necessarily progressing | **CONTRADICTED** (as universal) | Non-forward passes can disrupt defences and gain space |
| Directional practices transfer better to competition | **UNKNOWN** | No test |

## 17. Contradictions and counterintuitive findings (evidence-based only)

1. **Possession games give more touches, passes and load than scoring games** (+39% touches, +99% passes), and more mental challenge.
2. **Possession games were slightly *less* enjoyable**, especially compared with games with goalkeepers.
3. **No-direction games produced the most spatial exploration.** One-direction games produced better passing decisions.
4. **More targets can make play *more individual***: basketball four-basket games increased dribble-drives and dispersion, and "might impair team playing".
5. **One goal showed a trend towards *better* decisions than three goals** in elite female players.
6. **Two targets made futsal positioning *more regular*, not less predictable.**
7. **Offside restricted tactical behaviour and reduced running.**
8. **Artificial rules vs free play produced little average change in a later free match**; individuals responded very differently.
9. **Successful elite soccer teams were associated with possession play**, while counter-attacks are also more efficient per attack. Both directions have support in context.
10. **Younger players found small possession games harder.** Possession isn't automatically the "easy" option.

## 18. Ten unanswered questions

1. Does possession-game training transfer to directional match behaviour (escaping pressure, progressing, attacking a goal)?
2. Does directional-game training transfer better than possession training, measured in an unconstrained reference game and in competition?
3. Do multiple targets change **scanning** and option perception, and does any change persist once targets return to one?
4. Does practising **direction reversal after turnover** improve first-action quality in real transitions?
5. Do directional restrictions (forward-only, must-switch, must-use-wide) cause players to reject correct actions, and does that persist?
6. How do defences differ (goal-side positioning, screening, forcing wide) with vs without something to protect, and does that learning transfer?
7. What information do players use to judge **progress** (disruption, space gained, angle) rather than forward distance, and can it be trained?
8. In Gaelic football and hurling, how do through / around / over solutions relate to scoring, and how did the 2025 rules change them?
9. What directional structure suits children vs adolescents vs adults? Is there any valid way to set it for a group or player?
10. Can a volunteer coach reliably tell "progress" from "forward movement" in real time, and what low-cost indicators could show it?

## 19. Must-not-claim list

1. ❌ "Directional games teach better decision-making." Only one acute study shows better passing decisions.
2. ❌ "Possession games don't transfer" or "rondos are a waste of time."
3. ❌ "Rondos improve creativity in games." The RCT measured a paper creativity test.
4. ❌ "Adding a goal increases involvement." It *reduces* touches and passes.
5. ❌ "Directional practice transfers better to matches."
6. ❌ "Forward play is better", or "backward passes are negative."
7. ❌ "End zones teach penetration."
8. ❌ "Multiple goals improve scanning."
9. ❌ "Reversing direction after turnovers teaches transition."
10. ❌ "Through / around / over is a scientific model."
11. ❌ "Forcing forward (or wide, or switch) teaches players when to do it."
12. ❌ Any Gaelic football or hurling claim about directional *training* effects. None exists.
13. ❌ "Science tells us the right directional set-up for this age group / player."
14. ❌ Treating soccer, futsal or basketball findings as GAA evidence without labelling them cross-sport.

## 20. Plain-language translation for a volunteer Gaelic coach

> **What adding direction changes.** Put goals in and the game has a point: somewhere to get to, and something to protect. Defenders start protecting the goal instead of just chasing the ball. But players get *fewer* touches and passes than in a keep-ball game, and the running drops a bit.
>
> **What possession games are still good for.** Keep-ball games give lots of touches and passes, lots of looking for space, and they're hard work physically and mentally. That's useful. Just don't assume they teach players how to get *up the pitch* or *score*. Nobody has shown that either way.
>
> **Forward isn't always progress.** A pass back or across can pull defenders out of shape and open a better chance next. Top-level analysis values passes by how much they upset the defence, not just by how far forward they go. Sometimes going direct is right too, especially when the other team isn't set. It depends on the picture.
>
> **Careful with "rules".** "Forward passes only" or "must use the wing" can ban the right play. If a player turns back to draw the press and then breaks through, that's often good football. Don't punish it just because it breaks your rule.
>
> **What to watch** (not just whether the rule is obeyed):
> - Are players looking at *where the chance is*, or just where the rule says?
> - After a turnover, is the first action sensible?
> - Are defenders protecting what matters, or just chasing?
> - When you take the rule away, do players still choose well?

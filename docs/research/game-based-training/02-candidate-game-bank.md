# Candidate Game Bank: Sourced Invasion-Sport Training Games

**Companion to:** [`01-research-report.md`](./01-research-report.md). Evidence labels (**[E] [GB] [C] [I]**) and the source-access limitation are explained in §0 of the report.

## Inclusion rules

- **Every game here is documented in a named source.** None was invented. Where a source gives only partial rules, the entry says what is missing. It does not fill the gap.
- **"Why it works"** is **[I]** unless it quotes a reported finding.
- Games are **not** pre-adapted to Gaelic football. The "Gaelic football transfer" field describes the *concept* that could transfer and marks it **[I]**. Any Gaelic version has to be designed and then tested.

## Tier key (my judgement, [I])

- **A — Shortlist.** Strong mechanism, and either Gaelic-specific or directly analogous evidence, and it samples information Gaelic football players actually use.
- **B — Situational.** Useful for a specific purpose, but has a known representativeness or load trade-off.
- **C — Reference / caution.** Included because it is widely used or instructive, but weak as a primary learning game for Gaelic football.

Structure codes (S1–S9) refer to the taxonomy in report §5.

## Gaelic games

### G1 — Gaelic football 4v4 pitch-length ladder · Tier A
- **Source.** [E] Malone & Collins (2016), *IJPAS* 16(1) ([T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/24748668.2016.11868874); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/299437604_The_Influence_of_pitch_size_on_running_performance_during_Gaelic_football_small_sided_games)).
- **Structure.** 4v4. Pitches 40×20, 60×20 and 80×20 m (width fixed, length varied). Participants were 34 sub-elite adult male players. Scoring rules and bout durations are not in the abstract.
- **Reported behaviour.** The 60×20 m pitch produced the most high-speed running (>17 km/h) and sprinting (>22 km/h), more than both 40×20 and 80×20. HR effects were trivial.
- **Why it works [I].**
  - Length sets how far players must run to support or recover.
  - At 80 m, a 4v4 game probably stretches into kicked, less-contested play, so there are fewer repeated sprints.
  - At 40 m there is no room to reach speed.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** This is already Gaelic football. The value is the **non-linear** result: "bigger" does not always mean "more running". Pitch length should be chosen deliberately.
- **Limitations.** Physical outcomes only. No learning or decision outcomes. Sub-elite adults. Pre-2025 rules.

### G2 — Gaelic football SSG, small vs large pitch (technical profile) · Tier A
- **Source.** [E] *IJPAS* 19(6), 2019 ([T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24748668.2019.1689003); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332112580_The_Physical_and_Technical_Demands_of_Gaelic_Football_Small-Sided_Games)).
- **Structure.** The same Gaelic football SSG played on small, medium and large pitches. Exact dimensions and numbers are not captured in the retrieved abstract. **Check the full text.**
- **Reported behaviour.**
  - Smallest pitch: more possessions, **hand passes, tackles** and **points**, and the most accelerations.
  - Largest pitch: most **kick passes**, better **goal conversion**, and the most total distance and HSR.
- **Why it works [I].** Density decides whether the nearest safe option is a hand-pass distance or a kick-pass distance. Space behind the defence decides whether goal chances appear.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].**
  - This is the most direct evidence-based lever for shifting a team away from the 3.2:1 hand-pass culture of 2023 ([RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/sport/football/2023/0928/1407954-new-study-outlines-stark-extent-of-possession-football/)).
  - It does this **without banning** the hand pass.
  - Pairs naturally with 3/2/1 scoring (report §4.3).
- **Limitations.** Technical *counts* are not learning. There is no measure of kick-pass quality under pressure. Pre-2025 rules.

### G3 — Hurling SSG: possession vs normal play vs regular goals vs small goals · Tier B
- **Source.** [E] Malone et al. (2017), *JSCR* 31(6) ([JSCR full text](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/Fulltext/2017/06000/Effect_of_Game_Design,_Goal_Type,_and_Player.5.aspx); [TU Dublin](https://arrow.tudublin.ie/ittsciart/118/)).
- **Structure.** 48 hurlers. Four designs (possession, normal play, regular goals, small goals) × 4-, 5- and 6-a-side.
- **Reported behaviour.**
  - Small goals gave the highest running distance across formats.
  - 4-a-side gave about 94% HRmax regardless of design.
  - Goal games were *not* less intense than possession games, which contradicts earlier soccer findings.
- **Why it works [I].** Small goals force play into the goal area, with frequent turnovers and repeated recoveries.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** Evidence that, in Gaelic games, **directional games do not cost intensity** compared with keep-ball. That removes a common argument for possession-only conditioning games.
- **Limitations.** Hurling, not football. Load outcomes only. "Small goals" do not exist in Gaelic football, so any GF version would need a representative target (see G7).

### G4 — GAA academy Game-Based Approach (conditioned games plus tactical curriculum) · Tier A as a framework
- **Source.** [E] Kinnerk et al. (2025), *RQES* 96(4) ([T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02701367.2025.2496263); [SHAPE America summary PDF](https://convention.shapeamerica.org/Common/Uploaded%20files/document_manager/events/best-of/Gaelic-Football-Coaches-Use-of-a-Game-Based-Approach-Impacts-Game-Performance.pdf)).
- **Structure.** U14 and U15 academy squads. Multiple-baseline design framed by Complex Learning Theory. The intervention used conditioned games, a tactical curriculum and player huddles.
  - **The specific games are not described in the retrieved abstract. They must be extracted from the full text before any game is lifted from this study.**
- **Reported behaviour.**
  - All decision-making variables improved in both squads.
  - Skill execution improved only at U14.
  - Sessions shifted to playing form and non-linear sequencing.
  - Players liked the conditioned games and the tactical curriculum, but not the huddles.
- **Gaelic football transfer.** Direct. This is the only Gaelic football learning-outcome intervention found.
- **Action item.** Obtain the full text and the SHAPE summary. The actual game list is the highest-value missing item in this bank.

### G5 — Go Games (GAA small-sided competition format) · Tier B (youth only)
- **Source.** [GB] [GAA Learning — Go Games](https://learning.gaa.ie/GoGames); [Connacht GAA Go-Games rules](https://connachtgaa.ie/go-games-rules/); [LGFA Go Games Policy 2023](https://ladiesgaelic.ie/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Go-Games-Policy-Document-2023.pdf).
- **Structure (as reported by Connacht GAA).**
  - Maximum 7-a-side at U6/7, 9-a-side at U8/9, 11-a-side at U11, 13-a-side at U12.
  - Pitch scaled to numbers.
  - No league tables and no knockouts.
- **Why it works [I].** Fewer players per ball means more involvements (compare the hockey evidence in G6). Removing high-stakes outcomes lowers selection pressure on the youngest.
- **Limitations.** A *competition* format, not a training game. No outcome studies were found.

## Field hockey

### G6 — Reduced-numbers game (field hockey, youth) · Tier A
- **Source.** [E] Timmerman, Farrow & Savelsbergh (2017) ([ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319214746_The_effect_of_manipulating_task_constraints_on_game_performance_in_youth_field_hockey); [IAT record](https://iat.uni-leipzig.de/datenbanken/iks/nwls/Record/4046790)).
- **Structure.** 25 players aged about 10.6–14.6. Four 25-minute games manipulating player numbers and/or density.
- **Reported behaviour.**
  - Fewer players: +2.68 successful passes, +3.73 skilled actions and +3.77 successful actions per player, and a better environment for decision-making.
  - Higher density: −0.59 unsuccessful dribbles, +38 m high-intensity running and +21.2 m sprinting.
- **Why it works [I].** Each player is closer to the ball more often and has fewer teammates to hide behind.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** A strong general principle: reduce numbers to multiply decision repetitions, then re-expand to restore structure.
- **Limitations.** Youth players. Short study.

### G7 — Two-goals game (field hockey U14) · Tier A
- **Source.** [E] Timmerman, Savelsbergh & Farrow (2019), *RQES* 90(2) ([ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1216413); [T&F](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02701367.2019.1571678)).
- **Structure.** 13 U14 players. 3v3 or 6v6. Two goals per team to attack. Two 7.5-minute halves.
- **Reported behaviour.** +0.61 goals compared with the normal game.
- **Why it works [I].** Two targets split the defence's attention. Attackers are rewarded for switching the point of attack and scanning.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** Gaelic football has two scoring targets in one frame: goal and posts, and now the arc as well. A two-target concept maps onto "which way do they shift?" decisions. A literal two-goal Gaelic football game would be *less* representative. The transferable idea is **multiple scoring options pulling a defence apart**.
- **Limitations.** Very small sample.

### G8 — Possession game (field hockey U14) · Tier B
- **Source.** [E] Timmerman et al. (2019) ([ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1216413)).
- **Reported behaviour.** +4.82 passes, −1.48 dribbles and −0.69 tackles compared with the normal game.
- **Trade-off [I].** Builds collective ball retention, but has no penetration or scoring decision. Useful when the problem is "we give it away cheaply". Weak for learning to attack.

### G9 — Cage hockey · Tier B
- **Source.** [E] Timmerman et al. (2019) ([ERIC](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1216413)).
- **Structure.** A small enclosed area (the ball stays in play).
- **Reported behaviour.** +1.46 passes and +7.32 m/min compared with the normal game.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** The Gaelic football analogue is continuous-play rules (no dead ball), which raise ball-in-play time. Enclosure itself is not representative.

## Basketball

### G10 — 3v3 with point-possession reward ("scoring team keeps the ball") · Tier A
- **Source.** [E] *PLOS ONE* (2024), 18 youth males aged about 13 ([PLOS](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0313656); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11698363/)).
- **Structure.** 3x3 half court. After scoring, the attacking team gets a new possession.
- **Reported behaviour.** More total, low- and moderate-speed distance, a faster game pace, and more accelerations and decelerations. Players found it **more enjoyable** than regular 3v3.
- **Why it works [I].** Scoring earns another attack, and conceding means defending again straight away. That raises stakes and tempo without a prohibition.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** A **reward** (not a ban) mechanism. It resembles the real consequence of the 2025 kickout rules, where winning restarts sustains attacking spells. It is a candidate for a Gaelic football "score = keep possession" variant, which would need testing.

### G11 — 3v3 passes-based scoring · Tier B
- **Source.** [E] same study as G10.
- **Structure.** The basket's value depends on the number of passes before the shot.
- **Reported behaviour.** Highest perceived exertion. The authors suggest it may promote adaptive behaviours under higher perceived effort.
- **Caution [I].** Rewards passing count, not passing *purpose*. It risks sterile circulation, the very trend the GAA's own 2023 analysis criticised in elite Gaelic football.

### G12 — 3v3 no-dribble (dribble restriction) · Tier B
- **Source.** [E] [Frontiers in Physiology, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1550580/full); [No-dribble game drill study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276279079_Physiological_and_Technical_Demands_of_No_Dribble_Game_Drill_in_Young_Basketball_Players).
- **Reported behaviour.** More passes, more turnovers and more time at higher locomotor intensity.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** Analogous to solo or hand-pass limits. A prohibition, so use it sparingly (report §4.5).

### G13 — 2v2 / 4v4, half vs full court · Tier B
- **Source.** [E] Klusemann et al. (2012), *J Sports Sci* 30(14) ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2012.712714)); [Li et al., 2025 meta-analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244406/).
- **Structure.** 16 elite juniors. 2v2 or 4v4 × half or full court × 4×2.5 or 2×5 minutes.
- **Reported behaviour.** Format changes HR, RPE and technical involvement. Extreme formats (1v1, 2v2) give the highest HR.
- **Use.** Planning intensity. Limited tactical content.

### G14 — 4v3 continuous (advantage/disadvantage waves) · Tier A as a concept
- **Source.** [C] [Online Basketball Drills — 4v3 Continuous](https://www.online-basketball-drills.com/4v3-continuous); [Basketball Coach Weekly — "4v3 Contest"](https://www.basketballcoachweekly.net/basketball-drills-skills/4v3-contest-builds-scramblers).
- **Structure (per source).**
  - Four attack three. The first defender stops the ball, the second closes out the shot, the last protects the rim.
  - After the possession, the defenders become attackers the other way against three waiting defenders.
  - First to 7 points.
- **Why it works [I].**
  - Every possession is a transition.
  - The attack must exploit an overload before the defence recovers.
  - The defence must prioritise ball, then shot, then basket while outnumbered.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].**
  - The closest template for turnover-origin possessions, which are about half of elite Gaelic football possessions ([IJPAS 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24748668.2023.2250972)).
  - Also for "solo and go" moments.
- **Limitations.** **No learning-outcome research was found.** It is conventional practice only.

### G15 — Advantage-start 1v1 (instead of "check-ball" starts) · Tier A as a concept
- **Source.** [C] [Transforming Basketball — Advantage-Starts](https://transformingbball.com/advantage-starts-1-on-1-basketball-training-2/).
- **Structure.** 1v1 reps begin with the attacker already holding an advantage (a live drive, a closeout), instead of from a static check.
- **Why it works [I].** Matches start from broken play more than from set starts. This samples the information players actually face.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** 1v1 duels in Gaelic football almost always start in motion: a carry at speed, a runner on a support line. Static-start 1v1s are unrepresentative.
- **Limitations.** Practitioner source only.

### G16 — Contested vs uncontested shooting (design principle) · Tier A
- **Source.** [E] Gorman & Maloney (2016), *PSE* ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1469029216300942)); replicated and extended by [PSE 2025](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1469029225000275).
- **Reported behaviour.** With a defender, ball release is faster and jump time is longer. Effects depend on context.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** Shooting practice (points, arc two-pointers, placed balls from play) without a closing defender rehearses a *different movement*. Any Gaelic football shooting game should include a live or semi-live closer.

## Soccer

### G17 — Numerically unbalanced SSG (low and high superiority/inferiority) · Tier A
- **Source.** [E] [Frontiers in Psychology, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01464/full); [Frontiers, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1813770/full); [SciELO](https://www.scielo.br/j/jpe/a/rCF4TNqdRhSyW8ZWNFfs9vC/).
- **Structure.** Possession or directional SSGs such as 4v3, 4v2 and 4v6.
- **Reported behaviour.**
  - The inferior team covers more high-intensity distance and rates the game harder.
  - Superiority increases walking.
  - Defensive lines retreat and compress against superiority.
  - The authors suggest *low* superiority for developing tactical individual actions and *high* inferiority for physical load.
- **Why it works [I].** Imbalance creates a guaranteed free player. The attack's problem becomes *finding* them. The defence's problem becomes *delaying and compacting*.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** Matches the Australian football match evidence that local numerical advantage predicts scoring ([PLOS ONE 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0254591)). Best used with *decaying* advantage (late defenders enter).

### G18 — SSG with floaters (inside vs outside) · Tier B
- **Source.** [E] [Castellano et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5260558/); [Floaters and tactical behaviour](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320805917_The_influence_of_floaters_on_players%27_tactical_behaviour_in_small-sided_and_conditioned_soccer_games); [Futsal floater position](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8304258/).
- **Reported behaviour.**
  - Floaters raise passes and technical actions and change team shape.
  - They lower intensity.
  - Outside floaters enlarge the effective playing area.
- **Caution [I].** Defenders always face superiority, and floaters never defend. That is an unrepresentative role for the floater.

### G19 — Long vs wide pitch configuration · Tier A
- **Source.** [E] [Different pitch configurations…, 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8507751/).
- **Reported behaviour.** Long and wide configurations produced more counter-attacks, higher offensive penetration and more scoring opportunities than the standard shape.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** Corroborates G1 and G2: pitch *shape* is a tactical lever. A long, narrow Gaelic football pitch would be expected to favour direct kick-passing and runs in behind. Needs testing.

### G20 — Goal-format variation (number and position of goals) · Tier B
- **Source.** [E] [Goal format in SSGs, 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5968898/); [Castellano et al., 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5260558/).
- **Reported behaviour.** The number and placement of goals changes the frequency of technical actions and offensive scenarios in prepubescent players.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** A concept source for representing the arc as a separate scoring zone (report §8.2). Not a literal template.

### G21 — Touch-restricted SSG (1-touch / 2-touch) · Tier C
- **Source.** [E] [Rumpf et al., 2025, systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12558969/).
- **Reported behaviour.** More passes and higher intensity, but more unsuccessful passes as well. Tactical evidence is scarce and inconsistent.
- **Why tier C for Gaelic football [I].** A prohibition with weak tactical evidence. The Gaelic football "limit hand passes" or "no solo" analogues should be brief, deliberate interventions only.

### G22 — Rondo (5v2 / 6v2 / 8v2) · Tier C
- **Source.** [E] [MDPI 2026 (load by format)](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/6/2775); [PMC 2025 (U13 creativity and problem-solving)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12244377/). [C] Near-universal convention in soccer.
- **Reported behaviour.** Load varies with format. An 8-week programme improved problem-solving and creative-thinking measures.
- **Why tier C for Gaelic football [I].**
  - No direction and no scoring decision.
  - The circle geometry doesn't exist in Gaelic football.
  - Hand-passing keep-ball in a small area rehearses exactly the pre-2025 possession style the rule changes aimed to reduce.

### G23 — Transition-bonus SSG ("goal within 5 s of regain counts double"; "attackers drop two back on loss") · Tier B
- **Source.** [C] [Soccer Coach Weekly](https://www.soccercoachweekly.net/drills-and-games/transition-at-speed-small-sided-game) (practitioner magazine).
- **Why it works [I].** A time-limited reward for fast attacks after a turnover. A structural consequence for losing the ball.
- **Gaelic football transfer [I].** Directly relevant to turnover conversion. **Unvalidated.** Test before adopting.

### G24 — Play-Practice-Play session frame (US Soccer) · Tier A as a frame
- **Source.** [GB] [US Soccer — Five things to know about PPP](https://www.ussoccer.com/stories/2018/02/five-things-to-know-about-playpracticeplay); [US Soccer PPP overview PDF](https://www.iowasoccer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/169/2023/09/coach_play_practice_play.pdf).
- **Structure.**
  - Informal 2v2–4v4 on arrival with little coaching.
  - A "practice" phase that must be game-like: opponents, directional, "no interior cones, zones, or restrictions".
  - A final free play phase in which the coach observes silently.
- **Why A [I].**
  - Matches the GAA's own "game early" guidance ([GAA Learning](https://learning.gaa.ie/GamesBasedApproach)).
  - Directly counters the drills-first habit documented in Gaelic football coaches ([Kinnerk et al., 2019](https://journals.humankinetics.com/view/journals/iscj/6/2/article-p211.xml)).

### G25 — Whole-part-whole session frame (FA) · Tier B
- **Source.** [GB] [FA Boot Room](https://www.thefa.com/bootroom/resources/coaching/how-to-use-the-england-dna-in-your-coaching-session).
- **Structure.** Game → focused practice (with opposition, direction and decisions) → game.
- **Note [I].** Keeps the technical "part" opposed and directional, which addresses the weak skill-execution gains of pure GBAs (report §3).

## Analogue (Australian football)

### G26 — Match simulation / large-sided game · Tier C unless measured
- **Source.** [E] [*J Sports Sci* 2022](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02640414.2021.1995245); [Browne et al., 2020, *PLOS ONE*](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0242336).
- **Reported behaviour.** Compared with AFL matches, match simulation had more goals, fewer passes per minute, fewer turnovers and tackles, and more centralised passing networks. SSGs were less representative than match simulation for pressure and time in possession.
- **Lesson [I].** "Game-like" is an empirical claim. Measure it: pressure, turnovers, pass mix and shot mix compared with matches, before trusting a large-sided game as preparation.

## Shortlist rationale: the "exceptionally useful" candidates for Gaelic football [I]

If only a handful of concepts go forward to Gaelic football design and testing:

1. **G1 + G2 + G19 — pitch dimension as a tactical dial.** Gaelic football-specific evidence, no prohibitions, and it targets the kick-pass deficit.
2. **G14 + G15 + G17 — advantage/disadvantage starts with decaying overloads.** Samples the turnover-origin possessions that make up about half the elite game.
3. **G10 — reward-based scoring rules.** Changes intensity and enjoyment without removing options. Extend it to the 3/2/1 scoring structure.
4. **G16 — always contest the shot.** A low-cost rule with direct evidence that it changes the skill.
5. **G24 / G4 — session frame.** Game-first, non-linear sequencing, with the specific G4 games still to be extracted from Kinnerk et al. (2025).

**The next research step, before any game is finalised:**
1. Retrieve the full texts of Kinnerk et al. (2025), Barrett, Kinnerk & Kearney (2025) and the 2019 Gaelic football SSG paper.
2. Record exact dimensions, rules and assessment tools (e.g., the decision-making and skill-execution coding instruments).

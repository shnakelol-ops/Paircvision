## 1. Conflicting findings

| # | Conflict | Sources | Possible explanation [interpretation] | What would resolve it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Area → technical counts.** A soccer meta-analysis finds no change in passes or dribbles with pitch size. A Gaelic minor-grade study finds a clear shift in pass *type* (hand vs kick). | S7 vs GA3 | The meta-analysis pools pass *counts*. GA3 separates pass *type*. Gaelic football offers two very different passing actions that suit different distances, which soccer passing counts do not capture. | A GF study with number fixed, area varied and pass type coded, reported per player. |
| C2 | **Area → running is non-linear in Gaelic football.** 4v4 HSR peaked at 60×20 m, not 80×20 m. Soccer meta-analysis: larger → more HSR. | GA2 vs S7 | At 80×20 m (4:1 aspect ratio) the game may become kick-dominated, with fewer repeated contests. Or aspect ratio, not area, drives the result. | Hold aspect ratio constant while varying area in GF. |
| C3 | **Possession vs goal games: which is more intense?** | S20 (possession more intense) vs GA8 (goal games not less intense, in hurling) | Different sports, formats and goal types. The authors of GA8 note the contrast themselves. | Direct replication in GF. |
| C4 | **Higher density in hockey → more high-intensity running.** In soccer, larger area usually → more HSR. | H1 vs S7 | Hockey players may make more repeated short sprints in tighter space. Ball speed on turf differs. Abstract-level wording may also be ambiguous. | Full-text check of H1 definitions. |
| C5 | **Multiple targets: effect or no effect?** | S16, S18 (effects) vs S19 (no technical–tactical effect with 1–3 small goals, elite women 8v8) | Level, sex, format size (8v8 vs smaller) and target type differ. S16 measured spacing; S19 measured technical–tactical counts. | Same outcome measures across levels. |
| C6 | **Bout duration → technical actions.** | S40 (no effect, 2–6 min, soccer) vs B13 (short-intermittent → more possessions, dribbles and shots, basketball) | Different sports and definitions ("duration" vs "regime"). Basketball has more restarts per minute. | — |
| C7 | **"Exaggeration" reliably produces the target behaviour.** | S43 (goal exaggeration → more goals and dribbling) vs S50 (representation vs exaggeration: no difference, U10) | The type of exaggeration and the age differ. Constraint compliance ≠ intended behaviour. | Pre-registered manipulation checks. |
| C8 | **Instruction helps or hinders.** | S43 (instruction → better passing networks) vs S44 (stacked explicit + implicit rules hinder) vs GA4 (huddles disliked, yet decision-making improved) | Single, clear instructions may help. Stacked constraints and frequent stoppages may not. | Dose studies of instruction within games. |
| C9 | **Defensive system matters?** Coaching convention says zone vs man changes the problem. B12 found no technical or RPE difference. | B12 vs convention | Technical counts may be the wrong outcome; the difference may be tactical or perceptual. | Tactical/positional measures for zone vs man. |

## 2. Popular constraints with surprisingly weak evidence

| Constraint | Popular belief | What was found | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **End zones / scoring by line** | Teaches penetration and running in behind | Only a physical study (S21). No tactical or learning data. | D (tactical) |
| **Transition waves** (3v2 continuous, "counter-attack games") | Teaches transition recognition and speed | Physical load only (S39). Tactical results of the one design study not visible (S37). | D (behaviour) |
| **Recovering defenders / fading overloads** | Teaches players to recognise and exploit a *temporary* advantage | **No study found.** The nearest is S26: overloads rotating each minute → broader exploration. | E |
| **Direction reversal** | Improves reorientation and scanning | No study found | E |
| **Mandatory N passes before scoring** | Teaches patience and circulation | No results found (a trial is registered) | D/E |
| **Touch restrictions** | Improves speed of play and passing | Increases passes **and errors**; tactical evidence scarce (S32, S33) | B within soccer, but not for "improves passing" as a *learning* claim |
| **Rondos / possession games** | Develops passing decisions | Acute passing volume ↑, dribbling and tackling ↓ (H2). One U13 creativity study (report 01). No transfer data. | C |
| **Zone / channel restrictions** | Teaches positional discipline | Reduces long-term exploration and slows ball flow (S34) | C |
| **Floaters** | Makes attacking easier and more successful | Yes, and also **less penetration and lower intensity** (S24, S29) | B |
| **Coach questioning in huddles** | Builds tactical understanding | Gaelic youth players disliked it; its isolated contribution to learning is unknown (GA4) | C |

## 3. Manipulations that change behaviour but may harm representativeness

| Manipulation | What it changes | What it distorts or sacrifices | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small area per player | More involvements, more scanning | Match spacing (SSG APP <150 m² vs ~320 m² in 11v11) and match-type scanning situations | S12, S45 |
| Fixed superiority | More attacking actions, better pass efficacy | Less pressure; the superior side becomes less intense; the defence is permanently outnumbered | S23, S24, B4, B6 |
| Floaters | More passes | Less penetration; floaters never defend; lower intensity | S24, S29 |
| Possession-only | More passes | Fewer dribbles and tackles; no scoring decision | H2 |
| Touch restriction | More passes, faster release | Lower pass success, fewer duels | S32, S33 |
| Carry restriction | More passes | More turnovers; no 1v1 carry decisions | B9 |
| Zones | Positional structure | Less exploration, slower ball flow | S34 |
| Unopposed / low-pressure practice | Higher success | The movement itself changes (shot kinematics); pressure is under-sampled | B16; X4 |
| Match simulation | "Full" game | Not match-like: more goals, fewer turnovers and tackles, centralised passing | X5 |
| Multiple targets | Wider play | May teach conservative retreat rather than switching | S16 |

**General point [interpretation].** SSGs routinely under-supply pressure. In Australian football, disposals in SSGs were *more* effective than in matches (X4). Any manipulation that raises success rates should be read as possibly *lowering* representativeness, unless pressure is measured.

## 4. Confounding problems found repeatedly

1. **Player number × area per player.** Most "player number" studies also change APP (S3, S21, H4). Only H1, H3, S5 and S10 separate them. They show that:
   - area drives locomotion more than number does (S5);
   - with APP fixed, hockey physical outputs barely change (H3);
   - number still raises per-player involvement when separated (H1).
2. **Area × dimensions × aspect ratio.** GA2 varied length only, so area and aspect ratio (2:1 → 4:1) moved together. GA3 varied both dimensions. Only S13 held area constant while varying shape, and only S8 varied width and length separately (and found crossover).
3. **Numbers → APP → skill.** Skill level moderates responses to area (S9), numerical relations (S22) and interventions (S46).
4. **Order effects in learning studies.** S46 always ran superiority before equality. S47 had no visible control.
5. **Outcome status and pacing.** Winning or losing (GA10, S27) and knowing the duration (S42) change behaviour. Most SSG comparisons do not control these.
6. **Constraint compliance vs decision-making.** A rule that forces passing (no-dribble, touch limits) raises passes by *compliance*. Only studies that code decision quality (S46, S47, B4, S35, GA4) can say anything about decisions.
7. **Rule era (Gaelic).** All Gaelic studies (GA1–GA10) pre-date 2025.
   - SSG-internal effects are unaffected.
   - Their *match relevance* is reduced. The value of kicking from distance changed with the two-point arc. Kickout contests now start beyond 40 m. The 3-in-each-half rule changes structural numbers in the full game.
   - This especially limits R5 (pitch size → kick vs hand pass): which of those actions is "match-representative" has itself shifted.

## 5. Unknowns: relationships with no located evidence

Grouped by importance to Gaelic football. Every item is graded **E**.

### Gaelic-specific
- Any de-confounded GF study of player number vs area per player.
- Any GF study of width vs length vs aspect ratio.
- Any GF study of target type or scoring-zone manipulation, including **two-point-arc scoring value** as a training constraint.
- Any GF study of **kickout or restart** manipulation in training games.
- Any GF study of **carry constraints** (solo, bounce, hand-pass limits) in training. The only related item is a competition-rule trial reported in the media.
- Any GF study of **numerical superiority, floaters, or the 3-in-each-half structure** as a training constraint.
- Any **post-2025** GF training-game study.
- Any female Gaelic football SSG study.
- Which specific conditioned games in GA4 produced the decision-making gains.

### Cross-sport (unknown everywhere)
- **Recovering defenders / fading overloads** and recognition of temporary numerical advantage.
- **Direction reversal.**
- **Restart type and location.**
- **Transition-game behaviour** (post-turnover reaction, recovery runs, exploitation speed) as a measured outcome.
- **Mandatory-pass rules**, beyond physical or RPE outcomes.
- **Scanning** in any sport other than soccer, and scanning × manipulation beyond format size.
- **Transfer**: does an in-game behaviour change under a manipulation carry over to unconstrained games or competition? No study located tests this directly for a single manipulation.
- **Dose**: how many exposures to a manipulation before behaviour changes persist. S48 hints at >17 sessions for technical execution.
- **Engagement**: enjoyment is measured only for encouragement, reward rules, bout structure and SSGs-vs-drills. Motivation, perceived competence and motivational climate are essentially unmeasured (S51).

# Game-Based Training in Invasion Sports: A Research Report for Gaelic Football

**Scope:** Gaelic football (with hurling where the evidence is Gaelic-specific), soccer, basketball, field hockey. Australian football is used only as a close analogue where it adds evidence that nothing else supplies.
**Frameworks covered:** TGfU, Game Sense, Play Practice, constraints-led approach (CLA), nonlinear pedagogy (NLP), representative learning design (RLD), small-sided and conditioned games (SSCGs).
**Companion file:** [`02-candidate-game-bank.md`](./02-candidate-game-bank.md)
**Date compiled:** September 2026

## 0. How to read this report

### 0.1 Evidence labels

Every substantive statement carries one of these labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[E]** | **Evidence.** Reported in peer-reviewed research. The source is linked. |
| **[GB]** | **Governing-body / coach-education guidance.** Official material from the GAA, the FA, US Soccer, USA Basketball, England Hockey, the KNHB, etc. This is policy or recommended practice. It is **not** proof that the practice works. |
| **[C]** | **Coaching convention.** Common practice or practitioner resources (coaching websites, magazines). Plausible and widely used, but **not validated**. |
| **[I]** | **Interpretation.** My own synthesis or inference. Treat it as a hypothesis to test, not as a finding. |

### 0.2 Method and an important limitation

- Sources were found by structured web searches of governing-body sites, academic databases, journal pages and reputable coaching outlets.
- **Limitation:** this research environment blocked direct retrieval of full-text articles (publisher sites, PubMed and PMC were not reachable). Every research claim below therefore comes from **abstracts, indexed summaries and publisher or repository metadata** shown in search results, not from reading the full papers.
- Effect sizes and percentages are quoted as reported in those abstracts. **Before any figure is used in coach-facing material or in the product, check it against the full text.**
- No figures, games or studies have been invented to fill gaps. Where evidence is missing, the report says so (§8).

## 1. Executive summary

1. **Games do improve decision-making, but the evidence is moderate and varied.** [E]
   - A 2025 multilevel meta-analysis (28 studies, n = 1,600) found that game-based approaches (GBAs) had a positive but heterogeneous effect on in-game decision-making and on motor skill in skill tests ([Manninen et al., 2025, *European Physical Education Review*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1356336X241245305)).
   - An earlier meta-analysis found significant decision-making gains (ES = 0.89). Gains in skill execution were not significant, and the GRADE quality of evidence was low ([Effects of Teaching Games…, 2020, *IJERPH*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7013807/)).
2. **Gaelic football now has direct intervention evidence.** [E]
   - A GBA intervention with U14 and U15 academy squads improved every decision-making variable in both squads. Skill execution improved only at U14. Session content shifted toward playing-form activities and non-linear sequencing ([Kinnerk et al., 2025, *RQES*](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02701367.2025.2496263)).
   - Players liked the conditioned games and the tactical curriculum, but were **less receptive to "player huddles"**.
3. **Coaches say one thing and do another.** [E]
   - Inter-county Gaelic football coaches (n = 150) reported mostly training-form work in pre-season and more playing-form work only toward peak season. They typically ran drills first and games second ([Kinnerk et al., 2019, *ISCJ*](https://journals.humankinetics.com/view/journals/iscj/6/2/article-p211.xml)).
   - The same pattern appears in soccer: elite youth coaches used more training-form than playing-form activities and heavy prescriptive instruction, with low self-awareness of the gap ([Partington & Cushion, 2013](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1600-0838.2011.01383.x); [Ford, Yates & Williams, 2010](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02640414.2010.506490)).
4. **The design variables are known. The dose–response is only partly known.**
   - In every sport studied, player numbers, area per player, target type, numerical balance, floaters, touch/dribble limits and scoring rewards reliably change physical load and technical counts [E].
   - Their effect on *tactical learning* is much less well documented [E]: [Ometto et al., 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1747954118769183) and the touch-restriction review by [Rumpf et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12558969/).
5. **Gaelic football-specific SSG data exist.** [E]
   - On Gaelic football SSG pitches, **smaller pitches gave more possessions, hand passes, tackles and points**. **Larger pitches gave more kick passes, higher goal conversion and more high-speed running** ([Physical and technical demands of Gaelic football SSGs, IJPAS 2019](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24748668.2019.1689003)).
   - This matters because the elite game had drifted to a hand-pass-dominant style: 3.2 hand passes per kick pass in 2023, against 2:1 in 2011 ([RTÉ, 2023](https://www.rte.ie/sport/football/2023/0928/1407954-new-study-outlines-stark-extent-of-possession-football/)).
6. **Representativeness can't be assumed, even in "games".** [E]
   - In elite Australian football, SSGs were *less* representative of match pressure and time in possession than match simulations. Disposals were more effective in SSGs than in matches, which suggests that SSGs under-supplied pressure ([Browne et al., 2020, *PLOS ONE*](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0242336)).
   - Match simulations themselves also departed from matches: goals were scored more often, there were fewer passes, fewer turnovers and fewer tackles per minute, and passing networks were more centralised ([Match simulation practice may not represent competitive match play…, *J Sports Sci* 2022](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/02640414.2021.1995245)).
7. **The 2025 Gaelic football rule changes make much existing representative design obsolete.** [E]/[I]
   - The changes include the 40 m two-point arc, kickouts that must travel beyond the arc, at least 3 outfield players in each half, "solo and go" after a foul, and restrictions on back-passes to the goalkeeper ([GAA FRC explainer](https://www.gaa.ie/article/football-review-committee-rule-enhancements-explainer)).
   - Championship scoring rose from about 34 to about 46 points per game between 2024 and 2025 ([RTÉ Brainstorm, 2026](https://www.rte.ie/brainstorm/2026/0915/1590930-coaching-science-gaelic-football-rule-changes-outcome/)). [E-journalistic]
   - [I] Pre-2025 kickout and possession research describes a different game. Training games now need to show the **arc**, the **kickout landing zone** and the **3-in-each-half structure** as information sources.
8. **Most-transferable concepts for Gaelic football.** [I, grounded in the evidence above]
   - Scale **area per player** deliberately to choose hand-pass versus kick-pass behaviour.
   - Use **scoring-zone or target manipulation** to represent the two-point arc.
   - Use **advantage/disadvantage waves** to represent turnover transitions, which account for almost half of elite possessions ([A transition game?, IJPAS 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24748668.2023.2250972)).
   - Use **reward rules** (possession retained after a score, or bonus scores) rather than prohibitions where possible.
   - Protect **pressure**: it is the variable most often lost in practice.

## 2. Theoretical frameworks: what each claims and how well supported it is

### 2.1 Teaching Games for Understanding (TGfU)

- **Origin** [E]:
  - Bunker & Thorpe (1982), *Bulletin of Physical Education*. The model was a response to technique-led games teaching that produced pupils who "knew little about games" ([Wikipedia summary with citations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teaching_games_for_understanding); [original curriculum model PDF](http://www.bugeyed.ca/seeds/wp-content/uploads/2013/06/Bunker-and-Thorpe-The-curriculum-model.pdf)).
  - The model runs as a cycle: game form → game appreciation → tactical awareness → decision-making → skill execution → performance.
  - It uses four pedagogical principles: sampling, representation, exaggeration and tactical complexity.
- **What it contributes to game design** [I]:
  - **Representation**: keep the game's tactical structure, scaled down.
  - **Exaggeration**: change rules or space to over-present one tactical problem.
  - These two principles are exactly the levers later formalised by CLA.
- **Evidence caution** [E]:
  - Serra-Olivares et al. (2015) compared a 3v3 U10 soccer game modified by *representation* with one modified by *exaggeration* (enhancing the "penetrate the defence" problem).
  - They found **no significant differences** in decision-making units or execution between the two games ([J Hum Kinet](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4519216/)).
  - [I] A rule tweak labelled as "exaggeration" does not guarantee the target behaviour will appear. It has to be checked by observation.

### 2.2 Game Sense (Australia)

- **Origin** [E/GB]:
  - Australian Sports Commission, 1996; den Duyn, 1997, *Game Sense: Developing Thinking Players* ([WorldCat](https://search.worldcat.org/title/game-sense-developing-thinking-players-a-presenters-guide-and-workbook/oclc/38818516)).
  - Game Sense was a reaction against "lines, laps and lectures". The game or game form becomes "the focus and starting-point" of the session.
  - The ASC later published thematically grouped Game Sense games ([Game Sense research in coaching, University of Sydney PDF](https://www.sydney.edu.au/content/dam/corporate/documents/faculty-of-arts-and-social-sciences/research/research-centres-institutes-groups/adpn/game-sense-research-coaching-findings-reflections.pdf)).
- **Distinguishing feature** [I]:
  - Game Sense puts more weight than TGfU on **questioning** and on the coach as a facilitator in high-performance settings.
  - Questioning is exactly the element Kinnerk et al. (2025) found players less receptive to when it took the form of huddles (§1).

### 2.3 Play Practice (Launder)

- [E/C] Launder's *Play Practice* (2001; 2nd ed. with Piltz) sets out **shaping, focusing and enhancing** play as the coach's three tools ([Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/Play_Practice.html?id=VfFRThmE1oEC); [EBSCO research starter](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/education/teaching-games-understanding-tgfu)).
- [I] "Focusing the challenge" is the practitioner equivalent of "exaggeration". "Enhancing" covers motivation: scoring systems, competitions, affect.

### 2.4 Constraints-led approach (CLA)

- [E] The CLA is grounded in ecological dynamics. Behaviour emerges from the interaction of **individual, task and environmental constraints**, and the coach designs by manipulating task constraints ([Renshaw, Davids, Newcombe & Roberts, 2019, Routledge](https://www.routledge.com/The-Constraints-Led-Approach-Principles-for-Sports-Coaching-and-Practice/Renshaw-Davids-Newcombe-Roberts/p/book/9781138104075)).
- [E] SSCGs are the principal CLA tool in team sports. The constraints of different SSCGs "facilitate emergence of continuous interpersonal coordination tendencies" ([Davids, Araújo, Correia & Vilar, 2013, *Exerc Sport Sci Rev*](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/acsm-essr/abstract/10.1097/jes.0b013e318292f3ec~how-small-sided-and-conditioned-games-enhance-acquisition-of)).

### 2.5 Nonlinear pedagogy (NLP)

- [E] NLP was proposed as a theoretical explanation for *why* TGfU works, using dynamical-systems theory ([Chow et al., 2007, *Review of Educational Research*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/003465430305615)).
- [E] Its key ideas:
  - **Representativeness**.
  - **Constraint manipulation**.
  - **Functional variability**: variability is useful, not noise.
  - **Attentional focus** (external rather than internal).
  - **Task simplification rather than task decomposition**: keep the perception–action coupling intact rather than breaking the skill into parts.

### 2.6 Representative learning design (RLD)

- [E] Pinder, Davids, Renshaw & Araújo (2011) adapted Brunswik's "representative design". Practice should sample the **informational constraints** of performance, so that actions are coupled to the same perceptual information as in the match. They separate this concept from simple "ecological validity" ([J Sport Exerc Psychol PDF, AUT mirror](https://www.aut.ac.nz/__data/assets/pdf_file/0007/380698/Representative-Learning-Design-and-Functionality-of-Research-and-Practice-in-Sport.pdf); [QUT ePrints](https://eprints.qut.edu.au/47250/)).
- [E] **Affective learning design** extends RLD: practice should also sample the *emotional* context of competition ([Headrick et al., 2015, *Psychology of Sport and Exercise*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1469029214001137)).
- [E] **Direct evidence that representativeness changes the skill itself**:
  - *Basketball.* Adding a defender made the jump shot's ball release significantly faster and the jump time longer compared with unopposed shooting ([Gorman & Maloney, 2016, *PSE*](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1469029216300942)).
  - *Futsal.* Pass regularity and accuracy varied with the number of action possibilities available in the practice task ([Travassos et al., 2012, *J Sports Sci*](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2012.712716)).
- [E] **Measuring representativeness.** Browne et al. (2020) built a working framework for measuring RLD in Australian football. They compared constraint interactions (pressure × time in possession) and their frequencies in SSGs, match simulations and matches ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0242336)).
  - [I] This is the most transferable *method* in the whole literature for a performance-analysis product such as PáircVision. Gaelic football has the same kick/hand-pass disposal structure as Australian football.

### 2.7 Integrating frameworks for planning

- [E] **Skill acquisition periodisation.** Farrow & Robertson (2017) reinterpret the SPORT principles (specificity, progression, overload, reversibility, tedium) for skill training ([*Sports Medicine*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-016-0646-2)).
- [E] **Challenge-based framework.** Hodges & Lohse (2022) distinguish **practice-to-learn, practice-to-maintain and practice-to-transfer**. They argue that which difficulties are "desirable" depends on specificity to competition ([*J Sports Sci*](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02640414.2021.2015917); [preprint](https://msl.kin.educ.ubc.ca/sites/default/files/news-files/PrePrint_ChallengeFrameworkforCOACHING_dEC2021_JSS.pdf)).
- [E] **Tactical Periodisation for Gaelic football.** Mangan, Collins, Burns & O'Neill (2022) propose training through four game moments: attack, defence, and the two transitions. They integrate physical, technical and tactical content and isolate very little, taking the amateur status of the game into account ([*IJSSC*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/17479541211016269); [TU Dublin Arrow](https://arrow.tudublin.ie/ittsciart/142/)).
  - This is a *conceptual model*, not an outcome study.

## 3. Evidence on outcomes: does game-based training work?

| Question | What the evidence says | Label and source |
|---|---|---|
| Do GBAs improve decision-making? | Yes: positive, heterogeneous effect on in-game decision-making | [E] [Manninen et al., 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1356336X241245305) |
| Do GBAs improve technical skill? | Small positive effect in skill tests (g = 0.36) in one meta-analysis; non-significant for skill execution in another (low GRADE) | [E] [Manninen 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1356336X241245305); [IJERPH 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7013807/) |
| In Gaelic football specifically? | U14/U15 academy: decision-making improved in both squads; skill execution only at U14 | [E] [Kinnerk et al., 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02701367.2025.2496263) |
| In competitive team sport generally? | GBAs mainly support the cognitive and affective domains; coaches struggle with planning and practice design | [E] [Kinnerk et al., 2018, *Quest*](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/00336297.2018.1439390) |
| Is the research base mature? | Still needs better verification of whether the approach was actually delivered as intended, better tactical measures, and more coaching-context studies | [E] [Harvey & Jarrett, 2014](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1030004) |
| Fitness from SSGs? | SSGs can match or exceed match intensity; fitness gains are similar to generic training | [E] [Hill-Haas et al., 2011, *Sports Med*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.2165/11539740-000000000-00000) |
| Basketball fitness from SSGs? | No significant difference from governing-body training for aerobic and change-of-direction gains (10 studies, n = 253) | [E] [BMC SSMR 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-025-01184-5) |
| Hurling fitness from SSGs? | Compared against other methods in a club hurling study | [E] [Malone, Hughes & Collins, 2017, *IJSSC*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1747954117727887) |

**Interpretation** [I]:
- The case for games rests mainly on **decision-making and engagement**.
- The evidence does **not** show that games alone build technique better than good technical practice. The Gaelic data (skill execution improved only at U14) are consistent with that.
- This supports a **hybrid** in which technical detail is placed inside or next to representative games, not a pure "the game is the teacher" model. Moran's (2026) warning points the same way: in unmodified games, stronger players dominate touches ([RTÉ Brainstorm](https://www.rte.ie/brainstorm/2026/0617/1578537-gaa-coaching-drills-training-games-based-approach-skills-development/)) [E-commentary].
- [E] Barrett, Kinnerk & Kearney (2025) look specifically at *developing skill within* a GBA ([*IJSSC*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/17479541241311673)). The full text could not be retrieved. Read it before finalising any skill-within-game protocol.

## 4. Design variables: what each one does (cross-sport evidence)

This is the core of the report. For each variable: what it reliably changes, the behaviour it tends to produce, and the evidence.

### 4.1 Player numbers

| Finding | Sport | Label and source |
|---|---|---|
| Fewer players → higher intensity (when area per player is held or increased). Smaller formats are more reproducible. | Soccer | [E] [Hill-Haas et al., 2011](https://link.springer.com/article/10.2165/11539740-000000000-00000) |
| 1v1/2v2 give higher mean and peak HR than 3v3/4v4 | Basketball | [E] [Li et al., 2025 meta-analysis, *Biol Sport*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12244406/) |
| 2v2 vs 4v4, half vs full court, 4×2.5 vs 2×5 min: format changes load and technical involvement. Citing literature summarises more short-distance shooting in 2v2. Verify in the full text. | Basketball | [E] [Klusemann et al., 2012, *J Sports Sci*](https://doi.org/10.1080/02640414.2012.712714) |
| Fewer players → more successful passes (+2.68), skilled actions (+3.73) and successful actions per player. More advantageous for decision-making. | Field hockey (youth, aged ~10–14) | [E] [Timmerman, Farrow & Savelsbergh, 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319214746_The_effect_of_manipulating_task_constraints_on_game_performance_in_youth_field_hockey) |
| 4-a-side gave the highest relative intensity (about 94% HRmax), whatever the game design | Hurling | [E] [Malone et al., 2017, *JSCR*](https://journals.lww.com/nsca-jscr/Fulltext/2017/06000/Effect_of_Game_Design,_Goal_Type,_and_Player.5.aspx) |

[I] **Gaelic football transfer:**
- Small numbers maximise involvement and repetitions of contested decisions.
- They also *remove* the structure-level cues (support depth, the 3-in-each-half constraint, kickout shapes) that only exist with more players. The choice of numbers should follow the target decision, not habit.

### 4.2 Space: area per player, shape and density

| Finding | Sport | Label and source |
|---|---|---|
| 4v4 on 40×20, 60×20 and 80×20 m: the **60×20 m pitch** gave the most high-speed running and sprinting (non-linear). Pitch size had trivial effects on HR. | Gaelic football (sub-elite adult, n = 34) | [E] [Malone & Collins, 2016, IJPAS](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/24748668.2016.11868874) |
| Smallest pitch → more possessions, **hand passes, tackles, points**, accelerations. Largest pitch → more **kick passes**, higher goal conversion, more distance and HSR. | Gaelic football | [E] [IJPAS 2019](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24748668.2019.1689003); [ResearchGate record](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332112580_The_Physical_and_Technical_Demands_of_Gaelic_Football_Small-Sided_Games) |
| Larger hurling SSG pitches → higher %HRmax, TRIMP and RPE | Hurling | [E] [Malone & Collins, 2017, *JSCR*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/308275235_The_Influence_of_Pitch_Size_on_Running_Performance_and_Physiological_Responses_During_Hurling-Specific_Small-Sided_Games) |
| Long and wide pitch configurations → more counter-attacks, deeper offensive penetration and more goal-scoring opportunities than the standard shape | Soccer (youth) | [E] [Different pitch configurations…, 2021](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8507751/) |
| Higher density (less space) → fewer unsuccessful dribbles and more high-intensity running and sprinting | Field hockey (youth) | [E] [Timmerman et al., 2017](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/319214746_The_effect_of_manipulating_task_constraints_on_game_performance_in_youth_field_hockey) |
| SSGs under-deliver high-speed running and sprinting compared with matches. Relative areas above ~225–300 m² per player are needed to approach match HSR. | Soccer | [E] [Frontiers systematic review, 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2023.1116293/full) |

[I] **The most important Gaelic football-specific finding in the whole review is pitch size.** Area per player is a **tactical dial**, not only a fitness dial:
- Tight areas produce hand-pass and tackle-rich football.
- Longer areas produce kick-passing and goal chances.

The 2023 elite trend (3.2 hand passes per kick pass) and the 2025 rules, which reward kicking from distance, make that dial central to Gaelic football session design.

### 4.3 Targets and scoring method

| Finding | Sport | Label and source |
|---|---|---|
| **Two-goals game** → more goals (+0.61) than a normal game | Field hockey U14 | [E] [Timmerman et al., 2019, *RQES*](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1216413) |
| Small goals → highest running distance in 4-, 5- and 6-a-side | Hurling | [E] [Malone et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28538297) |
| The number and position of goals changes technical actions and offensive scenarios | Soccer (prepubescent) | [E] [Goal format in SSGs, 2018](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5968898/) |
| Scoring targets (small goals vs goalkeepers) and outer floaters change team dispersion, shape and space creation | Soccer | [E] [Castellano et al., 2016, *J Hum Kinet*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5260558/) |
| **Point-possession reward** (keep the ball after scoring) → more distance, faster pace, more accelerations and decelerations, higher enjoyment | Basketball 3v3, U13–14 | [E] [PLOS ONE 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0313656) |
| **Passes-based scoring** → higher perceived exertion | Basketball 3v3 | [E] same source |

[I] **Gaelic football transfer:**
- The scoring system is where the 2025 rules and training games meet most directly. Gaelic football now has **three** differently valued scoring targets: goal = 3, arc point = 2, inside-arc point = 1.
- A training game that uses the full modern scoring system trains a new decision: *take the 2 now, work inside for the 1, or go for the 3.* No published training study on this decision was found (§8).

### 4.4 Numerical balance and floaters

| Finding | Label and source |
|---|---|
| Numerical superiority changes interpersonal coordination. Defensive lines retreat and teams compress. | [E] [SciELO study](https://www.scielo.br/j/jpe/a/rCF4TNqdRhSyW8ZWNFfs9vC/) |
| Players in **inferiority** cover more high-intensity distance and perceive the game as harder. Players in superiority walk more. High inferiority (e.g., 4v2, 4v6) raises load on the outnumbered team. | [E] [Frontiers in Psychology, 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01464/full) |
| Floaters raise passes and technical actions but *reduce* physiological intensity | [E] [Floaters and tactical behaviour](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/320805917_The_influence_of_floaters_on_players%27_tactical_behaviour_in_small-sided_and_conditioned_soccer_games); [Frontiers, 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/sports-and-active-living/articles/10.3389/fspor.2026.1813770/full) |
| Floater position (inside vs outside) changes offensive performance in futsal | [E] [Futsal floaters](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8304258/) |
| In **real Australian football matches**, a greater team numerical advantage entering the forward 50 raised the odds of scoring (OR 1.93). More total bodies inside 50 *lowered* the probability of scoring. | [E] [PLOS ONE 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0254591) |

[I] **Gaelic football transfer:**
- The Australian football match finding is the best available proxy for the value of overload in a kick/hand-pass code.
- It supports designing games around **creating and exploiting local overloads**, not overloads handed out by the rules.
- The 2025 "minimum 3 outfield players in each half" rule means structural overloads in Gaelic football are now bounded by rule, so the relevant overloads are *temporary and local*.

### 4.5 Touch, dribble and hand-pass restrictions

| Finding | Label and source |
|---|---|
| 1–2 touch limits raise passes and intensity, but raise both successful *and* unsuccessful passes. Tactical effects are scarce and inconsistent. | [E] [Rumpf et al., 2025, systematic review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12558969/) |
| Basketball no-dribble 3v3 → more passes and more turnovers, higher intensity | [E] [Frontiers in Physiology, 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/physiology/articles/10.3389/fphys.2025.1550580/full); [No-dribble game drill study](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276279079_Physiological_and_Technical_Demands_of_No_Dribble_Game_Drill_in_Young_Basketball_Players) |
| The Gaelic football Expert Advisory Group is trialling a **limit of three consecutive hand passes** and a **backcourt rule** in the 2026 Ryan Cup | [E-journalistic] [RTÉ Brainstorm, 2026](https://www.rte.ie/brainstorm/2026/0915/1590930-coaching-science-gaelic-football-rule-changes-outcome/) |

[I] **Caution for Gaelic football:**
- Prohibition rules such as "max 3 hand passes" are the *most artificial* lever: they remove options that exist in the real game.
- The pitch-size evidence (§4.2) shows that the same shift toward more kicking can be produced by **changing space**, which keeps every option available.
- Prefer space and reward over bans. Use bans briefly and deliberately, as the competition-engineering trial itself does.

### 4.6 Game design type: possession, directional, cage

|---|---|---|

### 4.7 Instruction and coach behaviour inside games

- [E] Youth soccer coaches who used questioning, CLA and playing-form activities still **over-coached**: high instruction and stop-start activity that reduces problem-solving demands ([O'Connor, Larkin & Williams, 2017](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/17408989.2017.1294678)).
- [E] In Gaelic football, **player huddles** were the least-liked element of an otherwise well-received GBA ([Kinnerk et al., 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02701367.2025.2496263)).
- [I] A game is only as game-like as the time the ball is actually live. Stoppages for questioning have a cost that should be weighed against their benefit.

## 5. Classification of recurring game structures across sports

This taxonomy is my synthesis [I]. Each class is anchored to sourced examples. Game IDs refer to the game bank.

| # | Structure | Defining features | Sourced examples | Main behaviours produced | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **S1** | **Non-directional possession / keep-ball** | No goal. Score by passes or by keeping the ball. Often uses floaters. | Rondos (soccer); hockey possession game; possession SSGs with floaters | Passing volume, support angles, pressing the ball [E] | No penetration or scoring decision. Low representativeness for attacking choices [I]. |
| **S2** | **Directional SSG with real targets** | Two teams, real goals or posts, scaled numbers and space | GF 4v4 SSGs; hurling SSGs; basketball 3v3 half court; US Soccer PPP "Play" phases | Full attack-defence coupling. Load set by area per player [E]. | May under-supply HSR and pressure. Stronger players dominate [E]. |
| **S3** | **Target-manipulated directional** | Multiple goals, small goals, zone-dependent scoring | Hockey two-goals game; soccer goal-format studies; basketball passes-based scoring | Switching play, width, scanning between targets [E/I] | Invented targets may not exist in the sport [I] |
| **S4** | **Numerically imbalanced / floaters** | N vs N−k; internal or external neutral players | Soccer 4v3, 3v3+2; futsal floaters | Superior side: penetration, patience. Inferior side: compactness, delay, high load [E]. | Floaters reduce intensity and defensive realism [E] |
| **S5** | **Advantage/disadvantage waves (transition)** | Continuous waves: the defending team becomes the attacking team at once, usually outnumbered | Basketball 4v3 continuous; "advantage starts" 1v1; soccer transition-bonus games [C] | Recognising transitions, recovery, fast attack decisions [C] | Rarely researched as a learning intervention [E-gap] |
| **S6** | **Rule-conditioned SSG** | Touch, dribble or hand-pass limits; reward rules | Touch-restricted soccer; no-dribble basketball; point-possession basketball | Tempo, passing volume, off-ball movement [E] | Prohibitions remove real options and raise error rates [E/I] |
| **S7** | **Space-configured SSG** | Pitch shape, density, cages, zones | Long/wide soccer SSGs; cage hockey; GF pitch-size SSGs | Kick vs hand-pass balance; counter-attack frequency [E] | Mostly studied for load, less for learning [E] |
| **S8** | **Match simulation / large-sided** | Near-full numbers, often with phases | Australian football match simulation; GF "backs vs forwards" [C] | Structure, set plays, roles [E/C] | Measurably unrepresentative on scoring, passing and pressure [E] |
| **S9** | **Isolated / unopposed technical practice** | No opponents, or passive ones | Ball mastery; unopposed shooting | Volume of technical repetitions [C] | Changes the movement itself (shooting). Weak transfer in theory [E]. Still widely used by elite academies [E]. |

## 7. The Gaelic football context: what representative design now has to sample

### 7.1 Match facts that shape design

- **Transitions matter.** [E] Almost half of all possessions in elite Gaelic football (2020–21 knockout championship; 1,257 post-turnover possessions) came from turnovers. Winners were better at securing and retaining possession ([IJPAS 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24748668.2023.2250972)).
- **Where and how possession starts matters.** [E] In the 2016 SFC (6,174 possessions), winners had more possessions, higher productivity and fewer turnovers. Opposition kickouts, possession duration and the area where possession started were associated with possession outcome ([Determinants of successful possession, IJPAS 2020](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/24748668.2020.1758433)).
- **Kickouts (sub-elite, pre-2025).** [E] 65% were won by the kicking team. Short kickouts inside the 45 m line and quick kickouts (0–10 s) did best. The most common outcome after winning a kickout was a turnover, then a score ([Timmons, Collins & Mangan, 2022](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/rpanxx/v22y2022i4p526-540.html)).
  - [I] The "short kickout" finding **does not transfer directly**: since 2025, kickouts must travel beyond the 40 m arc ([GAA explainer](https://www.gaa.ie/article/football-review-committee-rule-enhancements-explainer)).
- **The pre-2025 style trend.** [E-journalistic, from the GAA's own analysis of 521 matches]
  - 2:1 hand-to-foot pass ratio in 2011 → 3.2:1 in 2023.
  - Passes to goalkeepers per game went from 11.1 to 23.1.
  - Turnovers fell to about 29 per match ([RTÉ, 2023](https://www.rte.ie/sport/football/2023/0928/1407954-new-study-outlines-stark-extent-of-possession-football/)).
- **Decision-making factors.** [E] Focus groups with Senior and U17 players identified four themes:
  - pre-match context (tactics, match importance, opposition);
  - current match context (**score and time remaining**);
  - visual information (positioning, space, visual search);
  - individual differences (self-efficacy, risk propensity, fatigue).
  Source: [McLoughlin et al., 2023, *Frontiers in Psychology*](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1142508/full).
  - [I] Score and time remaining are *constraints a coach can set* in a game (see affective learning design, §2.6).

### 7.2 The 2025+ rules as design constraints [E for the rules; I for the implications]

| Rule (from the [GAA FRC explainer](https://www.gaa.ie/article/football-review-committee-rule-enhancements-explainer), [RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/sport/football/2025/0311/1501368-gaa-approves-all-frc-changes-including-to-3v3-rule/)) | Information a representative game must contain [I] |
|---|---|
| 40 m arc: clean kick from on or outside the arc = 2 points | The arc must be marked, and scoring must be weighted 3/2/1. The shooter's choice of distance becomes a decision. |
| Kickouts must travel beyond the 40 m arc | Restart games need a contest zone beyond the arc, not short-kickout rehearsal |
| At least 3 outfield players kept in each half | Games with a half-way constraint; defending-half and attacking-half minimums create fixed local-number problems |
| Solo and go (tap and play within 4 m of the foul) | Fouls restart instantly, so fouled-then-go transitions are a real moment to train |
| Back-pass to goalkeeper restricted | Goalkeeper "recycle" options are limited, which changes support structure under pressure |

In October 2025 a Special Congress passed a further package of motions for 2026 ([Laois GAA explainer](https://laoisgaa.ie/gaa-rule-changes-2026-explained/); [RTÉ](https://www.rte.ie/sport/football/2025/1004/1536804-football-changes-sail-into-gaa-rule-book/)). [I] Check the current rulebook before encoding any rule into a training game.

### 7.3 Governing-body position [GB]

- **GAA (Tobar / Games-Based Approach).** Coaches are told to build sessions around games, including a game early in the session. The best way to develop decision-making is to expose players to game situations in training ([GAA Learning, A Games Based Approach](https://learning.gaa.ie/GamesBasedApproach)).
- **Go Games** are the GAA's small-sided competitive format for children:
  - maximum 7-a-side at U6/7, 9-a-side at U8/9, 11-a-side at U11 and 13-a-side at U12 (as reported by Connacht GAA);

- **Comparable bodies:**
  - FA: whole-part-whole and a TGfU foundation phase ([FA Boot Room](https://www.thefa.com/bootroom/resources/coaching/how-to-use-the-england-dna-in-your-coaching-session)).
  - US Soccer: Play-Practice-Play ([US Soccer](https://www.ussoccer.com/stories/2018/02/five-things-to-know-about-playpracticeplay)).

## 8. Transferable concepts for Gaelic football

Each concept is **[I] interpretation**, with the underlying evidence cited. These are *design principles to test*, not new games.

1. **Area per player as the kick/hand-pass dial.** GF SSG data show that small pitches produce hand passing and large pitches produce kicking ([IJPAS 2019](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24748668.2019.1689003)). Choose pitch length for the passing behaviour you want *before* adding any rule.
2. **The scoring system as a decision source.** Reward rules change behaviour and affect in basketball ([PLOS ONE 2024](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0313656)). The 3/2/1 structure is the Gaelic football equivalent and should appear in games wherever shooting is involved.
3. **Local overload, not gifted overload.** In Australian football matches, numerical advantage entering the scoring zone predicts scoring, but crowding reduces it ([PLOS ONE 2021](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0254591)). Use imbalanced starts (S4/S5) that decay into equal numbers as recovering defenders arrive.
4. **Transitions as the default event.** Almost half of elite possessions start with a turnover ([IJPAS 2023](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/24748668.2023.2250972)). Continuous-wave structures (basketball 4v3 continuous, S5) are the most direct template.
5. **Guard pressure.** SSGs can quietly lose pressure ([Browne et al., 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0242336)). Measure pressure on the ball carrier and time in possession in training games, not just in matches. This is a direct product opportunity for PáircVision.
6. **Prefer rewards and space over bans.** The evidence for prohibitions is mixed and error-inflating ([Rumpf et al., 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12558969/)).
7. **Put the game context in the game.** Score and time remaining shape decisions ([McLoughlin et al., 2023](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1142508/full)). Start games from match states ("down 2, 3 minutes left") to sample the affective and decision context ([Headrick et al., 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1469029214001137)).
8. **Keep technique in the loop.** Skill execution gains from GBAs are the weakest link ([Manninen 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/1356336X241245305); [Kinnerk 2025](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02701367.2025.2496263)). Use a whole-part-whole or PPP rhythm with brief, opposed technical focus ([FA](https://www.thefa.com/bootroom/resources/coaching/how-to-use-the-england-dna-in-your-coaching-session); [US Soccer](https://www.ussoccer.com/stories/2018/02/five-things-to-know-about-playpracticeplay)).

## 9. Evidence gaps (not filled by invention)

No published research was found on the following. Each needs original testing:

1. Training games built around the **40 m two-point arc** decision.
2. Training games for **post-2025 kickouts** (beyond the arc), and their learning effects.
3. Training effects of the **3-in-each-half** constraint on structure.
4. **Solo-and-go** transition training.
5. Learning (rather than load) outcomes of Gaelic football SSG pitch sizes. The existing Gaelic football SSG studies measure physical and technical *counts*.
6. Longitudinal senior-level GBA interventions in Gaelic football. Kinnerk et al. (2025) was U14/U15.
7. Advantage/disadvantage wave games as a *measured* intervention in any of the four sports. They are strong in convention but thin in evidence.
8. Female Gaelic football SSG research. Kickout research exists for LGFA ([Investigating successful kickout performance…, IJPAS 2024](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/24748668.2024.2416738); [MDPI 2026](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/16/7/3277)), but no SSG learning studies were found.

## 10. Implications for PáircVision (brief) [I]

- The product already records kickouts, turnovers and shot outcomes, including two-pointers. Those match-derived frequencies are exactly the "competition side" of a representativeness comparison (Browne et al., 2020).
- If training games were tagged with the same events, the app could report how closely a club's training resembles its matches on:
  - kickout contest rate;
  - turnover rate;
  - hand-to-kick pass ratio;
  - shot distance mix.
- That is a measurable, evidence-grounded use of these findings. It is **not** a recommendation to build this now.

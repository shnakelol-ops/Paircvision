# 06 — Research Implications

Answers to the seven audit questions. They draw on [`03`](./03-manipulation-behaviour-matrix.md), [`04`](./04-evidence-ledger.md) and [`05`](./05-conflicts-gaps-and-unknowns.md).

**Access caveat.** Every conclusion here rests on **abstract-level evidence**. Full text could not be retrieved in this environment (ledger §0). Conclusions are stated at the confidence that allows.

## 1. Which manipulation → behaviour relationships are genuinely well supported?

Grade **B** or better at moderate-to-high confidence. Every one of these is **acute**: it describes behaviour during the manipulated game, not learning.

| Relationship | Evidence | Why it counts as well supported | Key caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fewer players → more individual technical involvements per player** (R1) | Hockey (H1, de-confounded; H2), basketball (B3), soccer (S3, S4) | 3 sports; at least one study separates number from area | Frequency, not quality. The physical-intensity part of the popular claim is mostly an *area* effect (S5, H3). |
| **Larger area per player → greater team dispersion / surface area** (R3) | Soccer meta-analysis (S7) plus originals (S9, S12) | Many studies, consistent | Soccer only. Technical counts do **not** reliably change (S7). |
| **Numerical superiority → more attacking actions; the underloaded defence compacts** (R8) | Soccer (S22, S24, S25), basketball (B4, B6, B7) | 2 sports, several independent groups, consistent direction | The superior side's intensity drops (R9). Pressure falls. |
| **Numerical superiority → lower intensity for the superior team** (R9) | Soccer (S23, S24, S38), basketball (B6) | Consistent | Physical only. |
| **Floaters → more passing, lower intensity, less penetration** (R13) | Soccer and futsal (S24, S28, S29) | Consistent within soccer/futsal | Single sport family. |
| **Possession-only objective → more passing, fewer dribbles and tackles** (R14) | Hockey (H2), soccer (S15) | 2 sports | Acute only. |
| **Touch restriction → more passes *and* more errors** (R15) | Soccer review (S32) plus original (S33) | Consistent within soccer | No Gaelic analogue has been tested. |
| **Opposition/pressure changes the skill and the decision** (R17) | Basketball kinematics (B16), soccer review (S35) | 2 sports, different outcome types that agree | Lower success under pressure is the *point*, not a flaw. |
| **Coach encouragement → higher intensity** (R20) | Soccer (S2), basketball (B14, B15) | Consistent | Motivational, not tactical. |

**Nothing reaches "well supported" as a *learning* or *transfer* relationship for any single manipulation.**
- The learning evidence (S46, S47, S48, S49, GA4, X1, X2) tests **packages** of game-based practice.
- Those packages show better decision-making (GA4 in Gaelic football: grade A, but one study).
- They show no clear technical advantage over other methods.

## 2. Which relationships look transferable to Gaelic football but are untested?

These are grade **B** or **C** elsewhere, with **no Gaelic test**. Transfer is plausible because Gaelic football shares the invasion structure: two teams, a contested ball, and goal-directed scoring with goalkeepers.

| Relationship | Evidence elsewhere | Why transfer is plausible | Why it might **not** transfer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fewer players → more involvements (R1) | Hockey, basketball, soccer | The mechanism (fewer teammates sharing one ball) is sport-general | — (low risk) |
| Superiority → more attacking actions, compact defence (R8/R9) | Soccer, basketball | General invasion mechanism | GF has no offside and allows hand passing and carrying. Superiority may be exploited by carrying rather than passing, which is untested. |
| Floaters → more passing, less penetration (R13) | Soccer/futsal | General | The GF hand pass makes retention already easy. Floaters may exaggerate the retention bias the 2025 rules tried to reduce. |
| Possession-only → more passing, fewer tackles (R14) | Hockey, soccer | General | Same retention concern. |
| Pressure changes execution and decisions (R17) | Basketball, soccer | Kicking under a closing defender plausibly behaves like a contested jump shot | Magnitude unknown for kick passing and point-taking. |
| Aspect ratio (area fixed) → counter-attacks and penetration (R6) | Soccer, one clean study | GF pitches are long; transition matters | One study only; GF has larger scoring zones (posts above the goal). |
| Width/length crossover (R7) | Soccer, one study | Geometric | — |
| Area per player → team dispersion (R3) | Soccer | Geometric | GF team shape is also governed by the 3-in-each-half rule since 2025. |
| Carry restriction → more passes and turnovers (R16) | Basketball | GF has carrying (solo/bounce) | GF carrying rules differ; hand-passing may replace the lost carry. |

**The only relationships with direct Gaelic evidence (grade A):**
- **R5.** Small GF pitch → more hand passes and tackles; large → more kick passes and goal conversion. One study, n = 16, confounded, abstract only, pre-2025.
- **GA2.** Non-linear HSR across pitch lengths. Physical only.
- **R25.** A GBA package improves youth decision-making. One study, package-level.

## 3. Which popular coaching constraints have surprisingly weak evidence?

Summarising 05 §2:

- **Recovering defenders / fading overloads**: no study found (**E**). This is despite being arguably the most match-like way overloads occur.
- **Transition waves / counter-attack games**: physical data only (**D** for behaviour).
- **End zones**: physical data only (**D** for tactical).
- **Direction reversal, mandatory passes, restart type**: nothing found (**E**).
- **Touch restrictions** as a way to *improve* passing: they increase passes by compliance, with more errors. There is no learning evidence.
- **Zones/channels**: reduce exploration (S34).
- **Coach questioning huddles**: poorly received by Gaelic youth players (GA4).
- **Man vs zone defence** as a differentiating constraint: no technical difference found in basketball (B12).

## 4. Which findings conflict?

See 05 §1 for the full list. The most consequential:

1. **Does area per player change technical actions?** No in the soccer meta-analysis (S7); yes for *pass type* in Gaelic football (GA3). This may be a genuine sport difference, because Gaelic football has two distance-specific passing actions.
2. **Running response to Gaelic football pitch length is non-linear** (GA2), unlike the monotonic soccer pattern.
3. **Possession vs goal games: which is more intense?** Soccer (S20) and hurling (GA8) disagree.
4. **Does "exaggeration" produce its target behaviour?** S43 says yes; S50 says no.
5. **Instruction within games:** helps coordination (S43) but hurts when stacked (S44).

## 5. Which manipulations change behaviour but may harm representativeness?

See 05 §3. In short:
- **Every "facilitating" manipulation trades pressure for success.** This covers superiority, floaters, possession-only, large area and unopposed work.
- **Every "prohibiting" manipulation trades options for volume.** This covers touch, carry and zone limits.
- **Small formats** do not recreate match spacing (S12) or match-like scanning situations (S45).
- **Large-sided "match simulation"** is not automatically match-like either (X5).

The underlying problem is that **most studies measure the training game, not its correspondence to competition.** Only S12 (soccer), X4/X5 (Australian football) and S45 (scanning) compare against match behaviour.

## 6. What important relationships remain unknown?

The highest-value unknowns for Gaelic football (full list in 05 §5):

1. **Isolated effects of any single manipulation on Gaelic football learning.** GA4 tested a package.
2. **Post-2025 rule-era effects**: the two-point arc as a scoring constraint, kickouts beyond the arc, and the 3-in-each-half rule as a structural constraint.
3. **Restart (kickout) manipulations.** No study found in any sport.
4. **Recovering-defender / fading-overload designs** and recognition of temporary advantage.
5. **Transition behaviour** as a measured outcome in any sport.
6. **Carry constraints** specific to Gaelic football.
7. **Transfer** of any manipulated behaviour to competition, in any sport.
8. **Decision quality** (not counts) under most manipulations.
9. **Engagement** beyond enjoyment in a handful of studies.
10. **Female Gaelic football** SSG behaviour.

## 7. Is there enough evidence to justify an evidence-based Game Design Grammar for Gaelic football?

**Short answer: not yet as an *evidence-based* grammar. Yes as an *evidence-informed, hypothesis-labelled* grammar**, provided every rule in it carries its grade.

### What the evidence can support today
- **A small core of acute, cross-sport "levers" (grade B)**, each with known side-effects:
  - player number (involvement);
  - area per player (spacing);
  - numerical superiority (attacking opportunity vs pressure);
  - floaters (retention vs penetration);
  - possession-only (passing vs dribbling/tackling);
  - opposition pressure (skill and decision fidelity).

  These can be stated as *"this lever tends to move this behaviour in this direction, and it costs you this."*
- **One Gaelic-specific lever (grade A, low confidence):** pitch dimension shifting the hand-pass vs kick-pass balance.
- **One Gaelic-specific learning result (grade A, package-level):** GBA sessions improved youth decision-making.

### What it cannot support
- Claims that any manipulation **teaches** or **transfers** a behaviour. There is no isolated learning evidence.
- Claims about **restarts, recovering defenders, transitions, direction reversal, end zones or mandatory passes**, which are D/E.
- Claims about the **post-2025 game**. Every Gaelic study pre-dates the rules.
- Precise **dose or thresholds** (e.g., "use X m² per player for Y"). The only GF dimensions studied are 40–80×20 m for 4v4 and three minor-grade pitches with unknown numbers.

### What would make a defensible grammar possible
1. **Full-text verification** of the ~15 priority sources (05 §6), especially GA2, GA3 and GA4.
2. **Gaelic-specific, de-confounded acute studies.** Vary one factor at a time (number with APP fixed; area with shape fixed; aspect ratio with area fixed; target value, including 2-point scoring) and code **pass type, decision quality and pressure**, not just counts.
3. **Match-correspondence measurement.** Compare training-game behaviour distributions with post-2025 match distributions: pressure on the ball carrier, time in possession, kick/hand-pass ratio, shot distance mix, turnover rate, kickout contest rate.
   - [Interpretation] This is where a match-and-training tagging product could produce the missing evidence, using the representativeness method of X4 (Browne et al., 2020).
4. **At least one longitudinal Gaelic study** that isolates a single manipulation and tests retention in a neutral assessment game.

### Recommended framing if work proceeds
- Treat each grammar rule as a **graded hypothesis card**: lever → expected behaviour → evidence grade → confounds → sacrifice.
- **Default to "unknown"** wherever the matrix shows D or E.
- Mark every Gaelic rule as **pre-2025 evidence** until re-tested.
- Keep **acute behaviour change** and **learning** as separate claims in all coach-facing language.

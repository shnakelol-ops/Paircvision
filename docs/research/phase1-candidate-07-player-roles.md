> **Central question.** What does assigning a player a role actually manipulate in a practice? Does a role:
> - create a useful game problem;
> - constrain the player's possible solutions;
> - supply part of the solution; or
> - simply describe tactical responsibility?
>
> **Families so far (GAME MOMENT gate §22):**
> 1. **CHOOSE** the problem (GAME MOMENT).
> 2. **CHANGE** the problem (SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION, SCORING).
> 3. **SUPPLY vs DETECT / CREATE** (INFORMATION; cross-framework principle **CF-1**).
>
> **Chain kept separate:** ASSIGNED ROLE → BEHAVIOUR WHILE ASSIGNED → RECOGNITION OF NEED → SELF-ASSIGNMENT / COORDINATION → LEARNING → RETENTION → TRANSFER.
>
> **Standing rules:**
> - Practice behaviour ≠ learning.
> - **Do not infer explicit roles are bad.** Shared tactical responsibilities may be exactly what a coach intends to teach.

## 1. Defining "role"

| Example | What it actually specifies | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| "You are corner-back." | A **position** within a structure | **Position** (a structural assignment) |
| "Protect the middle." | A **responsibility / intention** | **Responsibility** (instructional; INFORMATION-type) |
| "Stay connected to #14." | A **relationship** to a teammate (instruction) | **Tactical instruction** (INFORMATION) |
| "You are the spare defender." | A numerical function | **NUMBERS** (who is the extra player) + responsibility |
| "You can only defend." | A **restriction** on legal action | **RULES** |
| "You are a neutral player." | Plays for whichever team has the ball | **NUMBERS** (floater; frozen N5) + RULES |
| "Your job is to provide support." | Responsibility | **Responsibility** (instructional) |
| "Press the receiver." | A specific **action** instruction | **Tactical instruction** (INFORMATION) |

**Finding.** "Role" is an umbrella for at least five different things:
1. position;
2. responsibility / intention;
3. action instruction;
4. restriction;
5. numerical function.

Only **position** and **responsibility** are "roles" in any distinct sense.

## 2. Position ≠ role

**Proposed:**
- **Position** describes where, or within what structure, a player operates.
- **Role** describes a responsibility or function.

**Evidence and terminology:**
- The research literature uses "positional role" to mean position category: centre back, full back, defensive midfielder, etc.
  - FUT-SAT studies: U-17 (n = 268) found no difference in the quantity of tactical actions by positional role. Forwards had lower defensive tactical quality than full backs. A FUT-SAT field test (U16–17, n = 54) found **no positional-role differences** (AO).
- Role-clarity psychology uses "role" to mean **responsibilities** (scope, behaviours, evaluation, consequences; Eys, Carron, Beauchamp & Bray, 2005; AO).
- **Terminology is inconsistent across fields.**

**Verdict: the distinction is useful for PáircVision, but not established terminology** (PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS, taxonomy).
- **Position** = structural assignment.
- **Responsibility** = the job attached to it or given independently.

## 3. Role vs rule

| Statement | What it does | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| "Your job is to protect the middle." | Specifies an **intention**. The player may still leave the middle if they judge it right | **Responsibility** (instruction) |
| "You may not leave the middle." | Removes legal actions | **RULES** (× SPACE: positional zone) |

**Does the distinction survive?** Yes, and it parallels SCORING's legal-vs-meaningful analysis:
- A responsibility leaves options **legal**. It changes what the player **intends** or prioritises.
- A rule removes options.
- Mandatory positional zones (a rule) reduced teammate co-adaptation (Gonçalves et al., 2017; frozen SPACE).
- **No study compares a zone rule with a responsibility instruction for the same behaviour.**

**Status: PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS (taxonomy)**, with the edge case that strong social pressure to obey can make a responsibility **functionally** a rule.

## 4. Role vs information

| Statement | Proposed classification |
|---|---|
| "Look for moments when the middle needs protecting." | **Information / attention cue** (what to notice) |
| "You are responsible for protecting the middle." | **Responsibility** (what to prioritise) |
| "Stay in the middle." | **Rule** (what is allowed) |

**Attack: is this artificial?**
- **Partly.** The first two are both **verbal instructions** that shape intention. Research treats them together as **instructional constraints**.
- **Cordovil, Araújo, Davids et al. (2009)**, *Eur J Sport Sci* 9(3):169–179 (AO): 11 experienced female basketball players, 350 1v1 trials.
  - **Neutral, risk-taking or conservative instructions** changed emergent behaviour.
  - Conservative instructions increased time to cross mid-court and trajectory variability.
- **Passos and colleagues** (soccer 1v1, sub-18): instruction specificity influenced intentionality, decisions and actions (AO / SIO).

**Verdict.**
- Information and responsibility are **both instructional constraints**. They differ in **what** they specify (a cue vs a priority), not in **type** of manipulation.
- The **rule** is a different type.
- **Collapse "information" and "responsibility" into one instructional category** (INFORMATION-family) for taxonomy, while keeping the coach-language difference.

## 5. Role vs numbers

## 6. Role assignment and behaviour: evidence

| Study (access) | Manipulation | Observed | A / L / T |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cordovil et al. (2009) (AO) | Instructions: neutral / risk-taking / conservative (basketball 1v1) | Time to cross midline and trajectory variability changed | **A** |
| Passos et al. (soccer 1v1; AO / SIO) | Instructional constraints | Intentionality, decisions, actions changed | **A** |
| **González-Rodenas et al. (2024)**, *J Hum Kinet* (AO) | **Formation** 2-3-1 vs 3-1-2 (U19, 7-a-side, n = 18) | Attack: 2-3-1 ↑ height, width, surface area. Defence: 2-3-1 ↑ height, ↓ length, surface area, stretch index. Opponents' shapes also changed | **A** |
| Field location and **player roles** in 1v1 / 2v1 (*Hum Mov Sci* 2017; *Front Psychol* 2019; AO) | Roles (defender / midfielder / attacker) as participant characteristic | Roles and foot preference affected interpersonal coordination | **A** (descriptive of role as player characteristic) |
| Positional-role FUT-SAT studies (U-17, n = 268; U-13; U16–17 field test; AO) | None (comparison by usual position) | Few or no differences; some defensive-quality and decision-speed differences | Descriptive |
| **Fixed-role SSGs** (*Front Sports Act Living* 2025; U-12, n = 31; 18 weeks; AO) | Fixed attacker / defender roles in SSGs vs control | Agility, reaction time, speed, change of direction ↑ (physical); no change in agility without the ball | **L (physical)** |
| **Wang & Liu (2023)**, *Ann Oper Res* 325 (AO) | None (NBA longitudinal) | Higher **positional concentration** associated with better player and team performance, especially for non-stars | Competition association |

**Summary:**
- Instructions and formations change **acute** behaviour (SUPPORTED, acute).
- Learning / retention / transfer of tactical roles: **none**.

## 7. CF-1 stress test: "Your role is always to support the ball carrier."

| Link | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Assigned responsibility | Given |
| Performed responsibility | Expected (instructions change acute behaviour: Cordovil; Passos) |
| Recognised need (without assignment) | **No study** |
| Self-assigned response (who supports, when) | **No study** |
| Retained / transferred | **No study** |

**Verdict.**
- Improved support **while assigned** is not evidence of support **recognition**.
- The assignment answers "who supports?" for the players.
- **CF-1 applies directly** (SUPPORTED as evidence status).
- **Caveat.** If the coach's aim is **team coordination** (everyone knowing who supports), the assignment may be **the thing being taught**, not a shortcut.

## 8. Player agency

| Design | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Coach-assigned roles | Instructions and formations change acute behaviour |
| Players choose or swap roles | **No located study** |
| Roles emerging from game state (whoever is nearest presses) | Ecological theory (self-organisation) only; no comparative study |

- **Does role prescription change autonomy, exploration or tactical behaviour?**
  - Positional zone **rules** reduce co-adaptation (SPACE).
  - More rule manipulations reduce exploration (*Front Psychol* 2019).
  - For **responsibility** assignments (not rules): **no data**.
- **Autonomy is not assumed superior.**
  - *RQES* 2024 (SCORING gate): prescriptive coach instruction was associated with **more connected, cooperative** passing networks than constraints alone.
- **Status: UNKNOWN.**

## 10. Attacking roles

- Width provider, depth / support, target player, runner, playmaker, screen / block, decoy, third-player movement, inside / outside support.
- **Evidence:**
  - formations shift team width and height acutely (González-Rodenas 2024);
  - **no study** shows that assigning, say, "width provider" teaches width creation.
- **Assignment guarantees presence, not learning** (CF-1).
- **UNKNOWN.**

## 11. Defensive roles

- Marker, cover defender, press player, sweeper, screen, recovery defender, zonal responsibility.
- **Distinguish:**
  - **Team tactical coordination**: a shared defensive system *requires* assigned responsibilities.
  - **Individual game understanding**: knowing when to press or cover without being told.
- Evidence covers the former only acutely (formations; instructions). Neither learning outcome has been tested.
- **A prescribed role may be essential to a shared system without proving generalised defensive learning** (PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION; consistent with CF-1).

## 12. Transition and role change

- Roles flip instantly: attacker → defender; support → press; carrier → off-ball.
- **Evidence:**
  - Transition moments matter (frozen TIME / DIRECTION: counter-attacks vs unset defences; first action after a turnover in Gaelic).
  - **No study** examines practice of **role switching** after possession change, or recognition that one's role has changed.
- "Recognising that one's role has changed is more important than practising the role": **UNKNOWN. Not assumed.**
- Links to the GAME MOMENT gate's transition hypothesis: a called turnover removes change **detection**.

---

## 13. Team coordination vs player adaptability

- **Applying the GAME MOMENT distinction** (practitioner heuristic there):
  - Assigned roles may establish collective coordination (game model, responsibilities).
  - Open roles may expose individual perception and self-organisation.
- **Evidence:**
  - Coordination: formations change collective shape (acute).
  - Instruction → cooperative networks (*RQES* 2024).
  - Role clarity ↔ role efficacy (psychology).
  - Adaptability: no role-specific evidence.
- **"Prescription bad / freedom good": REJECTED.**
- **Status: PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC** (both purposes legitimate; untested comparatively).

---

## 14. Representative design

- If competition contains tactical roles (goalkeeper, kickout receiver, sweeper, press leader, designated marker), then assigning them in practice **samples competition** (representative learning design, Pinder et al., 2011; theory).
- **Practice-only roles** (neutral, joker, bounce player, server) do **not** exist in competition. They change NUMBERS / RULES in ways competition doesn't (frozen N5: floaters have unrepresentative loads).
- **Verdict (PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS):**
  - Assigning **genuine match roles** can **increase** representativeness.
  - Practice-only roles reduce it unless deliberately used for a specific purpose.
  - No learning evidence either way.

## 16. Role clarity

**Evidence (psychology, AO):**
- **Bray & Brawley (2002)**, *Small Group Research* 33(2): role clarity and role efficacy relate to role performance effectiveness.
- **Beauchamp et al. (2005)**, *J Appl Soc Psychol*: multidimensional role ambiguity predicts lower role satisfaction.
- **Eys et al. (2005)**, *Small Group Research*: athletes' perceived sources of role ambiguity.
- Related multilevel work: role ambiguity explained variance in role efficacy (~21–22 %; SIO).
- Ambiguity decreases across a season (SIO).

**Separation.**
- This literature concerns **team psychology and organisation**: satisfaction, efficacy, perceived performance.
- It is **not evidence** that assigned practice roles improve **skill or tactical learning**.
- **Must not be used that way.**

## 17. Youth development

- **Within-sport position rotation vs early positional specialisation:**
  - **no located study** in invasion sports;
  - claims (academy rotation; specialise at 14–16; rotate for "football intelligence") are **practitioner / advocacy** (SIO).
- Sport-level early-specialisation guidance (paediatric recommendations) concerns **sports**, not positions. **Not transferable without evidence.**
- **Verdict: UNKNOWN.** "Youth players benefit from experiencing different roles" is a **PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC**.

## 18. Expert / adult teams

- High-performance teams assign specific responsibilities: pressing triggers, set-piece and kickout jobs, match-ups, attacking shape.
- **Why (reasoning, consistent with evidence):**
  - Coordinated collective behaviour needs shared expectations (formations change collective shape).
  - Role clarity relates to efficacy (psychology).
  - Positional concentration associates with NBA performance.
- **When is explicit role prescription simply good tactical coaching?** When the **aim is coordination**:
  - restarts;
  - defensive systems;
  - a game model;
  - opponent-specific plans.
- **PáircVision must not make coordinated team tactics impossible.** CF-1 says only that assigned behaviour doesn't demonstrate **recognition**. It doesn't say assignment is wrong.

## 21. Role → decision removal

| Role assigned | Decision the player no longer makes | Decisions still open | Possible benefit | Possible cost | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "You are the presser." | **Who** initiates pressure | Angle, speed, timing, reaction to opponent | Coordination; clarity | Recognition of press moments by others untrained | None (logic) |
| "You are the cover." | Who covers | Depth, angle, when to step | Defensive balance | Others may never learn to cover | None |
| "You provide width." | Who goes wide | When to come inside; timing of runs | Width guaranteed | Width creation by others untrained | Formation effects (acute) |
| "You always support the carrier." | Who supports | Angle, distance, timing | Support present | Support recognition untrained | CF-1 |
| "You are the target player." | Where the ball goes first | Hold-up vs lay-off | Clear outlet | Predictability; others' movement reduced | None |
| "Sweeper." | Who sits behind | When to step out; communication | Protection; numbers behind the ball | Others' 1v1 defending exposure reduced | Gaelic commentary (man-marking skills declined in sweeper era: opinion) |
| "Man-mark #14." | Whom to defend | How tight; when to release; switching | Clear match-up | Zonal awareness untrained | None |
| "Kickout receiver." | Who shows for the kickout | Movement type; timing | Restart coordination (representative) | Others don't learn to show | None |
| "Press leader / trigger." | When the press starts (for others) | Individual pressing actions | Collective timing | Others don't read triggers themselves | None |
| "Neutral." | Which team to help (fixed by rule) | Passing choices | Overload | Unrepresentative load (N5) | NUMBERS gate |
| "Recovery defender." | Who recovers | Recovery line; delay vs tackle | Transition defence practice | Others don't learn recovery | None |
| "Decoy runner." | Whether to make the run | Run timing, angle | Creates space for others | The decoy runs even when a direct run is better | None |
| "Goalkeeper as playmaker." | Who restarts / builds | Pass choice | Build-up structure | — | Descriptive |

**Key insight (PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION).**
- Most role assignments **remove the "who" decision** (allocation of responsibility).
- They leave the **"how / when"** decisions open.
- CF-1's concern is therefore specific: **self-allocation and recognition of need** go unpractised, not all decision-making.

## 25. Coach language

| Term | Everyday coaching meaning | Suggested PáircVision use (not scientific) |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Where you play in the structure | Structural assignment |
| Role / job | What you're responsible for | **Responsibility** (the "who does what") |
| Responsibility | Same as job | Same |
| Instruction | What the coach tells you to look for or do | INFORMATION-type |
| Rule | What is allowed or required | RULES |
| Principle | A general idea (e.g., "support the ball") | Team-wide guidance, not assigned to one player |

**No fake scientific distinctions.**
- The literature treats most of these as **instructional constraints** vs **task rules**.
- The useful coach-facing split is **job** (who does it) vs **rule** (what's allowed) vs **principle** (what everyone should look for).

## 29. Volunteer coach translation (U12 Gaelic)

> **Giving someone a job can really help.** If everyone knows who goes for the kickout, who marks their best forward, who holds the middle, your team works together. In matches you need that. Practising it is proper coaching.
>
> **But be honest about what it teaches.** If you tell one player "you always support the ball", you'll see support. You picked who supports and made that decision for them. The others never had to spot that the player on the ball was stranded.
>
> **So mix it.** Sometimes give jobs, so the team learns to work together. Sometimes don't, and watch: does anyone notice the carrier needs help? Who goes? Do they sort it out between them?
>
> **A job isn't a rule.** "Your job is to mind the middle" still lets them leave if something better is on. "You're not allowed out of the middle" doesn't. Choose which one you mean.

## 31. Taxonomic verdict

### **D — SPLIT PLAYER ROLES INTO MULTIPLE CONCEPTS**

**Why not A (CHANGE-THE-PROBLEM lever):**
- A role's effects on teammates and opponents are frozen-lever effects (§22): NUMBERS, SPACE, TIME, DIRECTION.

**Why not B (a single different type of decision):**
- "Role" is not one decision. It bundles position, responsibility, instruction, restriction and numerical function.

**Why not a pure C (absorb):**
- One component survives that no lever describes: **ALLOCATION OF RESPONSIBILITY**, i.e. **who** is responsible for **which** part of the problem, set by the coach or self-organised by players.

**Split and destinations:**

| Component | Destination |
|---|---|
| Restriction ("can only defend", "may not leave zone") | **RULES** |
| Numerical function (neutral, joker, bounce, permanent attacker, spare man) | **NUMBERS** (+ RULES) |
| Action instruction / intention ("press the receiver", "be risky") | **INFORMATION** (instructional constraints) |
| Target player | **DIRECTION** (target form) |
| Server; recovering defender | **GAME MOMENT** (generation) / **TIME** (entry) |
| Formation / position structure (effects) | **SPACE** (collective shape) × NUMBERS |
| **Allocation of responsibility** (who presses, covers, supports, marks, receives): coach-assigned vs self-organised | **Surviving concept: RESPONSIBILITY ALLOCATION**, in the "coach supplies vs players create" family (§32) |
| **Role rotation** (which player experiences which responsibility over time) | **Exposure decision**, alongside GAME MOMENT (who meets which problem) |

## 32. Framework implication

**Changing the game, assigning responsibility, and removing a decision are different things:**
- **Changing the game** (SPACE–SCORING) alters the problem everyone faces.
- **Assigning responsibility** decides **who** handles which part of the problem. It changes others' problems only **through** frozen-lever effects (numbers behind the ball, width, pressure).
- **Removing a decision.** Allocation removes the **self-allocation** decision ("who should go?") and usually the **recognition of need** ("does anyone need to go?"). It leaves the **execution** decisions (how, when) open.

**Relation to CF-1.**
- Responsibility allocation is a **direct instance** of CF-1: behaviour from a coach-assigned job is not evidence players can recognise the need or self-assign.
- **Unlike most CF-1 cases, allocation is often the deliberate teaching target** (team coordination, game model, restarts). CF-1 limits the **inference**, not the practice.

**Is there a fourth family?**
- The evidence does **not** require one.
- Responsibility allocation fits the third family: **what the coach supplies vs what players must detect / create**.
- That family now has two clear members:
  - **INFORMATION** (what the coach tells players to notice or intend);
  - **RESPONSIBILITY ALLOCATION** (who the coach makes responsible).
- **Role rotation** extends the first family (**choose the problem**) to the individual: **which player** meets which problem, how often.

**Open question for the final gate.**
- Is "coach control" one family with two members, or should INFORMATION and ALLOCATION be separate families?
- Decide after the INFORMATION audit. **Do not force symmetry.**

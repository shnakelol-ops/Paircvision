# PáircVision Phase 1 — Practice Design Lever #3: TIME

**Type:** Scientific evidence review. Research only: no drills, sessions, UI or production code.
**Frozen levers this document builds on:**
- [SPACE review](./phase1-lever-01-space.md) + [SPACE final gate](./phase1-lever-01-space-final-gate.md)
- [NUMBERS review](./phase1-lever-02-numbers.md) + [NUMBERS final gate](./phase1-lever-02-numbers-final-gate.md)

The final gates override the earlier reviews where they differ.
**Status:** v1 review, September 2026. A final falsification gate is expected to follow, as with Levers 01 and 02.

> **Central question.** What actually happens when a coach changes the time available to perceive, decide, act, recover or exploit an opportunity?
>
> **Carried-forward rule.** Behaviour *produced* inside a constrained game is not evidence it has been *learned*. "Learned" is reserved for retention, transfer or competition evidence.
>
> **Five separations that must not collapse:**
> - less time ≠ faster perception
> - faster decision ≠ better decision
> - faster action ≠ faster decision
> - more rushed actions ≠ improved speed of play
> - behaviour under a countdown ≠ learning after the countdown disappears

## 0. Method, labels and access limits

### 0.1 Labels (required on important claims)

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **RESEARCH EVIDENCE** | Peer-reviewed study or review. Strength is given separately. |
| **ESTABLISHED PRACTICE** | Governing-body rules or guidance, or widespread coaching use. Not tested for the claimed learning effect. |
| **PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC** | Coaching media, analytics blogs, industry or league statistics, rules of thumb. |
| **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS** | Our own inference. Untested. |

Confidence levels are H / M / L / VL, as in Levers 01–02.

### 0.2 Access limitations

- The research environment's network proxy **blocked full text** on PubMed/PMC, Frontiers, SAGE, Taylor & Francis, Wiley, Ovid/JSCR, ResearchGate, MDPI and university repositories.
- **All verification is abstract-level or search-index-level.** Figures quoted come from abstracts or indexed summaries.
- **(index)** marks attribution taken from a search-engine summary only. **†** marks an unresolved citation, author list or figure. Variable-level results must be confirmed from full text before PáircVision surfaces any statement to coaches.

### 0.3 Honest summary of the evidence base

- **Touch restrictions** are the best-researched temporal manipulation: a 2025 systematic review plus several recent studies. The evidence is mostly soccer, acute, and about physical and technical outcomes. Tactical evidence is "very scarce".
- **Time pressure / possession clocks** have a handful of acute SSG studies (soccer counter-attack, basketball reduced shot clock). In laboratory and video work, time pressure hurts novices more than experts.
- **Scanning** is the strongest area for "player creates time". There is match association evidence (elite soccer), and two recent intervention studies show scanning can be increased and **retained** (in small-sided test games).
- **Match temporal windows** (counter-attacks, counter-pressing) have substantial soccer match-analysis evidence. **Gaelic football match analyses point the other way from the "score in six seconds" heuristic** (§6, §9).
- **Learning under artificially reduced time:** **no study was found** showing that practice with artificially reduced time improves performance under natural match pressure once the constraint is removed. **UNKNOWN.**

## 1. Defining TIME: one lever or several?

### 1.1 Clock time vs functional time

**Functional time** is the time a player has before an action opportunity closes. It depends on:
- opponent distance and closing speed;
- the player's own action capabilities (speed, reach, kick or strike range);
- body orientation;
- ball speed;
- support options.

**Clock time** is the time a rule or countdown allows.

**Is the distinction supported?** **Yes, at the level of theory and laboratory or dyadic research.**
- **Affordance-based control** holds that performers perceive what they *can* do, scaled to their own action capabilities. Time-to-contact information is action-relevant only when referenced to the actor's capabilities, and performers must stay within the boundary between possible and impossible actions (Fajen, 2005, *Perception*; Fajen, 2007, *Ecological Psychology*) (**RESEARCH EVIDENCE**, M; mostly interception tasks).
- **Invasion-game dyads:** attacker–defender *interpersonal distance* and its rate of change govern outcomes (Passos et al., 2008, rugby union), and the closing of gaps informs pass decisions (Correia et al., 2011) (**RESEARCH EVIDENCE**, M; see Lever 01).
- **Match behaviour:** elite players **scan less under tight opponent pressure** (Jordet et al., 2020), which is consistent with pressure reducing functional time for information gathering (**RESEARCH EVIDENCE**, M).
- **Expertise:** experts pick up perceptual cues faster and more accurately (Mann et al., 2007, meta-analysis of 42 studies). The same clock time is effectively **more functional time** for a skilled player (**RESEARCH EVIDENCE**, H for the expert advantage; the "more functional time" framing is a PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS).

**Implication.** A countdown sets an **upper bound** on clock time. It does not set functional time, which is governed mostly by SPACE, NUMBERS and the opponent (**PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS**, theory-consistent).

### 1.2 Proposed structure: TIME is a family, not one lever

| Family | What the coach changes | Examples | Overlap |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1 Functional time** (emergent) | Not set directly; comes from space, numbers and opponent behaviour | Time before pressure arrives; natural time pressure | **Outcome of SPACE × NUMBERS**, not an independent lever |
| **T2 Clock rules** | Explicit time limits | Possession / shot clocks, countdowns, "score within X seconds", required waiting periods | TIME proper |
| **T3 Action-count rules** | Limits on actions, which indirectly limit time | Touch limits, carry / solo limits | **Hybrid TIME / RULES**: changes *what* can be done, not only *when* (§3) |
| **T4 Timing of numbers** | When players enter or leave | Delayed defender entry, recovering defenders, temporary overload duration | **Hybrid TIME / NUMBERS** |
| **T5 Restart timing** | Speed of re-starting play | Quick restarts, delays, ball-out-of-play time | TIME / RULES |
| **T6 Exposure structure** | Bout duration, work:rest, game length | Bout duration, recovery intervals | Mostly **physical load**; also pacing |
| **T7 Temporal predictability** | Whether players know the time structure | Known vs unknown duration; random vs fixed windows | Affects pacing and strategy |

**PáircVision position (HYPOTHESIS).** Treat TIME as **several related manipulations**. Teach coaches that **functional time is mostly created or destroyed by space, numbers and the opponent**. Clocks and touch limits are blunter tools that also change *which actions are allowed*.

## 2. TIME manipulation framework

Format: **MANIPULATION → OBSERVED EFFECT → LIKELY MECHANISM → EVIDENCE → CONTEXT → SIDE EFFECT → CONFIDENCE**

### 2.1 Time available before pressure arrives (functional time; via space, numbers, defender start distance)

- **Observed:** smaller spaces and more defenders give shorter interpersonal distances, more pressure and more duels (Lever 01). Under tight pressure, elite players scan less (Jordet et al., 2020).
- **Mechanism:** shorter time-to-contact.
- **Evidence:** RESEARCH EVIDENCE (M), Levers 01 and 02 plus scanning studies.
- **Side effect:** technical breakdown. Higher HR has been associated with lower decision quality (J Sports Sci Med, 2025, from the SPACE gate).
- **Confidence:** M (acute).

### 2.2 Possession / time-on-ball limits, countdowns, "score within X seconds"

- **Observed:**
  - U17 soccer counter-attack SSGs (4v4 vs 5v4): a limit on the duration of the offensive action **worsened tactical decision-making and execution** (Rochael & Praça, 2024, *IJSSC*).
  - A companion study found **no physical differences** between time-pressure and no-time-pressure formats (Rochael & Praça, 2025, *Proc IMechE P*).
  - Basketball U17 3v3 with a **reduced shot clock** increased physical demand (more jumps) compared with a half-court regular game (Bredt et al., 2020, *Biology of Sport* 37(2):131–138).
- **Mechanism:** less clock time → fewer options explored, more hurried shots or passes.
- **Evidence:** RESEARCH EVIDENCE (L–M), acute.
- **Side effect:** rushed, lower-quality decisions. Players may learn to "beat the clock" rather than read the defence (**PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS**).
- **Confidence:** L–M.

### 2.3 Touch limits (indirect temporal constraint)

See the deep dive in §3. Summary: more passes, higher intensity in adults, mixed pass success, and very scarce tactical evidence.

### 2.4 Scoring windows / transition windows after regain or loss

- **Observed (match):**
  - Counter-attacks and fast attacks are more effective than positional attacks in soccer (Tenga et al., 2010; Sarmento et al., 2018; a J Hum Kinet 2025–26 study of La Liga, EPL and Serie A over 5 seasons: counter-attack efficiency ≈16.0% vs ≈9.8% for positional attacks).
  - Top teams **regain the ball faster** after loss (Vogelbein, Nopp & Hökelmann, 2014).
  - Counter-press success is strongly predicted by the regaining team's speed about **2 seconds** after the change of possession (Bauer & Anzer, 2021).
- **But in Gaelic football, longer possessions (31–90 s) were associated with higher scoring rates** (Timmons, Collins & Mangan, 2026, sub-elite). In elite Gaelic football, "prolonged possessions with fewer attacking phases" returned more scores (IJPAS 2023). See §6 and §9.
- **Evidence:** RESEARCH EVIDENCE (M, match association).
- **Side effect of fixed training windows:** they may teach speed where patience is what the code rewards (**PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS**, supported by the Gaelic data).
- **Confidence:** M for soccer windows; M for the Gaelic counter-evidence.

### 2.5 Delayed defender entry / recovering defenders / temporary overload duration

- **Observed:** **no training study isolates these** (NUMBERS gate). Match data show counter-attacks against **imbalanced** defences are more effective (Tenga et al., 2010). Overloads that change every minute broadened exploration in adults (Canton et al., 2019).
- **Mechanism (theory):** the overload has a closing time window, so recognition is coupled to time.
- **Evidence:** ESTABLISHED PRACTICE; RESEARCH EVIDENCE only for match relevance.
- **Side effect:** unknown.
- **Confidence:** VL (training).

### 2.6 Restart speed / required delays

- **Observed:**
  - Gaelic football kickouts took **32.1 s on average**, with the ball out of play **38%** of match time in anticipation of kickouts (Ulster Senior Championship 2015, eight matches; GAA provincial sport-science report) (**ESTABLISHED PRACTICE / descriptive**).
  - In sub-elite Gaelic football, **1–90 s of ball out of play before a possession increased scoring odds** (Timmons et al., 2026).
  - The 2025 GAA rules introduced "solo and go" (immediate restart after a foul) and sanctions for deliberate kickout delay (**ESTABLISHED PRACTICE: rule**; no evaluation).
- **Mechanism:** a quick restart catches the defence unorganised. A delay lets both sides organise.
- **Confidence:** VL–L.

### 2.7 Work / rest intervals, bout duration, game duration

- **Observed:**
  - 3v3 bouts of 2, 4 and 6 minutes: no duration × bout interaction for HR; **no significant technical differences**, with a tendency towards fewer passes in the longest bout (Fanchini et al., 2011, *JSCR* 25(2):453–458).
  - Hurling: exercise-to-rest ratios changed physical and physiological performance (Malone, Hughes & Collins, 2019, *JSCR* 33(1):180–187; detail †).
- **Mechanism:** fatigue and pacing.
- **Evidence:** RESEARCH EVIDENCE (M, physical).
- **Side effect:** "shorter = more intense" holds only partly (§10).
- **Confidence:** M (physical); L (technical / tactical).

### 2.8 Temporal predictability vs uncertainty

- **Observed:** knowing or not knowing the duration **changes pacing**. Short *and undisclosed* durations produced more aggressive pacing (Ferraz et al., 2018, *PLoS ONE* 13(2):e0192399). Knowing the duration changed positioning and pacing (IJERPH, 2020, 17(11):3843). In rugby league, knowing the end-point changed pacing (index †).
- **Mechanism:** players regulate effort against an anticipated end.
- **Evidence:** RESEARCH EVIDENCE (L–M).
- **Side effect:** a predictable countdown may produce end-spurts and strategic waiting rather than reading the play (**PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS**).
- **Confidence:** L–M.

### 2.9 Natural time pressure from opponents (vs an artificial clock)

- **Observed:** tighter opponent pressure → less scanning (Jordet et al., 2020). The counter-press speed at 2 s predicts success (Bauer & Anzer, 2021).
- **Mechanism:** functional time shrinks through opponent approach, which is *specified by perceptual information* (defender distance and speed) rather than by a clock.
- **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS:** natural pressure keeps the **information–action coupling** that representative learning design requires (Pinder et al., 2011). A clock replaces it with non-game information.
- **Confidence:** L (the comparison between natural and clock pressure is untested).

### 3.2 What touch limits do and don't do

- **Do (acute):** ↑ passing frequency; ↓ receptions and carries; usually ↑ intensity in adults. Pass success **depends on the limit**: 1-touch clearly lower; 2-touch mixed across studies.
- **Don't (per evidence):** reliably change team spacing (Coutinho et al., 2022). **No study measured scanning, penetration, creativity or decision *quality* under touch limits.**
- **Contradictions:**
  - Physical load: adult increase (review) vs youth *decrease* in distance and accelerations (JSMPF, 2025).
  - Technical: 2-touch better (Birinci et al., 2026) vs worse (JSMPF, 2025).

### 3.3 Do touch limits manipulate time, or rules?

**Both, and mostly rules.**
- A touch limit does not specify seconds. A player may take two slow touches.
- A touch limit **removes action possibilities**: dribbling past a player, carrying into space, shielding, holding the ball to fix a defender, and (in Gaelic football) soloing or bouncing to create a scoring angle.
- The more-passes / fewer-receptions pattern (Rosten et al., 2025) is consistent with changed *action options*, not only time.

**Classification (PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS, evidence-consistent):** touch limits are primarily an **action-possibility (RULES)** manipulation, with an indirect temporal effect. They should be catalogued under **RULES** with a cross-reference to TIME.

### 3.4 Can touch limits force players to reject a correct game action?

**Logically yes. Empirically unmeasured.**
- If the best option is to carry into open space or to hold the ball and draw a defender, a 1- or 2-touch rule makes that option illegal.
- No study has coded "correct action rejected because of the rule". The closest evidence is structural: free play produces more receptions, and touch limits remove carries.
- **Status:** **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS (strong theoretical basis).** It is consistent with the SPACE-gate principle "avoid constraints that systematically punish genuinely good game decisions" (Lever 01, P8).
- **Gaelic relevance (HYPOTHESIS):** Gaelic football's carry (four steps, solo / bounce) and hurling's solo-running with the sliotar are core attacking actions. Soccer-style touch limits may remove *more* of the real game in Gaelic codes than in soccer.

## 4. Creating time vs removing time

### 4.1 A. Coach removes time

Countdowns, touch limits, earlier defender release, smaller spaces, underloads. **Acute effects:** faster or more hurried actions, more passes and, where measured, **lower decision quality** (Rochael & Praça, 2024; the novice decline in basketball time-pressure research). **Learning effects: UNKNOWN** (§8).

### 4.2 B. Player creates time

| Time-creating behaviour | Evidence | Label | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Scanning before reception** | Scanning is linked to more successful passing outcomes; elite EPL players scan less under tight pressure, and scanning has "a small but positive role" (Jordet et al., 2020, 9,574 possessions). Prior exploration has been linked to faster subsequent passes, more turns and more forward passes (as reviewed in McGuckian et al., 2018). Players who scanned were ≈3× more likely to complete a successful pass (Hintermann et al., 2025) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (association) | M |
| **Scanning is trainable (with retention)** | **"Heads Up Girls!"** (*Front Sports Act Living*, 2025): 36 U19 players, 5 weeks of scanning-focused sessions vs control. **Scans per situation ↑ and were maintained at retention**, but **only in elite, not grassroots, players**. Tested in 4v4 SSGs; **11v11 transfer untested.** Verbal instructions ↑ scans before reception by 27% with no loss of subsequent success (Hintermann et al., 2025, *Eur J Sport Sci*) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (**learning, with retention**; transfer untested) | L–M |
| **Anticipation / perceptual expertise** | Experts are faster and more accurate at cue pick-up (Mann et al., 2007) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE | H (expertise); trainability via on-field practice L |
| **Fast, frugal option generation** | First-generated options were usually best, and more inspection time did not improve choices (Johnson & Raab, 2003, handball video). Intuitive decision-makers were faster *and* better; experts were more intuitive (Raab & Laborde, 2011) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (lab / video) | M |
| **Body orientation / first action** | Exploration is linked to more turns with the ball (McGuckian et al., 2018; association). Free play produces more receptions (Rosten et al., 2025). No study isolates body orientation | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (indirect) + PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC | L |
| **Moving away from pressure / support movement / creating separation** | Interpersonal distance governs dyadic outcomes (Passos et al., 2008). Space generation by off-ball movement is quantifiable (Fernández & Bornn, 2018, conference). Decoy runners influence defenders (Sherwood et al., 2018) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (mechanism) + PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC (coaching use) | L–M |
| **Moving the ball quickly** | Counter-attacks vs imbalanced defences more effective. Top teams regain faster. Early penetration after regaining key in MLS counter-attacks (González-Rodenas et al., 2016) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (match) | M |
| **Fixing an opponent before releasing** | Mechanism consistent with dyad research. No direct study | PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC | VL |
| **Using width / depth** | Lever 01: larger spaces ↑ inter-player distances and passing success | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (acute) | M |

### 4.3 Verdict

- **Supported:** functional time can be *extended* by player behaviour. Scanning, anticipation and expertise in cue pick-up are associated with better on-ball outcomes. **Scanning can be trained and retained (in SSG tests)**, at least in elite youth players.
- **Heuristic:** body orientation, fixing a defender and support angles "create time". They are plausible and mechanism-consistent, but not directly tested.
- **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS (strong):** practice should sometimes aim to **increase the usable time players create**, rather than only demanding they act in less time. This follows from:
  - the scanning evidence, which gives the only learning-with-retention evidence in this lever;
  - the finding that removing time lowers decision quality acutely, and lowers it most for less-skilled players.

## 5. Temporal windows

| Window | Evidence | Label | Conf. |
|---|---|---|---|
| Counter-attack after regain (soccer) | Counter-attacks / fast attacks more successful than positional attacks. Sarmento et al. (2018) coded durations as 0–10 / 11–20 / 21–30 / 31+ s. J Hum Kinet (2025–26): counter-attack efficiency ≈16% vs ≈10% positional. MLS: early penetration and regains in advanced zones matter | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (match) | M |
| Counter-press after loss (soccer) | The regaining team's speed about 2 s after the possession change strongly predicts counter-press success; about 20–30 counter-press situations per team per match (Bauer & Anzer, 2021). Top teams regain faster (Vogelbein et al., 2014) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (match) | M |
| "Score within 6 s" / "win it back in 5–6 s" | Popular coaching rules; analytics blogs claim scoring probability "climbs sharply" below ≈6 s | **PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC** (not peer-reviewed thresholds) | VL (as a threshold) |
| **Gaelic football after turnover** | **Longer possessions (31–90 s) associated with higher scoring** (sub-elite). **Prolonged possessions with fewer attacking phases → greater score return** (elite). Possession duration is among the strongest predictors of outcome (elite) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (match) | M |
| Odd-man advantage before recovery | Natural imbalance → effective counter-attacks (Tenga et al., 2010). Duration of advantage **not studied** | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (partial) | L |
| Quick restarts | Gaelic: 1–90 s out of play associated with scoring odds (sub-elite). GAA "solo and go" (rule) | RESEARCH EVIDENCE (association) + ESTABLISHED PRACTICE | L |
| Basketball shot clock | Offences are more efficient early in the shot clock (industry analytics; selection effects likely). NCAA 35 → 30 s trial reported pace and efficiency rising (governing body) | PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC / governing-body report | L |

**Are fixed windows like "score within six seconds" supported?**
- **As universal thresholds: no.** Soccer match data support that *fast attacks against unorganised defences* are relatively effective, and that the *first seconds after a turnover* matter for counter-pressing. They do **not** establish any single universal threshold. Most published fixed windows are **coaching constraints**.
- **In Gaelic football, the association runs the other way for possession duration.** Sub-elite and elite analyses associate *longer* possessions with scoring. Possession duration is confounded with territory and shot selection, so this is **association, not causation**.
- **Windows vary by sport, level and situation.** Soccer counter-press ≈ seconds; Gaelic possession–score relationships ≈ tens of seconds. Basketball has formal 24/30 s clocks. **No universal threshold should be invented.**

## 6. SPACE × NUMBERS × TIME

### 6.1 Interaction propositions

| Proposition | Status | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Smaller space naturally reduces functional time | **SUPPORTED** (acute) | Lever 01: shorter interpersonal distances, more duels; tight pressure → less scanning |
| Larger space ↑ time but ↑ movement demand | **SUPPORTED** (acute) | Lever 01 meta-analyses: larger pitch ↑ distance and HSR; ↑ passing success |
| Overloads give the ball carrier more time | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** | Superiority ↓ load and ↑ passes; average-skill children made better pass decisions with superiority ("more time to make better decisions", Práxedes et al., 2018 — authors' interpretation) |
| Underloads reduce time | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** | Inferior teams under more pressure; high-inferiority conditions described as reducing available space and time (Coutinho et al., 2022) |
| Congestion reduces usable time despite unchanged clock time | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** | AFL: more bodies inside 50 ↓ scoring regardless of advantage (NUMBERS gate); mechanism inferred |
| Temporary overloads: duration matters | **UNKNOWN** (no study of overload duration) | — |
| Delayed defenders manipulate both NUMBERS and TIME | **Logically true; effects unstudied** | — |
| Countdown effects differ in tight vs large spaces | **UNKNOWN** (no study crosses clock × area) | — |
| Touch limits behave differently at different densities | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** | Jäger et al. (2026): touch × player-number interaction at constant relative pitch size (detail †) |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## 10. Common coaching assumptions tested

| Assumption | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **"Two-touch makes players think faster."** | **Not supported.** | Two-touch changes action options, ↑ passes and changes load. No study measured decision *speed* or thinking. Decision quality is unmeasured; pass success is mixed. |
| **"A countdown improves decision-making."** | **Contradicted acutely; learning unknown.** | Time pressure worsened tactical decision-making and execution in counter-attack SSGs (Rochael & Praça, 2024). Novices decline under time pressure. |
| **"Less time forces scanning."** | **Contradicted.** | Elite players scan *less* under tight pressure (Jordet et al., 2020). Scanning *before* reception creates time, and is trainable through scanning-focused practice, not time restriction. |
| **"Players need to play quicker."** | **Context-dependent; not universally supported.** | Fast counter-attacks vs unorganised defences are effective in soccer. In Gaelic football, longer possessions were associated with scoring. "Quicker" should mean quicker *perception and ball movement when the window exists*, not rushed actions. |
| **"Fast transitions should be trained with a 5 / 6 / 10-second scoring rule."** | **Practitioner heuristic; thresholds not evidence-based.** | Counter-press timing matters (≈2 s speed predicts success in soccer), but no study validates a fixed scoring window for training. Gaelic data contradict short windows as a general aim. |
| **"Limited touches stop players dwelling on the ball."** | **Partly true acutely, with a cost.** | Fewer receptions and carries, more passes. May forbid correct carries or holds (unmeasured). Pass success sometimes lower. |
| **"Time pressure makes training more match-like."** | **Not supported as a general claim.** | Clock pressure differs from natural opponent pressure in the information it provides (PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS). No representativeness study compares them. |
| **"Shorter games increase intensity."** | **Partly supported, weakly.** | Bout duration (2 / 4 / 6 min) showed no HR interaction and no significant technical differences (Fanchini et al., 2011). Short or undisclosed durations → more aggressive pacing (Ferraz et al., 2018). |
| **"Delayed defenders create realistic transition decisions."** | **Unknown** (no training study). | Mechanism plausible; match data support the value of imbalanced defences. |
| **"If players execute successfully under greater time pressure, they have learned to play faster."** | **Not supported.** | Acute performance ≠ learning; no retention or transfer evidence for time restriction. |

## 11. Contradictions and unintended effects

1. **Touch limits and physical load:** ↑ in adults (review) vs ↓ distance and accelerations in elite youth (JSMPF, 2025).
2. **Two-touch technical success:** better than free play (Birinci et al., 2026) vs worse (JSMPF, 2025).
3. **Time pressure and scanning:** coaches expect pressure to force scanning; match data show *less* scanning under pressure.
4. **Soccer "fast is better" vs Gaelic "longer possessions associated with scoring".** Both are association studies. The codes may reward different temporal patterns, or the confounds may differ.
5. **Scanning training worked for elite, not grassroots, players.** Capability may limit who benefits.
6. **2-touch ↑ executive-function performance acutely (Birinci et al., 2026)**, yet time pressure *lowers* decision quality (Rochael & Praça, 2024). Cognitive activation ≠ better game decisions.
7. **Basketball: offences are more efficient early in the shot clock** (industry), which is not evidence that forcing early shots improves efficiency (selection effect).
8. **Unintended effects (PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS):**
   - "Clock-watching" instead of opponent-reading.
   - End-spurt pacing.
   - Rejection of correct carries or holds under touch limits.
   - Speed without quality.
   - Novices excluded from meaningful decisions.
   - Transitions practised as races rather than reads.

## 13. Ten major unanswered questions

1. **Does practising with artificially reduced time** (countdowns, possession clocks, touch limits) **improve perception or decision-making under natural match pressure after the constraint is removed?** UNKNOWN.
2. **Natural vs clock pressure:** which produces better retention and transfer of decision quality?
3. **Do touch limits cause players to reject correct actions** (carries, holds, fixing a defender), and does this persist after the limit is removed?
4. **Creating time:** does scanning training transfer to full games (11v11 / 15v15), and why did grassroots players not benefit?
5. **What temporal windows actually exist in Gaelic football and hurling** after turnovers, kickouts and puckouts? Why is longer possession associated with scoring, and what are the confounds?
6. **Overload duration:** how long do natural overloads last in Gaelic games, and does training with time-limited overloads improve exploitation?
7. **Clock × space × numbers:** does a countdown behave differently in tight vs large spaces, or with +1 vs equal numbers?
8. **Age and skill scaling:** what level of temporal restriction lets novices still perceive and explore, and does it change what they learn?
9. **Touch limits in Gaelic codes:** what do soccer-style touch limits remove from Gaelic football and hurling (solo, bounce, hand-pass sequences, carries), and what does that do to decision-making?
10. **Low-cost indicators:** what can a volunteer coach observe to tell "rushed" from "quick", and "created time" from "given time", in real matches?

## 14. What PáircVision must NOT claim

1. ❌ "Less time makes players think / decide faster." There is no evidence, and time pressure lowered decision quality acutely.
2. ❌ "Two-touch makes players think faster" or "improves decision-making."
3. ❌ "Countdowns improve decision-making."
4. ❌ "Pressure forces scanning." Elite players scan *less* under tight pressure.
5. ❌ Any universal transition window ("score within 6 s", "win it back in 5 s") as evidence-based.
6. ❌ "Gaelic teams should attack faster." Match data associate longer possessions with scoring (association only; confounded).
7. ❌ "Players who succeed under a clock have learned to play faster."
8. ❌ "Time pressure makes training more match-like" as a general truth.
9. ❌ That touch limits are purely a time manipulation. They mainly change what actions are allowed.
10. ❌ Any Gaelic football or hurling evidence on touch, time or shot-clock *training* effects. None exists.
11. ❌ That the 2025 GAA restart rules have measured effects on play.
12. ❌ That scanning training "works for everyone". It improved elite but not grassroots U19 players in the one study.

## 15. Plain-language implications for a volunteer Gaelic coach

> **TIME: how long players have to see, decide and act.**
>
> - **The clock isn't the real clock.** How much time a player really has depends on how close the nearest opponent is, how much space there is, and whether a teammate is free. Shrink the pitch or add a defender and you've already cut their time.
> - **Rushing isn't the same as quick.** In research, putting a clock on attacks made decisions *worse*, not better, especially for less experienced players.
> - **Pressure doesn't make players look around.** Top players actually look around *less* when a defender is tight. Looking *before* the ball arrives is what buys time, and that can be practised: in one study it stuck weeks later, though only with the stronger group.
> - **Touch limits change the game, not just the speed.** "Two touches" means more passing, but it also bans soloing, carrying and holding the ball to draw a man. Those are often the right play in Gaelic football.
> - **"Score in six seconds" isn't a Gaelic rule of nature.** In Gaelic football research, *longer* possessions (half a minute to a minute and a half) went with more scores. Quick breaks after winning the ball in contact matter too. Speed matters when the chance is there, not always.
> - **What you see isn't what they've learned.** If players get the shot off inside your countdown, the game is working. To see whether they've *learned*, take the clock away and watch a normal game next week: do they spot when to go fast and when to keep the ball?

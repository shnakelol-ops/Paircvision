# PáircVision Phase 1 — Lever 06: STARTING POSITIONS — Scientific Audit

**Type:** Scientific evidence audit. Research only: no drills, sessions, UI, product features or production code.
**Frozen context (V1):** [SPACE](./phase1-lever-01-space-final-gate.md) · [NUMBERS](./phase1-lever-02-numbers-final-gate.md) · [TIME](./phase1-lever-03-time-final-gate.md) · [DIRECTION](./phase1-lever-04-direction-final-gate.md) · [SCORING](./phase1-lever-05-scoring-final-gate.md)
**Status:** v1 audit, September 2026. A practitioner audit and a Final Evidence & Falsification Gate are expected to follow.

> **Central question.** What changes when a coach manipulates where players begin a practice episode, and is that manipulation genuinely distinct from SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION?
>
> **Secondary question (kept separate).** Does repeatedly starting players in a particular picture help them recognise and solve that picture when it emerges naturally in competition?
>
> **The critical chain:** SUPPLIED POSITION → BEHAVIOUR FROM POSITION → RECOGNISED POSITION → SELF-CREATED POSITION → LEARNED → RETAINED → TRANSFERRED.
> - These are different claims.
> - **Did the player learn to solve the picture, or did the coach supply the picture?**
>
> **Standing rules:**
> - Practice behaviour ≠ learning.
> - There is far more evidence about what manipulations change *during* practice than about what players retain and transfer (five-lever finding, SCORING gate §25).

## 0. Method, access and evidence markers

- **Searches (September 2026)** covered:
  - initial interpersonal distance and co-positioning in attacker–defender dyads;
  - field location as a constraint;
  - representative learning design;
  - transition and advantage starts;
  - contextual interference and variable practice in sport;
  - scenario, pattern and phase-of-play training;
  - set-piece / restart practice;
  - body orientation;
  - Gaelic kickout positioning and press structures;
  - removal / retention / transfer after structured starts.
- **Access.**
  - Proxy refusal continued (Frontiers, PubMed / PMC, SAGE, T&F, ScienceDirect, university repositories).
  - **No FULL TEXT** was read.
  - **AO** = abstract only; **SIO** = search-index only; **UNR** = unresolved.
- **Labels:** SUPPORTED · PARTIALLY SUPPORTED · CONTRADICTED · UNKNOWN · PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC · PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS. Confidence H / M / L / VL.
- **Honest headline.**
  1. The **only experimental literature that explicitly manipulates starting configuration** is **ecological-dynamics dyad and small-group research** (1v1, 2v1). It is acute, and uses research tasks rather than training interventions.
  2. **No study** trains players from prescribed starts, removes the prescription, and tests whether players recognise or create the relationship themselves.
  3. **Generic variable-practice evidence** (contextual interference) is **contested** in sport and concerns motor skills, not tactical starting pictures.
  4. **No Gaelic study** manipulates starting positions in practice. Gaelic evidence is descriptive match analysis of kickouts and presses.

## 1. Defining the candidate lever

**Candidate:** *STARTING POSITIONS manipulates the initial spatial and relational configuration from which an episode of play begins.*

| Manipulation | Time-zero effect | Frozen lever it maps onto | Anything left over? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attacker / defender starting locations | Interpersonal distances; occupied space | SPACE (relational / occupied space) | The *arrangement* is transient |
| Distance between opponents | Pressure; usable time | TIME (usable time), SPACE | — |
| Goal-side vs wrong-side defender | Who can protect the target | DIRECTION × SPACE | **Defensive organisation state** (set / unset) |
| Players ahead / behind the ball | Local numbers able to affect play | NUMBERS (local vs global) | — |
| Wide / narrow starts | Width, spread | SPACE | — |
| High / low starts | Field position relative to target | DIRECTION × SPACE | — |
| Staggered / recovery starts (chaser) | Temporary local overload; recovery | NUMBERS (temporary) × TIME (entry timing, frozen TIME family) | — |
| Restart location | Field position | SPACE × DIRECTION | Competition-prescribed starts (§15) |
| Goalkeeper position | Target protection | DIRECTION (target form) × NUMBERS | — |
| **Ball location and possession state** | Who has the ball, where, how it arrives | Not captured by any frozen lever | **Yes: initial ball / possession state** |
| Numbers positioned locally | Local superiority | NUMBERS | — |
| Defender deliberately displaced | Unset defence | NUMBERS (temporary), TIME | **Organisation state** |
| Attacker between lines | Relational position | SPACE (relational) | — |
| Transition / turnover simulations | Which game *moment* is sampled | TIME (transition windows) × DIRECTION (polarity) | **Game-moment sampling** |
| Kickout / restart set-ups | Prescribed by the sport | SPACE × NUMBERS × DIRECTION | **Representative prescribed start** |
| **Body orientation at the start** | What can be seen and done first | Not a frozen lever | **Yes: orientation** |

**Finding.** Almost every *effect* of a starting configuration is expressed through SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME or DIRECTION at time zero. **What remains** is not an additional effect type. It is:
1. **which game moment is sampled** (restart, transition, set defence, broken play);
2. **initial ball / possession and orientation state**;
3. **who produced the configuration** (coach-supplied vs player-created).

## 2. Initial condition vs ongoing constraint

| | Example | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| **Initial condition** | Player starts wide; can go anywhere once play begins | Candidate STARTING POSITION |
| **Ongoing constraint** | Player must stay wide | SPACE (zones) / RULES (positional restriction) / future PLAYER ROLES |

**Test:** *A starting position specifies the initial condition but does not constrain subsequent action.*

- **Useful distinction: yes** (PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS, taxonomy).
- Evidence gives it indirect weight. Mandatory positional zones (an *ongoing* constraint) reduced teammate co-adaptation (Gonçalves et al., 2017; SPACE gate). No comparison exists with an initial-only placement.
- **Corollary:** a starting position's influence **decays** as play unfolds. How fast is **not measured** in any study we found.

## 4. What does the starting position directly change?

| Variable | Changed by start? | Better described as |
|---|---|---|
| Available passing lanes | Yes (at t0) | SPACE (relational) |
| Opponent distance / pressure | Yes | SPACE × TIME (usable time) |
| Support | Yes | NUMBERS (local) × SPACE |
| Local numerical superiority | Yes | NUMBERS (local vs global; frozen) |
| Width / depth | Yes | SPACE |
| Time available | Yes | TIME (usable ≠ clock; frozen) |
| Direction of movement / orientation | Yes | DIRECTION (target-relative) + orientation (§10) |
| Field position | Yes | SPACE × DIRECTION |
| Defensive organisation | Yes | **Not captured by a frozen lever as such** (organisation state) |
| Transition state | Yes | TIME (windows) × DIRECTION (polarity); **moment sampling** |

**Conclusion.** Starting position is **the mechanism by which the coach sets the initial values** of frozen-lever variables. Its **effects are manifestations of those levers**, with two partial exceptions:
- defensive organisation state;
- ball / orientation state.

## 5. STARTING POSITION × TIME

- A defender starting 2 m vs 10 m away changes the attacker's **usable time** (TIME gate T1).
- Dyad research shows interpersonal distance (with relative velocity) governs whether the attacker can destabilise the defender (Duarte et al., 2010; AO).

**Interpretation (PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS, not forced):**
- STARTING POSITION describes **how the coach creates** the condition (a placement).
- TIME describes **what the player experiences** (usable time).
- Staggered or delayed entries are already in the frozen TIME family ("entry and recovery timing").
- **A delayed defender is TIME. A displaced defender is a starting position producing a TIME effect.**

---

## 6. STARTING POSITION × NUMBERS

- 6v6 globally, 3v2 locally at the start.
- The NUMBERS gate froze that **local numerical relationships matter to the immediate decision** (P3, partially supported), and that **GIVEN ≠ RECOGNISED ≠ CREATED ≠ EXPLOITED** (N7, hypothesis).
- **A local overload produced by placement is a GIVEN advantage.**
  - The coach manipulated **position** in order to manipulate **local numbers**.
  - The effect belongs to NUMBERS. The *method* is placement.
- **Starting positions are a principal mechanism for creating temporary or local overloads without changing global numbers** (PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION).
- The overload **decays** as recovering players arrive. That decay is a TIME property.

---

## 7. STARTING POSITION × SPACE

- Same pitch, same numbers, attackers start very wide: **pitch dimensions are unchanged; occupied and relational space have changed.**
- The SPACE gate froze that useful space is **relational and dynamic** (P4) and that area per player alone cannot describe the game (P2).
- A wide start is a **time-zero value of relational / occupied space**.
- **Conceptually distinct addition: none**, except that the configuration is **supplied** rather than **created** (§23–24).

---

## 8. STARTING POSITION × DIRECTION

- "Attack begins facing goal with defenders recovering" vs "attack begins facing away under pressure": **the target structure is identical, so DIRECTION is unchanged.**
- What changed:
  - **orientation** relative to the target (§10);
  - **organisation state** (recovering vs set);
  - **usable time** (TIME).
- Dyad research supports **angular co-positioning relative to the target** as information that regulates 1v1 coordination (*Hum Mov Sci* 2015; 2017; AO).
- Orientation relative to the target is a **time-zero variable DIRECTION does not cover** (DIRECTION = target structure, frozen).

## 9. Ball starting position

| Start | What it sets |
|---|---|
| Goalkeeper starts with ball | Restart; possession secure; the defence can set |
| Defender / midfielder starts with ball | Build-up phase; pressure depends on placement |
| Loose ball | Contest; possession undetermined |
| Sideline restart / kickout | Competition-prescribed start (§15) |
| Turnover | Transition moment; polarity just flipped |
| Coach serves ball | Coach controls timing and location. **Service quality is itself a variable** |
| Ball behind / in front of a player | Orientation and receiving demand |

- **The initial state must include players *and* ball.** The ball's location and possession status determine which game moment is being sampled. No frozen lever covers this.
- **Classification:** part of STARTING POSITIONS (narrowed as INITIAL STATE; §36). PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS (taxonomy).

## 11. Transition training (priority)

**Common transition starts:**
- turnover;
- loose ball;
- defenders out of position;
- attackers ahead of the ball;
- numerical advantage;
- recovery runs;
- set vs unbalanced defence.

| Question | Evidence | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Transition moments matter in matches | Counter-attacks more effective vs imbalanced defences (Tenga et al., 2010); first action after a turnover associated with Gaelic possession success (Timmons et al., 2026) (frozen gates) | RESEARCH (association) |
| Transition starts produce transition behaviour in practice | Tautologically yes; acute | — |
| **Repeated transition starts improve recognition of natural transitions** | **No study** | **UNKNOWN** |
| Exposure vs recognition | A transition start **supplies** the transition (the coach declares it). In matches, players must **detect** it. The detection component is removed by the start | **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS** (logical) |

**Critical point.** A coach-declared turnover removes the most important perceptual event of transition: **noticing that possession changed and that the defence is unset**. The start gives more exposure to the *post-recognition* problem, not to recognition.

## 12. Representative learning design

- **Pinder, Davids, Renshaw & Araújo (2011)**, *J Sport Exerc Psychol* 33:146–155 (AO):
  - representative learning design;
  - **action fidelity** (responses in practice should correspond to those in performance);
  - **functionality** of practice information.
- The theory implies that **practice information should sample the performance environment**. A starting configuration is a sample of a game state.
- **Theoretical rationale (not empirical evidence):**
  - A carefully selected start can **increase useful repetitions** of a real game situation.
  - An unrealistic start can create a problem **that rarely occurs in that form**, lowering action fidelity.
- **Empirical test of start representativeness on learning: none found.**
- **Can an unrealistic start teach players to solve a rarely encountered problem?** **UNKNOWN.**
- **Can a selected start increase useful repetitions without destroying representativeness?** **Plausible (theory). Untested.**

## 13. Repetition without repetition

- Bernstein's phrase is widely used in ecological-dynamics coaching. **No study** compares a fixed starting picture vs a **family** of related starting pictures for tactical learning.
- **Nearest evidence: contextual interference (motor skills), which is contested:**
  - **Ammar et al. (2023)**, *Educational Research Review* 39:100537 (meta-analysis; AO): **no evidence** of CI benefits in sports practice at acquisition, retention or transfer.
  - **Czyż et al. (2024)**, *Scientific Reports* (meta-analysis; SIO): **high CI improved retention** in motor learning.
  - Two meta-analyses reach **conflicting conclusions**, with further methodological debate (*Educ Psychol Rev* 2024 and 2025; SIO).
- **Not applicable without qualification.** CI concerns scheduling of different motor skills, not variation of tactical starting states.

**Verdict.** "Variable starts are better than fixed starts" is **UNKNOWN**. Generic CI evidence does not settle it.

---

## 14. Rehearsal vs recognition

- **Fixed start:** defender here → attacker there → ball here, every repetition.
  - Players may learn to solve **that picture**. They may also anticipate, rehearse a set answer, and stop scanning.
- **Evidence:**
  - *Front Psychol* (2019): more rule manipulations reduced exploratory behaviour (restrictions, not starts; AO).
  - Pattern play and scenario literature is **practitioner-level** (SIO).
- **Direct evidence that fixed starts produce rehearsed rather than adaptive behaviour: none.**
- **Status: PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS** (theory-consistent: a predictable start reduces the information players must pick up).

## 15. Set plays / restarts

- **Competition-prescribed starts:**
  - Gaelic: kickouts, puckouts, throw-ins, frees, sidelines, 45s / 65s;
  - soccer: corners, free kicks;
  - rugby: lineouts, scrums;
  - basketball: inbounds;
  - hockey: face-offs.
- **Key distinction (PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS; representative-design inference):**
  - When the **sport itself** fixes the starting condition, rehearsing that configuration **samples a real starting state**. Fixed starts are **more representative** here than in open play.
  - In open play, configurations are **never handed over**. A fixed constructed start supplies what players must normally create or detect.
- **Evidence:**
  - Corner-kick analyses describe scripted routines contributing a meaningful share of goals (SIO; a 2025 comparative analysis; *Applied Sciences* systematic review 2025).
  - Practitioner recommendations to train set pieces with live opposition and transition (SIO).
  - **No learning study** compares restart rehearsal designs.
- **Gaelic:** kickout outcomes depend on strategy and press structure (LGFA; descriptive). Quick kickouts associated with better retention (sub-elite; AO).
  - **Current rules** (kickout beyond the 40 m arc; three players in each half) **change the kickout start**. Pre-2025 descriptions must be qualified (DIRECTION / SCORING gates; rule text still unverified).

## 17. Pressure starts

- Defender at touching distance / 2 m / 5 m / recovering / arriving on the pass.
- **Evidence:**
  - Dyad research (defender–ball distance 1–2.5 m; Duarte et al., 2010) links interpersonal distance and relative velocity to coordination and outcome (acute).
  - Passos et al. (2008): < 4 m + relative velocity in rugby.
- **What it manipulates:**
  - pressure and **usable time** (TIME);
  - action affordances (dribble vs pass vs shoot; futsal decision-making is linked to interpersonal distance, Pizarro et al., 2021, AO);
  - technical demand.
- **Classification:** the *method* is starting position; the *effect* is **TIME × SPACE**. The frozen TIME gate already covers "pressure is player-relative" (T7).

---

## 18. Chaser / recovery starts

**A staggered start creates:**
- a temporary local overload (NUMBERS);
- a temporal advantage (TIME: entry / recovery timing);
- directional pressure on the recovering defender (DIRECTION: goal-side recovery);
- recovery behaviour.

**Verdict.** STARTING POSITION here is **the method used to instantiate** NUMBERS × TIME × DIRECTION conditions. **No independent effect identified.**

---

## 19. Advantage / disadvantage starts

- Head starts, trailing defenders, wave games and transition overloads are primarily **ways of manufacturing temporary advantage**.
- **NUMBERS framework:** these create **GIVEN** superiority.
  - Whether players **RECOGNISE** or **CREATE** superiority when it is not given is the **untested** question (N6 / N7).
  - Advantage starts inherit NUMBERS' verdict: **exploitation of a given advantage ≠ recognition or creation**.

---

## 20. Starting positions and decision-making

- Initial configuration changes **which decisions** occur:
  - dribble vs pass vs shoot tied to interpersonal distance and angle;
  - drive direction tied to defender posture;
  - shot attempts in 2v1 starts.
  - All acute.
- **Decision quality, solution variability, scanning, anticipation, recognition:** not shown to improve.
- **Do not infer improved decision-making because decisions differ.**

## 21. Starting positions and technique

- Starts can alter technical demand: receiving under pressure, on the move, after turning, handpass in contact.
- Dyad studies manipulate starts to study **coordination**, not technique learning.
- **No study** deliberately manipulates starts to train technique with retention testing.
- **UNKNOWN** beyond acute demand.

## 23. Player agency

| Design | What players must decide before play |
|---|---|
| Coach places every player | Nothing positional |
| Players self-organise before a restart | Where to stand, relative to ball, teammates, opponents |
| Partially constrained start | Some positioning decisions |

- **Pre-positioning removes positioning decisions.** This is true by construction.
  - If the coach always places the support player correctly, **players start in support**. Whether they learn **to support** (to get there themselves) is untested.
- **Evidence:**
  - No study compares coach-placed vs self-organised starts.
  - Indirect: explicit positional zones reduced co-adaptation (SPACE gate).
  - Self-organisation is central to ecological theory (theory only).
- **Status:** the logical point is **SUPPORTED by construction**. Learning consequences are **UNKNOWN**.

## 24. The "coach solved it already" problem

**Example:** the coach wants width, so starts two players on the touchlines every repetition. Players appear wide.

- **The practice has shown that players can play *from* width.** It has not shown they can **create** width or know **when** to.
- **Direct analogues in frozen gates:**
  - NUMBERS: given overload ≠ recognised or created overload (N6).
  - SCORING: rewarded behaviour increase ≠ learning (SC-2).
  - DIRECTION: obeying a directional rule ≠ learning (DIR-4).
- **Classification:** **SUPPORTED as a statement of evidence status** (no study shows supplied positions become self-created). The design risk is **logical**.

**The constructive counterpart (PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS).** Starting positions can also **withhold** a solution:
- start with **no support**, **no width**, or a defender goal-side;
- so players repeatedly face the problem of **creating** it.

That use supplies the **problem**, not the **answer**.

## 27. Age and expertise

## 30. Attack these assumptions

| Assumption | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Starting positions are an independent lever | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED as a design dimension; effects not independent** | Effects expressed through frozen levers; residual content: moment, orientation, organisation, agency, predictability |

## 32. Must-not-claim list

1. ❌ "Starting wide teaches width."
2. ❌ "Starting in support teaches support."
3. ❌ "Transition starts teach transition recognition."
4. ❌ "Fixed scenarios improve tactical understanding."
5. ❌ "Variable starts improve adaptability" (or "fixed starts cause rehearsed behaviour").
6. ❌ "Representative starts improve match transfer."
7. ❌ "Repeated starting pictures become automatic match behaviour."
8. ❌ "A coach-placed overload teaches players to find overloads."
9. ❌ "Removing the start shows what players have learned" (same session).
10. ❌ "Young players need prescribed positions" / "elite players need fewer."
11. ❌ "Contextual-interference research shows variable starts are better." Contested and not tactical.
12. ❌ "Kickout research shows how to set up kickout practice." It is descriptive and mostly pre-2025.
13. ❌ Any claim that a starting configuration's effects persist for a known duration.

## 33. Ten unanswered questions

1. Do players who repeatedly start in a supplied picture (width, support, overload) create that picture themselves in free play later?
2. Does starting with a solution **withheld** (no support, no width) produce more self-created solutions than starting with it supplied?
3. Do coach-declared transitions train the **recognition** of natural transitions, or only the response after recognition?
4. Are fixed or varied starting pictures better for later recognition of the same problem in matches?
5. How quickly does a starting configuration's influence decay: seconds, or the whole episode?
6. Does letting players self-organise before a restart improve their positioning in matches compared with coach placement?
7. Do rehearsed competition restarts (kickouts, puckouts) transfer better than constructed open-play starts, because they are real starting states?
8. Does starting orientation (facing vs back to goal) change what players learn about turning and scanning?
9. Do children and novices benefit from clearer starting pictures, or lose chances to learn positioning?
10. Under current Gaelic rules, which starting states (kickout, turnover, broken ball) occur most, and do practice starts reflect that frequency?

## 34. Volunteer coach translation (U12 Gaelic)

> **Putting players in the right place isn't the same as teaching them to find it.** If you start two players out wide every time, you'll see width. But you put it there. The real question is whether they'd go wide by themselves when it's needed.
>
> **Starting positions are still useful.** They let you create the same problem again and again. Want practice at breaking out when there's no one to hand-pass to? Start the ball carrier with no support. Now they have to work it out. That's using the start to give them the **problem**, not the **answer**.
>
> **Be careful with "pretend turnovers".** If you shout "turnover!" and set up the break, you've done the hardest bit for them: noticing the turnover. In a match, nobody shouts.
>
> **Real restarts are different.** A kickout always starts from the same kind of picture, so practising it from that picture is realistic. Open play never starts neatly, so a neat start in open play gives them something they won't get on a Sunday.
>
> **Check without the setup.** Every so often, let them start however they like, or just play. Do they find the width, the support, the extra player themselves? Look again next week too.

## 36. Survival verdict

### Does STARTING POSITIONS deserve to remain an independent PáircVision lever?

**YES BUT NARROWED** (provisional; the final gate may absorb it).

**Why not YES.**
- Almost all *effects* of starting positions (pressure, usable time, local overloads, relational space, width, proximity to target) are **time-zero values of SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION**. They belong to those frozen levers.
- Chaser, delayed-defender and advantage starts are **methods** of creating NUMBERS × TIME conditions.

**Why not NO.** The interaction matrix's last column repeatedly identifies content **no frozen lever covers**:
1. **Game-moment / possession state**: restart, turnover, loose ball, set vs unset defence.
2. **Initial orientation**: body relative to target and opponents.
3. **Supplied vs withheld vs player-created configuration** (agency).
4. **Predictability / variability** of the initial state.
5. **Competition-prescribed vs constructed starts.**

**Narrowed candidate (renamed provisionally INITIAL STATE):**

> *The game state from which an episode begins (ball and possession status, the organisation of both teams, players' locations and orientation) and whether that state is supplied by the coach, withheld, or created by the players. Its effects on space, numbers, time and direction are recorded under those levers.*

**If the final gate absorbs it instead, components go to:**

| Component | Destination |
|---|---|
| Distances, width, relational space | SPACE |
| Local overloads, advantage starts | NUMBERS |
| Pressure, delayed / recovering entries, transition windows | TIME |
| Proximity to target, polarity after turnover | DIRECTION |
| Orientation; score / state scenarios | INFORMATION (candidate) |
| Fixed player placements that persist | PLAYER ROLES / RULES |
| Supplied vs created configuration | Cross-lever principle (alongside NUMBERS N6, SCORING SC-2, DIRECTION DIR-4) |

**Central answer.** A coach placing players can **guarantee** that players meet a problem. It does not show that they learned to recognise or create the picture. **Whether starting-position practice transfers: UNKNOWN.**

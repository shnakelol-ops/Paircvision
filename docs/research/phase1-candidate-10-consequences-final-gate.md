## Executive verdict

**C — CONSEQUENCES dissolves. EVENT-CONTINGENT survives as internal shorthand, and GAME MOMENT receives a minimal clarification.**

- **No common coaching manipulation** of what happens after an event escapes GAME MOMENT, SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION, SCORING or ACTIONS (§1).
- **EVENT-CONTINGENT** is **internal notation**: an existing manipulation that begins, ends or changes when a defined event occurs. Not a lever, not a principle on its own, not coach-facing (§2).
- **GAME MOMENT** is **clarified, not expanded**: the moments players meet include those that **follow** an event within an episode, whether through continued play, a reset, a coach feed or a manufactured change (§3).
- **No CONTINUITY lever and no separate continue / reset check** (§4).
- **Sanctions, external rewards and competition formats** sit **outside Phase 1**; any practice effects they cause are classified by existing properties (§17–19).
- **CF-1 v2, DECISION ALLOCATION and GI-2: unchanged.**
- **Frozen principles: CO-1, CO-2.**
- **No candidates from the original ten remain pending.**

## 1. Final kill test

**Question:** is there a common coaching manipulation where what happens after an event materially changes the practice but cannot be described by the existing properties?

**Re-check of the scientific audit's 12 pairs and the practitioner cases:**

| Case | Existing description |
|---|---|
| Play continues vs whistle and reset on turnover | GAME MOMENT (which moments are met) + DIRECTION (polarity on turnover) |
| Regaining team +1 for 10 s | NUMBERS, event-contingent (+ TIME window) |
| Scoring team keeps the ball | GAME MOMENT (next start) |
| Shooter out for 30 s after a miss | NUMBERS × TIME, event-contingent |
| 6 s to shoot after regain | TIME, event-contingent |
| First pass forward after regain | ACTIONS, event-contingent |
| Failed press → opponent +2 | SCORING |
| Direction reverses after a score | DIRECTION, event-contingent |
| Coach whistles to start counterattack | GAME MOMENT + coach call (COACH INTERVENTION, deferred); GI-2 |
| Push-ups after a mistake | Outside Phase 1 |
| Pitch shrinks after a score | SPACE, event-contingent |
| Waves (attack ends → next attack) | GAME MOMENT (repeated starts) |
| Second ball dropped mid-play (rugby) | GAME MOMENT (inserted moment); GI-2 for its cue |
| "Double-crossing" player who can claim possession | GAME MOMENT (manufactured turnover) via a special permission (ACTIONS) |
| Winner stays on | Outside Phase 1 (competition format); effects on who plays → NUMBERS / participation |

**Strongest counterexample:** *entering a 5v4 because possession was just won* vs *starting in a 5v4*. Same numbers and similar space, yet players carry the disorganisation of the moment before and must notice the change.
- **Resolution:** the difference is **how the moment arises** (emergent vs supplied), already recorded in the GAME MOMENT gate (§7–8), plus **GI-2** (is what tells players it's on still there?). The disorganised bodies and distances are SPACE / TIME at the moment of change. **Nothing unexplained remains** (§5).

**Result: CONSEQUENCES fails permanently as a Phase 1 property.**

## 2. EVENT-CONTINGENT

**Definition (frozen as internal notation):**

> **EVENT-CONTINGENT: an existing practice manipulation that begins, ends or changes when a defined event occurs.**

| Example | Classification |
|---|---|
| Regain → six-second attack | TIME, event-contingent |
| Turnover → extra attacker enters | NUMBERS, event-contingent |
| Score → targets reverse | DIRECTION, event-contingent |
| Turnover → first pass must be forward | ACTIONS, event-contingent |
| Failed press → opponent's next score worth double | SCORING, event-contingent |
| Score → pitch shrinks | SPACE, event-contingent |
| Score → scoring team restarts with the ball | GAME MOMENT (next start) |

| Candidate status | Decision |
|---|---|
| Useful internal modifier | **Yes** |
| Unnecessary terminology | No: it prevents "CONSEQUENCES" re-emerging and records **when** a lever applies |
| Framework principle | **Only inside CO-1**; not a principle on its own |
| Coach-facing concept | **No** |

**Not a seventh CHANGE THE PROBLEM lever.** Any of the six properties may be event-contingent.

## 3. GAME MOMENT clarification

**Frozen definition (unchanged):** *GAME MOMENT is the coach's choice of which situation of the game a practice episode begins from, such as a turnover, kickout, loose ball, or settled or unsettled defence, and how often each situation is practised.*

**Minimal clarification (added):**

> **The situations players meet include those that follow an event within an episode. Whether a follow-up moment arises through continued play, a reset, a coach feed or a deliberately manufactured change is part of the coach's GAME MOMENT choice.**

**Guardrails:**
- GAME MOMENT still **chooses the problem**; it is **not** a CHANGE THE PROBLEM lever.
- It is **not** a sequencing engine or state machine. It names **which moments are met and how they arise**, nothing more.
- "How the moment arises" (supplied vs emergent) was already in the GAME MOMENT gate (§7–8); the clarification extends it to **follow-up** moments.

A **Later taxonomy clarification (Candidate 10)** note is added to the GAME MOMENT gate (§29).

## 4. Continue vs reset

**Final decision:** no CONTINUITY lever; no separate internal continue / reset check. **GAME MOMENT** decides whether follow-up moments are met; **GI-2** decides whether players must recognise the moment themselves.

| Case | What it means | Explained by |
|---|---|---|
| Natural turnover → continue | Transition met; recognition required | GAME MOMENT (follow-up met, emergent); GI-2 satisfied |
| Natural turnover → stop / reset | Transition not met | GAME MOMENT (follow-up not sampled) |
| Coach calls turnover | Transition met; recognition supplied | GAME MOMENT (supplied); GI-2; call → COACH INTERVENTION |
| Coach feeds a second ball | A new moment inserted | GAME MOMENT (manufactured); GI-2 for its cue |
| Start directly in counterattack | Transition-like picture without the preceding event | GAME MOMENT (selected start) + NUMBERS / SPACE |
| Freeze / replay the same picture | Picture repeated; continuity broken on purpose | GAME MOMENT (repetition) + coach intervention (deferred) |

**Nothing unexplained remains.**

## 5. Sequence

**Start in 5v4 counterattack** vs **enter 5v4 because possession was just won**:

| Difference | Explained by |
|---|---|
| Moment arises from play vs is chosen | GAME MOMENT (supplied vs emergent; now including follow-up moments) |
| Must notice the change | GI-2 |
| Both teams disorganised in bodies, distances, orientation | SPACE / TIME at the moment of change; GAME MOMENT configuration |
| Players' decisions in the moment before affect the moment after | The same episode continuing: GAME MOMENT (follow-up met) |

**Decision:** GAME MOMENT + GI-2 **already explain this**, with the one-sentence clarification in §3. **No new concept.**

## 6. Recognition vs execution

- **Already present** as the GAME MOMENT gate's transition test (§8, PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS) and as **GI-2** (Candidate 09).
- **Coach calls TURNOVER:** recognition of WHEN is supplied; decisions about what to do next remain.
- **Natural turnover:** recognition is required, but its presence **does not guarantee** players learn it.
- **Decision: no new principle.** The distinction is carried by **GI-2** and **DECISION ALLOCATION**. Recognising a transition and solving what follows it are separable objectives; that is an application, not a new rule.

## 7. Natural continuation

| Statement | Status |
|---|---|
| Allowing the game to continue lets players experience what follows an action or decision | **SUPPORTED** (descriptive; true by construction) |
| Experiencing the consequence teaches the lesson | **UNKNOWN** (no study; practitioner belief only) |

**"Let the game teach them" is not frozen** as a scientific principle. The distinction is written into **CO-2**.

## 8. Stop / reset legitimacy

Stopping or resetting can legitimately:
- increase repetitions;
- isolate a problem;
- permit technical correction;
- recreate a tactical picture;
- coordinate players;
- manage safety or time;
- focus on execution rather than recognition.

**Continuous practice is not ranked above reset practice.** Which fits depends on the objective (CO-2). **MULTIPLE-PRACTITIONER SUPPORT** for both; **no learning comparison exists**.

## 9. Coach-created transitions

| Device | Classification | When it fits |
|---|---|---|
| Whistle turnover | GAME MOMENT (supplied); call → COACH INTERVENTION | Execution after transition; organisation; volume |
| Second ball (fed / dropped) | GAME MOMENT (manufactured / inserted) | Volume; sudden change with controlled frequency |
| Wave | GAME MOMENT (repeated starts) | Attacking a transitioning defence; volume |
| Possession switch rule | GAME MOMENT / DIRECTION | Control of who attacks next |
| Timer switch | GAME MOMENT + TIME | Even exposure; pacing |
| Coach feed | GAME MOMENT | Volume; picture repetition |

**No good / bad hierarchy.** If recognising the transition is **not** the objective, these can be entirely appropriate. If it **is**, apply **GI-2**: success after a supplied signal is not evidence of recognition.

## 10. Transition

- **Preserved:** transitions are important in Gaelic football. About **half of elite possessions** originate from turnovers (*IJPAS* 2023; AO / SIO); rugby practice design has cited ~56 % of possessions from transition sources (McKay & O'Connor 2018; AO).
- **Evidence does not establish the best way to train transition.** No study compares transition-training formats on learning, retention or transfer.
- **Do not infer:** transition importance → transition-game effectiveness.
- **Not reproduced:** "60 %+ of scores come from turnovers" (unverified; must-not-claim).

## 11–16. Existing-lever boundaries (confirmed)

| # | Case | Home | Confirmed |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | Turnover score worth 2 | **SCORING** (value changes) | ✓ |
| 11 | Turnover creates 6v5 | **NUMBERS**, event-contingent | ✓ |
| 12 | Score → scoring team receives next possession | **GAME MOMENT** (next start; score value unchanged) | ✓ |
| 13 | After regain, first pass must go forward | **ACTIONS**, event-contingent | ✓ |
| 14 | After regain, six seconds to shoot | **TIME**, event-contingent | ✓ |
| 15 | After turnover, another attacker enters | **NUMBERS**, event-contingent | ✓ |
| 16 | After score / turnover, attack the opposite target | **DIRECTION**, event-contingent | ✓ |

**SCORING boundary (frozen):**
> **Changing what an outcome is worth is SCORING. Changing another property after the outcome is that property, event-contingently.**

**SCORING gate edge cases resolved:**
- "Next goal wins" / "a draw is enough" (win conditions): **SCORING** (value of future outcomes); if the episode then ends, that is GAME MOMENT.
- "Bonus that also returns possession": **SCORING + GAME MOMENT** (bundled; effects confounded).
- "Regain = immediate 5v3": **NUMBERS**, event-contingent (+ GAME MOMENT if it starts a new episode).
- "Turnover = opposition starts near your goal": **GAME MOMENT + SPACE**.

## 17. Punishment

- **Decision:** running, push-ups, forfeits and similar sanctions after mistakes are **outside Phase 1** (discipline / motivation / coaching environment). They do not change the game problem.
- **Evidence limitation preserved:**
  - **No evidence found** of a learning benefit.
  - Youth harm evidence identified is **qualitative** (punishments associated with hindered developmental needs; small samples).
  - **No stronger causal claim** is made.
- **Distinguish:** in-game costs (lose a player, lose points) change NUMBERS or SCORING and **are** practice design.

---

## 18. External rewards

- **Decision:** prizes, tokens, "winners skip the run" and similar are **outside Phase 1** (motivation / competition environment).
- **SCORING** remains the home for changes to the **value structure inside** the practice.
- Motivation research (e.g., expected tangible rewards and intrinsic motivation; contested) is **not reopened**.

## 19. Winner-stays-on

**Composite, so classified in parts:**

| Aspect | Home |
|---|---|
| The competition format itself (stakes, staying on, being knocked off) | **Outside Phase 1** (competition / motivation environment; SCORING gate's separate "competing and stakes" property, still research debt) |
| Who plays and for how long | **NUMBERS / participation** (practice effect) |
| What the next episode is | **GAME MOMENT** |
| Fatigue and exposure imbalance | Load / participation consequences, recorded as side effects |

**Decision:** the format is outside Phase 1; any practice manipulation it produces is classified by existing properties. **No single home forced.** Competitiveness and motivation claims remain **UNSUPPORTED** for practice (must-not-claim).

## 20. Error-learning literature

**Not imported into Gaelic tactical recommendations:**
- errorless learning (golf putting robustness; meta-analysis shows no overall advantage vs errorful learning);
- error amplification / augmentation (laboratory reaching tasks);
- error management training (workplace / software learning).

**Transfer to invasion-game tactical learning: unestablished.** Feedback-based uses belong to deferred COACH INTERVENTION.

## 22. CF-1 v2 (unchanged)

Behaviour after a supplied condition does not establish that players could make the decision that condition supplied; it remains evidence about decisions still left to them. The automatic 3v2 and the called turnover are covered ("an overload"; "a called moment"). **No amendment.**

## 26. Coach-facing simplicity

**Not added as coach-facing terms:** CONSEQUENCES, CONTINUITY, NEXT STATE, EVENT-CONTINGENT.

**Candidate guidance wording (not frozen, not universal):**
> *If what happens next is part of what you're working on, let players experience it. If it isn't, reset and work on what matters.*

It sits under GAME MOMENT (which moments players meet) and the frozen question. **Deep framework. Simple coaching.**

## 27. Final taxonomy verdict

## 28. Final Phase 1 architecture

```
CHOOSE THE PROBLEM
  GAME MOMENT — which situations players meet and how often, including follow-up
    moments within an episode and how they arise (continued play, reset, feed,
    manufactured change)

CHANGE THE PROBLEM
  SPACE · NUMBERS · TIME · DIRECTION / TARGET STRUCTURE · SCORING · ACTIONS
  (any of these may be event-contingent — internal notation only)

CROSS-FRAMEWORK
  DECISION ALLOCATION (lens)
  CF-1 v2 (supplied decisions: inference limit)

DEFERRED
  COACH INTERVENTION (instruction, calls, cues, questioning, demonstration, feedback)

OUTSIDE PHASE 1
  sanctions / punishment · external rewards · competition formats and stakes

  The actual Gaelic game / rules being prepared for
```

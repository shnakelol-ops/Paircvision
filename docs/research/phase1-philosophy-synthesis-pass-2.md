## 0. Headline answer to "did we discover anything new?"

**Scientifically, almost nothing.** Nearly every load-bearing idea in Phase 1 has a clear precedent:
- practice performance ≠ learning (decades of motor-learning and learning science);
- manipulating task features to change the problem (constraints literature; coaching acronyms such as CHANGE IT and STEP);
- exaggerating a problem through modified games (TGfU's modification-exaggeration, 1984);
- moments of the game (Tactical Periodization's four moments);
- who makes which decisions (Mosston's Spectrum);
- keeping the information a decision depends on (Representative Learning Design and the perception-action literature).

**What may be distinctive** is not a new theory but **how those ideas are organised**: explicitly **conditional** (every method is legitimate for some objective), **theory-agnostic** (it borrows design ideas without committing to a learning theory), equipped with **inference limits** (CF-1 v2, GI-2) that are unusually explicit about what practice behaviour can and cannot show, and **applied to Gaelic games** with evidence labelling. That is a coaching / product framework result, not a scientific one. **Pass 1's verdict C stands** (§30).

## 6. Representative Learning Design and GI-2

**GI-2:** *If recognising a moment is part of what practice is for, practice must contain what tells players it is on.*

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is GI-2 an application of RLD? | **Largely, yes.** RLD asks practice to sample performance information–action couplings; GI-2 asks the same for the specific information a targeted recognition depends on |
| Narrower? | **Yes.** GI-2 applies **only** when recognition is the objective and explicitly **permits** removing that information otherwise. RLD is a general design ideal |
| Conceptually different? | **In stance, slightly:** RLD rests on ED theory; GI-2 is stated as an **inference limit** (what success can show) plus a design check, with no theoretical commitment |
| Independently rediscovered? | **Yes, in the sense that Phase 1 arrived at it from its own evidence** (live bowler vs ball machine; opponent vs light; called vs natural moments). **Not novel:** the underlying evidence is largely from RLD researchers (Pinder et al.) |

**Classification:** **ESTABLISHED BUT REFRAMED** (narrower, conditional, theory-agnostic operational rule).

## 7. Direct / explicit instruction

- **Compatibility:** Phase 1 accepts that coaches may legitimately supply decisions, positions, patterns, tactical responses, technique and coordination (CF-1 v2 clause; DA-3; AC-3; GI-2; CO-2). **PáircVision is compatible with explicit instruction.** Explicit-instruction advocates also accept that guidance should fade with expertise (expertise reversal), and include questioning (Rosenshine; Lemov). Neither is hostile to the other.
- **Where Phase 1 limits inference after instruction:**
  - Successful execution after being told **shows execution under those conditions**, not that players would **choose** that action unprompted (CF-1 v2; AC-2).
  - If instruction or a call supplies the **cue** for a moment, success is not evidence of **recognising** it (GI-2).
  - Performance during instruction is not learning (Soderstrom & Bjork; SPACE P9 etc.).
- **The distinction Phase 1 contributes:** **legitimacy of instruction** (often high) vs **evidence of independent decision-making** (limited to decisions left open). This is compatible with explicit-instruction theory, which also separates guided performance from independent practice.

## 11. GAME MOMENT

- **Precedents:** TP's four moments (organisation / transitions); "phases of play" in coaching; scenario practice (high-performance Gaelic coaches; McKay's unstructured-possession scenarios); RLD's sampling of performance situations.
- **What Phase 1 adds:** GAME MOMENT is framed as a **selection and sampling** decision (which moments, how often, how they arise, including follow-up moments), separated from the properties that change the problem, with GM-3's statement that **no evidence says how often** to practise each.
- **Classification:** **ESTABLISHED BUT REFRAMED / SYNTHESIS OF EXISTING IDEAS.**

## 12. Decision Allocation and the coach question

| | Mosston's Spectrum | TGfU | PáircVision DA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Unit | Decisions in the **teaching episode** (pre-impact, impact, post-impact: planning, execution, evaluation) | **What to do / how to do** in the game | **WHO / WHAT / WHEN / HOW** of **in-game** tactical and technical decisions |
| Question | Who makes which decisions, when? | Does the player understand what to do and how? | What am I telling them, and what am I leaving them to solve? |
| Use | Defines teaching styles | Defines teaching goals | Reads **any** manipulation or intervention for what it supplies; pairs with inference limits |

**Verdict:**
- The **axiom** (teaching is decisions; who makes which) is **Mosston's**. PáircVision **rediscovered** it (Phase 1 cited Mosston and TGfU as foundations).
- The **application** to in-game decisions supplied by **practice design itself** (a set-up, an overload, a whistle, a rule), not only by teacher talk, is a **possibly distinctive application**.
- The coach question is a **plain-language simplification**.
- **Classification:** DA = **SYNTHESIS OF EXISTING IDEAS / POSSIBLY DISTINCTIVE APPLICATION**; coach question = **ESTABLISHED BUT REFRAMED**.

## 13. CF-1 v2

**CF-1 v2:** behaviour from a supplied condition isn't evidence players can make the decisions made for them; evidence about decisions left to them remains meaningful.

| Candidate precedent | Relationship |
|---|---|
| Learning vs performance (Soderstrom & Bjork 2015; motor learning) | **Close parent**: performance under support is an unreliable index of learning. CF-1 v2 is a **specific case**: the support supplies a decision |
| Guidance hypothesis | **Loosely related** (dependence on supplied information); evidence weak (C09). Not a precedent for the inference claim |
| Explicit instruction: guided vs independent practice | **Related**: explicit-instruction models distinguish guided performance from independent performance |
| Mosston | **Related**: if the teacher makes a decision, the learner does not practise it |
| Assessment validity (a test must require the construct it claims to measure) | **Structurally the same logic**; not searched here (general measurement principle) |
| Contextual interference; scaffolding | Not true precedents; do not stretch |

**Classification:** **SYNTHESIS OF EXISTING IDEAS**, stated as a **decision-specific inference limit**, which may be a **POSSIBLY DISTINCTIVE APPLICATION** to practice design. Not novel science.

## 14. GI-2

- **Strongest precedent:** RLD / representative design (Pinder et al. 2011; Brunswik) and the perception–action evidence (live vs machine; opponent vs light).
- **GI-2 is:** an **existing principle in simpler language**, **narrowed** into a **conditional operational rule** with an explicit **permission** to remove information when recognition isn't the aim.
- **Classification:** **ESTABLISHED BUT REFRAMED.** The INFORMATION CHECK (§19) is the operational form.

---

## 15. Practice behaviour ≠ learning

- **Provenance:** decades old in motor learning (retention and transfer tests; learning without performance change, and vice versa) and learning science (Soderstrom & Bjork 2015). Also central to explicit-instruction and ED / CLA writers alike.
- **Status:** **ESTABLISHED SCIENCE**, not PáircVision-specific. Phase 1 **rediscovered and applied it lever by lever** (SPACE P9, N6, T8, DIR-4, SC-2, AC-2).
- **What is PáircVision's:** the **consistency** of applying it to every manipulation, and the must-not-claim discipline that follows.

---

## 16. "The method follows what you're trying to develop"

- **Precedents:** pedagogical pluralism / matching method to objective is widespread coaching and teaching wisdom (e.g., the "adaptable coach" literature comparing traditional and CLA practice, Lindsay & Spittle 2024; high-performance Gaelic coaches' "judicious deviation" from representative play). Instructional-alignment ideas in education point the same way (**not researched in this pass**; provenance detail UNKNOWN).
- **Can it become central despite not being novel?** **Yes.** Centrality depends on usefulness and honesty, not originality. It is also the statement that best describes Phase 1's structure (every principle conditional on objective).
- **Caution:** the statement presupposes a clear objective, which Phase 1 cannot supply (Pass 1 §30). Its usefulness depends on Game Understanding work.

## 20. What we merely rediscovered

PáircVision independently rediscovered several well-established coaching-design and learning principles:
1. Practice performance is not learning (learning science; motor learning).
2. Changing task features changes behaviour (constraints literature; coaching acronyms).
3. Modified games can exaggerate a tactical problem (TGfU 1984).
4. Practice can be organised around moments of the game (TP).
5. Teaching can be analysed by who makes which decisions (Mosston).
6. Practice should keep the information decisions depend on (RLD).
7. Guidance and support may affect what can be inferred and possibly what is learned (explicit instruction; guidance literature).
8. Game-based coaching is widely misunderstood as hands-off (Kinnerk's and Moran's research; CLA proponents).

**That is a successful result.** Arriving independently at the same places from Gaelic-focused evidence suggests Phase 1's reasoning was sound.

## 21. What may actually be distinctive (framework / product, not science)

1. **Conditionality throughout:** every method is legitimate for some objective, with the conditions stated. Most approaches carry a default preference; Phase 1 deliberately does not.
2. **Explicit inference limits** (CF-1 v2, GI-2) applied to the **design** itself, separating "legitimate to supply" from "evidence of independent decision".
3. **Classification architecture:** GAME MOMENT (selection) vs six properties (problem) vs cross-framework reading vs deferred intervention, with precedence rules and kill tests.
4. **Evidence labelling** carried into every claim, including must-not-claim lists aimed at popular misreadings.
5. **Theory agnosticism:** usable by coaches on either side of the ED vs cognitive debate.
6. **Gaelic application:** Gaelic rule context, Gaelic evidence marked separately, borrowed findings labelled.
7. **Simplicity layer:** one coach-facing question over a deep internal structure.

**Distinctive coaching / product framework: plausibly yes. Novel scientific theory: no.**

## 27. Genuine differences

**Different because PáircVision explicitly takes another position:**
1. **No default preference** for games, player freedom or questioning (vs Kinnerk, Game Sense, TGfU, GAA).
2. **Supplied answers are explicitly legitimate** where the supplied decision isn't the objective (vs strong game-first readings; compatible with explicit instruction).
3. **Representativeness is conditional** (GI-2) rather than a general ideal (vs RLD / CLA).
4. **No learning mechanism adopted** (vs ED / CLA, Complex Learning Theory, cognitive load theory).
5. **Refuses to infer learning from practice behaviour**, lever by lever (shared in principle with learning science, but applied more explicitly than in the practice-design material reached).

**Different because PáircVision has not yet taken a position:**
1. Purpose of coaching / what is worth developing.
2. Questioning vs telling; feedback; demonstration; freezing.
3. Progression; age and development.
4. Game model / principles of play.
5. Season planning.

**This distinction is crucial:** most apparent "differences" from established approaches are **gaps**, not positions.

## Final verdict

### **C — an evidence-informed practice-design and decision framework, compatible with multiple coaching approaches.**

**Why C:**
- It is **not A**: nothing in it is a distinct philosophy, and nothing is scientifically novel.
- It is **not B yet**: it draws on multiple traditions, but it has not taken the positions (purpose, content, pedagogy) that would make it a philosophy built from them.
- It is **not D**: it is not one existing methodology translated into Gaelic coaching. Its closest relatives differ by dimension (CLA and TGfU for design, Mosston for decisions, RLD for information, TP for moments, explicit instruction for legitimacy of supplying, learning science for inference).
- **What it is:** a theory-agnostic way of **constructing** and **interrogating** practice that coaches from several traditions could share, with unusually explicit rules about what practice can and cannot show.

**The path to B** is the three-part structure the product owner identified:
- **WHAT to coach** → Game Understanding;
- **HOW to construct the practice problem** → Phase 1 (this framework);
- **HOW to coach within that practice** → Coach Intervention;
- with **learning / progression** (did anything change over time?) across all three, currently the weakest evidence.

**Deep framework. Simple coaching. Practice behaviour ≠ learning.** Similarity is not validation; difference is not superiority.

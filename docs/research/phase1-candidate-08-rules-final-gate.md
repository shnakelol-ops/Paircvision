## Executive verdict

**B — RULES dissolves. ACTIONS survives as the residue under CHANGE THE PROBLEM.**

- **RULES is a mechanism, not a lever.** A rule is one way a coach changes a practice. What it changes is usually SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION or SCORING, and it is recorded there.
- **ACTIONS survives** because one thing a rule can change is described by no other lever: **which actions players are allowed, banned or required to use** (touch limits, no dribbling, weak foot only, kicking only, hand-pass limits, no back pass, must overlap, must switch).
- **Name: ACTIONS** (§3). Definition, boundary and precedence rule in §2 and §4.
- **No new coach-facing question.** Everything sits under *"What am I telling them, and what am I leaving them to solve?"*
- **CF-1 v2: no amendment.**
- **Four frozen principles** (AC-1 to AC-4), from twenty-six candidates.
- **Tactical learning, retention and transfer after action restrictions: UNKNOWN.**

## 1. Final RULES verdict

**Test:** *a rule is primarily a mechanism a coach uses to manipulate a practice, not necessarily the property of the game being manipulated.*

| Coach says | Mechanism | Property changed | Primary home |
|---|---|---|---|
| "Stay in your channel" | Rule | Where players may be | **SPACE** |
| "An extra player may enter" | Rule | Who takes part | **NUMBERS** |
| "Score within six seconds" | Rule | Deadline | **TIME** |
| "Attack either of two targets" | Rule / set-up | Target structure | **DIRECTION** |
| "Double points for a switch" | Rule | Value | **SCORING** |
| "Maximum two touches" | Rule | **Which actions are legal** | **ACTIONS** |

**Verdict: the distinction survives.**
- Every row uses the same mechanism (a rule). Only the last row changes a property no other lever describes.
- Rules are also not the only mechanism: the same properties can be changed by set-up (cones, goals), equipment, scoring sheets or instruction. The **property**, not the mechanism, is what the framework classifies.
- **Practitioner support:** coaches call every modification "rules", "conditions" or "constraints" (practitioner audit §21). None treats "rules" as one kind of thing.
- **Scientific support:** the literature's "rule manipulation" covers player numbers and court size (basketball scoping review) as readily as touch limits (scientific audit §1).

**RULES is dissolved as a PáircVision lever.** The word remains everyday vocabulary and names a mechanism.

**Not to be confused with:** the **rules of Gaelic football and hurling** (the official laws), which define the reference game (§15).

## 2. Does ACTIONS genuinely survive?

**Candidate definition:** *ACTIONS describes which player actions are available, prohibited or required within the practice.*

### Kill test: can each lever already represent it?

| Lever / layer | Can it represent "no dribbling" or "two-touch"? | Why not |
|---|---|---|
| SPACE | No | Same pitch, same zones. The dribble is banned everywhere |
| NUMBERS | No | Same players on each side |
| TIME | **Partly** (secondary effect) | Time on the ball shortens, but the TIME gate (§5, T5) already found touch limits "alter action possibilities, not merely time". A countdown leaves dribbling legal |
| DIRECTION | No | Same targets. "No back pass" restricts actions relative to the target; the target is unchanged (DIRECTION gate) |
| SCORING | No | Nothing's value changes; the action is illegal, not worthless |
| GAME MOMENT | No | Same starting situation |
| INFORMATION (pending) | No | An instruction ("try not to dribble") leaves the dribble legal. That is the legal-alternative boundary (Candidate 07 §9) |
| DECISION ALLOCATION | **Describes it, does not replace it** | The lens reads *every* manipulation for the decisions it supplies. It is not itself a property a coach changes (Candidate 07 §11). ACTIONS is the property; the lens analyses it |

**Result: ACTIONS is not redundant.** Two practices can be identical in space, numbers, time, targets, scoring and starting moment and still differ in what players are allowed to do. Evidence shows that difference changes behaviour (more passes, fewer receptions and dribbles, more unsuccessful passes, higher intensity: scientific audit §5).

### Exact boundary

**ACTIONS applies when the manipulation's content is *which action types* are legal or required**, such as:
- technique or limb (weak foot, kick only, hand-pass only);
- ball contacts (one-touch, two-touch, touch minimums);
- ball-carrying (no dribbling / soloing, step limits);
- pass or play types and their sequences (no back pass, hand-pass limits, three passes before a shot, must go through a target player);
- physical contact (no tackle, tag instead of tackle);
- required movement actions (must overlap, must press).

**Precedence rule (to avoid double-counting).** Classify by what the rule's content specifies. List the other effects as secondary; do not home them twice.

| The rule specifies… | Primary home |
|---|---|
| **where** players or the ball may be | SPACE |
| **how many / who** may take part in a contest | NUMBERS |
| **how long / by when** | TIME |
| **towards which target** | DIRECTION |
| **how much an outcome is worth** (including *not counting*) | SCORING |
| **which starting situation** | GAME MOMENT |
| **which action** | **ACTIONS** |

**Boundary cases settled here:**
- **Validity rules** ("score only counts after a switch"): the shot is still legal; only its value is zero. **Home: SCORING**, flagged as functioning like a requirement. This **amends** the SCORING gate §4, which homed validity rules in RULES, because RULES no longer exists as a home.
- **Eligibility rules** ("only the nearest defender may tackle"; "the floater can't score"): they set **who** may contest, which fixes the effective numbers at the ball. **Home: NUMBERS**, with WHO fixed in the lens.
- **Offside as a practice manipulation:** it removes legal space behind the line. **Home: SPACE**, DIRECTION secondary. As a sport law, it belongs to the reference game (§15).
- **Sequence requirements with a count** ("three passes before a shot"): **ACTIONS** (a required sequence), TIME secondary.
- **Route requirements** ("must use the wing", "must use the target player"): **ACTIONS** (a required action on the way to the target); SPACE or DIRECTION secondary. **Player confinement** ("wingers stay in the channel") is **SPACE**.

**Re-homing of items earlier gates sent to "RULES":**

| Item | Earlier home | New primary home | Secondary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Touch limits (TIME gate §5) | RULES | **ACTIONS** | TIME |
| Forward-only / no back pass (DIRECTION gate) | RULES | **ACTIONS** | DIRECTION |
| Must switch before scoring (DIRECTION, SCORING gates) | RULES | **ACTIONS** | DIRECTION, SPACE |
| Must use wing / compulsory channel (DIRECTION gate) | RULES | **ACTIONS** (ball route) or **SPACE** (player confinement) | The other |
| Must cross a line within X passes (DIRECTION gate) | RULES | **ACTIONS** | TIME |
| Offside (DIRECTION gate) | RULES | **SPACE** (as a practice manipulation) | DIRECTION |
| Validity ("only counts if") (SCORING gate §4) | RULES | **SCORING** | ACTIONS-like |
| "N passes before you may shoot" (SCORING gate) | RULES | **ACTIONS** | TIME |
| Scores only allowed from a zone (SCORING gate) | RULES × SPACE | **SCORING** (validity) | SPACE |
| "Only the nearest may challenge" (Candidate 07 §9) | RULES | **NUMBERS** | Decision allocation: WHO |
| "If you're the spare, you can't tackle" (Candidate 07 §9) | RULES | **NUMBERS** | — |

| Candidate | Includes allowed / banned / required? | Implies restriction only? | Clear internally? | Pretends to be established science? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **ACTIONS** | **Yes** | **No** | **Yes, with its one-line definition** | No | **Selected** |

| ACTION RESTRICTIONS | No (misses requirements) | **Yes** | Yes | No | Rejected |
| ACTION CONDITIONS | Yes | No | **No**: "conditions" means any modification in UK / Irish coaching | No | Rejected |
| PERMITTED ACTIONS | Weak on requirements | Leans that way | Yes | No | Rejected |
| ACTION OPTIONS | Weak on requirements; "options" overlaps with decisions | No | Confusable with DECISION ALLOCATION | No | Rejected |
| ACTION RULES | Yes | No | **No**: revives the dissolved RULES and clashes with sport rules | No | Rejected |
| ACTION SET | Yes | No | Yes, but technical | Borrowed from game theory / AI | Kept only as an internal synonym |

**Frozen name: ACTIONS.** Always used with its definition:

> **ACTIONS: which actions players are allowed, banned or required to use in the practice.**

- It parallels SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION, SCORING as a **property of the problem**.
- It is **PáircVision terminology**, not an established scientific category.
- **Known risk:** "actions" alone is very broad. The definition must travel with the word.
- Coach-facing wording, if ever needed, may differ (e.g., "what they're allowed to do").

## 4. Boundary examples

| Manipulation | Primary home | Secondary effects | Decisions removed (lens) | Decisions remaining |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Two-touch** | ACTIONS | TIME (less time on ball); intensity ↑; more passes, fewer receptions | Whether to carry, hold or dribble | Pass target, receiving orientation, support movement, timing of release within two touches |
| **One-touch** | ACTIONS | TIME (strong); pass success lowest (index-level) | Carry / hold / control-then-decide | Pass target, body shape, off-ball movement |
| **Weak foot only** | ACTIONS | Technical difficulty; tempo may drop | Which foot | All tactical choices |
| **No dribbling / no soloing** | ACTIONS | Off-ball movement ↑ (practitioner); SPACE use | Whether to carry | Shoot / pass / move; spacing; timing |
| **Kicking only** | ACTIONS | SPACE (kick distance); NUMBERS locally | Hand-pass, carry-and-offload choices | Kick type, target, timing, support |
| **No backwards pass** | ACTIONS | DIRECTION bias | Whether going back is better here | Forward / lateral option; timing |
| **Must overlap** | ACTIONS | NUMBERS locally | Whether an overlap is needed | Who overlaps, timing, angle, what the carrier does |
| **Must switch (before scoring)** | ACTIONS | SPACE, DIRECTION | Whether to switch | When and how to switch; who |
| **Hand-pass limit** (e.g., max three in a row) | ACTIONS | SPACE (forces kicking or carrying); TIME | A fourth hand-pass | Almost all tactics |
| **Only the nearest defender may tackle** | NUMBERS (effective 1v1 at the ball) | SPACE (others cover) | Who challenges (WHO fixed) | Whether, when and how to tackle; covering positions |
| **Stay in channel** | SPACE | NUMBERS locally; ↓ co-adaptation | Where to be (WHO / WHERE fixed) | Actions inside the channel |
| **Minimum three passes** | ACTIONS (sequence) | TIME; possession | Whether to shoot before three | Who passes, where, when to shoot after three |
| **Score within six seconds** | TIME | Urgency; decisions under pressure ↓ (TIME gate) | How long to build | Route, who, how |
| **Double points for a switch** | SCORING | Switch frequency ↑ | **None** (the switch stays optional) | All |
| **Must use target player** | ACTIONS (route) | DIRECTION | Which route | Timing, pass type, support |

**No double-counting:** each manipulation has one primary home. Secondary effects are noted for reading behaviour, not re-homed.

## 5. DOING vs CHOOSING

**Candidate (practitioner audit §26):** *practise doing it* vs *practise choosing it*.

### Stress tests

| Case | Doing? | Choosing? | What the test shows |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weak foot only | **Yes**: more weak-foot repetitions | Not the foot choice | A restriction can be right because the objective is doing |
| No back pass, when the aim is knowing when forward vs back is useful | Some (forward-pass execution) | **The key choice is removed** | The restriction contradicts the objective |
| Set-piece rehearsal | **Yes**: execution and timing of a supplied plan | Mostly supplied, legitimately | Prescribing most decisions can be exactly right |
| Press recognition | Pressing actions | **Whether / when to press must stay open** | A "must press every turnover" rule would remove the thing being learned |
| No dribbling (basketball, practitioner) | Catch-and-decide readiness | **New choices appear**: shoot / pass / cut become more frequent and urgent | One restriction can reduce one choice **and increase exposure to others** |
| Two-touch rondo | One-touch / two-touch technique | Pass target and support angle remain | Practices contain **both** |

**Verdict.**
- **Not a binary.** Most practices involve both, and one rule can remove one choice while intensifying others.
- **Not a new concept.** "Choosing" is the WHAT / WHETHER / WHEN part of DECISION ALLOCATION; "doing" is largely HOW (execution). The distinction is **DECISION ALLOCATION read from the coach's objective**.
- **Not complete.** Practices may target execution, perception (reading cues), decision, coordination with teammates, or combinations. "Doing vs choosing" compresses those.
- **Status: PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS**, consistent with practitioner reasoning (the touch-limit disagreement; the FA No. 10 case; Tristan Thomas) and with CF-1 v2. **Not a scientific model.**
- **Role:** **explanatory language** that helps a coach (or a later product) apply the frozen question. **Not frozen** as a principle or a question.

## 6. Decision Allocation integration

**Does ACTIONS need an additional coach-facing question? No.**

A coach who honestly answers *"What am I telling them, and what am I leaving them to solve?"* about a two-touch game already sees that "carry or not" is decided for the players and that the pass target is left to them.

**Internal reading of action restrictions through WHO / WHAT / WHEN / HOW** (lens, not a measurement model):

| Restriction type | Mostly fixes | Usually leaves |
|---|---|---|
| Prohibition (no dribble, no back pass) | Narrows **WHAT** | WHO, WHEN, HOW of the remaining options |
| Requirement (must overlap, must switch) | Fixes **WHAT**, often part of **WHEN** (before scoring) | WHO, HOW, exact timing |
| Touch / contact limit | Parts of **WHAT** and **HOW** (no carry; release soon) | Pass target, WHO supports, WHEN to move |
| Technique restriction (weak foot, kick only) | **HOW** (limb / technique) | All tactical WHAT / WHEN / WHO |
| Sequence requirement (three passes first) | **WHEN** a shot is allowed | WHO, WHAT, HOW within the sequence |

**A rule can remove one decision while leaving others alive.** That is DA-1 applied; nothing new is frozen.

## 7. CF-1 v2 applied

**CF-1 v2 (frozen):** *When a coach supplies part of the solution, players' behaviour from it is not evidence that they can make the decisions that were supplied for them. Evidence about the decisions they still had to make is unaffected. This limits what can be inferred. It does not mean supplying conditions is poor practice.*

| Case | Not evidence of | Still evidence of |
|---|---|---|
| Weak foot required | Choosing when the weak foot is the right foot | Weak-foot execution under game pressure |
| Must press after every turnover | Recognising whether pressing was on | How the press was coordinated: who went, angles, cover |
| No back pass | Judging forward vs back | Quality and timing of forward options |
| Two-touch | Judging when to carry | Pass selection, support angles |
| Must overlap | Recognising when an overlap helps | Overlap timing and the carrier's use of it |

**Amendment needed? No.** "Supplies part of the solution (… a mandated action …)" already covers requirements, and a prohibition supplies the solution by removing alternatives. CF-1 v2 applies to ACTIONS **unchanged**.

## 8. Forced behaviour

| Claim | Status | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| **Forced behaviour ≠ evidence players understand when to choose it** | **SUPPORTED** (as a statement of inference; CF-1 v2). No study shows forced tactical behaviour producing later appropriate choice | Does **not** show forcing harms learning |
| **Forced behaviour is not automatically poor practice** | **SUPPORTED**: technical-test learning under non-dominant-foot restriction (Haaland & Hoff 2003; U13 RCT 2025; AO); practitioner consensus on legitimate purposes (practitioner audit RP-7) | Game transfer of technical gains **UNKNOWN**; tactical benefit of forcing **UNKNOWN** |

**Legitimate reasons for reducing choice** (where supported):
- **Technical practice**: repetitions of a technique (science: technical tests; practitioners).
- **Tactical rehearsal**: set pieces, restarts, game-model patterns (practitioners; Candidate 07 DA-3).
- **Coordination**: a shared answer beats individual ones (practitioners; DA-3).
- **Safety**: contact restrictions (governing-body formats; not a learning manipulation).
- **Habit-breaking, diagnosis, exposing teammates' problems** (practitioner reasoning; untested).

## 9. Problem exposure vs solution prescription

**Proposed formulation:** *Practice manipulations differ in how much of the solution they supply to players.*

- This is **DA-1** ("Any practice condition, rule or instruction can be read by which decisions it makes for the players and which it leaves them") phrased as a quantity.
- The practitioner audit showed most rules **both** raise a problem's frequency **and** remove some answers (§23 there).

**Decision: fully captured by DECISION ALLOCATION. No new concept.** "Problem-amplifying" and "solution-prescribing" are **retired** as candidate terms. The practitioner word "exaggeration" remains ordinary vocabulary.

## 10. Temporary restriction

**Can the framework say: "Restrictions can be temporary tools rather than intended match behaviours"?**

- **Yes, as a statement of purpose.** Practitioners use restrictions they never want in matches: one-touch rondos, weak-foot-only games, no-dribble games, regain deadlines (practitioner audit §10). Elite practice includes "judicious deviation" from representative play.
- **Status: SUPPORTED** (practitioner practice; logical).

**What it does not say:**
- It does **not** say temporary restriction **improves transfer**. **UNKNOWN.**
- It does **not** say the restriction must be removed (§11).
- It does **not** say the behaviour under the restriction will reappear appropriately without it.

Folded into **AC-3** rather than frozen separately.

## 11. Rule removal

**Evidence:**
- **Science:** one youth programme (Gonzalez-Artetxe et al., reported across three papers) found **little average change** in an unconstrained 8-a-side post-test after four sessions with artificial rules on touches, interactions and space, with **large individual variation** and mixed team-line effects. Delay relative to training: UNR. No retention or competition test.
- **Practice:** coaches often finish with a free game. **No accessible coach explicitly removes a specific rule and checks whether the behaviour survives appropriately.**

**Status of "add rule → remove rule → observe whether behaviour survives appropriately":**
- **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS / CHECK**: a reasonable **observation method**, **not** an established coaching method and **not** a learning test.

**Conditions if used:**
1. **Same-session persistence is not retention.**
2. **A drop in the behaviour is not failure.** Players may now use it only when it fits. Judge **appropriateness**, not frequency (SCORING gate §13).
3. **For DOING-purpose restrictions** (weak foot), the check is whether the skill **appears when useful**, not whether it appears as often.
4. **Transfer to competition: UNKNOWN.**

## 12. EXPOSE → EXPLORE → RELEASE → RETAIN

- **Candidate 08 does not change its status.** It remains a **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS**.
- **What Candidate 08 adds (clarification only):**
  - **RELEASE is especially relevant to ACTIONS.** Action restrictions often produce behaviour that is **not** intended match behaviour, so what happens without them is the question that matters.
  - For a **doing** objective, EXPLORE may be minimal by design (repetition, not search).
  - The only empirical analogue of RELEASE (Gonzalez-Artetxe) showed **no clear average benefit**.
- **No evidence upgrade.**

## 13. Constraint stacking

**Rejected:** a one-rule maximum; "fewer constraints are always better"; "complexity is bad". None is supported.

**Defensible concern:** *multiple manipulations make it harder to identify what is producing the observed behaviour.*
- **Logical:** effects of simultaneous manipulations are confounded.
- **Empirical:** touch restriction × player numbers interact (Jäger et al., 2026; AO); more rule manipulations ↓ exploratory behaviour (*Front Psychol* 2019; AO); pass-participation rules ↑ mental fatigue (García-Calvo et al., 2021; AO).
- **Practitioner:** "too many rules … can prevent … the tactical outcomes desired" (Science for Sport, SIO); keep rules few and refereeable (Rugby Coach Weekly, SIO).
- **Status: SUPPORTED as an inference limit** (logic + interaction evidence). **Not** a rule about how many constraints to use.

Folded into **AC-4**.

## 14. Players gaming the rule

**Candidate wording:** *"Players respond to the game actually created, not necessarily the lesson the coach intended."*

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Is it true of the evidence? | **Yes**: rule-specific acute effects; more unsuccessful passes under touch limits; individual variability; purposeless passing under pass counts |
| Is it true of practitioner experience? | **Yes**: forced two touches at the wrong moment; passes without purpose; cheating that frustrates |
| Does "game" read better than "task"? | **Yes**, for coaches. Internally equivalent |
| Does it imply players are at fault? | **No**, and it must not. It points to the design |
| Does it imply the rule is bad? | **No.** Gaming shows what the rule **actually rewards**. That may be a mismatch worth fixing, or a harmless side effect of a rule used for repetitions |

**Useful to the coach?** **Yes**: it turns unexpected behaviour into information about the design.

**Status: PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS, consistent with evidence and practitioners.** Folded into **AC-4**.

## 15. Official sport rules

**Final boundary:**
- **Official laws define the reference game.** Current Gaelic football and hurling rules are the game players are preparing for.
- **Coach-added rules modify practice relative to that reference.** They are classified by the property they change (§2).
- **A sport rule can become a practice-design focus** when deliberately rehearsed (e.g., practising kickouts under the arc rule, or the three-per-half structure). That is **GAME MOMENT** (which situation) plus the relevant lever, not a coach-added ACTIONS change.
- **Removing a sport rule in practice** (e.g., playing without three-per-half) **is** a manipulation, classified by what it changes (here NUMBERS × SPACE).
- **Governing-body trials** (2026 Ryan Cup: hand-pass limits, backcourt rule, fisted-point ban; Fitzgibbon trials) are **competition-level** ACTIONS-type changes, not coaching evidence. **Effects: unevaluated.**

**Do not confuse** the rules of Gaelic football with RULES as a PáircVision lever candidate. The latter is dissolved; the former is the reference.

**Caveat:** 2025–26 Gaelic rule wording (40 m kickout arc, three-per-half, tap-and-go, two-point arc, goalkeeper restrictions) comes from **secondary sources**. The Official Guide text is **unverified**.

## 16. Technical vs tactical

**Rejected:** "technical = isolated / restriction is fine; tactical = game / restriction is bad".

**Tested formulation:** *the appropriateness of removing a choice depends partly on which capability the coach is trying to develop: execution, perception, decision, coordination, or a combination.*

- **Supported by:** CF-1 v2 logic; technical-test evidence for weak-foot restriction; practitioner disagreement on touch limits, which is a disagreement about **objective** (practitioner audit §3, §18).
- **Status: PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS**, consistent with evidence and practitioners.
- **Becomes part of AC-3** (the legitimacy principle). The capability list is **explanatory language**, not a taxonomy to freeze.

## 17. Coach intervention

- **Finding (practitioner audit §19):** in every credible account, observation, questioning, freezing and live redesign are central. "Set up the game and stand back" is criticised as a misinterpretation (Moran et al., 2026; *Sports Coaching Review* 2024).
- **Decision: do not freeze in Candidate 08.** Intervention concerns what the coach **says and does**, which is **INFORMATION (Candidate 09)** and later coaching-process work. Freezing it here would duplicate that work.
- **Recorded for Candidate 09:** "the condition does not do the teaching alone" is **MULTIPLE-PRACTITIONER SUPPORT**.

Touch limits change action structure and physical load; they are not shown to speed up decision-making.** | **SUPPORTED** (acute) | M | Mostly soccer SSGs; tactical measures scarce; two-touch passing results inconsistent |
| **AC-2** | **Behaviour under a required or banned action is evidence about how players executed and coordinated what was left to them. It is not evidence that they would choose that action at the right moments without the rule. Tactical learning, retention and transfer after action restrictions are unknown.** | **SUPPORTED** (as evidence status; CF-1 v2 applied) | M | One reference-game study (short; little average change; large individual variation). Does not show restrictions harm learning |
| **AC-3** | **Restricting players' actions is legitimate when the choice being removed is not what the practice is for: repetitions of a technique, rehearsal of a team pattern, coordination, or safety. Such restrictions are tools, not intended match behaviour. Non-dominant-side restriction improves technical test performance; whether that carries into games is unknown.** | **SUPPORTED** (technical-test RCTs; practitioner consensus on purposes) | M | Game transfer unknown; tactical benefit of restriction unknown |
| **AC-4** | **Players respond to the game actually created, not necessarily the lesson the coach intended. Unexpected or "gaming" behaviour shows what the practice rewards; it is not by itself player failure or proof the practice is poor. Several manipulations at once make it harder to tell which one is producing what players do.** | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** (acute rule-specific and unintended effects; touch × numbers interaction; more rules ↓ exploration; practitioner reports) + logic | L–M | No study codes "gaming"; no threshold for stacking |

Plus **CF-1 v2** (unchanged) and **DA-1** (applies to ACTIONS unchanged).

**Not frozen:**
- DOING vs CHOOSING (explanatory language, §5);
- problem-amplifying vs solution-prescribing (retired, §9);
- the rule-removal check (hypothesis, §11);
- "rewards instead of requirements" (single practitioner);
- coach intervention (to Candidate 09).

## 21. Coach-facing simplicity gate

**Test:** can everything important sit under the frozen question?

> **"What am I telling them, and what am I leaving them to solve?"**

| Candidate 08 idea | Reachable from the frozen question? |
|---|---|
| A rule bans or requires an action | Yes: that is "telling them" part of the answer |
| Which decisions survive a restriction | Yes: "what am I leaving them to solve?" |
| Restriction is fine if the removed choice isn't today's aim | Yes, once the coach knows what today is for |
| Behaviour under the rule isn't proof of choosing | Yes: the choice was told, not solved |
| Players respond to the game created | Needs a short **explanation**, not a question |

**Internal supporting distinction:** *"Am I asking them to practise doing something, choosing something, or both?"*
- **Useful** as explanatory language beneath the frozen question.
- **Not frozen as a coach-facing question.** A second question would split attention for little gain, and the practitioner evidence showed no genuine gap (practitioner audit §9).

**Terms that must not reach coaches:** mechanism vs property, ACTIONS as a category name, legal-alternative test, precedence rule, validity rule, WHO / WHAT / WHEN / HOW (unless a coach wants to unpack it).

**Deep framework. Simple coaching.**

## 22. Final taxonomy verdict

### **B — RULES dissolves; ACTIONS survives as the residue under CHANGE THE PROBLEM**

**Why not A (RULES as a peer lever):** a rule is a mechanism. Treating it as a lever would double-count every zone, clock, target and bonus already homed in SPACE, TIME, DIRECTION and SCORING (§1).

**Why not C (rename to a better concept):** the name test (§3) found no better term than ACTIONS. "Action allowances" is dropped; the concept is unchanged.

**Why not D (dissolve both):** the kill test (§2) found a property no other lever describes. Absorbing it into TIME would repeat the error the TIME gate already corrected; absorbing it into DECISION ALLOCATION would confuse a lens with what it analyses.

**Why not E:** no new architecture is needed. ACTIONS fits CHANGE THE PROBLEM, and the lens layer already handles "how much of the solution is supplied".

**Why not F:** both audits and this gate converge.

## 23. Architecture after Candidate 08 (research level)

```
CHOOSE THE PROBLEM
  GAME MOMENT (which situation, how often)
  + which player meets which problem

CHANGE THE PROBLEM
  SPACE · NUMBERS · TIME · DIRECTION (target structure) · SCORING (value, incl. validity)
  · ACTIONS (which actions are allowed, banned or required)      ← new
  pending: CONSEQUENCES

WHAT THE COACH SUPPLIES vs WHAT PLAYERS SOLVE
  pending: INFORMATION (Candidate 09): instructions, cues, feedback, questioning, intervention

CROSS-FRAMEWORK
  CF-1 v2 (unchanged)
  DECISION ALLOCATION lens (unchanged; now also carries "how much of the solution is supplied")

REFERENCE
  The official rules of the sport define the reference game (not a lever)
```

**Changes from the Candidate 07 architecture:**
- "pending: RULES" is removed from CHANGE THE PROBLEM.
- **ACTIONS** is added.
- **SCORING** now explicitly includes validity rules ("only counts if").
- A **REFERENCE** line records that sport rules are the game being prepared for, not a manipulation.
- **No symmetry forced.** ACTIONS is narrower than SPACE or NUMBERS and is expected to be used less often.

1. Whether any action restriction (tactical or technical) leads to appropriate choice of the action in later free play, retention tests or competition. **UNKNOWN.**

3. Whether restrictions that intensify other choices (no-dribble) develop those choices. **UNKNOWN.**
4. Whether rewards rather than requirements preserve decision learning better. **UNTESTED** (single practitioner argument).
5. Whether removal-and-observe improves coaching decisions. **UNTESTED.**

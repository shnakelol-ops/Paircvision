## 2. Tactical coordination: what is fixed, what stays variable

| Context | What the coach fixes (as described) | What remains variable | Source (type) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basketball help defence** | A **relational** rule: the **closest** player helps; the next closest "helps the helper" | *Whether* help is needed (read the drive); timing; recovery; closeout | P1, P2 (DIRECT) |
| **Soccer pressing** | A **relational** rule: the **nearest** player presses; the next covers; the rest balance. Plus **triggers** (bad touch, back pass, receiver facing own goal) | Recognising the trigger; angle; speed; when to jump | P3 (DIRECT, coach education) |
| **Elite soccer** | Spaces and elements shown "on the tactics board" | "In actual play, players must make correct decisions in a flash" | P4 (DIRECT quote, secondary) |
| **Elite soccer** | Principles and "action patterns" | Flexibility to apply them in different match situations | P5 (secondary) |
| **Rugby ruck defence** | **Positional-relational** jobs: the first player at the ruck is guard / pillar, the next is bodyguard / post; one organiser behind | Line speed; who fills which slot depends on arrival order; reading the attack | P6 (DIRECT, coach education) |
| **AFL defence** | Zones; a "loose man" (reads and intercepts); **hand over** opponents moving through zones | Reading play; interception timing | P7 (secondary) |
| **Gaelic defending** | Man-marking skills **and** reading the game; balance through depth and cover | Communication, vision, decisions ("make the forward make the decision") | P8 (DIRECT, provincial) |
| **Gaelic kickout press** | Zonal (occupy space) or player-to-player (within ~1 m of the opponent) | Contest, breaking ball, timing | P13 (research, descriptive) |
| **Restarts / set plays** | Named jobs (receivers, blockers) | Execution; adaptation to the opponent's set-up | Previous audits (AFL stoppages; kickout structure) |

**Key finding.**
- In defensive coordination, the dominant form is **not a named role**. It is a **relational allocation rule**: "nearest", "closest", "first to the ruck", "next closest".
- The coach supplies **the rule for deciding who**. The player still has to **recognise which relation applies to them, right now**.

## 3. Responsibility vs instruction (is allocation just INFORMATION?)

| Statement | What it supplies | What the player must still do |
|---|---|---|
| "John, you press." | **WHO** (named) | Recognise when; how |
| "Nearest player presses." | The **allocation rule** | Recognise *am I nearest?*; when; how |
| "Press when the receiver's picture is poor." | The **trigger / cue** (when) | Recognise the trigger; who (unspecified) |
| "Can you recognise when we can press?" | A **question** | Everything |

**Assessment.**
- Rows 2–4 are clearly **information** of different specificity: a rule, a cue, a question.
- Row 1 is also delivered as information, but it **binds a person to a job**. That changes who else is released from the decision.
- **Practitioner material does not treat named allocation as a different kind of thing from instruction.** It sits on a continuum of how much the coach specifies.
- **Verdict (PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION):** responsibility allocation is **not a separate manipulation type from INFORMATION**. It is **one dimension of what information supplies**: the **WHO** dimension. See §19.

## 4. Individual vs collective responsibility

| Type | Example | Found in practice? |
|---|---|---|
| **Named individual** | "Sean marks their 14" | Yes: opponent-specific man-marking; restart jobs (P13, P14; INFERENCE from Gaelic commentary) |
| **Positional** | "Our 11 drops in"; the half-forward line tracks back | Yes: Gaelic "half-forward lines … clear roles" (analysis, SIO) |
| **Relational** | "Nearest presses; next covers" | **Yes, dominant in defensive principles** (P1, P3, P6, P7) |
| **Shared / team** | "All 18 have defensive responsibilities" | Yes (P7) |
| **Emergent / self-organised** | Players decide via communication | Implied by communication emphasis (P8; rugby organiser) and by Klopp's "decisions in a flash" (P4) |

**Finding.** The practitioner world distinguishes **named / positional / relational / shared** allocation far more often than "fixed vs free roles". The science audit's fixed-vs-free framing **under-describes practice**.

## 5. WHO vs HOW / WHEN

**Scientific-audit finding:** *assigning a responsibility often removes the WHO decision and sometimes the DOES-THIS-NEED-DOING decision, while leaving HOW and WHEN partly open.*

| Example | WHO | DOES IT NEED DOING? | WHEN | HOW | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Nearest presses" + triggers | Rule supplied; recognition kept | Trigger supplied as cue; recognition kept | Kept (trigger reading) | Kept | P3 |
| Basketball "closest helps" | Rule supplied | **Kept** (read the drive) | Kept | Kept | P1, P2 |
| Rugby guard / pillar | Arrival order decides; rule supplied | Largely fixed (every ruck needs a guard) | Kept (line speed) | Kept | P6 |
| Named man-marker | **Removed** | **Removed** (always mark) | Partly kept (when to release / switch) | Kept | P13, P14 (INFERENCE) |
| Kickout receiver (restart plan) | **Removed** | Removed | Partly kept | Kept | INFERENCE |
| "Loose man" (AFL) | Removed (named) | **Kept** (read and intercept) | Kept | Kept | P7 |

**Complication.**
- The AFL loose man and basketball help show that a named or relational allocation can **fix WHO while deliberately leaving DOES-IT-NEED-DOING to the player**.
- So "removes the recognition decision" is **not general**. It depends on whether the job is **unconditional** (always mark X) or **conditional** (you are the one who reads and helps).

**Refined (PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS):**
- Allocation always fixes **some** of WHO.
- It removes RECOGNITION only when the job is **unconditional**.
- **Conditional jobs keep recognition alive.**

## 6. Recognition (the central CF-1 question)

**Question:** if a coach always tells a player who presses, covers, supports or receives, what opportunity does the team have to learn who should take responsibility when nobody tells them?

- **Practitioner evidence of a deliberate progression from assigned responsibility to self-organisation: NOT FOUND** as an explicit method in accessible sources.
- **What is found:**
  - **Relational rules** (nearest / closest) are taught as the **mature** form, not as a stepping stone. The FIBA-derived rationale is **speed**: "when help is necessary, there isn't time to think; players have to just react" (P1/P2 context; SIO; attribution to a specific source **UNR**).
  - Rules **reduce** real-time deliberation **deliberately**.
  - Constraints-led and GBA coaches emphasise questioning (Kinnerk, reported; GAA GBA). Accessible sources don't link this specifically to responsibility allocation.
- **Status:** the CF-1 concern stands **logically**. Practitioner progression toward self-organisation is **UNKNOWN / not found**. **No superiority claim.**

## 8. Structured tactical rehearsal (the opposing case, full weight)

**Why elite coaches prescribe responsibilities (DIRECT or strongly inferable):**
1. **Speed.** "No time to think": simple rules allow instant reaction (basketball help; P1/P2 context).
2. **Coordination.** Defending "is always collective, never individual" (P3). Pressure–cover–balance only works if roles interlock.
3. **Game model.** Principles and action patterns (P5); tactics-board preparation (P4).
4. **Precision at restarts.** Set-piece and kickout jobs (AFL stoppages; kickout structures).
5. **Opponent-specific plans.** Man-marking the key forward (Gaelic commentary; FRC-era one-to-one defending, P14).

**When is explicit allocation simply good coaching (PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION, consistent with practitioners):**
- when the **performance objective is the coordinated execution itself**: restarts, defensive systems, specific match-ups;
- when **decision time is too short** for deliberation;
- when **introducing** a structure the team doesn't yet share.

**PáircVision must not become "never tell players what to do".**

## 13. Failure modes

| Failure mode | Grounding |
|---|---|
| Nobody acts because responsibility is unclear | Rationale behind "simple rules … clarity on who has help responsibility" (basketball; SIO). Role-ambiguity psychology (scientific audit) |
| Everyone reacts to the same problem | Implied by "one presses, next covers, next balances" (P3) |
| Player follows the assigned job despite the picture changing | **PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION** (e.g., a man-marker tracking into irrelevance); not found as practitioner report |
| Coach becomes permanent decision-maker | CF-1 logic; CLA practitioner criticism (previous audit) |
| Excessive freedom destroys coordination | Implied by the collective-defending rationale (P3, P6) |
| Responsibility becomes position-dependent rather than game-dependent | AFL: shift from fixed one-on-one to zones and hand-over (P7). Practitioners moved **away** from position-bound jobs |

## 14. Responsibility progression: NAMED → POSITIONAL → RELATIONAL → PLAYER-COORDINATED

**Test the candidate:** "Sean presses" → "our 11 presses" → "nearest player presses" → players recognise and communicate who presses.

| Evidence | Direction |

**Verdict.**
- **Useful as a taxonomy of allocation types (PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS).**
- **Not supported as a progression or hierarchy.** Relational and named allocation coexist, even at the top level.

## 15. CF-1 practitioner test

**CF-1:** *behaviour from a coach-supplied condition isn't evidence players can recognise or create it.*

- **Implicit operation of CF-1 by respected coaches:**
  - Klopp's split between board preparation and in-game decisions (P4, secondary);
  - Connacht GAA's insistence on "reading" the game alongside man-marking (P8);
  - relational rules that keep recognition with the player (P1–P3).
  - These all show awareness that supplied structure ≠ in-game recognition.
- **Coaches unconcerned with self-recognition:**
  - where execution **is** the objective (restart jobs, opponent-specific marking, "no time to think" help rules), **executing the allocated responsibility is the performance goal**.
  - CF-1 then limits only the inference ("they learned to recognise"). It doesn't limit the practice.
- **Status:** CF-1 is **SCIENTIFICALLY CONSISTENT** with practice and **practitioner-compatible**. It is not contradicted.

## 16. Volunteer-coach translation (U12 Gaelic)

> **Give someone a job when…**
> - the whole team needs to know it: who takes the kickout, who stands where for their kickout, who marks their best forward;
> - or when there's no time to think in the match: someone has to go right away.
>
> Practising those jobs is good coaching.
>
> **Leave it to them when…** you want them to learn to spot it themselves.
>
> **The in-between often works best: give them a rule, not a name.**
> - "Nearest player goes to the ball; the next one covers."
> - Now nobody's told exactly who. Each player has to look and ask: "Is that me?"
>
> **Watch for:**
> - two players going to the same ball, or nobody going;
> - a player still doing their "job" when the game has moved on.
>
> Those are the moments to ask: "Whose job was that? How did you know?"
>
> **At this age, let them try different positions over the year.** That's GAA Go Games policy. Then each player meets different jobs.

## 19. Taxonomy recommendation (not frozen)

### Recommendation: **C — treat RESPONSIBILITY ALLOCATION as a cross-framework dimension: the WHO that the coach supplies**

With a flag for **D** (a broader structure) for the final gate.

**Why not A (a distinct practice-design decision):**
- Practitioners deliver allocation **as information** (names, rules, cues) on a continuum of specificity (§3).
- It is not a separate kind of manipulation.

**Why not B (simply absorb into INFORMATION):**
- Absorbing it hides the specific thing CF-1 cares about: **which decision** (who / whether / when / how) the coach has taken away.
- The WHO dimension recurs outside INFORMATION:
  - NUMBERS: who is the spare;
  - GAME MOMENT: who restarts;
  - RULES: who may do what.

**C:** RESPONSIBILITY ALLOCATION = the **WHO** dimension of what the coach supplies, with four observed forms:
1. **named**;
2. **positional**;
3. **relational**;
4. **shared / emergent**.

**Flag D for the final gate.** The evidence points to a broader cross-framework structure, **DECISION ALLOCATION**, of which WHO is one axis:
- **WHO** acts;
- **WHETHER** it needs doing;
- **WHEN**;
- **HOW / WHERE**.

Every lever and instruction fixes some of these and leaves others to players.

## 20. Most important synthesis questions

### Can a coach allocate responsibility while preserving meaningful player decision-making?

**Yes (practitioner-supported; learning effects untested).** Decisions that stay alive under common allocations:

| Allocation | Decisions still alive |
|---|---|
| Relational rule ("nearest presses") | Am I nearest? Is it on (trigger)? When? Angle / speed? What does the next player do? |
| Conditional named job ("you're the loose man / helper") | Whether to intervene; where; when; recovery |
| Unconditional named job ("mark 14") | When to pass on or switch; positioning; tackling timing; when to join the attack |
| Restart job ("you receive") | Movement to get free; first touch direction; next pass |

### Is sophisticated coaching less about prescription vs freedom, and more about deliberately deciding which decisions the coach fixes and which the players keep?

**PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS.** Not a conclusion.

**For it:**
- Practitioner practice shows coaches **selectively** fixing the WHO (relational rules, named restart jobs).
- They supply cues for the WHEN (triggers).
- They leave HOW largely to players (Klopp's "decisions in a flash"; basketball rotation reading).

**Against / limits:**
- **No practitioner describes their coaching in these terms.**
- **No study compares** "decision-allocation-aware" design with other designs for learning.
- The hypothesis may be **true as a description** and still **untested as a design method**.

**Carry to the final gate** as the candidate unifying question for the "coach supplies vs players detect / create" family:

> **"Which decisions am I making for the players here: who, whether, when, or how? And which ones do I want them to keep?"**

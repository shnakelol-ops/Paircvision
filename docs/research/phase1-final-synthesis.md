# PáircVision Phase 1 — Final Synthesis: How a Coach Changes a Practice

**Type:** Synthesis of the completed Phase 1 research programme. Research only: no production code, sessions, drills, UI or commercial product design.

**Status:** Final, September 2026. This document **summarises**; it adds no new evidence and makes no new taxonomy decisions. **Where it differs from a final gate, the final gate governs**, and where gates differ, the later gate's "Later taxonomy clarification" notes govern (§15).

> ### ⚠️ ACCESS LIMITATION (applies to all of Phase 1)
>
> - **No source in Phase 1 was read in full text, listened to or watched.** The network proxy refused publishers, PMC pages, federation documents (GAA, FA, FIBA), coaching sites, podcasts and video throughout.
> - Every finding rests on **abstracts (AO)** or **search-index summaries (SIO)**. Effect sizes and quotes are as indexed.
> - Agreement between a scientific audit and a practitioner audit **does not by itself raise confidence**: both used the same restricted access.
> - The 2025–26 Gaelic rule wording (kickout arc, three-per-half, tap-and-go, two-point arc) comes from **secondary sources** and is **unverified** against the Official Guide.
> - Kinnerk's primary sources were **never accessed**; his positions are **UNKNOWN** except where journalists report them.
> - **Everything below is provisional on access.**

## 1. Executive summary

**What Phase 1 asked.** A coach can change a practice game in many ways. Which of those ways are genuinely different, what does each actually change, and what does the evidence say about whether players **learn** from them?

**What it found.**

1. **The original list of ten "levers" was not ten things of the same kind.** It mixed properties of the game problem, a choice about which problem to practise, a way of reading all of them, a mechanism, coaching behaviour and things outside practice design.
2. **Six properties of the problem survive** as CHANGE THE PROBLEM levers: **SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION, SCORING, ACTIONS**.
3. **One selection decision survives:** **GAME MOMENT** (which situations players meet, how often, and how they arise).
4. **A cross-framework layer** reads every manipulation: **DECISION ALLOCATION** (which decisions the coach supplies), **CF-1 v2** (what behaviour can and cannot show) and **GI-2** (what happens when the information a decision depends on is missing or replaced).
5. **Four candidates dissolved:** PLAYER ROLES, RULES, INFORMATION and CONSEQUENCES. Each left a precise residue (a lens, a lever, an inference limit, internal notation) rather than a lever of its own.
6. **Coach behaviour** (instruction, calls, cues, questioning, demonstration, feedback) is a separate, **deferred** framework: COACH INTERVENTION.
7. **The evidence is strong on what practice manipulations do to behaviour in the moment and almost silent on learning, retention and transfer.** That asymmetry is the single most important finding of Phase 1, and it shapes every claim PáircVision may make.
8. **Gaelic-specific evidence is thin:** mostly physical load, match description and a small number of coaching studies. Almost every tactical finding is borrowed from soccer and marked as such.
9. **One coach-facing question survives:** ***"What am I telling them, and what am I leaving them to solve?"*** Everything else stays internal. **Deep framework. Simple coaching.**

- STARTING POSITIONS is a **choice of problem**, not a change to it;
- PLAYER ROLES and INFORMATION describe **what the coach supplies**, which is better read by a lens;
- RULES is a **mechanism** (most rules implement other levers);
- CONSEQUENCES is **timing** (other levers switched on after an event).

## 3. The final Phase 1 architecture

```
CHOOSE THE PROBLEM
  GAME MOMENT — which situations players meet, how often, and how they arise
    (coach-supplied or emerging from play; including follow-up moments within
    an episode through continued play, reset, feed or manufactured change)

CHANGE THE PROBLEM
  SPACE · NUMBERS · TIME · DIRECTION / TARGET STRUCTURE · SCORING · ACTIONS
  (any of these may be event-contingent: applied only after a defined event —
   internal notation only)

CROSS-FRAMEWORK (internal framework logic)
  DECISION ALLOCATION — lens: which decisions (who / what / when / how) the coach supplies
  CF-1 v2 — inference limit for supplied decisions
  GI-2 + INFORMATION CHECK — inference limit and design check when the information a
    decision depends on is missing or replaced (only when recognition is the objective)

DEFERRED (not Phase 1; pending research)
  COACH INTERVENTION — instruction, calls, cues, questioning, demonstration / video /
    diagrams, opponent information, feedback, amount and timing of talk

OUTSIDE PHASE 1
  Sanctions / punishment · external rewards · competition formats and stakes ·
  broader motivation / discipline environment

REFERENCE
  The actual Gaelic game and its rules: what practice prepares for (not a lever)
```

**No symmetry was forced.** The layers are different kinds of thing: one choice, six properties, one reading lens with two inference limits, one deferred framework, one set of exclusions and one reference.

## 4. CHOOSE THE PROBLEM: GAME MOMENT

**Definition (frozen, with Candidate 10 clarification):**
> GAME MOMENT is the coach's choice of which situation of the game a practice episode begins from (such as a turnover, kickout, loose ball, or settled or unsettled defence) and how often each situation is practised. The situations players meet include those that follow an event within an episode; whether a follow-up moment arises through continued play, a reset, a coach feed or a deliberately manufactured change is part of that choice.

| # | Principle | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **GM-1** | Where players, opponents and the ball begin changes what happens in the first moments of play; those effects are described by SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION | SUPPORTED (acute) + taxonomy |
| **GM-2** | Coaches choose which game moments players practise and how often; this decides which problems players meet, not what they learn | SUPPORTED; learning UNKNOWN |
| **GM-3** | No evidence establishes how often each moment should be practised; frequency, importance, need, game model and opponent are inputs, not rules | SUPPORTED (evidence status) |
| **CO-2** | Whether play continues or resets after an event decides which follow-up moments players meet; which is right depends on the objective; experiencing what follows is not the same as learning from it | PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS; learning UNKNOWN |

**Evidence anchor:** in elite Gaelic football, about **half of possessions** originate from turnovers (match analysis). Transitions matter; **how to practise them is unknown**.

## 5. CHANGE THE PROBLEM: the six levers

Each lever: what it is, what it reliably does, what it does **not** show.

### 5.1 SPACE — size, shape and zones of the practice

- **Reliable (acute):** bigger areas → more running, less contact; smaller → more touches, tackles, scrambles (soccer; supported in hurling and elite Gaelic; **one sub-elite Gaelic study contradicts** the running pattern).
- **Key ideas:** area per player is a starting point, not the whole story; useful space is **relational** (reachable before it closes); rigid zones can reduce teammates' co-adaptation (one study).
- **Not shown:** that small spaces improve **decision quality** (more and quicker decisions ≠ better; one study points the other way); any "correct" Gaelic pitch size; any tactical or learning effect of space in Gaelic games.
- **Heuristics retained:** make a behaviour *useful* rather than *compulsory* (partially supported, untested head-to-head); avoid constraints that keep punishing a decision that would be right in a match (practitioner heuristic).

### 5.2 NUMBERS — who takes part, and overloads / underloads

| # | Principle | Status |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | Changing numbers changes involvement, intensity, team shape and which solutions work | SUPPORTED |
| N2 | Numbers are not space; area per player alone cannot describe the game | SUPPORTED / PARTIAL |
| N3 | Numerical advantage raises the chance of success; it does not guarantee it | SUPPORTED |
| N4 | Bigger overloads simplify and narrow the solutions players search for; no evidence-based "right" size | PARTIAL; no threshold |
| N5 | Floaters change which solutions are rewarded and have unrepresentative loads | PARTIAL |
| N6 | Behaviour produced by an overload is not evidence players can recognise, create, exploit or defend overloads at even numbers | SUPPORTED; transfer UNKNOWN |
| N7 | GIVEN / RECOGNISED / CREATED / EXPLOITED is an observation lens, not a learning sequence | PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS |
| N8 | Gaelic NUMBERS principles are borrowed unless marked GAA-supported | Evidence rule |

### 5.3 TIME — clocks, entry and recovery timing, restarts, windows

| # | Principle | Status |
|---|---|---|
| T1 | Usable time depends on opponents, space, numbers, support and capability; a clock sets only an upper limit | SUPPORTED |
| T2 | TIME is a family of manipulations *(touch limits now homed in ACTIONS, Candidate 08)* | SUPPORTED |
| T3 | Less time ≠ better perception; scanning falls under tight pressure; time limits worsened decisions acutely | SUPPORTED (acute) |
| T4 | No evidence that clocks, countdowns or touch limits improve decisions under natural pressure once removed | UNKNOWN |
| T5 | No universal transition window (3 / 5 / 6 / 8 / 10 s); successful Gaelic attacks are not universally rapid | SUPPORTED (negative) |
| T6 | Earlier information pickup is linked to better actions and can be increased in some players; "creating time" is coaching language | PARTIAL / heuristic |
| T7 | The same temporal constraint affects players differently | SUPPORTED; prescription UNKNOWN |
| T8 | Behaviour produced by a clock or touch rule is not evidence it survives removal | SUPPORTED |
| T9 | Timing emerges from player interaction; coaches shape it only indirectly | PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS |

### 5.4 DIRECTION / TARGET STRUCTURE — what is attacked and defended

| # | Principle | Status |
|---|---|---|
| DIR-1 | Changing the target structure changes the problem, not a single difficulty setting; keep-ball and target games are different problems | SUPPORTED (acute) |
| DIR-2 | A target gives the defence something to protect, and defending changes | PARTIAL (acute) |
| DIR-3 | The direction an action travels does not show whether it helped; progress is judged relative to the target and the defence's state | PARTIAL |
| DIR-4 | Behaviour produced by a directional structure is not evidence of learning | SUPPORTED (evidence status) |

*Forward-only, no-back-pass and must-switch rules are **ACTIONS** (Candidate 08); a coach call that switches targets is coach intervention with DIRECTION underneath (Candidate 09).*

### 5.5 SCORING — what outcomes are worth

| # | Principle | Status |
|---|---|---|
| SC-1 | Changing what outcomes are worth changes what players tend to attempt; size and direction vary | PARTIAL |
| SC-2 | More of a rewarded behaviour is not, by itself, evidence it has been learned | SUPPORTED (evidence status) |
| SC-3 | A higher-value option is not automatically better: value × probability, plus what follows | SUPPORTED (analysis) |
| SC-4 | Changing values can produce unintended behaviour, and opponents adapt | PARTIAL |

**Boundary (frozen, Candidate 10):** changing what an outcome is worth is SCORING; changing another property after the outcome is that property, event-contingently. "Only counts if" validity conditions are SCORING (the action stays legal; the score is worth zero).

### 5.6 ACTIONS — which actions players are allowed, banned or required to use

| # | Principle | Status |
|---|---|---|
| AC-1 | Changing which actions are allowed, banned or required changes what players do while it applies, often specifically and sometimes in unintended ways; touch limits change action structure and load, not proven decision speed | SUPPORTED (acute) |
| AC-2 | Behaviour under a required or banned action shows execution and coordination of what was left, not that players would choose it at the right moments without the rule; tactical learning after restrictions is unknown | SUPPORTED (evidence status) |
| AC-3 | Restricting actions is legitimate when the removed choice is not what the practice is for (technique repetitions, rehearsal, coordination, safety); non-dominant-side restriction improves technical test performance; game transfer unknown | SUPPORTED |
| AC-4 | Players respond to the game actually created, not necessarily the lesson intended; gaming shows what the practice rewards; stacking several manipulations makes causes harder to see | PARTIAL + logic |

## 6. The cross-framework layer

### 6.1 DECISION ALLOCATION (lens)

| # | Principle | Status |
|---|---|---|
| DA-1 | Any condition, rule or instruction can be read by which decisions it makes for players (who acts, what they do, when, how) and which it leaves them | PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS (built on Mosston's Spectrum and TGfU's what / how) |
| DA-2 | Giving a job mainly fixes WHO; relational and conditional jobs leave recognition, unconditional ones do not | SUPPORTED; learning UNKNOWN |
| DA-3 | Allocating responsibilities is legitimate when coordinated execution is the goal; it is not evidence players can self-allocate | SUPPORTED |

WHO / WHAT / WHEN / HOW is a **lens**, not a validated measurement model.

### 6.2 CF-1 v2 (inference limit for supplied decisions)

> When a coach supplies part of the solution (a position, an overload, a mandated action, a reward, a called moment or an assigned job), players' behaviour from it is not evidence that they can make the decisions that were supplied for them. Evidence about the decisions they still had to make is unaffected. This limits what can be inferred. It does not mean supplying conditions is poor practice. Where coordinated execution of a supplied job is the aim, supplying it is the practice.

**Status:** SUPPORTED as evidence status across every lever. Unchanged since Candidate 07; tested and **not amended** by Candidates 08, 09 and 10.

### 6.3 GI-1, GI-2 and the INFORMATION CHECK

| # | Principle | Status |
|---|---|---|
| GI-1 | Changing, removing or replacing what players can perceive changes the task, not just its difficulty; a practice can look like a match and still lack the relationship a decision depends on | SUPPORTED (acute) + synthesis |
| GI-2 | If recognising a moment is part of what a practice is for, the practice must contain what tells players it's on; when that is replaced by a coach signal or missing, success is not evidence of recognition (though it can show execution and coordination); when recognition is not the objective, replacing or omitting it can be exactly right | SUPPORTED (inference) + synthesis |

**INFORMATION CHECK (internal only; conditional):** *If recognising this is part of what the practice is for, is what tells a player it's on still in the practice, or has it been removed or replaced by a signal from me?*

**Why it exists:** the coach-facing question catches what the coach **tells**; it does not catch information that is simply **absent** (an unopposed pattern, a coach feed with no passer to read).

### 6.4 Event-contingent (internal notation)

> An existing practice manipulation that begins, ends or changes when a defined event occurs.

Examples: regain → six seconds (TIME); turnover → extra attacker (NUMBERS); score → targets reverse (DIRECTION); turnover → first pass forward (ACTIONS). **CO-1:** coach-created follow-ups work this way; what is new is only **when** they apply.

## 7. How to classify any manipulation (consolidated rules)

**Step 1 — Is it a manipulation of the practice at all?**
- Coach talk, calls, cues, questions, demonstrations, feedback → **COACH INTERVENTION** (deferred). If it replaces game information, apply **GI-2**.
- Sanctions, prizes, competition formats and stakes → **outside Phase 1**.
- The official game's own laws → **REFERENCE** (practising them is GAME MOMENT; removing them is a manipulation classified below).

**Step 2 — Is it a choice of which situation is met?** → **GAME MOMENT** (including coach-called or fed starts and follow-up moments).

**Step 3 — Otherwise classify by what its content specifies:**

| The manipulation specifies… | Primary home |
|---|---|
| **where** players or the ball may be (area, shape, zones, confinement, offside in practice) | SPACE |
| **how many / who** contests (overloads, floaters, eligibility such as "only the nearest may tackle") | NUMBERS |
| **how long / by when** (clocks, countdowns, entry delays) | TIME |
| **towards which target** (number, location, form, polarity of targets) | DIRECTION |
| **how much an outcome is worth**, including "only counts if" | SCORING |
| **which action** is allowed, banned or required (touch limits, no soloing, hand-pass limits, weak foot, must switch, no back pass, pass counts, no talking) | ACTIONS |

**Step 4 — Record secondary effects; do not home them twice.** If it applies only after an event, note it as **event-contingent**.

**Step 5 — Read it through DECISION ALLOCATION**, then apply **CF-1 v2** (supplied decisions) and **GI-2** (missing or replaced information) before inferring anything from what players did.

**Useful tests carried from the gates:**
- **Legal-alternative test** (Candidate 08): if the alternative stays legal, it is an incentive or instruction; if removed or compulsory, it is a rule, classified by content. Legal ≠ meaningful choice (a huge bonus can make a legal option pointless).
- **Kill test** (Candidates 08–10): a candidate lever survives only if two practices identical on every existing property can still differ materially on it.

## 8. The central evidence finding: behaviour now vs learning later

| Level | What Phase 1 found |
|---|---|
| **Behaviour during practice (acute)** | **Plentiful** across every lever: space, numbers, targets, scoring, touch limits and information changes all reliably change what players do, often in specific and sometimes unintended ways |
| **Behaviour straight after removing a condition** | **One reference-game programme** (artificial rules on touches, interactions and space vs free play; U14 / U16; four sessions; three papers from one programme): little average change in an unconstrained post-test, large individual variation |
| **Retention (delayed)** | Scanning frequency retained in elite youth players (not the success of actions); non-dominant-foot technical gains; point-light anticipation gains at one week (racket sport). **No tactical retention study** |
| **Transfer to competition** | **Essentially none** for tactical practice design. Perceptual training transfers partly to field tests (team-sport real-game effects about half the lab effects); competition evidence thin |
| **Gaelic learning evidence** | One Gaelic academy games-based intervention (U14 / U15) improved decision variables as a package; no lever-specific learning study |

**Consequences for every claim PáircVision makes:**
1. What players do in a practice shows **what the practice is producing**, not what they have learned (SPACE P9, N6, T8, DIR-4, SC-2, AC-2).
2. Supplied decisions, supplied information and supplied moments limit what can be inferred (CF-1 v2, GI-2).
3. **Transfer is UNKNOWN** for every tactical manipulation audited.
4. Technical restriction (weak foot) has the clearest learning evidence, on **tests**, not games.

## 9. Recurring findings across all ten audits

1. **Produced ≠ learned.** Appears independently in every lever.
2. **Supplied ≠ recognised ≠ created.** Overloads, positions, calls, jobs, mandates, bonuses: the same inference limit every time (CF-1 v2).
3. **More and faster ≠ better.** More decisions, more scans, more passes, more transitions and more repetitions are not better decisions or more learning.
4. **No magic numbers.** No evidence-based pitch size, overload size, transition window, feedback frequency, constraint count or practice frequency.
5. **Interactions, not additions.** Levers interact (touch × numbers; area × numbers; direction × area); stacking obscures causes.
6. **Large individual differences.** The same condition affects players differently; average effects hide it.
7. **Supplying is not bad coaching.** Telling, placing, calling, restricting and rehearsing are legitimate when the supplied decision is not today's objective (CF-1 v2 clause; DA-3; AC-3; GI-2; CO-2).
8. **Simple binaries fail.** Novice / expert, youth / senior, game / drill, continuous / reset, ask / tell, realistic / artificial: none survived as a rule.
9. **Practitioner wisdom is often sophisticated and often untested.** Good practitioners already separate scanning timing from frequency, triggers from calls, transitions from sanctions. Folklore ("two-touch makes you think faster", "scan more", "ask don't tell") does not survive.
10. **Several famous motor-learning rules shrank** after bias correction (external focus, self-controlled practice, reduced feedback frequency).
11. **Gaelic evidence is thin and mostly descriptive.** Match analysis and physical load, a handful of coaching studies, no cue or transfer studies.

## 10. Coach-facing layer

**The only frozen coach-facing question:**
> ***What am I telling them, and what am I leaving them to solve?***

It covers instructions, jobs, rules, set-ups, bonuses and calls: anything that tells players part of the answer. An experienced coach can unpack it into who / what / when / how.

**What stays internal (never coach-facing terminology):** lever names as a menu, DECISION ALLOCATION, CF-1 v2, GI-2, INFORMATION CHECK, event-contingent, legal-alternative test, precedence rule, action allowances, specifying information, substitution, state transitions.

**Candidate guidance wording recorded by the gates (not frozen; not universal):**
- *"When you see them doing it in the practice game, the game is producing it. It doesn't yet tell you they've learned it."* (SPACE)
- *"Is today about getting better at doing it, or getting better at choosing it?"* (ACTIONS; explanatory only)
- *"If you want them to spot it, make sure the thing they'd spot is actually there."* (INFORMATION)
- *"If what happens next is part of what you're working on, let players experience it. If it isn't, reset and work on what matters."* (CONSEQUENCES)
- *"Worth more isn't better."* (SCORING)

**Deep framework. Simple coaching.**

| Hypothesis | Where | Status |
|---|---|---|

## 12. Consolidated must-not-claim (Phase 1)

*Each gate holds its full list. These are the cross-cutting ones.*

**Learning and transfer**
1. ❌ "This practice teaches X" or "players learned X" because X appeared in the practice.
2. ❌ "Conditioned games / representative practice / realistic consequences guarantee transfer."
3. ❌ "Removing a condition and seeing the behaviour continue proves learning."
4. ❌ "A drop in behaviour after removal means the practice failed."
5. ❌ "More repetitions / transitions / scans / decisions mean more learning."

**Specific folklore**
6. ❌ "Small spaces improve decision-making."
7. ❌ "Two-touch makes players think faster."
8. ❌ "No-back-pass teaches penetration" / "zones teach width" / "pass counts teach patience" / "forced overlaps teach support."
9. ❌ "More scanning means better decisions" / "scanning creates time."
10. ❌ "One body cue explains expert anticipation" ("watch the hips").
11. ❌ "Video anticipation training automatically transfers to matches."
12. ❌ Any magic number: pitch size, overload size, transition window, constraint count.

**Coaching style**
13. ❌ "Questions are better than telling" / "discovery beats instruction" / "coach instruction harms learning."
14. ❌ "Supplied information, calls or restrictions are inherently bad coaching."
15. ❌ "Continuous play is better than resetting" / "stopping play harms learning."
16. ❌ "Novices need telling; experts need discovery" (and other age / level binaries).
17. ❌ "Game-like appearance means representative information."

**Sanctions and rewards**
18. ❌ "Punishment teaches accountability" / "running after errors improves learning."
19. ❌ "External rewards improve learning" / "winner-stays-on improves competitiveness."

**Evidence integrity**
20. ❌ Any Gaelic-specific tactical, perceptual or learning claim not marked as borrowed from another code.
21. ❌ "60 %+ of Gaelic scores come from turnovers" (unverified), or any rule-effect claim on the 2025–26 Gaelic rules (bundled changes; wording unverified).
22. ❌ Any Kinnerk attribution beyond what journalists report (exaggeration via conditions; heavy questioning).
23. ❌ Presenting PáircVision terms (DECISION ALLOCATION, CF-1, GI-2, ACTIONS, event-contingent, GAME MOMENT) as established scientific categories.
24. ❌ "External focus / self-controlled practice / reduced feedback reliably improve learning" (bias-corrected effects negligible or small).

## 13. Deferred and excluded

### 13.1 COACH INTERVENTION (deferred; pending research)

Carried research agenda (from Candidates 08–10):
1. Telling vs questioning by objective, learner and time; question quality (closed / leading; guessing without principles).
2. Live calls and "joysticking": what players come to rely on.
3. Feedback: frequency, timing, concurrent vs terminal, bandwidth (classic effects weaker than claimed).
4. Attentional focus and self-controlled practice (after bias correction).
5. Demonstration, video and diagrams: format × expertise; recall vs play.
6. Opponent-tendency information: help for experienced players; bias risk.
7. Pointing to information vs supplying the answer; guided discovery; explicit rules under pressure.
8. Scanning coaching: timing and content rather than frequency.
9. Perceptual training tools (occlusion, point-light, VR, strobe): test vs field vs competition.
10. Freeze / replay and stop-to-coach: when and how often.

### 13.2 Outside Phase 1

Sanctions and punishment; external rewards; competition formats and stakes (research debt from SCORING); broader motivation and discipline environment. Any **practice** effect they cause (who plays, next episode, load) is classified by existing properties.

---

## 14. Open questions for Phase 2

**Evidence gaps (no study exists anywhere we could reach):**
1. Any invasion-game manipulation isolated, removed and tested for **delayed retention and competition transfer**.
2. **Decision quality** (not frequency) as an outcome over weeks.
3. Head-to-head comparisons: useful vs compulsory; event-contingent vs continuous; continue vs reset; natural vs coach-called moments; asking vs telling in isolation.
4. **Gaelic-specific** tactical and perceptual studies: cues players read at kickouts, turnovers and presses; transition training; spatial effects on tactics; evaluation of the 2025–26 rules.
5. Whether training with substitute signals impairs later recognition.
6. Children: whether scaled formats and restrictions improve long-term development, not just involvement.

**Verification debts:**
7. Full-text verification of every AO / SIO source relied on for frozen principles.
8. 2025–26 Gaelic rule wording against the Official Guide.
9. Kinnerk's primary sources (2016 talk; TGfU.info interview; 2017 GAA slides).

**Framework debts:**
10. COACH INTERVENTION framework (§13.1).
11. Competition structure and stakes as a practice property (SCORING research debt).
12. Validated, low-cost indicators a coach could use to look for transfer without tracking data.

**Governance rules:**
1. **Final gates govern** their audits.
2. Later gates' **"Later taxonomy clarification (Candidate NN)"** notes govern **classification** in earlier gates; they never change earlier **evidence statuses**.
3. This synthesis **summarises**; where it differs from a gate, **the gate governs**.
4. A frozen item changes only through a new gate that states what it supersedes and why.
5. Evidence statuses are not upgraded because two audits agree, because a claim is repeated, or because practitioners believe it.

## 16. Implications for what comes next (flags only; no product design)

- **The framework is internal logic.** Any product built on it should expose the single coach-facing question and plain-language guidance, not the taxonomy (§10).
- **Claims discipline is part of the framework.** The must-not-claim lists (§12 and each gate) are constraints on any coach-facing wording, including Tactical Slate content, summaries and insights.
- **"Produced ≠ learned" must survive into any observation or feedback feature.** Observing a behaviour in a conditioned game is evidence about the game, not about learning.
- **Gaelic labelling.** Wherever a statement rests on soccer or other codes, it must say so.

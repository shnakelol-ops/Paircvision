## Executive verdict

**D — INFORMATION dissolves as a Phase 1 practice-design family.**

- **What players can perceive in a practice is almost always a consequence** of GAME MOMENT, SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION, SCORING and ACTIONS. Naming it again would double-count.
- **The genuine perceptual residue** (occlusion, screens, masking, hidden scoreboards) is **scientifically real but architecturally marginal** for a grassroots Gaelic coaching framework. It is recorded, not named.
- **Coach-supplied information** (instructions, calls, cues, questions, demonstrations, diagrams, opponent information) and **feedback** leave Phase 1 for a later **COACH INTERVENTION** framework.
- **Candidate 09's unique contribution survives** as one frozen principle and one internal check:
  - **the missing / substituted information problem** — a decision can be left to players while the information it depends on is absent or replaced by a coach signal.
- **CF-1 v2: no amendment.** A companion principle (GI-2) covers absent information.
- **No new coach-facing question.**
- **Frozen principles: GI-1, GI-2.** Scanning, questioning and telling are **not** frozen here.

## Resolution of the scientific / practitioner disagreement

| Issue | Scientific audit (C) | Practitioner audit (D) | Gate decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does a perceptual residue exist? | Yes | Rare in practice | **Yes, it exists** (§1, §3) |
| Does it earn a named property? | Possibly | No | **No.** Real but marginal; recorded under the internal check (§3) |
| Coach-supplied information | Separate family in Phase 1 | Move to intervention | **Move to COACH INTERVENTION (deferred)** (§5) |
| Feedback | Flag to split | Intervention | **Intervention** (§6) |
| Substitution / missing information | Synthesis; design check | Design check | **Frozen as principle GI-2 + internal check** (§7–9) |

**Why D wins:** the ACTIONS kill test was passed because a whole family of ordinary coaching manipulations (touch limits, no soloing, hand-pass limits) changed the problem and no lever described them. The INFORMATION kill test (§1) finds only **rare** manipulations that no lever describes; everything common is already described. The scientific audit was right that the residue is real; the practitioner audit was right that it does not earn a place in the framework.

## 1. Kill test

**Question:** can two practices match in GAME MOMENT, SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION, SCORING and ACTIONS but differ materially in what players can perceive?

| Case | Differs only in information? | Already caused by… | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Visual occlusion** (occlusion glasses / strobe during play) | **Yes** | — | **Genuine residue.** Rare; mostly a lab / specialist tool |
| **Screen blocking** (a barrier hiding the passer until release) | **Yes** | — | **Genuine residue.** Rare |
| **Hidden vs visible scoreboard** | **Yes** | — | **Genuine residue.** Minor; handled as a note (§16) |
| **Lights / colours replacing opponent cues** | Usually **no** | Opponent removed (**NUMBERS**) or a coach signal added (**coach help**) | Not information as a property |
| **Coach-called vs natural turnover** | **No** | **GAME MOMENT** (how the moment arises) + a coach signal | Already covered (GAME MOMENT gate) |
| **Bowling machine vs bowler** (invasion analogue: coach feed vs opponent pass) | **No** | Who delivers the ball: **NUMBERS** / **GAME MOMENT** | Consequence |
| **Video vs live opponent** | **No** | A different activity (off-pitch tool), not a practice-game setting | Outside Phase 1 practice design |
| **Silent game** | **No** | **ACTIONS** (speaking banned) | Consequence (loss of player talk) |
| **Unopposed vs opposed pattern** | **No** | **NUMBERS** (defenders absent) | Consequence |

**Result.**
- The residue is **real**: occlusion, screens and hidden state change perception with everything else equal.
- It is **rare** in the intended framework (grassroots and club Gaelic practice).
- **A rare laboratory manipulation does not earn a grassroots category.** INFORMATION **fails** the kill test as a named property.

## 2. Property vs consequence

| Manipulation | Framework property changed | Information consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Add a defender | NUMBERS | New pressure, new cues to read |
| Move the defender's start | GAME MOMENT / SPACE | Different angles and orientations visible |
| Ban talking | ACTIONS | Teammate calls removed |
| Change the target | DIRECTION | Different relationships become relevant |
| Add a countdown | TIME | A clock replaces or adds to game-state cues |
| Coach calls "now" | (Coach help) | The coach's signal stands in for the game's |

**Decision:** calling these INFORMATION **double-counts**. Game information is a **consequence** of how the practice is designed, and of what the coach adds. It is **not** another independent thing the coach sets.

**Exception kept visible:** when a consequence is **unintended**, e.g., removing defenders to simplify a pattern also removes what the decision depends on. That is why an internal check survives (§8–9).

## 3. Perceptual residue: strongest case, then decision

| Manipulation | Scientific validity | Common in Gaelic / invasion practice? | Belongs elsewhere? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Temporal occlusion (video) | **Strong** (meta-analysis, large test effects) | No (off-pitch tool) | Video training: outside practice-game design |
| Spatial occlusion | Moderate (research method) | No | — |
| Occlusion / strobe glasses in play | Near transfer only | Rare | — |
| Masking / point-light displays | Promising (badminton novices) | No | Off-pitch tool |
| Altered perspective (first-person video, VR) | Growing | Rare | Off-pitch tool |
| Screens / blockers on the pitch | Plausible | Rare | — |
| Augmented perceptual cueing (highlight colours) | **Weak** (colour cueing ≈ control in one study) | Rare | Coach help |

**Decision.**
- **Scientific validity: accepted** for several of these as **training tools**.
- **Architectural usefulness: low.** They are either off-pitch tools (video, VR, point-light) or rare specialist devices.
- **Recorded as "perceptual manipulations"** in the internal check (§9) and in deferred research (§28). **Not a named property.**

## 4. Game information: final status

**Status: an internal practice-design consideration, not a category.**

**Theoretically safer wording.** Saying "the game provides information" commits PáircVision to a direct-perception reading contested between ecological and cognitive traditions. PáircVision uses neutral wording:

> **What players can see, hear and feel in the practice, and the relationships among players, ball, space, targets and score that tell them an option is on or off.**

- This wording is compatible with ecological and cognitive accounts.
- It **describes**; it does not claim how perception works.
- Short internal form: **"what tells a player it's on"**.

## 5. Coach-supplied information

**Proposed distinction:** *Practice design shapes the problem players encounter. Coach intervention shapes how the coach helps players engage with that problem.*

| Stress test | Result |
|---|---|
| Does a coach call ever change the **problem**? | **Yes**: a whistle that starts the press removes the recognition part. The distinction holds only if that effect is **caught** on the problem side (GI-2, §8) |
| Are some intervention acts planned into the practice (e.g., "I will call each transition")? | **Yes.** Planned or not, the act is still the coach helping; its **effect** on the problem is caught by the check |
| Are instructions before practice (plans, triggers as team rules) intervention or design? | **Intervention** (what the coach tells), read by DECISION ALLOCATION |
| Opponent information (tendencies) | **Intervention** (supplied knowledge) |
| Demonstrations, diagrams, video | **Intervention** |

**Decision:** **instructions, calls, cues, questions, demonstrations, diagrams and opponent information leave Phase 1** for a later **COACH INTERVENTION** framework.

**Guard against tidiness:** the distinction is **not** watertight. A coach's signal can change the problem. That overlap is handled by **GI-2** and the **internal check**, not by keeping coach information in Phase 1.

## 6. Feedback

- **Decision:** feedback (information about performance or an action) belongs to **COACH INTERVENTION**, not the practice-design taxonomy.
- **Concurrent feedback** (during the action) is still **about** the action. If it tells players **what to do next** ("switch now!"), it functions as a **call**: intervention, with its problem-side effect caught by GI-2.
- No further feedback research here. The evidence status (guidance hypothesis weak; reduced frequency marginal) is recorded in the scientific audit and carried to deferred research.

## 7. Information substitution

**Pattern:** game signal → coach / practice signal (opponent body position → whistle; natural turnover → coach call; opponent / teammate movement → coloured light; pressure → verbal "go").

| Option | Decision |
|---|---|
| Frozen terminology | **No.** Not an established term; not coach-facing |
| Internal design check | **Yes**, part of the check (§9) |
| PáircVision hypothesis | **Its learning consequence is.** Whether training on a substitute signal impairs later recognition: **UNKNOWN** |
| Discard | **No.** The inference point is supported and important |

**What is frozen:** the **inference** (success after a substitute signal is not evidence of recognising the game signal), inside **GI-2**. **Not frozen:** any claim that substitution **harms learning**.

## 8. Missing information

**The gap.** *"What am I telling them, and what am I leaving them to solve?"* catches **supplied** answers. It does not catch a decision nominally **left** to players whose information is **absent**:
- an unopposed attacking pattern: players "choose" the run, but there is no defender whose movement would make one run better than another;
- a rondo with little visual richness (practitioner concern);
- a coach-fed ball where the passer's cues are missing.

In these cases **the coach has told them nothing**, yet players' behaviour cannot show they would recognise the moment.

**Decision:** yes, the framework needs this. It is Candidate 09's **unique contribution** and is frozen as part of **GI-2**, with an **internal check** (§9).

**CF-1 v2 relation:** CF-1 v2 covers decisions **supplied**. GI-2 covers decisions **left open without the information they need**. Companion principles; CF-1 v2 is **not amended**.

## 9. The internal check

**Candidate:** *"What in the game tells a player this is on, and is that still in my practice?"*

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Support | **Useful**: is there a carrier under real pressure whose situation signals where support is needed? |
| Width | **Useful if width should be read**; irrelevant if width is a supplied shape |
| Pressing | **Useful**: exposes whistles and calls |
| Shooting | **Useful**: goalkeeper position and closing defender |
| Transition | **Useful**: called vs natural turnover |
| Defensive cover | **Useful**: is there an attacker whose movement signals cover is needed? |
| Overload | **Useful**: does the overload emerge and have to be spotted, or is it announced? |
| Kickouts | **Useful**: opposition set-up and runner movement vs a nominated target |
| Technical kicking | **Irrelevant** |
| Set-piece rehearsal | **Misleading** if read as "must keep it": omission is deliberate |
| Unopposed tactical walkthrough | **Misleading** in the same way: the walkthrough's purpose is the plan |
| Opposed game | **Mostly satisfied** by construction; still useful for coach-called moments inside the game |

**Decision: concept survives; wording changes.** The unconditional wording misleads for technique, rehearsal and walkthroughs. Final internal wording:

> **INFORMATION CHECK (internal):** *If recognising this is part of what the practice is for, is what tells a player it's on still in the practice, or has it been removed or replaced by a signal from me?*

- **Internal framework logic only.** Not a coach-facing question. Not mandatory.
- Its conditional clause ("if recognising this is part of what the practice is for") is essential.
- Coach-facing, the frozen question remains the **only** question.

## 10. Recognition objective

**Formulation tested:** *if the learning objective includes recognising when a solution is appropriate, the practice may need to preserve the relevant information. If the objective is execution, technical repetition, coordination, a tactical walkthrough or safety, preserving the full recognition problem may be unnecessary.*

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Consistent with the evidence? | **Yes**: task changes when information changes (GI-1); CF-1 v2; ACTIONS AC-3 (removing a choice is legitimate when it's not the objective) |
| Does it imply "representative is always better"? | **No**: it is conditional on the objective |
| Does "preserve" mean full match realism? | **No**: only the **relationship** the decision depends on (§19) |
| Evidence that preserving it **improves learning**? | **None direct. UNKNOWN** |

**Decision:** adopted **inside GI-2**, as a statement of what a practice can and cannot show, **not** as a claim about learning.

## 11. Pressing stress test

| | Game / context information present | Coach help | Decisions supplied | Decisions remaining | Recognition required? | What success shows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Coach calls PRESS** | Present | Live call | WHAT, WHEN (+ WHO) | HOW, cover | **No** | Execution and coordination of the press |
| **B. Whistle means press** | Present but **not needed** | Signal | WHEN | WHO, HOW | **No** | Execution; who goes; coordination |
| **C. Predetermined trigger (team rule)** | Present and **needed** | Criterion taught in advance | WHAT + criterion | **Detecting** the situation; WHO; HOW | **Partly** (detect, not discover) | That players can spot a **taught** situation and act on it |
| **D. Coach directs attention** ("watch when he can't see forward") | Present and needed | Attention target | Where to look | Interpretation; WHETHER / WHEN / HOW | **Mostly** | Recognition with a pointer |
| **E. Player calls press** | Present | A teammate's call | For others: WHEN (+ WHAT) | Caller: recognition; others: HOW | **Caller yes; others no** | The caller's recognition; the team's response to a call |
| **F. Players identify it unprompted** | Present and needed | None | None | Everything | **Yes** | Recognition, decision and execution together |

**Why different versions suit different objectives (not ranked):**
- **A / B** when the objective is the **press itself** (shape, angles, cover) or early installation.
- **C** when a **team rule** is the objective and players must learn to spot it.
- **D** when recognition is the objective and players don't yet know where to look.
- **E** when **communication and coordination** are the objective.
- **F** when **full recognition** is the objective or to check what players have learned.

## 12. "Trigger"

**Decision:** PáircVision **does not use "trigger" unqualified** internally. Use three separate terms:

| Internal term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Cue** (game situation) | What in the game signals the opportunity (e.g., receiver facing own goal) |
| **Team rule** | What the team has agreed to do when it sees that situation |
| **Call / signal** | Whoever or whatever says "now" (coach, whistle, teammate) |

- **Coach-facing:** no new terminology. Coaches will keep saying "trigger"; that is fine.
- **Guardrail:** "cue" here means a **situation or relationship**, never a single body part by default (§15).

## 13. Scanning

- **Preserved:** *scanning frequency is not equivalent to useful information pickup or to good decisions.*
- **Already frozen in substance** at the TIME gate (T6: earlier information pickup is associated with better actions; increasing scanning has not been shown to cause better play).
- **Decision: not frozen as a Candidate 09 principle.** Recorded in the must-not-claim list and deferred to **concept-specific coaching research**. PáircVision is **not** a scanning methodology.

---

## 14. Questions vs telling

- **Evidence:** no questioning-specific advantage; bundled GBA effects; explicit instruction speeds early learning in lab tasks; one study shows fragility under anxiety.
- **Practitioner:** both used, by purpose; open questions without shared principles cause guessing; players can dislike huddles.
- **Decision: defer to COACH INTERVENTION.** No Phase 1 principle.
  - Phase 1 already says what matters for design: DECISION ALLOCATION reads what any question or instruction supplies; CF-1 v2 limits inference.
  - Two must-not-claims are recorded now ("questions are better than telling"; "telling prevents learning").

---

## 15. "Read the game"

**Internal meaning (if used):** *recognising the relationships in the unfolding game that make an option on or off, and acting on them.*

**Guardrails:**
- There is **not always one cue**; relationships and context usually matter.
- Perception need **not be conscious** or verbalisable.
- Recognition and action are **not** clean serial stages.

**Coach-facing decision:** "read the game" is **everyday language coaches already use and may keep**. PáircVision should **not** use it as framework terminology or as an instruction without context ("read which part: where the space is? who's free? when he's turned?").

## 19. Looks like a match vs contains the decision's information

| Case | Looks like a match? | Contains the relationship the decision depends on? |
|---|---|---|
| 15-a-side unopposed pattern | **Yes** | **No** (no defenders to read) |
| 3v2 with a live defender deciding when to engage | **No** | **Yes** |
| Full game with coach calling every press | **Yes** | Present, but **replaced** as the signal |
| Ball machine at match speed | Partly | **No** (passer's action absent) |

- **Evidence status:** that changing the information source changes coordination and gaze is **SUPPORTED (acute)**. That preserving the relationship **improves learning** is **UNKNOWN**.
- **No universal measure of representativeness** is claimed. The question is **which relationship the decision depends on**, not how match-like the practice looks.
- Folded into **GI-1**.

## 27. Final taxonomy verdict

### **D — INFORMATION dissolves. Game information becomes an internal design consideration; coach-supplied information and feedback move to COACH INTERVENTION (deferred).**

**Why not A:** one family would mix the problem and the help.
**Why not B:** game information is a **consequence** of existing properties (§2); naming it double-counts.
**Why not C:** the perceptual residue is real but rare and mostly off-pitch (§3). Scientific validity ≠ architectural usefulness.
**Why not E:** no new architecture needed; the check fits the cross-framework layer.
**Why not F:** both audits' evidence converges on the structure; only the residue's naming was disputed, and it is settled here.

## 29. Deferred COACH INTERVENTION research

Carried forward, not resolved here:
1. Telling vs questioning by objective, learner and time; question quality (closed / leading; guessing without principles).

4. Attentional-focus and self-controlled practice claims (negligible after bias correction).

7. Pointing to information vs supplying the answer; guided discovery; explicit rules and pressure.
8. Scanning coaching: timing and content rather than frequency.
9. Perceptual training tools (occlusion video, point-light, VR, strobe): test vs field vs competition transfer.

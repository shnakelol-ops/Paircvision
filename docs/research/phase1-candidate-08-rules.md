> ### ⚠️ ACCESS LIMITATION
> - No full text was accessible: publishers, PMC and GAA / FIFA documents were refused by the proxy.
> - All claims rest on **abstracts (AO)** or **search-index summaries (SIO)**.
> - **UNR** = unresolved detail.

> **Central question.** What does changing the rules of a practice actually change for players? When does a rule expose a useful game problem, and when does it simply force the behaviour the coach wants to see?
>
> **Second question.** Once SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION, SCORING, GAME MOMENT and instruction-like content are separated out, is RULES still a meaningful independent category?
>
> **Governing lens (Candidate 07):** *"What am I telling them, and what am I leaving them to solve?"*
>
> **CF-1 v2:** *behaviour from a supplied condition is not evidence players can make the decisions that condition made for them. Evidence about decisions still available to them is unaffected.*
>
> **Chain kept separate:** RULE ADDED → BEHAVIOUR WHILE RULE EXISTS → BEHAVIOUR AFTER REMOVAL → RETENTION → TRANSFER.
>
> **Nuance preserved.** Forcing an action ≠ understanding when to choose it. But forcing an action is **not automatically poor coaching**. A coach may remove decisions deliberately:
> - to increase repetitions;
> - to coordinate a team;
> - to simplify a problem;
> - to ensure safety;
> - to focus attention elsewhere.

---

## 0. Honest headline

1. **Rules reliably change immediate behaviour** (touch limits, pass requirements, positional zones, offside, participation requirements). The effects are acute and often rule-specific.
2. **The one reference-game study** (Gonzalez-Artetxe et al., 2022 / 2023 / 2025; artificial rules on **touches, player interaction and use of space** vs free play) found **little average change** in an unconstrained post-test, with **large individual differences**.
   - This also resolves the SCORING gate's research debt: those rules were **action / space rules, not scoring rules**.
3. **Technique restrictions** (non-dominant-foot practice) have **learning evidence on technical tests**, not game transfer. This is the clearest case of a rule that deliberately removes a decision for a non-tactical purpose, with measured benefit.
4. **Tactical learning or transfer from rules** (forward-only, must-switch, zones, pass counts): **no studies found. UNKNOWN.**
5. **Most "rules" are the mechanism by which a coach manipulates an existing lever.** One residue survives: rules that change **which actions are available or required**.

## 1. Defining "rule"

**Starting candidate:** *a RULE changes which actions or states are permitted, required or prohibited during the practice.*

| Form | Example | Classification test |
|---|---|---|
| MUST | "Must switch before scoring" | Requirement → RULE |
| MAY | "You may use the hand pass" | Permission (often default) |
| MAY ONLY | "May only kick" | Restriction → RULE |
| CANNOT | "Cannot pass backwards" | Prohibition → RULE |
| BEFORE X, Y MUST happen | "Three passes before a shot" | Sequence requirement → RULE |
| AFTER X, Y MUST happen | "After a turnover, attack within 6 s" | Sequence / time requirement → RULE (TIME effect) |
| X allowed only if Y | "Goal counts only after a wide entry" | Validity rule → RULE (SCORING-looking; SCORING gate §4) |

**Distinguished from:**

| Term | Why it is not (primarily) a rule |
|---|---|
| Sport rules | The competition's laws. **The reference game**, not a manipulation, unless modified |
| Task constraint (ecological term) | Umbrella term: rules, equipment, goals, space, numbers are all "task constraints" (Newell; Davids et al.). **Broader than RULES** |
| Tactical instruction | Leaves alternatives legal (INFORMATION; Candidate 07) |
| Responsibility | Who is accountable; alternatives legal (Candidate 07) |
| Scoring condition | Changes value, not legality (SCORING), unless it zeroes an outcome (then a validity rule) |
| Consequence | What happens next (CONSEQUENCES, unaudited) |
| Coach cue | INFORMATION |

**Literature terminology is inconsistent.**
- SSG research calls almost any modification a "rule change": touch limits, pass counts, number of players, scoring modes, goal types.
- The basketball rule-manipulation scoping review (*Children* 2023; 18 studies) counts **number of players** (66.7 %) and **court dimensions** (27.8 %) as "rule manipulations".
- **PáircVision will not adopt the literature's broad usage.** It keeps the narrow legality definition above.

## 2. The legal-alternative test

**Inherited (Candidate 07 §9):** if the alternative remains legal → instruction / responsibility / incentive. If it is removed, prohibited or compulsory → RULE.

| Pair | Legal alternative? | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| "Try to play forward." vs "You cannot pass backwards." | Yes vs no | INFORMATION vs RULE |
| "Nearest player presses." vs "Only the nearest player may challenge." | Yes vs no | Responsibility vs RULE |
| "Double points for a switch." vs "You must switch before scoring." | Yes vs no (for scoring) | SCORING vs RULE |

**Robust?**
- **Robust as a classification test**: legality is binary and checkable.
- **Not robust as a behavioural distinction.** A huge bonus, heavy social pressure or a severe penalty can make a legal alternative **functionally unavailable** (SCORING gate §6: legal ≠ meaningful choice).
- **Verdict:** a **useful PáircVision classification shorthand** (PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS). **Not** evidence that rules and incentives have different learning effects. That remains **UNKNOWN**.

## 3. Rules are not their effects

"Stay in your channel" is operationally a RULE. Its effect is **spatial** (occupied space, width).

**Taxonomy decision (PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS):** classify at **two levels**.
- **Mechanism:** *how* the coach changes the practice (rule / instruction / equipment / scoring / set-up).
- **Game property changed:** *what* changes in the problem (space, numbers, time, direction, value, available actions, information).

Frozen levers (SPACE–SCORING) describe **game properties**. RULES describes a **mechanism**. That mechanism can change several game properties, **plus one property no other lever covers: the set of actions available** (§4, §33).

## 4. Rule taxonomy

| Candidate family | Examples | Primary game property changed | Implementation of an existing lever? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Action restrictions | No kicking; no dribbling; no hand pass; weak foot only; no tackle | **Available actions** | **No: residue** |
| Action requirements | Must overlap; must switch; must use target player | **Available actions** (mandated) + decision allocation (WHAT fixed) | **No: residue** |
| Sequence rules | N passes before a shot; shot within X after a regain | Available actions in order; TIME for timed ones | Partly TIME |
| Possession requirements | Everyone must touch before a score | Available actions; NUMBERS involvement | Partly |
| Touch / contact restrictions | 1-touch, 2-touch; no contact | **Available actions** (TIME as a secondary effect; TIME gate) | **No: residue** (TIME gate assigned the primary home to RULES) |
| Movement restrictions | Cannot cross halfway; must stay in zone | SPACE (occupied / accessible space) | **Yes: SPACE** |
| Zone / channel rules | Minimum players per zone; wide channel only for wingers | SPACE (× NUMBERS locally) | **Yes: SPACE / NUMBERS** |
| Player-eligibility rules | Floater can't score; only the nearest may tackle; keeper can't receive back-pass | NUMBERS (effective participants) or available actions for specific players | Mixed |
| Transition rules | Direction reverses on turnover; ball must go back to a zone before attacking | DIRECTION polarity / GAME MOMENT / SPACE | **Yes** |
| Scoring-eligibility rules | Goal counts only after wide entry | Validity of scoring (SCORING gate §4: validity = RULES) | Boundary |
| Restart rules | Kickout beyond the 40 m arc; puckouts across the 45 | SPACE × GAME MOMENT (sport-authentic in Gaelic) | Yes (sport rule) |
| Temporal rules | Possession clock; attack within 6 s | TIME (frozen: countdowns) | **Yes: TIME** |
| Numerical participation rules | Three-per-half; minimum attackers | NUMBERS (× SPACE) | **Yes: NUMBERS** |

**Finding.** Seven or eight families are **implementations of existing levers**. The **residue** is rules whose primary effect is to change **which actions (techniques, contacts, action types, action sequences) are available or required**.

## 5. Touch restrictions (priority)

| Evidence (access) | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Systematic review, touch restriction in SSGs** (*Front Sports Act Living* 2025, doi:10.3389/fspor.2025.1705921; AO; authors UNR) | 1- and 2-touch ↑ intensity, RPE, sprinting / high-intensity running. ↑ passes, with **both more successful and more unsuccessful passes**. Free play ↑ ball touches. **Tactical evidence "very scarce", results "indifferent"** |
| **Rosten, Aune & Dalen (2025)**, *J Hum Kinet* (AO) | Junior players, n = 12; 4v4, max-2 vs max-3 vs free. Restrictions ↑ passes; **free play ↑ receptions**. Touch intervals shortest in the offensive zone |
| **Jäger, Rumpf, Barrett, Meixner & Lochmann (2026)**, *IJSSC* (AO) | Touch restriction × player numbers interaction on physical and technical parameters |
| **Birinci et al. (2026)** (TIME gate) | 2-touch ↑ Stroop performance and passing. **Stroop ≠ game decision** |
| **JSMPF (2025)** (TIME gate) | Mixed technical success |
| **Limited-touch 6-week RCT** (*JSSM* 2025; n = 47) (TIME gate) | **Fitness** gains only (anaerobic / sprint) |
| **Scanning** | **No study** links touch limits to scanning |
| **Enjoyment / cognitive load** | Pass-participation restriction ↑ mental fatigue and RPE (García-Calvo et al., 2021; AO). Touch-limit enjoyment: UNR |

**Belongs primarily to RULES, with TIME as a secondary effect.**
- **Confirmed.** It removes carries, holds and fixing actions. Time on the ball shortens indirectly and depends on space and density (TIME gate §5).

**Attack: "Two-touch makes players think faster."**
- **UNSUPPORTED.**
- Evidence shows more passes, more errors in some studies, and more physical intensity.
- The one "cognitive" gain (Stroop) is a lab test, not game decision speed.
- No study measures decision speed in games or transfer after removal.

## 6. Forward-only / no-back-pass rules

- **Searched:** forward-only passing; no-back-pass; mandatory forward play; recycling restrictions; progression conditions.
- **Result: no study located** that manipulates forward-only or no-back-pass rules and measures behaviour, let alone learning (confirms the DIRECTION gate).
- **Logic (Decision Allocation):**
  - The rule fixes the **WHAT** set (removes backward options).
  - It leaves WHEN / HOW of forward actions.
  - It removes the decision "is going backwards better here?" (DIRECTION DIR-3: backward ≠ negative).
- **Does forcing forward action teach when forward action is appropriate?** **UNKNOWN**; logically doubtful (CF-1 v2).
- **Unintended consequences** (logical; unmeasured):
  - forced low-percentage forward balls;
  - loss of the recycle that disorganises a press.

## 7. Width / zone / channel rules

| Evidence | Finding |
|---|---|
| Mandatory positional zones vs free positioning (Gonçalves et al., 2017; SPACE gate) | Zones **impaired teammate co-adaptation** and reduced physical output |
| Formations change collective shape (González-Rodenas 2024; Candidate 07) | Structural shape effects (acute) |
| "Wide pitch = wide play" | Unsafe (SPACE gate) |
| Forced occupation teaches width recognition | **No study** |

**Split:**
- What belongs to RULES: the prohibition or requirement ("must stay wide").
- What belongs to SPACE: the effect (occupied width, spacing).
- Decisions removed: **WHO** is wide, **WHETHER** width is needed, often **WHERE** (Decision Allocation: WHO / WHAT fixed).
- **Width recognition: UNKNOWN.**

## 8. Pass-count / possession rules

| Evidence | Finding |
|---|---|
| *Front Psychol* 2019 (10:1554) | Pass requirement before a shot → more passes, possession maintenance. More rule manipulations ↓ exploratory behaviour |
| Machado et al. (2022) *Sensors* | "Functional" SSCG (2-touch + ≥5 passes before shooting) changed physical / tactical profile |
| García-Calvo et al. (2021) *IJERPH* (semi-pro, n = 40) | Minimum number of players involved before a goal → ↑ mean speed, effort, **mental fatigue**; ↑ performance satisfaction |
| Practitioner (SCORING practitioner audit) | Pass-count conditions → "passes without purpose" |

**Attacks:**
- "Three passes teaches patience." → **UNSUPPORTED** (behaviour while rule exists; no learning data).
- "Minimum passes improves possession play." → **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED acutely** (more passes / possession under the rule). "Improves possession play" as learning: **UNKNOWN**.

## 9. Mandated behaviour

| Mandate | WHO | WHAT | WHEN | HOW | Decision left |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Must overlap before scoring | Open (someone) | **Fixed** (overlap) | Partly open | Open | Who overlaps; timing; angle |
| Must switch play | Open | **Fixed** | Partly open | Open | When to switch; how |
| Must use weak foot | Fixed (self) | **Fixed** (technique) | Open | Constrained | Tactical choices remain; technique choice removed |
| Must make supporting run | Often fixed | Fixed | Open | Open | Timing, angle |
| Must press after turnover | Open / fixed | Fixed | Often fixed (immediately) | Open | Angle, speed, cover |
| Must play through target player | Open | Fixed (route) | Open | Open | Timing; pass type |

**Finding.**
- Mandates mostly fix **WHAT** (the action type), often **WHEN** (sequence).
- They usually leave **HOW** and sometimes **WHO**.
- Players still learn **execution** decisions under a mandate. They do not practise **choosing** the mandated action (CF-1 v2).

## 10. Prohibitions

| Evidence | Finding |
|---|---|
| Removing options in general | More rule manipulations ↓ exploratory team behaviour (*Front Psychol* 2019) |
| Rule manipulation in youth basketball (scoping review, 2023) | Rules can ↑ participation and **variability** of actions |
| Offside (a sport-authentic prohibition) in SSGs | Changed depth, length : width, exploration; ↓ distance (Rumpf et al., 2026) |
| No-dribble / no-tackle / no-hand-pass / no-kick | **No located studies** with learning outcomes |

- **Does option removal promote exploration of alternatives, or narrow decision-making?** **Both are observed in different studies.** Removing some options can increase variability of the remaining actions. Adding rules can reduce collective exploration.
- **Direction is not predictable** from "removing options" alone. **Learning / transfer: UNKNOWN.**

## 11. Affordances / solution space

- In ecological dynamics, rules are **task constraints** that shape which actions are possible, and thus the **field of affordances** (opportunities for action).
  - A prohibition removes affordances.
  - A requirement makes one action obligatory, which is not an affordance in the strict sense but a constraint on selection.
- **Precision:** an affordance is an opportunity for action **relative to the player's capabilities and the situation**. A rule changes the **legal** set, not the **physical** opportunity.
  - A backward pass remains physically available under "no back pass". It is only illegal.
- **Theory ≠ proof.** Ecological dynamics explains **why** rules shape behaviour. It does **not** show that a given coaching rule improves learning.

## 12. Rules that exaggerate a problem

| Rule | Does it increase exposure to a problem without prescribing the solution? |
|---|---|
| Turnover must be attacked immediately (reverse direction) | **Yes**: more transitions. Solution (how to attack) left open. Note: also fixes WHEN |
| Defenders score for a regain | SCORING, not a rule. Increases pressing attempts (SCORING gate) |
| Possession side loses a player after an error | CONSEQUENCES × NUMBERS: raises the cost of error |
| Offside line | Sport rule. Creates depth problems; solution open |
| Minimum players in the attacking half | Creates numbers ahead of the ball; solution open |

**Distinction tested:**
- **Problem-amplifying rule:** makes a problem more frequent or obvious; alternatives stay open.
- **Solution-prescribing rule:** mandates the answer ("must switch").

**Status: PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS.**
- Conceptually clean, and it maps onto Decision Allocation (does the rule fix WHAT / HOW, or only the situation?).
- **No study compares** the two types for learning.
- **Do not freeze the terms yet.**

## 13. Representativeness

| Type | Example | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Sport-authentic rule | Offside; Gaelic three-per-half; kickout beyond the 40 m arc | Part of the reference game |
| Modified practice rule (scaled version of the sport) | Smaller goals; modified contact rules for children | Designed to keep the game's structure at scale |
| Artificial coaching rule | 2-touch; no back pass; must switch | Absent from competition |

- **Does artificiality itself matter?** Representative learning design (Pinder et al., 2011) says the key question is **whether the information–action couplings of competition are preserved**, not whether a rule is "real".
  - An artificial rule may preserve key couplings (e.g., a transition rule that increases real transition moments).
  - An authentic rule may be irrelevant to the problem.
- **"More game-like = always better": UNSUPPORTED** (DIRECTION gate §6: representativeness is multidimensional and untested for learning).
- **Evidence snippet:** adding a defender to shooting practice changes shooting (basketball research summary; SIO). Presence of opposition is a coupling that matters.

## 14. Remove the rule (priority)

| Study | Design | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Gonzalez-Artetxe et al.** (*PESP* 2022, 27(5); *Biol Sport* 2023, 40(4); pilot, *Proc IMechE P* 2025) | U14 and U16 (n = 32). Four sessions; **artificial rules manipulating touches, player interactions and use of space** vs free play. Pre / post tests in a **non-constrained 8-a-side match** (LPS tracking) | Individual: **little average change**; large individual variability. Team lines: artificial rules "consolidated collective tactical behaviour" in the team-possession phase; free play boosted team-line regularity. **Mixed, short, same programme** |
| Futsal NLP defensive intervention (*Front Psychol* 2020; U16, n = 8) | 12 sessions of modified games; competitive-match decisions measured | Defensive decisions improved. **No control; not a rule-removal design** |
| Non-dominant-foot training RCTs (§19) | Technique restriction → technical tests | Learning on **technical tests**, not game transfer |

| Level | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Behaviour while the rule exists | Plentiful (acute) |
| Behaviour immediately after removal | Gonzalez-Artetxe post-test (timing relative to the last session UNR) |
| Retention | **None** with delay |
| Competition transfer | **None** for tactical rules |

**Connection to EXPOSE → EXPLORE → RELEASE → RETAIN:**
- Gonzalez-Artetxe is the nearest empirical analogue of RELEASE.
- It shows **no clear average benefit** after a short programme.
- The framework remains a **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS**.

## 15. "Remove the rule and see"

- **Coaching heuristic:** use a rule temporarily, remove it, see whether players still choose the behaviour when appropriate.
- **Practitioner support:** Play-Practice-Play / Whole-Part-Whole / GAA "back to the Game" (SCORING practitioner audit).
- **Scientific support:** only as a **reasonable observation method** (SPACE P10). The single study using it found little average change.
- **Two cautions:**
  1. **Same-session persistence ≠ retention.**
  2. **A drop in behaviour after removal ≠ failure.** Players may now select the action only when appropriate. Judge **appropriateness**, not frequency (SCORING gate §13).
- **Status: PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC** (as practice). **Not a learning test** without delay and appropriateness criteria.

## 16. Constraint stacking

| Evidence | Finding |
|---|---|
| *Front Psychol* 2019 | More rule manipulations ↓ exploratory behaviour |
| García-Calvo et al. (2021) | Pass restrictions ↑ mental fatigue and RPE |
| Machado et al. (2022) | Stacked "functional" constraints (2-touch + 5 passes) changed profile |
| TIME gate T11 | Stacking creates difficulty without demonstrated learning |
| Cognitive Load Theory (TIME §8) | Educational-psychology transfer only; expertise reversal |

- **At what point can we no longer tell what players are responding to?** Logically, as soon as two constraints act together, effects are confounded (touch × numbers interaction shown; Jäger et al., 2026).
- **No universal maximum** exists or is claimed.
- **Status: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** (stacking changes behaviour and load and may reduce exploration). **"Stacking increases learning": UNKNOWN / unsupported.**

## 17. Unintended behaviour / gaming the rule

| Example | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Meaningless passes to satisfy a pass count | Practitioner report (SIO) |
| More unsuccessful passes under touch limits | Review (AO): both successful and unsuccessful passes ↑ |
| Camping in a bonus zone; avoiding a good back-pass; forced low-quality switch; waiting for the bonus | **Not studied**; logically expected |
| Individual players respond very differently to the same rules | Gonzalez-Artetxe one-by-one analysis (AO) |

**Test the wording:** *"Players respond to the task actually created, not necessarily the lesson the coach intended."*
- **PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS, consistent with evidence:**
  - rule-specific behaviour changes;
  - unintended error increases;
  - individual variability;
  - the practitioner gaming report.
- **Kept as a candidate principle**, not frozen.
- **"Players gaming a rule means the rule failed": UNSUPPORTED.** The gaming may reveal what the rule actually rewards. That is information for the coach.

## 18. Rule compliance ≠ understanding

- **Test:** *successfully obeying a practice rule is not evidence the player understands when the behaviour would be useful without the rule.*
- **SUPPORTED** (as evidence status; direct application of CF-1 v2).
- **Avoid overreach.** While obeying, players may still learn:
  - **execution** (HOW);
  - **timing** (WHEN, if left open);
  - **coordination with teammates**;
  - **technical skill** (weak-foot evidence).

## 19. Rules and technical practice

| Evidence | Finding |
|---|---|
| **Haaland & Hoff (2003)** (non-dominant-leg training; AO) | Improved bilateral motor performance in soccer players |
| **Non-dominant-foot RCT, U13** (2025; AO) | ↑ pass accuracy, agility, shot precision vs control |
| Bilateral training studies (2022–2025; AO) | ↓ asymmetry; accuracy / agility gains |
| Kicking-only / hand-pass-only (Gaelic) | **No located studies** |

- **Temporary restriction can legitimately increase repetitions of a technique**, with **measured technical learning on tests**. The strongest learning evidence for any rule type in this audit.
- **Removing a tactical decision can be intentional** when decision learning is not the objective (CF-1 v2 practice-neutral clause).
- **Transfer to game use of the weak foot: UNKNOWN** (tests ≠ games).

## 22. Gaelic rules

### 22.1 Sport rules affecting representative practice (football)

All from secondary sources. The Official Guide text is **unverified** (refused; SCORING gate).
- Kickouts must travel beyond the 40 m arc.
- Three outfield players must remain in each half.
- Quick-restart solo / "tap-and-go" after a foul.
- Two-point scores from outside the arc.
- Goalkeeper-related restrictions (**UNR**).
- Permanent from 2026.

**Implication.** Representative Gaelic practice now **includes** these rules. Practising without them removes competition information (e.g., the three-per-half numbers structure).

### 22.2 Trial rules (competition-level prohibitions; not law)

- **Football (Ryan Cup trial, autumn 2026):**
  - ban on fisted points;
  - "backcourt" rule (no passing back into own half);
  - maximum three consecutive hand-passes.
- **Hurling (Fitzgibbon Cup trial):**
  - ban on two consecutive hand-passes;
  - puckouts beyond the 45;
  - six steps;
  - converted sideline cut = 2 points.

These are **governing bodies using prohibitions to shape play**, a large-scale analogue of coach rules. **Effects: unknown / not yet evaluated.**

### 22.3 Coach-added rules

- **No Gaelic study** of coach-added practice rules found.

### 22.4 Attribution caution

- Match-behaviour changes since 2025 **cannot be attributed to one rule**. Multiple rules changed simultaneously (SCORING gate §22).

## 30. U12 volunteer coach translation

> **A rule can help when it makes a problem show up more.** For example, "after a turnover, the other team must attack straight away" gives lots of practice at the scramble after losing the ball. How they deal with it is still up to them.
>
> **A rule can also just make them obey you.** "Two hops before you can shoot" or "no passing back" will get you what you asked for. But you've taken away the choice. You won't know if they'd do it at the right time without the rule.
>
> **Sometimes taking away a choice is exactly right.** "Left foot only for ten minutes" gets them loads of practice with their weaker side. That's a good use of a rule, because you're not trying to teach a decision there.
>
> **Watch what they actually do.** If they're making pointless passes just to reach your number, the rule is teaching the wrong thing. Change it.
>
> **Take the rule away and look again.** Do they still do it when it's the right thing to do? And don't panic if they do it less. They might just be doing it only when it makes sense.

## 33. Taxonomic verdict

### **C — RULES splits into multiple concepts**, most of which are **mechanisms** (B-type), with **one residue** that behaves like a CHANGE-THE-PROBLEM property.

1. **RULE as mechanism (B).**
   - Zone / channel, movement, participation, eligibility, temporal, transition, restart and scoring-validity rules are **methods** of changing SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION, GAME MOMENT or SCORING.
   - Their effects are recorded under those levers.
   - **Not a peer lever.**
2. **ACTION ALLOWANCES (residue; candidate CHANGE-THE-PROBLEM property).**
   - Changes to **which actions are available or required**:
     - touch limits;
     - technique restrictions (weak foot, kick-only, hand-pass limits);
     - contact / tackle restrictions;
     - mandated or prohibited action types;
     - action sequences.
   - No frozen lever describes the **action set**.
   - Name provisional; the final gate decides.
3. **Legal-alternative test.** Retained as the **classification boundary** between RULES (mechanism), INFORMATION / responsibility (Candidate 07) and SCORING incentives.
4. **Sport-authentic rules.** **Not manipulations.** They define the reference game (e.g., current Gaelic rules). Modifying them is a manipulation.

**Not D (absorb entirely):** the action-set residue has no home.
**Not A (peer lever as "RULES"):** most rules are only mechanisms.

---

## 34. Framework implication

**What RULES adds that the other concepts don't explain:**
- **Only the action set:** what players are **allowed or required to do** (touch, technique, contact, action types and sequences).
- Everything else a rule does is already described by SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION, SCORING, GAME MOMENT or the Decision Allocation lens.

**Is a rule something that changes the problem, or a method of manipulating it?**
- **Mostly a method.**
- **When it changes the action set, it changes the problem itself.** Players face the same space and numbers with a different repertoire.

**Relation to the architecture:**

| Family / layer | Relation |
|---|---|
| CHOOSE THE PROBLEM | Restart / transition rules may select moments (GAME MOMENT) |
| CHANGE THE PROBLEM | **ACTION ALLOWANCES** is a candidate addition. Rule-mechanisms implement the other levers |
| SUPPLY INFORMATION | Instructions keep alternatives legal. Rules remove them. Legal-alternative test is the boundary |
| DECISION ALLOCATION lens | Mandates fix WHAT (often WHEN). Prohibitions narrow WHAT. Touch limits fix parts of WHAT / HOW. Eligibility rules fix WHO |

**No symmetry forced.** RULES does not survive as the tenth parallel lever. It dissolves into a mechanism plus one new property.

**Coach-facing complexity flag (Deep framework, simple coaching):**
- "Mechanism vs property", "action allowances" and "legal-alternative test" are **research-level distinctions**. They are too complicated for coach-facing terms.
- The coach-facing layer should stay at the single question, possibly with the release prompt (§31).

## Executive verdict

**C — FREEZE DECISION ALLOCATION as a cross-framework lens, not another lever.**

- **PLAYER ROLES dissolves.** Its parts go to RULES, NUMBERS, INFORMATION, DIRECTION, GAME MOMENT / TIME and SPACE (scientific audit §31).
- **RESPONSIBILITY ALLOCATION survives only as the WHO component of that lens.**
- **CF-1 is expanded** (CF-1 v2): the inference limit applies to **the specific decisions a supplied condition removed**.
- CF-1 v2 **explicitly does not imply** that supplied conditions are poor practice.
- **Coach-facing question:** "What am I telling them, and what am I leaving them to solve?"
- **Transfer / retention of self-allocation after assigned responsibility: UNKNOWN.**

## 1. Reopening the concept

| Candidate | Needed as a category? | Needed as a concept? |
|---|---|---|
| PLAYER ROLES | **No.** An umbrella of five different things (position, responsibility, instruction, restriction, numerical function) | No. Kept only as everyday coach vocabulary |

**Taxonomy vs coaching question.**
- A concept can be valuable without being a lever.
- DECISION ALLOCATION is valuable as a **way of looking** at levers, instructions and roles, not as one more thing to manipulate.

## 2. Central taxonomy question: what is RESPONSIBILITY ALLOCATION?

| Option | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A — independent practice-design decision | **Rejected** | Practitioners deliver allocation as names, rules and cues (practitioner audit §3). It is not separable from other design choices |
| **B — the WHO component of DECISION ALLOCATION** | **Adopted** | Captures what is specific (who acts) without inventing a category |
| C — INFORMATION supplied by the coach | **Partly true, but incomplete** | Allocation is usually *delivered* as information. The same WHO is also fixed by NUMBERS (the spare), GAME MOMENT (the restarter), RULES (who may act) and structures (formations) |
| D — cross-framework dimension | **True of the lens it belongs to** | See B and §11 |
| E — redundant | **Rejected** | Losing WHO would hide the distinction between named and relational allocation, which practitioners use constantly |

## 3. Stress test: WHO / WHETHER / WHEN / HOW

### 3.1 Prior art (no manufactured novelty)

| Existing framework | What it decomposes | Relation to PáircVision's lens |
|---|---|---|
| **Mosston & Ashworth, Spectrum of Teaching Styles** | Teaching as "a chain of decision-making". Styles are defined by **which decisions shift from teacher to learner** (command → self-teaching) | **Direct prior art for "decision allocation"** at the level of pedagogy (planning, execution, feedback decisions). Not about in-game tactical decisions |
| **TGfU (Bunker & Thorpe)** | Decision-making = **"what to do"** (tactical awareness) and **"how to do it"** (skill selection / execution) | Prior art for WHAT / HOW |
| **Hopper & Bell** (TGfU extension) | Read · respond · react · recover | Overlaps WHETHER (read) and WHEN / HOW |
| **GPAI** (Griffin, Mitchell & Oslin, 1997) | Decision-making, skill execution, support, cover, base, adjust | Assessment categories. Support and cover are responsibilities |
| **Game model / tactical principles** (e.g., Ramos et al., *Front Psychol* 2019: bi-directional self-organising tendencies) | How principles constrain global-to-local coordination | Prior art for "coaches fix some coordination, leave local decisions" |

**Verdict.** The idea that coaching can be described by **which decisions the coach takes and which the learner keeps** is **not novel** (Mosston). Decomposing decisions into what / how is standard (TGfU).

**PáircVision's contribution is only the application:** reading **practice-design constraints and tactical instructions** through a WHO / WHAT / WHEN / HOW lens. That is **PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS**.

### 3.2 Are the four dimensions sound?

| Test | Finding |
|---|---|
| Conceptually distinct? | **WHO** (which player) is distinct. **WHETHER** and **WHEN** are often fused in practice (a trigger answers both). **HOW** is distinct (execution) |
| Missing dimension? | **Yes: WHAT** (which option: pass, shoot, carry, press or hold). "Whether" is really one case of "what" (acting vs not acting). **WHERE** mostly falls under WHAT / HOW |
| Overlap | WHETHER / WHAT / WHEN overlap heavily in fast play |
| Falsely neat? | The four-way split can look more precise than decisions are. Real decisions are coupled and continuous (ecological dynamics) |
| Supported by literature / practice? | Components supported (Mosston; TGfU; practitioner triggers vs execution). The **four-way set is synthesis** |

**Modification adopted:** **WHO · WHAT · WHEN · HOW**, where WHAT includes "whether to act at all".
- It matches existing "what / how" language.
- It adds WHO for team coordination.
- **It is a lens, not a measured model.**

## 4. Concrete coaching cases

| Case | What the coach fixes | What the player still perceives / decides | Observable behaviour | What cannot be claimed about learning |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Pressing** ("nearest presses when receiver faces own goal") | WHO rule (nearest); WHAT / WHEN cue (trigger) | Am I nearest? Is the trigger present? HOW (angle, speed) | Press initiations; timing vs trigger | That players would press at the right moment without the cue, or self-coordinate without the rule |
| **Support** ("Aoife always supports the carrier") | WHO; WHAT (support) | WHEN (distance, timing); HOW (angle) | Support frequency and angles by Aoife | That anyone recognises when the carrier needs support |
| **Defensive cover** ("second defender covers") | WHO rule; WHAT | Am I second? WHEN / HOW (depth, angle) | Cover positions | That cover is recognised in new structures |
| **Kickout receiving** (planned receivers) | WHO; WHAT (show for the ball) | WHEN (movement timing); HOW (getting free) | Receiver movement; retention | That players could organise receipt without the plan (may not be the goal) |
| **Man-marking** ("our 11 marks their 6") | WHO; WHAT (mark 6) | WHEN to release or switch; HOW (positioning, tackle timing) | Tracking of #6 | That players can choose match-ups (not the aim) |
| **Attacking width** (a width provider named) | WHO; WHAT | WHEN to come inside; HOW (run timing) | Width maintained | That others create width when needed |
| **Transition** (coach calls turnover) | WHAT / WHEN (moment declared: GAME MOMENT) | WHO reacts; HOW | Response after the call | Recognition of natural transitions (GAME MOMENT hypothesis) |
| **Overload** (given 3v2) | Situation (NUMBERS, GIVEN) | WHO carries / passes; WHAT; WHEN; HOW | Exploitation | Recognition or creation of overloads (N6) |
| **Shooting** (two-point bonus) | Value (SCORING) | WHO shoots; WHAT (shoot vs pass); WHEN; HOW | Shot selection | That value-based choices transfer (SC-2) |
| **Restart structure** (set kickout routine) | WHO, WHAT, largely WHEN | HOW (execution); adapting to the opponent's set-up | Routine execution | Adaptation to unrehearsed set-ups |

**Result.** WHO / WHAT / WHEN / HOW **describes every case adequately.** It also shows that **frozen levers fix decisions too**:
- GAME MOMENT fixes WHAT / WHEN (the situation);
- NUMBERS fixes the situation, not WHO;
- SCORING fixes value, not choice.

The lens is **not rejected**. It is modified (WHETHER → WHAT).

## 5. Conditional responsibility

| Statement | WHO | WHAT (incl. whether) | WHEN | HOW | Purpose it may serve |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Sean presses their 6." | Fixed (named) | Fixed (press 6) | Open | Open | Opponent-specific plan; coordination |
| "Nearest player presses." | **Rule** (player must recognise "am I nearest?") | Fixed (someone presses) | Open | Open | Fast collective reaction |
| "Nearest presses when the receiver can't play forward." | Rule | **Conditional** (trigger) | Cued | Open | Coordinated, trigger-based press |
| "Recognise when we can lock them in." | Open | Open | Open | Open | Developing recognition and self-coordination |

- **Not ranked.** Each serves a different coaching purpose.
- **Assigned responsibility does not necessarily remove recognition** (practitioner audit §5).
- Conditional and relational forms keep recognition of **"is it me?"** and **"is it on?"** alive.

## 6. Tactical coordination exception

- If the objective is "our #11 marks their #6", then recognising who should mark #6 is **not the intended learning problem**. The allocated job **is** the desired performance.
- **Clarification (frozen in CF-1 v2, §12):** CF-1 limits **what can be inferred** about recognition or creation. **It does not imply coach-supplied conditions are poor practice.** Where coordinated execution of an allocated job is the goal, supplying it is legitimate coaching.
- Supporting practitioner rationale (practitioner audit §8):
  - speed ("no time to think");
  - collective defending;
  - restart precision;
  - opponent plans;
  - game models (e.g., Klopp and Nagelsmann, secondary; the game-model self-organisation literature).

## 7. Responsibility forms

| Form | Survives as a descriptive category? | Edge cases |
|---|---|---|
| **NAMED** ("Sean") | Yes | A named *conditional* job (AFL loose man) keeps WHAT / WHEN open |
| **POSITIONAL** ("our 11") | Yes | Merges with NAMED when positions are fixed; with RELATIONAL when position is defined by the moment ("whoever is at 11 now") |
| **RELATIONAL** ("nearest", "second", "first to ruck") | Yes | Requires perception; can fail when two players are equidistant, which is where communication does the work |
| **SHARED / EMERGENT** ("all 18 defend"; players sort it out) | Yes | "Shared" without a rule risks both nobody and everybody acting (practitioner failure modes) |

**Frozen as descriptive categories only.**
- **Rejected as a developmental progression.**
- Relational rules are presented by practitioners as an **end state**, and named jobs persist at elite level (practitioner audit §14).

## 8. Role rotation

- **Two different sampling decisions:**
  - **which game moment** is practised (GAME MOMENT, frozen);
  - **which player** experiences **which responsibility or problem** over time (rotation).
- **Evidence:**
  - GAA Go Games policy: players should experience several positions over a playing year (governing-body policy; SIO).
  - **No learning evidence** for rotation.
- **Is PLAYER SAMPLING a separate dimension?**
  - **Record it, but not as a new category.**
  - It is the **individual-level extension** of CHOOSE THE PROBLEM: "who meets which problem, how often".
  - Keeping it inside that family avoids unnecessary complexity.
- **Status:** PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS (placement); effects **UNKNOWN**.

## 9. RULES boundary (for Candidate 08)

**Proposed:** *Responsibility identifies who is accountable for responding to a game problem. A rule changes what actions or states are permitted, required or prohibited.*

**Survives, with edge cases for RULES to revisit:**

| Edge case | Why difficult |
|---|---|
| "Only the nearest player may challenge." | A rule that **allocates WHO** by prohibiting others |
| "You must press first." | Is a *required* action a rule, or a responsibility with no alternative? |
| Three-players-in-each-half (Gaelic) | A competition rule that **produces** positional responsibilities |
| "If you're the spare, you can't tackle." | A NUMBERS function enforced by a rule |
| Penalty for not doing your job (lose a point) | A responsibility enforced through SCORING, so a de facto rule |
| Social / coach pressure to obey a job | A responsibility that functions as a rule (scientific audit PR-5) |
| Man-marking with a "no switching" instruction | Responsibility plus prohibition |

**Test for RULES.** Is the alternative still **legal**? Yes → responsibility or instruction. No → rule. This parallels SCORING's legal-vs-meaningful analysis.

## 10. INFORMATION boundary (for Candidate 09)

- "Nearest player presses" **is** information. So why isn't allocation simply INFORMATION?
- **Candidate distinction:** INFORMATION concerns what the coach or environment tells or reveals. DECISION ALLOCATION describes which parts of the solution have been specified.
- **Adjudication: the distinction is about level of analysis, not category.**
  - INFORMATION is a **manipulation family**: cues, instructions, feedback, visibility.
  - DECISION ALLOCATION is a **lens applied to any manipulation**, informational or not, asking which decisions it fixes.
  - A spoken instruction can be analysed with the lens. So can a NUMBERS overload or a GAME MOMENT start.
- **Not artificial** only because the lens applies **beyond** INFORMATION (§11).
- If the lens applied only to spoken instructions, it would collapse into INFORMATION.

**For Candidate 09 to settle:**
- whether explicit instructions and relational rules are INFORMATION's core content;
- explicit vs implicit instruction effects (U-20 study; *RQES* 2024);
- whether coach-preference signalling (SCORING §20) belongs there.

## 11. Interaction with frozen levers

| Manipulation | Decisions it fixes (lens reading) |
|---|---|
| NUMBERS: a spare / floater | Fixes the **situation** (a given advantage). Floater rules fix the floater's WHAT (e.g., may not score) |
| DIRECTION: a target | Fixes where scoring happens (defines what counts as WHAT options) |
| SCORING: value | Fixes the **value** of options, not the choice |
| GAME MOMENT: a turnover start | Fixes the situation and, if called, the WHEN of recognition |
| SPACE: zones | Can fix WHERE (a WHAT / HOW constraint) |
| TIME: countdown | Fixes an upper bound on WHEN |

**Conclusion.**
- **DECISION ALLOCATION sits across the framework**, not beside the levers.
- Every lever, rule and instruction can be read by which decisions it fixes and which it leaves.
- That is the strongest reason for **C (lens)** over B (a new decision category).

## 12. CF-1 final integration: CF-1 v2

**Original CF-1:** *When the coach supplies a condition, players' behaviour from it is not evidence they can recognise or create that condition themselves. Whether they can later is UNKNOWN.*

**Candidate 07 expands it (frozen as CF-1 v2):**

> **When a coach supplies part of the solution (a position, an overload, a mandated action, a reward, a called moment or an assigned job), players' behaviour from it is not evidence that they can make the decisions that were supplied for them. Evidence about the decisions they still had to make is unaffected.**
>
> **This limits what can be inferred. It does not mean supplying conditions is poor practice. Where coordinated execution of a supplied job is the aim, supplying it is the practice.**

- **Status:** SUPPORTED (as evidence status across NUMBERS, DIRECTION, SCORING, GAME MOMENT and Candidate 07). The inference-limit logic is a PÁIRCVISION SYNTHESIS.
- **Change from v1:**
  - The limit is now **decision-specific**. A named press job still permits inferences about pressing **execution** (HOW) within that practice, but not about **self-allocation** (WHO).
  - The coordination clarification is added.

## 13. Prescription vs freedom

| Binary | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Coach-led = bad | **REJECTED** (coordination and restart jobs legitimately supplied) |
| Player-led = good | **REJECTED** (no evidence; excessive freedom risks coordination failure) |
| Prescription = poor learning | **UNKNOWN** (untested) |
| Freedom = creativity | **UNKNOWN** |
| Elite = rigid | **CONTRADICTED** (relational rules; conditional jobs) |
| Youth = free | **CONTRADICTED** (Go Games is structured; position exposure is itself a coach decision) |
| Constraints = discovery | **UNKNOWN**; constraints also supply decisions (§11) |

**Strongest defensible alternative (PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS):**

> *The useful coaching question is not "how much freedom am I giving?" but "which decisions am I deliberately making for the players, and which do I want them to make?"*

- **Classified honestly:**
  - **consistent** with Mosston's decision-shifting logic;
  - **consistent** with practitioner behaviour (relational rules, triggers);
  - **not tested** as a design method for learning.

## 14. Learning / retention / transfer

**Final targeted search:** assigned responsibility or instruction during training → responsibility removed → recognition / self-allocation / free-play / competition test.

**Found:**
- **Team-building intervention** in young elite footballers (8 weeks, pre / post / follow-up): improved **role clarity**, communication, coordination and perceived team performance (*Front Psychol* 2023; AO). These are **psychological and perceived-coordination outcomes**. It does **not** test self-allocation after removing assigned responsibilities.
- **Skill-acquisition RCT scoping review** (*Psychol Sport Exerc* 2024; AO): about half of studies used only immediate (≤48 h) retention, and about a quarter used transfer tests. It confirms how rarely transfer is tested. None targets responsibility allocation.

> **Transfer / retention of self-allocation after assigned responsibility: UNKNOWN (frozen).**

Immediate successful execution of an assigned job is **not** learning evidence.

## 20. Final taxonomy verdict

### **C — FREEZE DECISION ALLOCATION as a cross-framework lens, not another lever**

**Why not A or B:**
- Allocation (who, or any decision) is **not a separate thing a coach manipulates**.
- It is **a property of everything a coach manipulates or says** (§11).
- Listing it beside SPACE or GAME MOMENT would double-count.

**Why not D (absorb):**
- Absorbing it into INFORMATION would hide that levers, rules and set-ups also make decisions for players.
- The lens and CF-1 v2 are the most useful outputs of Candidate 07.

**How the lens interacts with the families (no new coach-facing complexity):**

| Family | Lens question (research level) | Coach sees only |
|---|---|---|
| **CHOOSE THE PROBLEM** (GAME MOMENT; which player meets which problem) | Does choosing / calling the moment make the "what / when" decision for them? | "What am I telling them, and what am I leaving them to solve?" |
| **CHANGE THE PROBLEM** (SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION, SCORING; RULES / CONSEQUENCES pending) | Which decisions does this constraint fix (e.g., a given overload fixes the situation, not the choice)? | Same single question |
| **WHAT THE COACH SUPPLIES vs WHAT PLAYERS SOLVE** (INFORMATION pending; jobs; CF-1 v2) | Which of who / what / when / how did the coach supply? What can therefore be inferred? | Same single question |

## 21. Updated framework architecture (research level)

```
CHOOSE THE PROBLEM
  GAME MOMENT (which situation, how often)
  + which player meets which problem (role / position exposure)

CHANGE THE PROBLEM
  SPACE · NUMBERS · TIME · DIRECTION (target structure) · SCORING (evaluative)
  pending: RULES (Candidate 08) · CONSEQUENCES

WHAT THE COACH SUPPLIES vs WHAT PLAYERS SOLVE
  pending: INFORMATION (Candidate 09): instructions, cues, feedback, visibility

CROSS-FRAMEWORK
  CF-1 v2 (inference limit, decision-specific; not a judgement of practice)
  DECISION ALLOCATION lens: who / what / when / how, applied to all of the above
```

**Change from the previous architecture:**
- The third family is no longer "CF-1 + possibly DECISION ALLOCATION".
- DECISION ALLOCATION and CF-1 v2 move to a **cross-framework layer**.
- The third family keeps **INFORMATION** (pending) as its manipulation content.

**No symmetry forced.**

**Audits under test:**

**Status:** Final gate, September 2026. **Where either SCORING audit and this gate differ, this gate governs PáircVision V1.**

> **Central question.** If players do more of a behaviour because it is rewarded, have they learned when that behaviour is useful, or only responded rationally to an artificial value structure?
>
> **The chain of different claims:** LEGAL → VALUABLE → CHOSEN → APPROPRIATE → LEARNED → RETAINED → TRANSFERRED.
> - Evidence for one link is not evidence for the next.
> - We do not need SCORING, bonus points, or nine principles to survive.
> - UNKNOWN is a successful result.

## 1. Attack the definition

**Candidate:** *SCORING = the value structure of a practice: what earns or costs points, how much, and to whom.*

| Comparison | Falsification attempt | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **RULES** | "Scoring rules are rules; SCORING is a subtype of RULES." | Formally true: point values are rules of the game. **But PáircVision's RULES lever concerns legality: what is allowed or required.** Value rules and action rules are different design operations. **Distinct**, with a shared border (§4) |
| **DIRECTION** | "Value is part of target structure." | Rejected. DIRECTION = *where* scoring can happen (frozen). SCORING = *how much* it is worth. The two co-occur (§20) but vary independently |
| **INFORMATION** | "Scoring works by telling players what matters." | **Partly unresolved.** Some of SCORING's effect may be informational (§11). The manipulation (changing values) is still distinct from changing signals, cues or instructions. **Boundary parked** |
| **CONSEQUENCES** | "Points are just a consequence of an outcome." | Provisionally rejected. Points change what an outcome is **worth**; consequences change what **happens next** in the game (§19). CONSEQUENCES is unaudited |
| **Normal sport scoring** | "Normal scoring isn't a manipulation." | Normal scoring is the **baseline value structure**. Altered scoring is a deviation from it. Both belong to the lever |
| **Competition** | "Keeping score and competing are SCORING." | **Rejected as part of SCORING.** Whether outcomes are compared between teams, and what depends on winning, is a **separate property of the practice** (§16) |
| **Feedback / reward / punishment** | "SCORING is feedback or reward." | These are **possible functions or mechanisms** of a value change, not the manipulation. Removed from the definition |
| **Score state** | "Current score is SCORING." | It does not change what a point is worth. **Primarily INFORMATION**, unresolved (§18) |
| **"to whom"** (team vs individual) | Is this a separate lever? | Kept. Allocating value to individuals or teams is a value decision |

### Verdict: **COHERENT WITH NARROWER DEFINITION**

- SCORING survives as **assigned value**:
  - the points attached to outcomes or actions (including negative points);
  - their size relative to each other;
  - who receives them.
- **Removed from SCORING:**
  - competition and stakes;
  - score state;
  - feedback, reward and punishment as mechanisms;
  - scoring-validity conditions ("only counts if…", which are RULES, §4–5).

## 2. What SCORING directly changes

**Proposed:** *SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION primarily alter the opportunities players encounter. SCORING directly alters the assigned value of possible outcomes.*

**Challenges:**
1. **Emergent consequences.** A bonus cannot create a passing lane. But players chasing the bonus move differently, and defenders respond, so lanes open and close.
   - Acute practice evidence: an explicit regain bonus changed teams' spatial spread and coordination (U-20 soccer; AO).
   - Competition evidence: value changes shifted shot locations, and defences then adapted (basketball; SIO).
   - SCORING therefore **indirectly** changes the opportunities that arise.
2. **"Opportunities" is too loose for the other four levers.**
   - TIME includes clocks and countdowns, which are rules, not physical space.
   - DIRECTION changes target structure.
   - "Structural conditions of play" is more accurate than "physical opportunities".

**Distinguish:**
- **DIRECT MANIPULATION**: what the coach changes (cones, numbers, clock, targets, values).
- **EMERGENT BEHAVIOURAL CONSEQUENCE**: what players and opponents then do, which in turn reshapes the opportunities available.

**Strongest defensible wording:**

> **SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION directly change the structural conditions of play. SCORING directly changes only the value assigned to outcomes or actions. Any change in the opportunities that then arise comes from how players and opponents respond. That is an emergent consequence, not the manipulation itself.**

Status: **PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION (taxonomy)**. It is consistent with the evidence that value changes alter behaviour (acute; competition).

## 4. SCORING vs RULES: final boundary

**Proposed:** *RULES change legal or required actions. SCORING changes relative value among actions or outcomes that remain legally available.*

**Survives, with one essential qualification:** *legal* choice ≠ *meaningful* choice (§6).

| Case | Legal status of the alternative | Primary lever | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Switch = 2 points (score otherwise 1) | Legal; worth 1 | **SCORING** | Meaningful choice depends on relative expected value |
| Must switch before scoring | Scoring without a switch is **not allowed** | **RULES** | — |
| Score only counts after a switch | Shooting without a switch is allowed, but **the score is invalid** | **RULES** (validity rule, like offside disallowing a goal) | Correction to the scientific audit's framing (§5) |
| Switch = 100 points | Legal; worth 1 | **SCORING (formally)**; **functionally near-compulsory** | Legal but not meaningful |
| Turnover = −1 | Legal; costly | **SCORING (negative)** | §17 |
| Back pass = −3 | Legal; heavily penalised | **SCORING (formally)**; potentially **de facto prohibition** | Severity is the question |
| Five passes = 1 point in keep-ball | No other scoring route | **SCORING** defining the only objective (DIRECTION zero condition) | — |
| "N passes before you may shoot" | Shooting earlier not allowed | **RULES** | — |
| Next goal wins | All goals legal | **SCORING × CONSEQUENCES** (win condition) | Edge case |
| Bonus that also returns possession | Legal | **SCORING + CONSEQUENCES** (bundled) | Effects confounded |
| Scores only allowed from a zone | Scoring elsewhere invalid | **RULES × SPACE** | — |
| Score from a zone worth more | Legal | **SCORING × SPACE** | — |

> **Later taxonomy clarification (Candidate 08).** Candidate 08 subsequently dissolved RULES as a peer lever. Where this document and the [Candidate 08 Final Gate](./phase1-candidate-08-rules-final-gate.md) differ on classification, **that gate governs**. The findings and evidence statuses above are unchanged.
>
> - **"Only counts if" (validity) conditions belong to SCORING** when the action remains legal but its scoring value becomes zero (e.g., "score only counts after a switch"; "scores only allowed from a zone").
> - Rules that make an action itself illegal or compulsory ("must switch before scoring"; "N passes before you may shoot") belong to **ACTIONS** (Candidate 08 Final Gate §2).
> - The same applies to "validity rule (RULES)" in §5 below.

## 5. The zero-point problem

The scientific audit said an alternative worth zero "functions as a RULE". **That was too coarse.** Three different things need separating:

| | Meaning | Example | Is the option a genuine choice? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ZERO IMMEDIATE SCORE VALUE** | The action earns no points itself | A backward pass, a hold-up, a switch without a bonus | **Yes**, if it has downstream value: it creates a later score, keeps possession, moves defenders, escapes pressure, opens space, or prevents a score against |
| **ZERO TACTICAL VALUE** | The action does nothing for the objective under the game's values | Shooting when "only scores after a switch count" | **No.** The outcome is pointless inside that game |
| **ILLEGAL ACTION** | Not allowed | "No back passes" | No |

**Correction:**
- Most actions in any invasion game earn zero points directly and remain genuine choices. Almost all passes are like this.
- **Only a condition that makes an *outcome* worthless** (a score that does not count) functions like a rule. That is a **validity rule** (RULES).
- **Zero-valued alternatives can remain genuine choices** when they carry downstream tactical value. That is consistent with DIRECTION DIR-3: progress is judged by effect, not by immediate reward.

## 6. Legal choice vs meaningful choice

**Claim tested:** *Bonus scoring preserves choice because alternatives remain legal.*

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Is legal availability sufficient for meaningful choice? | **No.** It is necessary, not sufficient |
| Can incentive magnitude functionally eliminate an option? | **Yes, logically.** If the bonus makes one option dominant in nearly all situations. Threshold **UNKNOWN** |
| Can score state eliminate it? | **Yes, logically.** E.g., two down with seconds left, where only a two-pointer can draw |
| Can probability of success restore its value? | **Yes.** A lower-value option with a much higher success probability can be worth more (SC-3) |
| Can downstream tactical value outweigh immediate point value? | **Yes.** Possession, position, and preventing a score against can outweigh a small immediate bonus |

**Strongest defensible conclusion:**

> **A bonus keeps alternatives legal, but whether a choice remains meaningful depends on relative expected value (points, probability and downstream effects) in each situation. "Preserves choice" cannot be asserted from the design alone. It must be observed.**

Status: **LOGICAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE**, not evidence-tested.

## 7. Bonus magnitude

**Final search** for +1 / double / triple / very large / escalating bonuses, calibration and dose–response in practice: **none found.**

- Competition analogue: raising a win from 2 to 3 points gave **mixed** results across leagues (AO). It is not a practice dose–response study.
- Practitioners: no documented calibration method (practitioner audit §6).

**Assessments:**
- **"Use the smallest scoring bias that meaningfully changes the problem": PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS.** No scientific basis.
- **Do we know smaller bonuses distort play less? No.**
  - A small bonus may change nothing, and so expose nothing.
  - A large bonus may produce clearer exposure but dominate.
  - Neither is tested.
- "Meaningfully" is undefined. The hypothesis is not yet testable as worded.

## 8. Value × probability

**Cautious formalisation:**

> *expected immediate scoring value ≈ score value × probability of success*

**What it omits:**
- possession after the attempt (retained, rebound, restart);
- turnover risk and where it happens;
- field position after a miss (e.g., a Gaelic kickout);
- time remaining;
- score state;
- opponent strength and defensive response;
- player-specific ability;
- future opportunities created or lost;
- how defences adapt over repeated attempts.

**Evidence that value interacts with probability and context:**
- basketball expected-value practice (practitioner) and shot-value shifts (competition);
- AFL accuracy by distance and angle (*JSAMS* 2024; AO);
- rugby penalty decision surfaces (arXiv preprint; SIO);
- NFL decisions deviating from models (Romer 2006; AO);
- Gaelic pre-2025 shot models: pressure, distance and shot type change success (AO).

**Verdict: SUPPORTED as analysis of options.** Frozen as **SC-3**, strictly as a statement about how to judge options, **not** a claim about what players learn.

|---|---|---|
| Process scoring increases the rewarded intermediate behaviour during practice | Regain bonus changed pressing-related spatial behaviour (U-20; AO). Machado et al. ## 10. Gaming the score

**Final search** (reward hacking, gaming conditioned games, meaningless repetitions, bonus-chasing, artificial risk aversion, predictable play): **no additional sport-practice evidence.** The only item remains the practitioner report of purposeless passing under pass-count conditions.

**Test:** *If players chase the bonus when it is no longer the best game action, the scoring system is dominating the practice.*

- This is **near-definitional**. "Dominating" means value overriding game judgement.
- The hard part is empirical: **judging the best game action** needs DIR-3-style assessment of effect in context.
- **Classification: LOGICAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE** (with low observability). Kept as **H-2**.

## 13. Return to normal play

| | Observation | What it can establish | What it cannot |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Behaviour **under** the bonus | The bonus changes behaviour in that game | Learning |
| **B** | Behaviour **immediately after** removal (same session) | After-effects: carry-over, momentum, instruction, fatigue, novelty | Retention. **B is not retention** |
| **C** | Delayed retention (≥ 24 h; reference game; baseline) | Possible learning in that game | Transfer |
| **D** | Competition transfer | The goal | Almost never cleanly measurable by a coach |

**Is same-session removal a useful observational test?** **Yes, as a screen, not a verdict.**
- **Frequency is ambiguous in both directions.**
  - If the behaviour **drops** after removal, players may have been complying with the bonus, **or** may now correctly use it only when appropriate.
  - If it **persists**, it may be momentum or instruction, **or** understanding.
- **The informative observation is appropriateness:** is the behaviour chosen **when it helps**, and skipped when it doesn't?

## 15. Transfer / retention: final search

**Searched for:**
- scoring constraint removed at post-test;
- bonus-scoring retention;
- conditioned-scoring transfer;
- longitudinal altered-scoring interventions;
- normal-scoring post-tests;
- unconstrained reference games;
- competition transfer.

**Result:**
- **No study** rewards a behaviour, removes the reward, and tests later whether players still recognise when it is useful.
- **Closest candidate:** Gonzalez-Artetxe et al. (2023 *Biol Sport*; 2022 *PESP*; 2025 pilot, *Proc IMechE P*). U14 / U16 players trained 4 sessions with **artificial rules** vs free play, and were post-tested in a **non-constrained 8-a-side match**.

  - **The rule types remain UNR.** A search summary juxtaposed this programme with games where "defending teams could score points for regaining possession", but **we could not confirm** that its artificial rules were scoring rules.
  - Even if they were, the results concern positional tactical behaviour, not recognising when a behaviour is useful.

> **When the altered scoring disappears, do players still recognise when the previously rewarded behaviour is useful? UNKNOWN.**

> **TRANSFER / RETENTION = UNKNOWN.**

## 17. Negative scoring

- **Fits the definition:** a negative value is assigned value.
- **Evidence for greater caution, reduced risk, fear of error, reduced exploration, or improved decision quality in sport practice: none.**
- **Psychological states must not be inferred from tactical behaviour.** Framing research (NFL, tennis; SIO) is outside practice design and is **not imported**.
- **Design caution (logical):** penalising an action that is sometimes correct (e.g., back passes; DIRECTION DIR-3) risks penalising good decisions. This is consistent with the frozen SPACE heuristic against systematically punishing good decisions.

## 18. Score state

- "You're four points up with three minutes left" does **not** change what a point is worth. It changes the **context** in which value is interpreted.
- **Provisional classification: INFORMATION (primary), interacting with SCORING and TIME.**
  - Where a scenario changes the **win condition** ("a draw is enough", "next score wins"), it touches SCORING × CONSEQUENCES.
- **UNRESOLVED.** Re-examine at the INFORMATION audit.

The original finding above and its evidence status are unchanged.

> - Selecting a score / time scenario (e.g., "four up, three minutes left") → **GAME MOMENT**.

## 19. SCORING vs CONSEQUENCES (provisional)

**Working boundary:**
- **SCORING** changes what an outcome is **worth**.
- **CONSEQUENCES** changes **what happens next** in the game.

| Example | Classification |
|---|---|
| Regain = +2 | SCORING |
| Regain = immediate 5v3 | CONSEQUENCES (× NUMBERS) |
| Score = retain possession | CONSEQUENCES |
| Turnover = −1 | SCORING |
| Turnover = opposition starts near your goal | CONSEQUENCES (× SPACE) |

**Counterexamples and edge cases:**
1. **Expected-value equivalence.** A consequence can be converted into expected points (possession near goal ≈ some expected score). The boundary is about **mechanism** (changing game state vs assigning value), not about whether value changes. Both change value in the end.
2. **Bundled rewards** (+1 and ball back) are confounded.
3. **Reset scoring** ("defensive score back to zero"): SCORING, but it behaves like a consequence for the drill's end condition.
4. **Win conditions** ("next goal wins"): change the value of all future outcomes. SCORING × CONSEQUENCES.
5. **Post-game stakes** (losers run): outside the game. Neither lever, or competitive structure (§16).

**PROVISIONAL.** Not frozen until CONSEQUENCES is audited.

> **Later taxonomy clarification (Candidate 10).** Candidate 10 subsequently dissolved CONSEQUENCES as a Phase 1 property. Where this document and the [Candidate 10 Final Gate](./phase1-candidate-10-consequences-final-gate.md) differ on classification, **that gate governs**. The original finding above and its evidence status are unchanged.
>
> - The boundary is now **frozen**: changing what an outcome is worth is **SCORING**; changing another property after the outcome is that property, event-contingently.
> - Regain = immediate 5v3 → **NUMBERS** (event-contingent; + GAME MOMENT if it starts a new episode). Score = retain possession → **GAME MOMENT** (next start). Turnover = opposition starts near your goal → **GAME MOMENT + SPACE**.
> - Bundled rewards (+1 and ball back) → **SCORING + GAME MOMENT** (confounded). Win conditions ("next goal wins") → **SCORING**; ending the episode is GAME MOMENT.
> - Post-game stakes (losers run) and competition formats (e.g., winner-stays-on) → **outside Phase 1**; any practice effects (who plays, next episode) are classified by existing properties.

| FRC rules made permanent (Special Congress October 2025; in force 2026; Official Guide effective 27 March 2026) | Secondary (SIO) |
| Goal 3, point 1 | Unchanged |

**Do not silently upgrade.** Any PáircVision rule wording must be checked against the Official Guide Part 2.

### 21.2 Four categories

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Rule facts** | As above (corroborated, unverified text) |
| **Observed 2025–26 adaptations** (descriptive, journalism / analysis) | More long-range attempts (~18 % of championship shots, SIO); structured attacks and screens to manufacture two-point chances; specialist long-range shooters including goalkeepers; mid-range avoidance by some teams |
| **Practitioner opinion** | "Slightly higher risk, twice the reward"; suggested "no-shot zone" between the 20 m line and the arc; seasonal deferral of two-point practice |
| **Causal evidence** | **None** |

### 21.3 Test: *The higher-value shot is not automatically the better shot*

**SUPPORTED** (SC-3 applied):
- Success probability depends on distance, pressure and shot type. Pre-2025 models: high pressure ≈ a shot taken about 9 m further out; frees far more successful than open play (AO).
- Probability also depends on player-specific range.
- After a miss, the kickout and possession outcome differ from those after a score.
- Defences adapt.
- **Qualification:** the probability figures come from **2019 data**, before the rule changes.

## 22. Causal claim about Gaelic scoring

**Prevented:** "The two-pointer caused the rise in Gaelic football scoring."

**What can be said:**
- Championship scoring rose from roughly 34 to 46 points per game between 2024 and 2025 (Kearney & Barrett, RTÉ Brainstorm 2026; SIO). This coincided with a **package** of rule changes (two-point score, kickout rule, three players in each half, quick restart, and others).
- Two-point attempts became a substantial share of shots (SIO).
- **The two-pointer's separate contribution is not isolated by any evidence we found.**

- **Final check: no evidence** that bonus scoring functions differently for children, adolescents, adults or elite players.

## 24. Five-lever synthesis

**Tested model:**
- SPACE → spatial opportunities;
- NUMBERS → numerical and interpersonal relationships;
- TIME → temporal opportunity;
- DIRECTION → target structure;
- SCORING → assigned value.

| Line | Attack | Defensible wording |
|---|---|---|
| SPACE → spatial opportunities | Also changes density, physical load and team shape | "SPACE changes the area and shape available, and so the spatial opportunities and demands" |
| NUMBERS → numerical relationships | Also changes space per player (interaction). Given ≠ created superiority | "NUMBERS changes who is available to play with and against" |
| TIME → temporal opportunity | TIME is a family: clocks, restarts, windows. Touch limits → RULES. Usable ≠ clock time | "TIME changes the temporal conditions (clocks, restarts, windows) that bound usable time" |
| DIRECTION → target structure | Holds (frozen) | As frozen |
| SCORING → assigned value | Holds. **It is a different kind of manipulation:** evaluative, not structural | "SCORING changes what outcomes are worth" |

**Symmetry warning.**
- The first four change **structural conditions of play**. SCORING changes the **evaluation of outcomes**.
- Presenting five parallel arrows hides that difference.

**Interactions.** SCORING can change how players exploit SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION **without altering those manipulations**:
- regain bonuses changed spatial spread (acute);
- value changes shifted shot locations (competition);
- the two-point arc makes the area beyond 40 m newly valuable (descriptive).

This happens through players' and opponents' responses (§2). **Status: SUPPORTED for behaviour (acute / competition); learning UNKNOWN.**

---

## 25. The bigger evidence pattern

**Across SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME, DIRECTION and SCORING:**
- Acute within-practice evidence (and, for SCORING, competition evidence) is plentiful.
- Evidence about what players **learn, retain or transfer** is scarce. For SCORING it is absent.

**Strongest defensible synthesis (SUPPORTED, as a statement about the distribution of evidence):**

> **Across five levers, research provides substantially more evidence about how practice manipulations change behaviour during practice than about what players subsequently learn from them. This describes the evidence base. It does not show that learning effects are small.**

## 29. Volunteer coach test (U12 Gaelic, wet Tuesday)

> **What changing the score actually changes.** It changes what things are worth, nothing else. The pitch, the numbers and the goals stay the same. But because players chase what's worth more, they'll start playing differently.
>
> **Why a bonus can be useful.** Make a score after a switch worth an extra point and you'll see players looking for the switch. It gets something happening that might otherwise be rare.
>
> **Why it can fool you.** You'll see lots of switches, and it'll look like learning. It might just be players being smart about your points. Some of those switches will be the wrong play.
>
> **Allowed isn't the same as a real choice.** A bonus leaves the other options allowed. But if the bonus is huge, nobody sensible will take them. Then it's a rule in disguise.
>
> **Worth more isn't better.** A two-pointer is worth double, but if it only goes over a third as often as a closer point, the point was the better shot. Distance, pressure and who's kicking all matter. So does what happens if it misses.
>
> **Doing it more doesn't prove they've learned it.** More of the rewarded action only shows the points worked while they were on.
>
> **Taking the bonus away.** Go back to normal scoring and watch. But count the **right** switches, not **all** switches. If they switch less, that might be good: maybe they're only doing it when it's on. Tonight only tells you what happens straight after. Look again next week.

## 30. SCORING V1: final verdict

### A. Does SCORING survive independently?

**YES BUT NARROWED.**
- It survives as **assigned value**.
- Moved out of SCORING:
  - validity conditions ("only counts if…") → RULES;
  - competition and stakes → a separate property;
  - score state → INFORMATION (provisional);
  - what happens next → CONSEQUENCES (provisional).

### B. Exact V1 definition

> **SCORING is the value assigned to outcomes or actions in a practice: what earns or costs points, how many relative to each other, and who receives them. It changes what outcomes are worth, not which actions are allowed, where scoring can happen, or what happens next.**

### C. Frozen principles

| # | Principle | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| **SC-1** | **Changing what outcomes are worth changes what players tend to attempt.** This is well shown in real competitions and in a few acute practice studies. The size and direction of the effect vary by setting. | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** (competition natural experiments; acute practice; merges SCO-1 and candidate A) |
| **SC-2** | **An increase in a rewarded behaviour during practice is not, by itself, evidence that the behaviour has been learned.** Whether it persists, and whether players recognise when it is useful after the reward is removed, is **UNKNOWN**. | **SUPPORTED** (as a statement of the evidence base: no removal, retention or transfer study) |
| **SC-3** | **A higher-value option is not automatically the better option.** Its worth depends on value × probability of success, plus what follows: possession, field position, time, score state, opponent and player ability. | **SUPPORTED** (as analysis of options: arithmetic; shot models in AFL, Gaelic pre-2025 and rugby; competition decisions). Not a learning claim |
| **SC-4** | **Changing values can produce behaviour nobody intended, and opponents adapt to whatever is rewarded.** The intended net effect is not guaranteed. | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** (competition natural experiments with conflicting results; one practitioner report) |

### D. Practitioner heuristics

- A bonus can draw attention to a problem without banning alternatives (candidate B). Attention is unmeasured; the choice is legal, not necessarily meaningful.
- Remove the bonus and look again (candidate E). Look at appropriateness, not frequency; look again after a delay.
- End sessions in a normal game (Play-Practice-Play / Whole-Part-Whole / GAA "back to the Game").
- Basketball "good shot" = value × likelihood.

### E. PáircVision hypotheses

| # | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| **H-1** | Process scoring rewards the action, not its appropriateness, and may replace the real objective with the coach's proxy (SCO-6) |
| **H-2** | If players chase the bonus when it is the wrong play, the scoring system is dominating the practice (candidate D; logical design principle) |
| **H-3** | Use the smallest scoring bias that changes the problem (candidate C; untested, undefined threshold) |
| **H-4** | Normal Gaelic scoring (goal / point / two-pointer) already poses value-and-risk decisions. Invented bonuses may teach a different value calculus (SCO-8 implication) |
| **H-5** | Part of SCORING's effect is informational (attention, coach preference), to be tested under INFORMATION |
| **H-6** | Asking whether the rewarded action helped (candidate F) separates reward from effect (grounded in DIR-3) |

### F. Key coach question

> **"Would this still be the right play if the points were normal?"**

- It separates **value set by the coach** from **value in the game**.
- It directs attention to **appropriateness** rather than frequency.
- It links naturally to returning to normal scoring (§13–14).
- It claims nothing about learning.

### G. What SCORING does NOT mean

| Not SCORING | Belongs to |
|---|---|
| Area, shape, zones | **SPACE** |
| Players available, overloads | **NUMBERS** |
| Clocks, restarts, windows | **TIME** |
| Where scoring can happen; target number, form and polarity | **DIRECTION** |
| Allowed / required actions; "only counts if…" validity conditions; pass counts before shooting | **RULES** |
| Score state; score visibility; cues; instructions; what a bonus signals about coach preference | **INFORMATION** (provisional) |
| What happens next (possession, restarts, overloads after an outcome) | **CONSEQUENCES** (provisional) |
| Competing to win; stakes; winners and losers | **Separate property** (research debt) |
| The resulting movement of players and opening of lanes | **Emergent behaviour**, not the manipulation |

Where this document and the [Candidate 09 Final Gate](./phase1-candidate-09-information-final-gate.md) differ on classification, **that gate governs**. The original finding above and its evidence status are unchanged.
>
> - Score state: a selected scenario → **GAME MOMENT**; score / time visibility → an internal information consideration only (§18 note).
> - Cues, instructions, and how a coach explains or signals a bonus → **COACH INTERVENTION** (deferred; pending research, not a completed framework). The research questions in §J.1 remain open there.
> - Candidate 08 separately re-homed the RULES row above (validity rules → SCORING; action requirements → ACTIONS).

> **Later taxonomy clarification (Candidate 10).** Candidate 10 subsequently dissolved CONSEQUENCES as a Phase 1 property. Where this document and the [Candidate 10 Final Gate](./phase1-candidate-10-consequences-final-gate.md) differ on classification, **that gate governs**. ### H. Transfer verdict

**Known:**
- Changing values changes behaviour acutely in a few practice studies (pressing spread; load; enjoyment).
- Changing values changes choices in real competitions, sometimes with side effects.
- Higher value is not automatically better (analysis).

**Unknown:**
- Whether any rewarded behaviour persists after the reward is removed.
- Whether players learn when the behaviour is useful.
- Whether altered scoring improves decision quality.
- Whether altered scoring transfers to matches.
- Whether bonus size matters.
- Whether keeping score or competing helps learning.
- Anything about scoring manipulations in Gaelic or hurling **practice**.

### I. Five-lever synthesis

**Defensible only in this form:**
- SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION change the **structural conditions** of play.
- SCORING changes the **value of outcomes**.
- SCORING can change how players exploit the other four without altering them, through players' and opponents' responses.
- Across all five, evidence about behaviour **during** practice far outweighs evidence about **learning**.

### J. Research debt

1. **INFORMATION:** scoring as attention or signal; explicit vs implicit bonuses; score state; score visibility; coach-preference signalling.
2. **CONSEQUENCES:** the provisional boundary (§19); bundled rewards; win conditions; reset scoring.
3. **Competition / stakes:** placement as a separate practice property; no sport-practice evidence.
4. **RULES:** validity rules ("only counts if…"); the legal vs meaningful choice threshold.
5. **Gaelic rule text:** Official Guide Part 2 and the March 2026 guidance document are unread (refused). Verify before any Gaelic wording is published.
6. **Gonzalez-Artetxe rule types:** if their "artificial rules" were scoring rules, that programme becomes the only reference-game evidence for SCORING.
7. **Gaelic post-2025 shot probabilities:** no peer-reviewed 2025+ model located.
8. **Source access:** every SCORING claim is abstract-level or lower.

### K. Freeze recommendation

**FREEZE SCORING V1**

**Reason.**
- The surviving lever is narrow and honest: a two-sentence definition and four principles, each with its true evidence status.
- Its central learning question is recorded as **UNKNOWN**, and no further search in this programme has produced removal, retention or transfer evidence.
- Freezing fixes what PáircVision may and may not say. It does **not** assert that bonus scoring works.
- **Conditions attached:**
  1. Revisit the INFORMATION, CONSEQUENCES and competition boundaries when those levers are audited.
  2. Verify the Gaelic rule text before publication.
  3. Re-open SC-2 immediately if a removal / retention study is found.

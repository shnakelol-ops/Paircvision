## 0. Headline answers

**Central question:** *How is Gaelic football currently understood and taught as a game?*

It is understood through three separate bodies of material that barely reference each other:

1. **Official principles of play.** Ulster and Connacht GAA publish lists of attacking and defending principles.
   - The lists are, almost word for word, **Allen Wade's 1967 FA soccer principles**: penetration, width, mobility, improvisation, support/depth; delay, depth, balance, concentration, control/restraint.
   - The Ulster and Connacht texts appear to share one lineage (**duplicate flag**, §2).
   - They are **imported, not Gaelic-derived**. They are **named, not operationalised**. No evidence base is cited in what we could see.
2. **One academic game model.** Mangan et al. (2022) adapt Tactical Periodization to Gaelic football.
   - It adds a fifth moment, **contest for possession**, to TP's four.
   - It is **theoretical and prescriptive**. It is a **method for building a team's own game model**, not a universal description of the game.
   - It is **not empirically validated**. TP itself has no empirical base (Afonso et al. 2020, from Pass 2).
3. **A growing performance-analysis literature.** It is concentrated in a few Irish research groups.
   - It describes possession sources, kickouts, turnovers, shot location and possession duration, and associates some of them with winning.
   - It is **descriptive and associational**, mostly **inter-county**, and almost entirely **pre-2025 rules**.

Official GAA pathway material (Tobar, Go Games, the Games-Based Approach) says **how** to coach (games, age-appropriate formats). It also defines "tactical prowess" in a sentence. We found **no official GAA national principles-of-play framework** and **no age-graded Game Understanding curriculum**.

**Secondary question:** *Is there already a sufficiently strong model that PáircVision should adopt or translate?*

**No.** There are useful pieces but no model strong enough to adopt:
- The official principles are too vague to observe and are borrowed from 1967 soccer.
- Mangan et al. is a TP template whose content is, by design, team-specific.
- The match evidence describes a game whose kickout, numbers and scoring rules have since changed.

**Landscape verdict: C** (§30). Existing frameworks provide pieces, but a substantial Game Understanding framework would still need to be developed. That framework must be operational, rule-current, and honest about what is association, what is theory and what is coaching wisdom.

**U12 test** (§29): **PARTLY.** We can say in 30 seconds what support is *for*. No Gaelic source tells a volunteer what good support *looks like* without either staying a slogan or becoming a pattern.

**Recommended next research** (§31): **not** a SUPPORT deep-audit first. First, a short **current-rules game-structure audit**: what moments and problems does the post-2025 game actually contain, including restarts? Then **operational behaviours**, with SUPPORT as the test case.

## 2. Existing principles of play — Ulster and Connacht deep audit

### 2.1 What the principles are (as far as can be established, SIO)

**Attacking principles:** *Penetration, Width, Mobility, Improvisation*. Support is described inside penetration and width rather than as a separately defined principle, in what we could see.

| Principle | Definition as rendered by the search index | Operational? |
|---|---|---|
| **Penetration** | Teams must be competent at moving the ball through the defence "as quickly and accurately as possible". | No. No criterion for "through", "quickly" or "accurately". |
| **Width** | Forwards must make space for penetrating players. Taking possession into the opposing half requires support "at pace", and forwards must create space, "which brings into play the principle of Width". | No. Width is justified by its purpose (space for penetrators) but not described as a position or behaviour. |
| **Mobility** | Not recoverable from summaries. | UNKNOWN |
| **Improvisation** | Not recoverable from summaries. | UNKNOWN |
| **Support** (embedded) | "Support play has to be at pace." Supporting players "should give verbal and non-verbal signals to the players in possession and to other players" (Connacht). | Partly. Signalling is observable; "at pace" is not a support criterion. |
| **Length / "long pitch"** (other GAA material) | "Making the pitch/area as long as possible both forward and back to create space between the lines"; "support on the ball". | Partly. It describes a team shape (stretch the pitch lengthways) and its purpose. |

**Defending principles:** *Delay, Depth, Balance, Concentration, Control or Restraint.* The Kerr & Flynn material adds tackling as the core skill.

| Principle | Definition as rendered | Operational? |
|---|---|---|
| **Delay** | "The key initial function of good defence is to DELAY": prevent the forward from doing what he wishes and give time for the defence to recover, rather than simply dispossessing. | Partly. The purpose is clear; there are no behavioural criteria (distance, body angle) in what we saw. |
| **Depth** | "Getting players behind the ball and in times of crisis getting bodies into the area to provide cover and depth." | Partly. "Behind the ball" is observable; "crisis" is not defined. |
| **Balance** | "About good Communication, Vision and Decision Making by the defender"; "the defence must maintain balance through depth and cover". | No. It is defined through other abstractions. |
| **Concentration** | "A concentration of defenders in the high scoring area." | Partly. It is observable if "high scoring area" is defined; the research-defined scoring zone (§4) predates the two-point arc. |
| **Control / Restraint** | Stay "close enough to avoid a score being taken but not too close to allow the forward to dummy or side step", while waiting for the right moment to tackle or "force the forward into a potentially low scoring area" (Kerr & Flynn). | **Yes, partly.** This is the most operational statement found: a distance band (a relation, not a number), a timing decision and a forcing direction. |
| **Tackling** | "Heads, Hands and Feet" to slow and control the opponent; body position when jockeying; channelling. | Technique plus tactic. It is observable. |

### 2.3 Same concepts, different names? — and the bigger finding

- **Ulster and Connacht do not use different names. They use the same text.** The distinctive sentences appear in both, for example "the key initial function of good defence is to DELAY" and "support play has to be at pace". **Duplicate flag:** treat them as **one source**, not two.
- **The list is Allen Wade's.** Wade's *FA Guide to Training and Coaching* (1967) and *Principles of Team Play* set out penetration, support/depth, mobility, width and improvisation in attack, and delay, depth, balance, concentration and control/restraint in defence. The Gaelic list matches almost term for term.
- **Classification: IMPORTED (soccer, 1967), not Gaelic-derived.**
- This is **not a criticism** in itself. Invasion games share structural problems. But it means:
  1. no Gaelic-specific test was ever applied to the list, as far as we can see;
  2. Gaelic-specific features are absent from the principles. These include no offside, the hand pass, the solo, the mark, the kickout, high fielding, the two-point arc and the three-in-each-half rule.
  3. "Official" here means "officially circulated", not "established for this sport".

### 2.4 Evidence status

**OFFICIAL COACHING PRINCIPLE · IMPORTED · THEORETICAL.**
- No evidence was cited in what we could see.
- The coach's *practice* of "coaching through principles of play" has itself been studied in soccer (e.g. a paper on coaches' knowledge generation through principles of play, SIO). That is evidence about coaches' use, not about the principles' validity.

## 3. Mangan et al. (2022) — deep audit

**Citation:** Mangan S., Collins K., Burns C., O'Neill C. (2022). *A tactical periodisation model for Gaelic football.* International Journal of Sports Science & Coaching, 17(1), 208–219. A GAA Learning presentation of the model also exists. **Access: AO / SIO. Full text blocked on both the journal and the repository.**

| Question | Finding | Confidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Exact purpose** | "To present a Tactical Periodisation model for Gaelic football, taking the sport's game structure, physical and technical demands and amateur status into account." | M (abstract) |
| **Theoretical basis** | Tactical Periodization (Frade, Porto); integration of tactical, technical, physical and physiological components; "train while playing" / specificity. | M |
| **Game model** | TP requires each team to define its **own** game model. The paper supplies a template and example principles, not a universal model. | M (inferred from TP structure plus the summary of an "example of principles of play scenario" figure) |
| **Phases / moments** | TP's four (offensive organisation, defensive organisation, defence→attack transition, attack→defence transition) **plus a fifth: contest for possession**. This covers kickouts and throw-ins; the GAA presentation justifies it with "~44 kickouts a match" and kickouts originating 29–33 % of possessions. | M |
| **Gaelic adaptations named** | No offside in attacking organisation. Transitions allow hand pass, kick pass or carry, giving "a greater number of options in transitional moments in comparison to soccer or rugby". | M |
| **Sub-principles** | **UNKNOWN.** A figure titled "Example of principles of play scenario" exists; its content was not recoverable. | — |
| **Training implications** | Weekly morphocycle adapted to amateur (two to three sessions) schedules; principles rotated through training units; minimal isolated physical work. | M |
| **Evidence base** | Match-demand data (kickout counts, possession origins, running demands) as context. **No test of the model.** | M |
| **Validation** | **None found.** No study was found that implements the model and measures learning or performance. | M |
| **Limitations** | The paper's own limitations are UNKNOWN. External: TP has "absence of empirical data" (Afonso et al. 2020). The Gaelic numbers cited are pre-2025 rules. | M |
| **Subsequent use** | The same research group (Timmons, Collins & Mangan 2025) reviewed match-play science, but that is not a test of the model. Practitioner blogs and commercial providers (e.g. DSS Coaching, practitioner blogs) discuss "Tactical Periodisation in Gaelic Games". **No independent empirical test found.** | M |

**Classification:** **THEORETICAL · PRESCRIPTIVE (as a method) · DESCRIPTIVE (of game structure, pre-2025).** It is not empirically validated.

**What it contributes to Game Understanding:**
1. **A Gaelic-specific moment structure.** "Contest for possession" is a genuinely Gaelic addition to the soccer template, and the most defensible single structural idea found in this audit.
2. A stated recognition that **Gaelic transitions contain more options** than soccer or rugby transitions.

**What it cannot contribute:** universal principles. TP explicitly makes principles **team-specific**. Adopting Mangan's example principles as PáircVision's principles would turn one game model into a universal one, which is the error §13 warns against.

- **What it studies:** possessions, sources, durations, locations and outcomes. It **does not** study off-ball behaviour: support positions, width, depth, pressure on the ball, compactness or line-breaking.

- **Rule era:** everything except the 2026 sub-elite paper (which used 2023–24 data) predates the 2025 trial rules. **No peer-reviewed study of the post-2025 men's game was found.**

## 5. Game moments — what Gaelic sources actually use

**We do not impose TP's four moments.**

| Source | How the game is divided | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ulster / Connacht principles | **Attack / Defence** only | Transitions and restarts absent |
| Mangan et al. / GAA TP presentation | Offensive organisation · Defensive organisation · Transition D→A · Transition A→D · **Contest for possession** | The only Gaelic source with five moments |
| Performance-analysis literature | By **possession origin**: own kickout · opposition kickout · turnover won · throw-in; plus counter-attack vs other attack | Origin-based, not phase-based. The most empirically grounded division. |
| GAA webinars | "Transition from defence to attack" as a topic | Topic-based |
| LGFA resources | "Develop quick attack from defence" | Topic-based |
| Tobar GBA | In attack / in defence (tactical prowess definition) | Two-way |
| Practitioner / media language | **Settled vs unsettled** attack/defence; "turnover ball"; "primary possession"; "breaking ball"; "kickout battle"; "set defence"; "sweeper" | **SINGLE / MULTIPLE PRACTITIONER.** Widely used; **no formal definitions found** |

**Findings:**
1. **Gaelic sources do not agree on a moment structure.** Three incompatible framings exist: attack/defence, TP-plus-contest, and possession origin.
2. **The empirically strongest framing is origin-based.** Turnovers (~40–48 %) and kickouts (~45–49 %) each account for roughly half of possessions and scores (pre-2025).
3. **Restarts are a distinct Gaelic problem** on every framing that includes them (§9).
4. **Settled vs unsettled** is widely used by coaches and media but undefined. It may be the most coach-useful distinction and the least evidenced.
5. **Under the 2026 rules**, the three-in-each-half rule guarantees a minimum attacking and defending presence in each half at all times. This changes the relationship between "defensive organisation" and "transition". **UNKNOWN** how; no study exists.

*This finding is about the content of moments. It does not reopen Phase 1's GAME MOMENT lever, which is about which moment a practice contains.*

The only operational items are **outcome indicators** (turnovers, shot location) and **one pattern** (McGeehin's zones). The behavioural layer in between — what a player does, and where — is **missing from Gaelic sources**.

## 8. Transition

### 8.1 Defence → attack

| Layer | What sources say |
|---|---|
| **Practitioner principle** | "Transition from Defence to Attack" (GAA webinar, 2020); LGFA "quick attack from defence"; commentary on "running game" teams (Armagh, Donegal). Mangan: Gaelic transitions have more options (hand pass, kick, carry). |
| **Descriptive match evidence** | Turnovers are about half of possessions. Winners gained more turnovers; "defensive counterattacking" discriminated winners (2014–15). Winners scored more from turnovers (2021–22). Turnovers won in contact were associated with scoring (sub-elite 2023–24). |
| **Contradiction in the evidence** | Counter-attacks of 26–35 s and **longer** passing sequences were more successful (2007–08). Durations of 31–90 s scored more, **but** regression favoured 1–10 passes (sub-elite 2023–24). "Prolonged possessions with fewer attacking phases" returned more scores (2020–21, summary). These results **do not support "transition = fast and direct"**. They also do not support "slow". Definitions of "counter-attack", "phase" and "possession" differ between papers. |
| **Causal claim** | **None exists.** No study manipulates transition behaviour. |

### 8.2 Attack → defence

| Layer | What sources say |
|---|---|
| **Practitioner principle** | Delay first; recover; get bodies behind the ball (Ulster / Connacht). |
| **Descriptive match evidence** | Almost none specific to the defending side of a lost turnover. Turnover *location* is analysed as a possession start, not as a defensive-transition problem. |
| **Causal claim** | None. |

**Finding:** "transition" is **MATCH-EVIDENCE SUPPORTED as important** (volume, and association with winning). What **good** transition behaviour is remains **UNKNOWN**, and the fragments that exist **contradict the fast-break stereotype**.

The three-in-each-half rule (2025+) now **guarantees** at least three attackers staying forward. That changes what defence→attack transition can use. No evidence yet.

## 9. Kickouts / restarts — a distinct Gaelic problem

**Why distinct:**
- Before 2025, kickouts began roughly half of all possessions and were the single largest source of scores (own kickout 45 %).
- Mangan et al. made "contest for possession" a separate moment.
- No other invasion game has an equivalent restart-after-every-score of this frequency. The closest analogues are the AFL centre bounce and rugby restarts (§17).

**Map (pre-2025 evidence unless stated):**

| Element | Evidence | Status under 2026 rules |
|---|---|---|
| **Possession** | The team taking the kickout won ~65 % (sub-elite). Successful LGFA teams won ~80 % own / ~29 % opposition. | Retention rates **UNKNOWN** under the new rules |
| **Short options** | Short kickouts inside the 45 m most successful (sub-elite, 2020–21) | **Largely removed.** Kickouts must travel beyond the 40 m arc; the kicking team's receivers must start outside the arc. |
| **Medium / long options** | Kickouts to the wings produced scoring opportunities on 31 % of occasions (TP presentation, pre-2025) | Now the **main** option set; mark rules interact |
| **Space creation / overload** | Opposition numbers inside the 65 related to kickout win percentage non-linearly (sub-elite) | The 13 m exclusion, the 40 m arc and the three-in-each-half rule all change the geometry |
| **Press** | LGFA: zonal or player-to-player presses, by number of players; zonal press with 11+ among the most effective | Men's game: the max-11 outfield in one half ceiling applies. Press effectiveness post-2025 **UNKNOWN**. |
| **Escape** | Quick kickouts (0–10 s) did better (sub-elite) | Quick kickouts still permitted (13 m rule). Effect **UNKNOWN**. |
| **Breaking ball** | Practitioner term; commentary says the new rules produced "more high fielding and contests" (the FRC's stated aim) | **No study found** |
| **Subsequent attack** | After a won kickout, the most common outcome was a turnover, then a score (sub-elite) | UNKNOWN |
| **Defending the opposition kickout** | LGFA defensive strategies (three defined) | Men's post-2025 **UNKNOWN** |

**Finding:** kickouts are the **best-researched Gaelic-specific problem**, and the **most invalidated by rule change**. Any Game Understanding content on kickouts written from pre-2025 evidence would describe a restart that no longer exists in the men's game.

## 10. Current rules — the reference game

**Access: SIO for all rows.** The official reference is the **GAA Official Guide effective 27 March 2026** / **GAA Rule Book, April 2026**, together with the **March 2026 guidance document on applying the football rules**. Neither could be opened. **Verify every row below against the official text before any Game Understanding content depends on it.**

**Timeline:**
1. **2025:** Football Review Committee (FRC) rule "enhancements" used on trial.
2. **Special Congress, October 2025:** all FRC proposals passed into the rule book (reported as 61–62 motions), in force from **1 January 2026**.
3. **2026:** a separate **Expert Advisory Group** (chaired by Kevin McStay) is trialling further rules in the third-level Ryan Cup: a fisted-points ban, a backcourt rule and a three-consecutive-hand-pass limit. **These are trials, not current rules.**

| Rule area | Current rule (as summarised) | Why it matters for Game Understanding |
|---|---|---|
| **Players in each half** | Each team keeps **at least three outfield players in each half** at all times (the goalkeeper does not count). Penalty reported as a free from the offending team's 20 m line. So **at most 11 outfield players** can attack or defend in one half. | Changes numbers in every moment. It removes the "15-behind-the-ball" defence and guarantees forward outlets. **It is the largest structural change for team shape.** |
| **Kickout** | Taken from the 20 m line; **must travel beyond the 40 m arc**. All players **13 m** from the ball. The kicking team's players wanting to receive directly must be **outside the arc** when it is kicked. A kickout not clearing the arc is a free against. | Removes the short kickout. Kickout research (§9) is largely superseded. |
| **Scoring** | **Two points** for a score kicked from on or outside the **40 m arc** (from play or a free) without touching another player. | Changes the shot-value map. The pre-arc "scoring zone" (32 m) is now one of two zones with different values. |
| **Solo and go** | A fouled player may restart immediately with a **solo (toe-tap)**, running unchallenged for ~4 m before full play resumes. (It replaced a requirement to kick-pass a minimum distance.) | Fast restarts after fouls; changes defensive recovery after conceding a free. |
| **Advanced mark** | Awarded for a clean catch **inside the 20 m line** from a kick **from beyond the 45 m line** (as summarised). | Rewards long direct kick-passing into the scoring area. |
| **Kickout mark** | A mark for a clean catch from a kickout (details not verified) | Rewards high fielding at restarts |
| **Goalkeeper** | **UNRESOLVED.** Summaries conflict. One says (2025) the goalkeeper cannot receive a pass from a teammate while both are in their own half. Another says (2026) a pass back to the goalkeeper is only allowed when both are inside the large rectangle, possibly only when the ball entered it from an opponent. | Either version **removes the goalkeeper as an easy recycling outlet under pressure**. This directly affects the §23 "escape pressure" problem. |
| **Dissent / delay** | Stronger penalties (e.g. advancing the free) | Minor for GU |
| **Hand pass / fisted points / backcourt** | **Trials only (Ryan Cup 2026).** Not current. | Watch: a backcourt rule or hand-pass limit would change support and escape behaviour substantially. |
| **LGFA** | Separate rules. The LGFA approved new playing rules and is trialling 12 rules in 2026 leagues and colleges. | **LGFA evidence must not be transferred to the men's game (or vice versa) without checking rule equivalence.** |

**Reported effect (SIO; RTÉ Brainstorm, 2026):** average championship scoring rose from ~34 points per game (2024) to ~46 (2025) and held at that level in 2026. The same source describes several viable styles: aggressive running game, playing through the lines, and physicality/primary possession. That is **commentary, not a study**, but it suggests the post-2025 game does **not** favour one tactical solution. This is relevant to §14.

**Candidate structure (not chosen):** PRINCIPLE → BEHAVIOUR → GAME CUE / RELATIONSHIP → POSSIBLE SOLUTIONS.
- The landscape suggests this chain is **necessary but under-specified**.
- Principles alone are unobservable. Behaviours alone become patterns.
- The cue/relationship layer is what would make a behaviour context-dependent rather than fixed. For example, the right support distance depends on where the defender is, not on a number.
- **No Gaelic source supplies that layer.** Soccer's FUT-SAT supplies it partly, as positional definitions relative to the ball and the opponents (§17).
- One structural amendment is suggested by the evidence: **technique / execution constraints** belong in the chain (§16). Otherwise a failed solution is read as a failed perception.

## 12. Perception / decision requirements

**Honest baseline:** research specifies decision information for Gaelic football at a **very general** level only.

**Gaelic evidence (Frontiers 2023, AO / SIO):** senior and U17 academy players watched paused video of in-possession moments. They reported three information sources:
1. **pre-match context:** coach tactics and instructions, match importance, opposition status;
2. **current match context:** score, time remaining;
3. **visual information:** player positioning and field space, and visual search strategy.

Senior players integrated these sources in a more complex way and projected future scenarios. Individual differences moderated both groups.

| Major concept | What must the player notice? | What decision? | Possible actions | Does research specify? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Support | Ball carrier's pressure; defenders' positions; space | Where and when to offer | Offer short, forward, lateral or deep; decoy; stay | **No** (Gaelic). Partly in soccer. |
| Ball carrier options | Positioning, space, score/time (Frontiers) | Shoot / carry / pass / play into space (Tobar) | Kick-pass, hand pass, solo, shot, two-point attempt | **Partly:** the categories of information, not the cues |
| Pressure (defending) | Distance to attacker; attacker's options; cover behind | Delay, tackle or force | Jockey, channel, tackle | **Partly** (Kerr & Flynn, practitioner) |
| Transition recognition | Possession change; own and opposition numbers; space | Go now or secure | Carry, early kick, recycle | **No** |

| Shot selection | Distance and angle; arc; pressure; score/time | Shoot for 1, shoot for 2, or work closer | Point, two-pointer, goal attempt, recycle | **Partly** (location → efficiency; pre-arc) |

**Connection to Phase 1 (without reopening it):** this is where Game Understanding would supply what Phase 1's **GI-2** needs to know — *what information must be preserved in practice* — and what **Decision Allocation** needs to know — *which decisions are there to be allocated*. At present the literature can name the categories of information but not the specific relationships. **UNKNOWN is the correct label for most cells.**

## 13. Principle vs pattern

| Level | Definition used here | Gaelic example found | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Principle** | A general purpose any team must address, stated without a specific solution | "Delay the attacker so the defence can recover" | Too vague to observe |
| **Game model** | One team's chosen way to address the principles | Mangan's TP template (per-team by design) | Mistaken for universal |
| **Team tactic** | A chosen solution to a principle | Sweeper; zonal kickout press; "running game" | Taught as *the* way |
| **Pattern** | A rehearsed sequence of roles/positions | McGeehin: to Zone B, hold 3–5 s, release to Zone C | Mistaken for a principle |
| **Play** | A set, scripted move | Kickout routines (practitioner) | — |
| **Technique** | How an action is executed | Tackle ("heads, hands, feet"); hand pass; solo | Mistaken for decision failure (§16) |

**Finding:** existing Gaelic material **mixes levels without labelling them**.
- The provincial handouts put principles (delay), technique (tackle), and a pattern (zones) side by side.
- A coach reading them cannot tell which statements are universal and which are one author's solution.
- **PáircVision's first design obligation, if it builds anything, is level-labelling.** This is a finding about the landscape, not a principle.

## 16. Technique ↔ Game Understanding

**Where execution limits tactical possibility in Gaelic football** (structural reasoning plus sources):
- **Kick-pass range and accuracy.** They limit direct play, the advanced mark and escaping a press over the top. After the 40 m-arc rule, **kickout length is a precondition** for any kickout strategy.
- **Two-point shooting.** Only exists as an option for players who can kick 40 m+ accurately. At U12 it may not exist at all, depending on pitch and rules.
- **Hand pass under pressure.** "Unsuccessful hand passes" discriminated losers (2014–15). That is a technical *and* decision indicator; the data cannot separate them.
- **Solo / carry and breaking the tackle.** Counter-attack success "requires players to have the ability to carry the ball, break the tackle and provide support" (2011).
- **Tackle.** Kerr & Flynn treat tackling as the core of defence. A player may read the moment correctly and still fail the tackle.
- **Catching / high fielding.** Rewarded by the kickout mark and the advanced mark.

**Evidence status:** the idea that decision and execution interact is **established in the measurement literature**. Kinnerk's GPOI scores control, decision and execution **separately** (AO). Otherwise it is **THEORETICAL** in Gaelic football.

**Implication for any future framework (landscape observation, not a design):**
- Observed failure has at least three explanations: *didn't notice*, *chose poorly*, *couldn't execute*.
- The GPOI-style separation of decision from execution is the only Gaelic-used instrument found that protects against treating every failed action as tactical misunderstanding.
- This also matches Phase 1's inference limits (produced ≠ learned), without reopening them.

| **Team shape (compactness, width)** | **No** (GPS database exists but was not analysed tactically) | — | Balance / compactness / width | — |

**Finding:**
- The **event** layer (possessions, turnovers, kickouts, shots) is measurable and has been measured.
- The **relationship** layer (support, pressure, compactness, line-breaks) has **not been measured in Gaelic football**. It would need positional data or trained observer coding with reliability checks.
- **The principles most coaches talk about are exactly the ones nobody has measured.**
- *(Research observation only. No Event Stats features implied.)*

## 21. Conflicts — tactical trade-offs

| Tension | Evidence on each side | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Retain vs progress** | Retention and low turnovers associated with winning. Progression / penetration is an official principle. Longer counter-attack sequences more successful (2007–08). | **Real trade-off; unresolved** |
| **Width vs compact support** | Width to draw defenders (official). Hand-pass support needs proximity (practitioner). | **Real; the hand pass sharpens it** relative to soccer |
| **Direct vs patient attack** | Advanced-mark rule rewards direct play. Durations of 26–90 s associated with scoring. Regression favours 1–10 passes (sub-elite). | **Contradictory evidence**; style-dependent (RTÉ 2026) |
| **Immediate counter vs secure possession** | Turnovers won in contact associated with scoring. "Prolonged possessions with fewer phases" returned more (summary; flagged). | **Unresolved** |
| **Press vs delay** | Delay is the "key initial function" (official). Zonal 11+ kickout press among the most effective (LGFA). | **Context-dependent:** restart vs open play |
| **Man-oriented vs zonal** | LGFA kickout strategies include both | **Style choice**; no open-play evidence |
| **Fixed shape vs adaptive spacing** | Mangan (TP: team model, organised). Tobar GBA (decisions not predetermined). | **Philosophical, not empirical** |

**Finding:** the Gaelic landscape contains all seven trade-offs. **None is resolved by evidence.** A framework that states one side as a principle would be taking a **game-model position** while presenting it as a universal truth.

## 22. The "good support" stress test

**Question:** can the literature tell an U12 coach what good support looks like without turning it into a rigid pattern?

| Layer | What exists | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Universal** | Support as a purpose: the ball carrier should have options. It is implicit in every source and in Bayer's "keep possession / progress". | **Probably universal** (THEORETICAL) |
| **Contextual** | Distance, angle and timing depend on defender positions, the carrier's pressure, the hand-pass vs kick-pass range, pitch zone and rules (e.g. three-in-each-half). | **Recognised in principle; not specified anywhere in Gaelic sources** |
| **Coaching language only** | "Support at pace"; "get up in support"; "show for it"; "give him an option" | **Most Gaelic support language is at this level** |
| **Evidence** | Gaelic: a descriptive mention in counter-attack success (2011); no measurement. Cross-sport: FUT-SAT offensive coverage (soccer, validated as assessment). | **Gaelic: UNKNOWN. Cross-sport: operational, not Gaelic-tested.** |

**Result:** **the literature cannot currently tell an U12 coach what good Gaelic support looks like** without either:
- (a) leaving it as a slogan ("give options"), or
- (b) importing a soccer positional definition that ignores the hand pass, the solo and no offside, or
- (c) giving a pattern ("run off the shoulder for the hand pass").

**Buildability judgement:** buildable in principle — the purpose is universal, and a relationship layer exists cross-sport. **Not buildable from Gaelic sources alone.** It needs translation plus Gaelic-specific checking.

## 23. The "escape pressure" stress test

**Problem:** a team keeps possession in a small grid but panics when pressed high in its own half.

**How existing frameworks explain it:**

| Framework | Explanation available | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Ulster / Connacht principles | Support, width, depth (attack) | Written from the defending side (press = pressure); no escape guidance |
| Mangan / TP | Offensive organisation (build-up) or contest for possession (if after a kickout) | Content team-specific; sub-principles UNKNOWN |
| Performance analysis | Turnover location (own-half turnovers), kickout retention | Describes the outcome, not the cause |
| Tobar GBA | Tactical prowess: carry / pass / play into space | Generic options; no cue |
| Cross-sport (soccer build-up vs press) | Numerical superiority, third-player, switching, the goalkeeper as an extra player | **The goalkeeper-as-outlet solution is restricted by current Gaelic rules** (§10) |

**Concepts genuinely involved** (from the landscape, not prescribed):

| Concept | Why it's in play |
|---|---|
| Support (form, not just presence) | Grid possession may use short lateral support that a directional press cuts off |
| Width / depth | Stretching the press; an option behind / beyond the press |
| Numerical relationships | The press can commit up to 11 outfield in one half; three opponents must stay back — **a rule-created structure** |
| Goalkeeper | **Rule-restricted as a recycling option** (exact rule UNRESOLVED) |
| Switching | Pressing is usually ball-side |
| Progression / direction | A small grid often has no goal direction; the real problem has one |
| Creating space | Occupying pressers; moving to create a pass |
| Pressure recognition | *When* the press arrives and from where (perception) |
| Technical execution | Kick-pass range under pressure; hand-pass accuracy; solo under contact |
| Match context | Score and time (Frontiers: players use them) |

**Result:**
- **One principle is not enough.** The escape-pressure problem involves at least five of the principles listed in §2 and §6, one rule structure, one technical ceiling and a perception issue.
- **Real game problems are inherently multi-principle.**
- This supports treating **game problems**, not single principles, as a candidate top-level unit (§26 B / D). That is a landscape observation, not a decision.
- It also shows why a practice that only preserves possession (the grid) may **remove the very information** the real problem depends on: direction, press trigger and rule-created numbers. This connects to Phase 1 GI-2 **without reopening it**.

## 25. Gap analysis

**What existing Gaelic material explains well:**
- that attack and defence have general purposes (as imported principles);
- that defenders should delay, stay goal-side and force opponents away from scoring areas (Kerr & Flynn: the most concrete material);
- where scores come from (turnovers and kickouts) and where shots are efficient (pre-rules);
- that restarts are a distinct problem (Mangan; the PA literature);
- that youth players should play games, in small formats, making decisions (Tobar, Go Games).

**What it explains poorly:**
- **what attacking concepts look like** (support, width, penetration, mobility);
- **team-shape defence** (balance, cover, compactness);
- **transition behaviour** (evidence is contradictory);
- **what players must perceive** (categories only);
- **settled vs unsettled** play (universally used, undefined).

**What exists but is inaccessible or too academic for ordinary coaches:**
- Mangan et al.: TP vocabulary and an elite, periodisation focus;
- performance-analysis papers: paywalled, statistical, elite-focused;
- LGFA kickout strategies: academic;
- FUT-SAT (soccer) operational definitions: academic and foreign-sport.

**What is genuinely missing:**
1. **Any description of the post-2025 men's game** (moments, restarts, team shape under three-in-each-half, two-point decisions).
2. **The behaviour → cue/relationship layer** for any principle in Gaelic football.
3. **Off-ball measurement** of any kind.
4. **Gaelic developmental evidence** about Game Understanding by age.
5. **Explicit level-labelling** (principle vs tactic vs pattern vs technique).
6. **Separation of decision from execution** in ordinary coaching material (it exists only in research instruments).

**Where PáircVision would merely duplicate:**
- re-listing Wade's principles under Gaelic names (Ulster / Connacht already do it);
- a TP game-model template (Mangan already does it);
- games-based pedagogy messaging (Tobar, DSS and GameSense already do it — see Pass 2's product warning);
- kickout statistics summaries (the research groups already do it, and they are now outdated).

## 28. Coach-facing simplicity

**Can existing principles be translated into ordinary coaching language without becoming inaccurate?**

| Concept | Plain translation attempt | Accurate? |
|---|---|---|
| Delay | "Don't dive in. Slow them down until help arrives." | **Yes.** Matches source purpose and Kerr & Flynn. |
| Concentration | "Crowd the area where they score most." | **Yes**, but the "area" changed with the two-point arc |
| Pressure | "Make it hard for the player on the ball: close, not too close." | **Yes** (distance band) |
| Width | "Spread out so defenders have to choose." | **Yes as purpose**; says nothing about when |
| Support | "Give the ball carrier someone to pass to." | **True but empty.** It doesn't say where, when, or how many. |
| Balance | "Don't all go to the ball." | **Partly.** Loses the "cover" meaning. |
| Penetration | "Get the ball past defenders toward goal." | Yes as purpose |
| Transition | "When the ball changes hands, react first." | **Contested.** "React first" can imply "go fast", which the evidence doesn't support. |

**Finding:**
- **Purposes translate easily** into plain language. Behaviours and cues do not exist yet to translate.
- The risk is not jargon. It is **plain-language slogans that feel complete but guide nothing**.
- A volunteer coach does not need Tactical Periodization, ecological dynamics or PA terminology to use purposes. But purposes alone won't let them *see* good or bad play.

## 29. U12 test

**Could we currently explain "good support" to a random U12 volunteer coach in 30 seconds, tell them what to look for, and avoid giving them a rigid pattern?**

### **PARTLY.**

- **What we could say (accurate):** "Good support means the player on the ball always has someone they can safely give it to, and ideally someone further forward. Where that is depends on where the defenders are, not on a set position."
- **What we can't say (not established):** *what to look for*. For example: how close for a hand pass under pressure; whether a supporting player should be behind, beside or ahead; how many options; when to move. **No Gaelic source defines these.** Soccer sources do, for a different game.
- **Pattern risk:** the easiest way to fill the gap is a pattern ("run off his shoulder"), which is exactly what the test forbids.

**Why PARTLY and not NO:** the purpose is sound and plain, and a cross-sport relationship layer exists to translate from. **Why not YES:** the "what to look for" part would be invented or imported, not established.

### Plain-language summary (U12 coach)

- People have written down "principles of play" for Gaelic football for years. Most were borrowed from soccer in the 1960s and never really tested for our game.
- Researchers have counted lots of things in county matches: where scores come from, how many kickouts each team wins, where shots go over. But the rules changed in 2025, so a lot of that describes a slightly different game.
- Nobody has yet written down, for our game, what good support or good spacing actually *looks like*, in a way you could spot from the sideline.
- So for now, the safest things to tell kids are *what they're trying to do* ("give your teammate an option", "slow them down till help comes"). We shouldn't give them fixed positions as if those were the rules of good play.

## 30. Landscape verdict

### **C — Existing frameworks provide pieces, but a substantial Game Understanding framework still needs to be developed.**

**Why not A (translate existing):** the official Gaelic principles are imported, unoperationalised and not Gaelic-tested. Translating them would re-issue Wade 1967 in Irish clothing.

**Why not B (strong but needs synthesis):** no existing framework is strong on the layer that matters (behaviours and cues). Mangan's model is a method, not content. The match evidence describes a pre-2025 game.

**Why not D (too weak):** there is enough to build on:
- universal purposes (cross-sport, theoretical);
- a Gaelic-specific moment addition (contest for possession);
- solid descriptive facts about possession sources and shot efficiency (to be re-checked post-2025);
- one concrete defensive-behaviour source (Kerr & Flynn);
- operational cross-sport definitions to translate (FUT-SAT);
- a Gaelic decision-information study.

**Qualifications:**
- "Developed" must mean **operationalised, rule-current, level-labelled, and evidence-labelled**. It must not mean **invented**.
- Much of what gets built will be **THEORETICAL or imported**, and must be labelled so.
- **Causal claims** about which principles produce winning or learning **are not available** and should not be implied.
- Much of the **existing Gaelic match evidence needs rule-era re-checking** before use.

## 31. Next research decision (recommended, not performed)

The recommendation follows the landscape findings, not earlier preferences.

**Recommended first: a current-rules game-structure audit.**
- **Scope:** what moments and recurring problems the **post-2025** men's game contains; how the three-in-each-half rule, the 40 m-arc kickout, the two-point arc, the mark rules and the goalkeeper restriction reshape them; and whether "settled / unsettled" and "contest for possession" survive as useful divisions.
- **Include:** verification of the 2026 Rule Book text (especially the goalkeeper rule), and any post-2025 analysis (published, conference or GAA Performance Analysis Day material).
- **Why first:**
  - every other topic (support, attack, defence, transition, kickouts) currently rests on a pre-2025 evidence base (§4, §9, §10);
  - Gaelic sources disagree on how to divide the game (§5);
  - architecture candidates A–D all depend on the top-level unit (§26).
- **Includes KICKOUTS by necessity:** they are the most rule-disrupted problem.

**Recommended second: operational behaviours, with SUPPORT as the test case.**
- Translate the cross-sport relationship layer (FUT-SAT offensive coverage; basketball spacing; rugby continuity) into Gaelic terms, checked against the hand pass, the solo, no offside and the current rules.
- Record every imported element.
- SUPPORT stays a **test case**, not the first framework module. The escape-pressure test (§23) shows single-principle modules would misrepresent real problems.

**Not recommended yet:**
- **Adopting / translating an existing model:** none is strong enough (§30).
- **Age progression:** there is no Gaelic developmental evidence to audit beyond formats. Revisit after behaviours exist.
- **Deep separate audits of ATTACK / DEFENCE / TRANSITION:** premature before the game-structure audit. TRANSITION especially, whose evidence is contradictory and rule-era-dependent.

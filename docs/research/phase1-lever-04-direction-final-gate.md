## 1. Definition audit

### 1.1 The candidate

> *DIRECTION manipulates the structure of the objective: what players attack, what they protect, and what constitutes progress relative to that objective.*

### 1.2 Alternatives tested

| Alternative definition | Is it a manipulation a coach sets? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Direction of **ball travel** | No. It is an **outcome** of play | Reject as a lever definition. It is a behaviour to observe |
| Direction of **player movement** | No. Outcome | Reject (outcome) |
| **Attacking orientation** (which way a team faces) | Only through where targets are | Derivative of target structure |
| **Territorial progression** | Only if the coach **scores** territory (end zone, line) | That is target *form* (DIRECTION) or scoring value (SCORING) |
| **Target structure**: presence, number, form, location of targets | **Yes** | Core |
| **Objective structure** (target structure + what counts as success) | Partly. "What counts as success" is often a **scoring rule** | Too broad: absorbs SCORING and RULES |
| **Scoring direction** | Yes, but it is the same thing as target location plus polarity | Merge into target structure |
| **Attack / defence polarity** (who attacks which target) | **Yes** | Core |
| **Direction following turnover** (does polarity flip?) | **Yes**, as a polarity rule | Core, but reversal is the *default* of every invasion game |

### 1.3 Falsification attempts

1. **"DIRECTION is just TARGET + SCORING + SPACE + RULES + INFORMATION."**
   - Largely true for the *broad* candidate definition.
   - "What constitutes progress" is either:
     - an **emergent evaluation** (not something the coach sets); or
     - a **scoring rule** (SCORING, e.g. points for crossing a line).
   - Remove that clause and what remains is **target structure and polarity**.
2. **"Target structure is just SPACE."**
   - **Rejected.**
   - Coutinho et al. (2024) held area and numbers constant and varied only directionality (none / multi / one). Spatial exploration, inter-player distances, pace and a passing-decision measure all changed (AO).
   - Rumpf et al. (2026) compared possession and scoring formats at matched formats (AO).
   - Target structure is **independently manipulable** and has **independent acute effects**.
3. **"It is just SCORING."**
   - **Rejected for presence, number and location of targets.** A keep-ball game has no target at all, so no scoring value can be changed.
   - **Accepted for values**: goal / point / two-pointer, and bonus points for a route.

### 1.4 Verdict: **COHERENT WITH NARROWER DEFINITION**

- DIRECTION survives only as **target structure**:
  - whether a practice has something to attack and protect;
  - how many such targets;
  - of what form;
  - where they are located;
  - which team attacks which, including whether that flips when possession changes.
- **Removed from the definition:**
  - "what constitutes progress" (it becomes an *evaluation* — §7 — or a SCORING rule);
  - direction of ball and player travel (outcomes);
  - directional restrictions (RULES).
- **Honest caveat.** The surviving lever might be better named **TARGET STRUCTURE**. We keep "DIRECTION" as the lever name for continuity only. **The definition, not the name, governs.**

## 2. Taxonomy boundaries

**Rule of classification:** classify by **what the coach changes**, not by what behaviour is hoped for. One manipulation, one primary lever.

| Manipulation | Primary lever | Secondary interaction | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Add / remove targets (goal, end zone, target player, gate) | **DIRECTION** | — | Changes target presence or form |
| Number of targets | **DIRECTION** | SPACE (dispersion effects) | Target structure |
| **Location of target** (central, wide, infield, off-centre) | **DIRECTION** | SPACE | The coach moves the objective, not the area. Its effect on usable space is an interaction |
| Target form: defended (goalkeeper) vs undefended; large vs small | **DIRECTION** | NUMBERS (a goalkeeper is an extra player) | Changes what must be beaten to score |
| Polarity: who attacks which target | **DIRECTION** | — | Core |
| Reversal after turnover, **triggered by the game event** | **DIRECTION** (polarity rule) | TIME (transition window) | A manipulation **only** when compared with a non-reversing format (e.g. attack-only waves, keep-ball). Otherwise it is simply the game's structure |
| Target switched by **coach call or external cue** | **INFORMATION** | DIRECTION | What is distinctive is the **signal source** (a non-game cue), not the target set |
| Channels / zones | **SPACE** | — | Frozen in the SPACE gate |
| Forward-only / no back pass | **RULES** | DIRECTION (defined relative to the target) | It restricts actions; the target is unchanged |
| Must use wing / compulsory channel | **RULES** | SPACE | Mandates an action in a sub-area |
| Must switch before scoring | **RULES** | DIRECTION | Mandates an action |
| Must cross line within X passes / actions | **RULES** | TIME | Action-count restriction |
| Offside | **RULES** | DIRECTION | Restricts depth relative to the target |
| Countdown to reach target | **TIME** | DIRECTION | Frozen in the TIME gate (countdowns) |
| Extra attacker toward the target | **NUMBERS** | DIRECTION | Frozen in the NUMBERS gate |
| Points for a particular route or target (bonus) | **SCORING** | DIRECTION | Changes the value of options, not the target set |
| Goal / point / two-point values | **SCORING** | — | Value, not structure |
| Scoring arc distance (Gaelic 2-point arc) | **SCORING** (by competition rule) | SPACE | Where a value applies |
| Through / Around / Over | **Not a manipulation** | Observation vocabulary (§9) | Describes solutions |
| Ball / player movement direction | **Not a manipulation** | Outcome | Observed, not set |

**Note on SCORING and INFORMATION.** Neither has yet been audited as a PáircVision lever. These boundaries must be **re-checked** when they are. If SCORING is not adopted as its own lever, value manipulations should sit under **RULES**, not DIRECTION.

> **Later taxonomy clarification (Candidate 08).** Candidate 08 subsequently dissolved RULES as a peer lever. Where this document and the [Candidate 08 Final Gate](./phase1-candidate-08-rules-final-gate.md) differ on classification, **that gate governs**. The findings and evidence statuses above are unchanged.
>
> - Forward-only, no-back-pass, must-switch, must-use-wing (ball route), must-cross-within-X-passes and similar requirements specifying permitted or required actions belong to **ACTIONS**, with DIRECTION as a secondary effect where defined relative to the target.
> - Target structure (presence, number, location, form and polarity of targets) remains **DIRECTION**.
> - Offside, as a practice manipulation, is homed in **SPACE**; player confinement to a channel remains **SPACE** (Candidate 08 Final Gate §2).

> **Later taxonomy clarification (Candidate 09).** Candidate 09 subsequently dissolved INFORMATION as a Phase 1 family. Where this document and the [Candidate 09 Final Gate](./phase1-candidate-09-information-final-gate.md) differ on classification, **that gate governs**. The original finding above and its evidence status are unchanged.
>
> - A **target switched by coach call or external cue** keeps its underlying **DIRECTION / TARGET STRUCTURE** classification where the target set or polarity changes; the call itself belongs to **COACH INTERVENTION** (deferred; pending research, not a completed framework).
> - "Coach calls, cues or signals that switch targets; instructions; feedback" (§G below) likewise move to **COACH INTERVENTION** (deferred; pending research, not a completed framework).

## 4. Possession vs directional practice: final adjudication

### 4.1 Rumpf, Jäger, Lloyd & Lochmann (2026): forensic check

*Frontiers in Sports and Active Living*, doi:10.3389/fspor.2026.1784555; PubMed 41835029; PMC12979140. **AO** (abstract wording via index; full text refused by proxy).

| Item | What can be verified | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Design | **Narrative mini-review**, not a systematic review or meta-analysis | AO |
| Sample | 14 studies; **221 male** players; 5 adult studies (n = 90), 9 youth (n = 131) | AO |
| Formats | 2v2, 3v3, 4v4, 5v5, 6v6, 7v7, 10v10 | AO |
| Sport | Soccer only | AO |
| Touches | +38.7 % total; +60.4 % individual (possession vs scoring) | AO |
| Passes | +99.0 %; passes per player +55.8 % | AO |
| Physiology | Mean HR +4.35 %; max HR +6.43 %; time >90 % HRmax +39.7 % | AO |
| Physical | Total distance +12.6 %; relative +8.43 %; speed +14.2 %; low accelerations +35.9 %; high accelerations +23.6 % | AO |
| Perception | RPE +24.2 %; mental challenge +18.4 % | AO |
| Enjoyment | −6.45 %, "especially when goalkeepers participated" | AO |
| Tactical | "Limited evidence" | AO |
| **How percentages were derived** | **UNR.** A narrative review does not normally pool effects. These look like averaged percentage differences across the studies that reported each variable, **not** meta-analytic pooled effect sizes. The number of studies per variable is **UNR**. "Significantly" may refer to within-study tests | **UNR** |
| Overlap | Coutinho et al. (2024) may be among the 14 (UNR). The authors' group also published the offside and touch-restriction SSG studies | Flag: do not double-count |

**Limitations:**
- male soccer only;
- heterogeneous formats, and heterogeneous "scoring" (small goals vs goalkeeper goals);
- acute sessions only;
- percentages are not effect sizes;
- the tactical evidence is thin;
- mental challenge and enjoyment are self-report.

**Disposition.** The **direction** of the differences (possession → more touches, passes, load, mental challenge; slightly less enjoyment) is usable at abstract level. **The exact percentages must not be presented to coaches as established effect sizes.** In coach-facing language, use "more", not "+99 %".

### 4.2 Coutinho, Gonçalves, Kelly, Santos, Figueiredo, Soares & Travassos (2024): forensic check

*IJSSC* 19(6):2438–2448. **AO.**

| Item | Verified | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Sample | 18 male youth players, 13.6 ± 0.4 y | AO |
| Conditions | NO direction (keep possession), MULTI direction, ONE direction | AO |
| NO | **Highest spatial exploration index** | AO |
| MULTI | Highest game pace, total and jogging distance; greater distance to nearest **teammate** | AO |
| ONE | **Better passing decision-making** than NO or MULTI; greater distance to nearest **opponent** | AO |
| What "spatial exploration index" means | In this research group's prior work, the SEI measures how widely a player's positions vary around their own average position. It measures **positional variability**, not visual or perceptual exploration. Definition for this paper is **UNR** | UNR |
| What "passing decision-making" means | **UNR.** Likely an observational rating of whether each pass was appropriate. **Critical confound:** a "correct" pass is judged against *that game's* objective. The standard differs between keep-ball and scoring a goal, so comparing decision quality **across** conditions may compare different criteria | UNR / **major caveat** |
| Numbers of games, bouts, effect sizes | UNR | — |

**Disposition:**
- "No direction → more positional variability" is usable (AO).
- "One direction → higher-rated passing decisions" is usable **only** with the caveat that the decision criterion changes with the objective.
- Neither shows learning.

### 4.3 Final verdict on "adding direction fixes what possession grids lack"

**Not supported as stated.** Possession and target formats produce **different acute profiles**. Neither is a deficient version of the other.

- Possession formats:
  - lack a target to orient to and protect (true by construction);
  - **gain** touches, passes, positional variability and load.
- Target formats:
  - add orientation and something to protect;
  - **lose** volume.

**What PáircVision can legitimately tell a coach (no winner):**
1. Keep-ball and games with targets ask different questions. Keep-ball: *can we keep it under pressure?* Targets: *can we keep it in order to get somewhere, and stop them getting somewhere?*
2. Keep-ball gives more touches, passes and work. Targets give a direction to face and something to defend.
3. Nobody has shown which one makes players better in matches.
4. Choose by the problem you want players to meet, not by which looks more like a match.

## 5. Transfer challenge

**Final search** (September 2026): possession training → match; directional SSG training → match; retention after constraint removal; unconstrained post-tests; competition transfer; longitudinal directional interventions; end-zone and multiple-goal training transfer; goal- vs possession-oriented practice transfer.

**Result: no study found** that trains groups with and without a directional structure and then tests them in an unconstrained reference game, a delayed retention test, or competition.

|---|---|---|

## 6. Representation challenge

Four different things are regularly run together:

| Concept | Meaning | What the evidence permits |
|---|---|---|
| **Structural similarity** | The practice *looks* like the match (it has goals and two directions) | True of target games by construction. **Tells us nothing about learning** |
| **Representative information** | The practice contains the information that guides action in matches: pressure, support, target location, defenders' orientation, score | Target games add target-relative information. **Possession games keep much of the interpersonal information** (pressure, support, lanes). Both **remove** some match information: keep-ball removes the target; small or multiple goals can remove the real goal's demands; a single net removes Gaelic point and two-point options |
| **Learning effectiveness** | Players improve on a retention test | **UNKNOWN** for direction |
| **Transfer** | Improvement in competition | **UNKNOWN** for direction |

**Test: "More representative ≠ automatically better practice" → SUPPORTED.**
- This is a statement about the evidence, plus logic.
  - No direction study links a representativeness measure to learning.
  - Linear vs nonlinear pedagogy outcomes are mixed (Woods et al., 2025, carried).
  - Representativeness is multidimensional, so one format can be more representative on one dimension and less on another.
- **PáircVision may say:** "A game with goals looks more like a match, but it can still leave out important match information, and keep-ball can keep some. Looking like a match is not proof it works better."
- **PáircVision may not say:** "Directional games are more representative, therefore better."

## 7. FORWARD vs PROGRESS: final gate

**Proposition under test:** *Direction describes where an action travels. Progress describes what the action does to the game situation relative to the objective.*

### 7.1 Falsification attempts

| Attack | Result |
|---|---|
| **Circularity.** Any action can be called "progress" after a score | **Valid risk.** "Progress" must be judged **at the time**, from observable change (§8), not from the eventual outcome |
| **Rugby proves too much** | Rugby shows **pass direction** ≠ progress. But rugby progress still depends on forward **carries** and **kicks**. Territory still matters. The lesson is "a backward *pass* can enable progress", not "forward movement is irrelevant" |
| **Direct play beats elaborate play** | Tenga et al. (2010): counter-attacks more effective than elaborate attacks **against imbalanced defences** (AO; OR ≈ 1.64 from index, UNR). When the defence is unset, forward and progress largely **coincide**. This limits the distinction; it does not falsify it |
| **Possession association is confounded** | Kempe et al. (2014) (successful teams ~ possession) and the Gaelic pattern (longer possessions ~ scoring) are **associations**. Better and leading teams keep the ball. They do not show that recycling *causes* progress |
| **Operationalisation** | Pro soccer has operational measures from tracking data: D-Def disruption (Goes et al., 2019; *Entropy* 2021, same group); space-control gain (Rein et al., 2017). So progress **can** be measured, with data. Whether a coach can judge it reliably **in real time** is **UNKNOWN** |
| **Cross-sport practice** | Basketball kick-outs and reversals, ice-hockey regroups, AFL switches: **practitioner** convention (SIO), consistent with the distinction, not evidence for it |
| **Gaelic** | Forward passes are the most common pass type (*IJPAS* 2017). Backward passes and passes to goalkeepers rose from 2011 to 2023 (GAA report; descriptive, pre-2025 rules). No Gaelic study links pass direction to value |

### 7.2 Sub-claims

| Sub-claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Backwards ≠ necessarily negative | **SUPPORTED** (as a negation of a universal): rugby law, disruption models |
| Forward ≠ necessarily productive | **SUPPORTED** (as a negation of a universal): disruption and space-control models value passes by effect, not direction |
| Rapid direct forward progression can be highly effective when conditions permit | **SUPPORTED** (conditional; soccer match association) |
| Recycling is sophisticated / better | **NOT SUPPORTED.** Must not replace "always forward" |

### 7.3 Verdict

**PARTIALLY SUPPORTED. Survives as DIR-3** in this wording:

> *The direction an action travels does not by itself show whether it helped. A backward or sideways action can improve the attack's position; a forward one can worsen it. Against an unset defence, quick forward play is often most effective. Against a set defence, it often is not. Whether an action progressed the attack can only be judged relative to the target and to the defence's state at that moment.*

## 8. "Use the ball to improve the picture": execution gate

### 8.1 Marker audit

| Marker | Directly observable? | Subjective element | Duplicates lever | Can conflict with |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Defender displaced (moved / turned) | **Yes** | Whether the displacement *helped* | — (D-Def is its analytics form) | A defender moving goal-side is displacement that helps the defence |
| Free player created | Partly | "Free" is a judgement | **NUMBERS** (created superiority) | Free but facing own goal / far from target |
| Pressure reduced / escaped | Partly (distance of nearest defender) | Threshold | **TIME / SPACE** | Escaping pressure backwards may cost angle and territory |
| Useful space opened | Partly | "Useful" | **SPACE** | — |
| Numerical advantage improved | Partly | Local count | **NUMBERS** | Advantage far from target may be irrelevant |
| Attacking / scoring angle improved | **Yes** (angle to posts or goal) | — | DIRECTION-native | Better angle, but a tighter marker |
| Player receives facing the target | **Yes** (body orientation relative to target) | — | **DIRECTION-native** | Facing target but under immediate pressure |
| Defensive line broken | **Yes**, if a line exists | Defining the "line" | DIRECTION-native | Receiver isolated beyond the line |
| Scoring threat increased | **No** without a model (no grassroots Gaelic expected-score model) | Largely subjective | — | Summarises the others |

**Findings:**
1. Only **three** markers are both directly observable and DIRECTION-native:
   - receives **facing the target**;
   - **line broken**;
   - **scoring angle** improved.
2. Several markers **duplicate** SPACE, NUMBERS or TIME.
3. Markers **conflict**: escaping pressure vs keeping angle; line broken vs support lost.
4. **No validated weighting** exists to combine them.
5. "Improvement" is **only** judgeable relative to the target and the context (score, time, defensive state).

### 8.2 Verdict: **COACHING HEURISTIC ONLY**

- The phrase is **not research-derived**. The idea underneath (pass value by effect, not direction) is partially supported (DIR-3).
- It must not appear as a principle.
- **Strongest defensible plain-language meaning:**

> *"After that pass or carry, were we better placed to score, or safer on the ball, than before, whichever way it went?"*

- Coach cues to support it, all observable:
  - Did someone receive **facing the posts**?
  - Did we **get past a line** of their defenders?
  - Did we get a **better angle**?

## 9. THROUGH / AROUND / OVER: final gate

### 9.1 What it is

- It is **coaching taxonomy language**, found in:
  - **soccer governing-body principles of play**: England Football, "penetration": play through, around or over (SIO);
  - **rugby coaching media**: Rugby Coach Weekly's 1-3-3-1 framework, where wide channel = around, middle = through, kicking = over (SIO).
- It is **not primarily rugby**; it appears in both.
- Equivalents:
  - American football horizontal / vertical stretch;
  - ice-hockey carry vs dump;
  - AFL corridor / switch / long kick.
- **No peer-reviewed validation** was found.

### 9.2 Tests

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Mutually exclusive? | **No.** A kick over the half-back line into the corner is both "over" and "around". A solo through a gap followed by a lateral hand pass is sequential |
| Exhaustive? | **No.** It omits holding, recycling, drawing a foul, and going backwards or sideways to reset |
| Ball route, player route, or solution? | **Mixed.** "Over" is usually ball route; "through" can be ball (pass through a gap) or player (solo through); "around" is often player route. The categories switch reference frame |
| One attack, several at once? | Yes, sequentially and sometimes simultaneously (decoy through, ball around) |
| Does Gaelic's kick pass make OVER useful? | **Yes, conceptually.** The kick pass and long puck bypass lines. But in Gaelic **"over" also means scoring a point** (over the bar). That is a **real ambiguity** in Gaelic coaching language |
| Is "through" too ambiguous? | **Yes.** Through a gap between two defenders, through the lines (depth), and through the middle channel are different things |
| Is even "useful observation language" defensible? | **Not demonstrated.** No study or coach-usability test shows it helps observation. Inter-rater agreement is unknown. "Useful" is **practitioner belief** |

### 9.3 BACK-TO-GO

- Going backwards is **an enabling action**. It changes the defence or the angle **so that** a later through / around / over becomes available (rugby passes, hockey regroup, basketball reversal).
- It is not a way **past** defenders, which is what the three categories describe.
- Adding it as a fourth route would mix **routes past a defence** with **preparatory actions**, purely for symmetry.
- **Rejected as a fourth category.**
- Its content survives in **DIR-3** (backwards ≠ negative).

### 9.4 Verdict: **KEEP 3 WITH CAVEAT**

Status: **PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC**. Caveats:
- not exclusive, not exhaustive;
- mixed reference frames;
- "through" is ambiguous;
- in Gaelic, "over" collides with "over the bar";
- enabling actions (back, sideways, hold) sit outside it;
- usefulness is unproven.

| Claim | Evidence (access) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Multiple targets increase width / dispersion | Travassos et al. (2014): 1 vs 3 targets ↑ inter-team distance, more time in lateral corridors (AO). Futsal (2018): 2 targets ↑ dispersion (AO). Four baskets ↑ dispersion (AO) | **SUPPORTED (acute)** |

**Distinctions:**
- **More options.** More targets = more places to score. True by construction.
- **More information.** Not the same. More targets may **dilute** target-relative information (which one matters now?).
- **Greater decision complexity.** Plausible; **not measured directly**.
- **Better decisions.** **Not shown.** The only trend favours fewer targets.
- **Learning.** **UNKNOWN.**

**Does adding options necessarily create better decision-making? No.** Nothing shows it, and one weak trend points the other way.

**Can multiple targets distort the game? Plausibly yes.**
- The four-basket individual-play shift is a measured acute example.
- In Gaelic games, multiple small goals remove posts, points and the two-point arc, which **are** the competition's multiple objectives (§14).
- Status: **SUPPORTED (acute example) / representative-design inference** for Gaelic.

**Principle under test:** *A constraint can produce the desired visible action while removing the player's need to decide whether that action is appropriate.*

**Classification: LOGICAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE.**
- It is true **by construction**. If an action is mandatory, the decision "whether" is removed from the game.
- Indirect evidence is consistent (restrictions ↓ exploration and co-adaptation).
- **No study** measures whether mandated actions reduce later decision quality once the rule is removed. The *consequence for learning* is **UNKNOWN**.
- It sits primarily with **RULES**, cross-linked to DIRECTION (hypothesis **H-2**).
- Why it matters for PáircVision:
  - **seeing the mandated action performed is not evidence of anything learned about when to perform it;**
  - a rule that **raises the value** of an action (SCORING) or makes it **more available** (SPACE / NUMBERS) keeps the decision with the player;
  - whether that difference affects learning is also **UNKNOWN**.

## 14. Gaelic representativeness gate

### 14.1 The Gaelic objective structure

| Feature | Directional implication |
|---|---|
| Goal (3 points) | A defended, small target, as in soccer |
| Point (over the bar) | A large, high, **range-and-angle** target. It can be scored from distance and from wide angles |
| Two-point score (outside the 40 m arc; football, 2025 rules) | Distance bands. Moving **closer can reduce** a score's value |
| Long kick / kick pass | Ball can bypass lines aerially: "over" is native |
| Hand pass | Short, fast circulation; backward and lateral hand passes are common |
| Carrying / solo | Ball can be carried through lines, so progress is not only by passing |
| Goalkeeper | Defends the goal; also a restart and recycling option (current restrictions **UNR**) |
| Kickout (must pass the 40 m arc, 2025 rules) | The restart's first direction is forced outward into a contested area |
| Large pitch | Wide switches and long kicks are real options |
| Three players in each half (2025 rules) | Guarantees attackers ahead of and defenders behind the ball |
| Hurling | Very long scoring range. Much of the directional problem is getting a free strike with time. The 2025 football rules do **not** apply |

- **Classification: REPRESENTATIVE-DESIGN INFERENCE** (rule-based logic). **Not direct evidence.**
- The logic is strong. Competition contains goal / point / two-pointer choices, a range-and-angle target and aerial scoring. A single small net contains none of these.
- **No Gaelic study** measures the behavioural or learning consequence.

## 18. Common finding across SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION

### 18.1 Test: *"Constraints reliably change what happens in practice more strongly than the evidence shows they change what players learn."*

- **"Reliably" is too strong.**
  - Physical-load effects are consistent (e.g. area → distance in the SPACE gate meta-analysis).
  - Tactical effects are **frequent but variable**: crossover effects (Frencken et al., 2013), large individual differences (Gonzalez-Artetxe et al., 2023), age moderation.
- **"More strongly than … learning" mixes categories.**
  - It compares an effect size with an evidence base.
  - What we actually know is an **evidence asymmetry**: abundant acute data, almost no learning or transfer data.
  - That asymmetry is **not** evidence that learning effects are weak.

**Strongest defensible wording (SUPPORTED, as a statement about the evidence base across four levers):**

> *Across SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION, there is far more evidence that constraints change what happens during practice than evidence that they change what players learn or do in matches. Physical effects are fairly consistent; tactical effects are common but vary between studies and players. The gap is in the evidence, not a demonstrated absence of learning.*

### 18.2 Test: *"The environment biases behaviour; it does not prescribe learning."*

- **"Biases behaviour": SUPPORTED** (acute; probabilistic, consistent with the SPACE gate's "probability lever").
- **"Does not prescribe learning": INTERPRETIVE FRAMING, not a finding.** We cannot show the environment never determines learning. We only lack evidence about what it does determine.
- **Defensible wording:**

> *The practice environment shifts the odds of what players do. Whether, and what, they learn from it has not been shown.*

## 19. RELEASE / RETAIN implications

- **Four consecutive levers** show acute effects with almost no learning or transfer evidence. That **strengthens the rationale** for checking behaviour **without** the constraint, because the constraint's own behaviour cannot be taken as learning.
- It **does not validate** EXPOSE → EXPLORE → RELEASE → RETAIN. No study has tested that sequence for any lever.
- The closest design in the literature is a pre/post unconstrained reference game (Gonzalez-Artetxe et al.). It found **little average change** and large individual differences.

| Observation | What it can show | What it cannot show |
|---|---|---|
| **Same-session behaviour after constraint removal** (RELEASE) | Immediate after-effects: carry-over, novelty, fatigue | Retention or learning |
| **Delayed retention** (RETAIN; ≥ 24 h, ideally a week or more; stable reference game; baseline) | Possible learning in that game | Transfer to competition |
| **Competition transfer** | The goal | Almost never measurable cleanly by a coach (opposition, score, selection confounds) |

**Status:**
- **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS** (unchanged from the SPACE gate P10) as a way of **organising observation**;
- rationale **strengthened** by the evidence gap;
- **not validated**.

## 22. Volunteer coach explanation (U12, wet Tuesday)

> **Keep-ball is useful.** When there are no goals, every child gets more touches and more passes, and it's hard work. It's good for learning to keep the ball when someone is chasing you.
>
> **Putting in a target changes the game.** Now there's somewhere to get to and something to protect. Players have to face up the pitch, and defenders have to guard something instead of just chasing the ball. That's a different problem, not a harder or easier version of the same one.
>
> **A target isn't automatically better.** Nobody has shown that games with goals make players better in matches than keep-ball, or the other way round. And a tiny goal leaves out things that matter in our game: points, the two-pointer, kicking over the bar.
>
> **Forward isn't always progress.** A kick forward straight to a marked player isn't progress. If they're not set, go quickly: that usually works best. If they are set, a hand pass back can be the smart move.
>
> **Backwards isn't always bad.** Going back can get a teammate on the ball facing the posts with time. Ask "did that leave us better placed to score, or safer on the ball?", not "did it go forward?".
>
> **What to watch:**
> - Does the player on the ball get to face the posts?
> - Do we get past a line of their players?
> - Do we pick the quick option when they're not set?
> - When defending, do players know what they're protecting?
>
> **Doing what the rule says doesn't prove they've learned it.** If you say "forward passes only", you'll see forward passes. That just shows the rule works. The real test is whether they choose well when the rule is gone, especially next week.

## 23. DIRECTION V1: final verdict

### A. Does DIRECTION survive as an independent lever?

**YES BUT NARROWED.**
- It survives only as **target structure**.
- Restrictions go to RULES; values to SCORING; cues to INFORMATION; countdowns to TIME; extra players to NUMBERS; zones to SPACE.
- "Progress" is an **evaluation**, not a manipulation.

### B. Exact V1 definition

> **DIRECTION is the target structure of a practice: whether there is something to attack and protect, how many targets there are, their form and location, and which team attacks which, including whether that changes when possession changes.**

### C. Frozen principles

| # | Principle | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|
| **DIR-1** | **Changing the target structure changes the problem players face, not a single difficulty setting.** Keep-ball formats produce more touches, passes, positional variability and physical and mental load. Target formats produce fewer of these but add something to face and to protect. More targets spread play and can make it more individual. Neither format is a lesser version of the other. | **SUPPORTED (acute behaviour only)**. Soccer, futsal and basketball; no Gaelic data (merges D1, D2, D5) |
| **DIR-2** | **A target gives the defence something to protect, and defending changes.** Defenders position relative to targets, tighten nearer goal, and regain the ball in different places and ways depending on the target form. Goal-side positioning, forcing wide and screening are **not** yet measured. | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (acute)** (D7) |
| **DIR-3** | **The direction an action travels does not by itself show whether it helped.** Backward or sideways actions can improve the attack's position; forward ones can worsen it. Quick forward play is often most effective against an unset defence and often not against a set one. Progress can only be judged relative to the target and the defence's state at the moment of the action. | **PARTIALLY SUPPORTED** (match analytics and associations, pro soccer; cross-sport practitioner consensus; no training evidence) (D4) |
| **DIR-4** | **Behaviour produced by a directional structure is not evidence of learning.** Whether directional or possession practice transfers better to matches, or whether any directional constraint improves play once removed, is **UNKNOWN**. | **SUPPORTED** (as a statement of the evidence base) (D3, D10) |

### D. PáircVision hypotheses (not evidence-supported principles)

| # | Hypothesis |
|---|---|
| **H-1** | A target works partly by making information *meaningful*: where danger and opportunity are, whether a player faces the objective (D8) |
| **H-2** | A rule that mandates a directional action removes the decision about whether that action is appropriate. **Logical design principle**; learning consequence UNKNOWN. **Primary home: RULES** (D6) |
| **H-3** | Direction reversal after turnover trains reorientation that transfers to real transitions |
| **H-4** | In Gaelic games, a single small target removes decision information present in competition: points, the two-point band, range and angle (representative-design inference) |
| **H-5** | Going backwards is usually an **enabling** action that makes a later forward solution available (not a fourth route) |
| **H-6** | EXPOSE → EXPLORE → RELEASE → RETAIN is a sound way to organise observation of directional constraints (rationale strengthened; not validated) |
| **H-7** | The same overload means different things with and without a target, and direction changes which overload is useful |

### E. Practitioner heuristics (vocabulary, not evidence)

- **"Use the ball to improve the picture"**: coaching heuristic only. Meaning: *"After that pass or carry, are we better placed to score, or safer on the ball, than before, whichever way it went?"*
- **Through / Around / Over**: keep 3 with caveats (§9.4).
- **"What am I protecting?"**: defensive DIRECTION question.
- "Switch the point of attack", "first look forward", "possession must have purpose", "go forward, support forward".
- Observable markers:
  - received facing the target;
  - line of defenders passed;
  - scoring angle improved.

### F. Key coach question

> **"What does this target ask my players to attack and protect, and what real-game decision does it add or take away?"**

- **Grounded in:** DIR-1 (targets change the problem) and DIR-2 (defending changes).
- **Humble about:** DIR-4 (it asks what the design *presents*, not what players will *learn*).
- **Gaelic-sensitive:** it prompts the goal / point / two-pointer check.

### G. What DIRECTION does NOT mean

| Not DIRECTION | Belongs to |
|---|---|
| Pitch size, shape, zones, channels | **SPACE** |
| Overloads, floaters, extra attackers toward goal | **NUMBERS** |
| Countdowns, transition windows, touch-limit time effects | **TIME** |
| Forward-only, no back pass, must switch, must use wing, offside, pass counts | **RULES** |
| Coach calls, cues or signals that switch targets; instructions; feedback | **INFORMATION** |
| Goal / point / two-point values; route bonuses | **SCORING** (or RULES if SCORING is not adopted as a lever) |
| The direction the ball or players travel | **Outcome**, not a lever |
| "Progress" | **Evaluation** relative to the target (DIR-3), not a manipulation |
| Through / Around / Over | **Observation vocabulary** |

> **Later taxonomy clarification (Candidate 09).** Candidate 09 subsequently dissolved INFORMATION as a Phase 1 family. Where this document and the [Candidate 09 Final Gate](./phase1-candidate-09-information-final-gate.md) differ on classification, **that gate governs**. The original finding above and its evidence status are unchanged.
>
> - Coach calls, cues or signals that switch targets, instructions and feedback belong to **COACH INTERVENTION** (deferred; pending research, not a completed framework). Where a switch changes the target set or polarity, that underlying change remains **DIRECTION / TARGET STRUCTURE**.
> - Candidate 08 separately re-homed the RULES row above to **ACTIONS** / **SPACE**.

### H. Transfer verdict

**Known:**
- Directional structure changes acute practice behaviour (DIR-1, DIR-2).
- The one reference-game rules study found little average change and large individual variation (not direction-specific).
- Directional-type RCTs measured only creativity tests or physical outcomes.

**Unknown:**
- Whether directional **or** possession practice improves match play.
- Whether either transfers better.
- Whether any directional constraint's behaviour persists after removal, in a delayed retention test or in competition.
- Anything at all about directional training in Gaelic football or hurling.

### I. Freeze recommendation

**FREEZE DIRECTION V1**

**Reason.**
- The surviving framework is small and honest: a narrowed definition, four principles, each labelled with its true evidence status, and explicit UNKNOWNs.
- Further research will not change it soon, because the missing evidence (transfer) does not exist anywhere in the literature we could reach.
- Freezing does **not** assert that transfer is established. It fixes what PáircVision may and may not say.
- **Two conditions attach:**
  1. The DIRECTION / SCORING / INFORMATION boundaries (§2) must be re-checked when those levers are audited.
  2. Current Gaelic rule details marked UNR must be verified against the 2026 rulebook before any Gaelic-specific wording is published.

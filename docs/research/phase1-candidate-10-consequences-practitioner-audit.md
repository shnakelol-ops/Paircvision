## 2. Practitioner meaning of "consequence"

| Word practitioners use | What they mean (as found) |
|---|---|
| **Transition** | The moment possession changes and what each team must do (K1, K3, K5, K6, K9) |
| **Reaction** / **"next action"** | What a player does straight after losing the ball or making a mistake (K9, K16) |
| **Turnover game / chaos** | A game built to produce frequent, unpredictable possession changes (K2, K3) |
| **Condition / rule** | A coach-made follow-up ("score and keep the ball"; regain clock) (K7, K10) |
| **Punishment** | Running, push-ups, benching for mistakes or losing (K12, K13) |
| **Reward** | Winning, staying on, the "joy of continuing to play" (K12) |
| **Consequence** | Mostly in the **discipline** sense (punishment), rarely for game follow-ups |

**Finding.**
- Practitioners **do** separate these, in their own words: "transition" (natural follow-up), "conditions" (coach-made follow-up) and "punishment" (sanction).
- They **do not** use "consequences" as a practice-design category; when they say "consequences" they usually mean **discipline**.
- **Supports** the scientific audit's split (game follow-up vs coach-made follow-up vs sanctions).

## 3. Continue vs reset (priority)

| Coach behaviour | Rationale found | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Let play continue after a turnover** | The possession change is the problem: transitions are where many possessions (and scores) come from; "recreate the chaos"; don't over-coach | K1, K3, K5, K9 |
| **Manufacture a live turnover inside play** | Match turnovers are hard to replicate; drop a second ball / "double-crossing" player so the change is **sudden and unpredictable** | K2 |
| **Repeat a situation quickly and continuously** | Put players into the same pressured situation (3v1, 3v2) repeatedly | K4 (Kinnerk, reported) |
| **Feed another ball / restart immediately** | Repetition volume; keep players working (basketball rebounding, shooting) | K11 |
| **Waves** (attack ends → next attack at once) | Attack with speed against a team still transitioning; defending rewarded by breaking out | K8 |
| **Stop to coach** | Observe first, then Freeze → Fix → Play; right intervention at the right time | K14, K15 |
| **Reset to a fixed start** | Not explicitly justified in accessible sources (inferred: repetition of one picture, control, time) | — |

**What coaches try to preserve by continuing:** the **sudden, unplanned** change of roles and the disorganisation of both teams (K1, K2, K6).
**What they try to gain by resetting / feeding:** **repetition** of a chosen picture and **control** (K4, K11), plus a chance to **coach** (K15).

**Finding.** Practitioners who talk about transition **want the possession change to be sudden and unscripted**, and several deliberately **manufacture** it inside live play (K2). That is a practitioner concern about **how a moment arises** (a GAME MOMENT generation issue) and about its **cue** (GI-2), not about a new property.

## 4. Transition: what coaches think players must learn

| What must be learned | Source |
|---|---|
| **Switch of mindset** from defence to attack on dispossession; move the ball fast to where the team can get reward | K5 (Nally, Gaelic) |
| **Exploit the opponent's disorganisation** in the first seconds | K6 (Klopp), K7 (Rangnick) |
| **Decision-making after winning turnover possession**: pass to space, realign | K3 (rugby) |
| **Reaction after losing it**: win it back at once, or recover | K6, K9, K16 |
| **Attack against a team still transitioning** | K8 |
| Unstructured possessions are the **most common** source of possession, so they must be practised | K1 |

**Finding.** Practitioners describe transition as **a moment with its own problem** (roles flip; both teams disorganised). They do not describe it as a property of practice. **MULTIPLE-PRACTITIONER SUPPORT** that transition is worth practising; **no learning evidence** (scientific audit §26).

## 8. Transition games: what they are claimed to teach

| Format | Claimed | Decisions actually left (PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION) |
|---|---|---|
| **Waves** (attack → next attack at once) | Attack with speed; defend then break out | Attackers: exploit a transitioning defence. Defenders: organise under time pressure. **Recognition of the turnover moment is largely scripted** (the wave starts on the end of the previous attack) |
| **3v3v3 / three-team games** | Succeed in attack and defence | Similar; the switch is rule-triggered |
| **Continuous game** | Transitions happen naturally | All, including recognising the moment and organising |
| **Second-ball drop / double-crosser** (K2) | Realistic, sudden turnovers | Recognition of an **unpredictable** change; not its **cause** (no error produced it) |
| **Score-and-keep-ball** (K10) | Keep attacking; defence must reset | Defence: reorganise fast. Attack: attack again; **no transition problem** for the scoring team |
| **Regain clock** (K7) | Urgency after regain | Route, who, how; deadline supplied |

**Do not accept "it's a transition game, therefore it teaches transition."** Each format supplies different parts: when the switch happens, what situation follows, or a deadline.

## 9. Natural vs coach-created turnover

| Version | Practitioner use | GI-2 test (if recognising the moment is the objective) |
|---|---|---|
| Players actually lose the ball | Continuous games, turnover games (K1, K3, K9) | Cue present (the error and the flip) |
| Coach calls "TURNOVER" | Common (drill-level) | **Cue replaced** by the call |
| Coach drops / feeds a second ball (K2) | Realistic suddenness | Cue **partly** present: the change is sudden and unpredictable, but not caused by play |
| Timer switches possession | Drill-level | Cue replaced by the clock |

**Finding.** Practitioners choose among these for **realism vs control**. K2 is an explicit practitioner attempt to keep **suddenness** while controlling **frequency**, which GI-2 already describes as a trade-off.

## 10. Reaction after a mistake

- Coaches talk about the **"next action"**: stay involved, win it back, don't hide (K16; K6).
- **Tactical** ("win it back immediately", counter-press) vs **motivational** ("don't hide", "reset mentally") are **mixed** in practitioner language.
- **Do coaches deliberately let mistakes unfold because the response is part of learning?** **Yes, in transition-focused material** (K1, K3, K9: let play run; don't over-coach). **Evidence of learning: none.**

## 12. Freeze / replay

- **Why destroy continuity?** To **show** the picture, **ask** what was seen, and **replay** it with the fix (K15; Candidate 09 trigger coaching).
- **Does repeating the picture sometimes outweigh natural follow-up?** Practitioners imply **yes** when the aim is a specific situation (K4: repeat situations; K15: fix then return).
- **Status:** PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC; learning effects **UNKNOWN**.

## 18. Winner stays on

- **Practitioner rationale found:** competitiveness, intensity, motivation (drill-level and folklore; volleyball "king of the court" formats).
- **Counter-concern:** players spending more time off the field feel rejected and are likelier to quit (youth-sport writing citing research; SIO).
- **Evidence:** one older lab study: winning raised intrinsic motivation vs losing (scientific audit). **No sport-practice study.**
- **Taxonomy home:** **competition structure / participation** (SCORING gate's separate "competing to win; stakes" property), with **NUMBERS / participation** side effects (who plays). **Not** a practice-design consequence.

## 21. Error consequence: exaggerated vs allowed to unfold

| Design | What it is |
|---|---|
| Turnover → immediate shot chance | **Coach-made** (GAME MOMENT: selected shooting moment) |
| Misplaced pass → opponent scoring chance | **Natural**, if the game lets it unfold (small pitch, live play) |

## 22. "Let the game punish the mistake"

- **Found in spirit:** "let the joy of continuing to play be the reward and the agony of not playing be the punishment" (K12, about participation); "don't over-coach; let players experience the chaos" (K9).
- **Meaning:** don't stop at once; let players feel what their decision caused.
- **Widespread?** **Moderately**, in games-based and anti-punishment writing.
- **Evidence of learning: none.** PRACTITIONER HEURISTIC.

## 23. Natural consequence as feedback

- **Practitioner reasoning:** letting play continue lets players experience the tactical result (defender jumps → space exploited) without explanation (K9, K12).
- **Scientific status:** intrinsic, task-generated information is a recognised source of learning information in motor learning; **whether players learn tactical lessons from it without coaching: UNKNOWN**.
- **Not claimed** that it automatically produces learning. COACH INTERVENTION remains deferred.

## 25. CF-1 v2

- **Coach-called turnover:** success shows **transition execution**, not **recognition** of the turnover.
- **Both already covered** ("an overload", "a called moment"). **No amendment.**

## 26. GI-2 applied to continue / reset

| Objective | Continue / natural | Reset / coach-created |
|---|---|---|
| **Transition recognition** | Keeps what tells players it's on (GI-2 satisfied) | Removes or replaces it (GI-2: success is not evidence of recognition) |
| **Transition execution** (what to do once it's on) | Fine, but fewer and messier reps | **Legitimate**: more clean reps; recognition not the aim |

**Finding.** GI-2 already carries the continue / reset distinction **for recognition**. For **execution**, coach-created transitions can be exactly right (Candidates 08–09 logic).

## 27. Recognition vs execution

- **Recognise the turnover** (spot the flip, the disorganised moment): needs a live, unpredictable change (K1, K2).
- **Execute after it** (first pass forward, support runs, counter-press shape): can be trained with called or fed transitions (K4, K8, K11).
- **Not ranked.** Practitioners use both; K2's second-ball drop is a hybrid that keeps suddenness.

## 28. Testing the continue / reset check

**Candidate:** *"Is what happens after this moment part of what I want them to practise?"*

| Case | Useful? |
|---|---|
| Turnover | **Yes**: decides continue vs reset |
| Score | **Yes**: restart, keep ball, or stop |
| Miss | **Yes**: rebound / kickout / reset |
| Kickout | **Yes**: does the contest for the kickout continue into play? |
| Technical kicking | Irrelevant |
| Set-piece rehearsal | Usually **no**, legitimately |
| Pressing | **Yes**: does the regain lead to an attack, or stop? |
| Support | Partly |
| Shooting | **Yes**: rebound / restart / transition after the shot |

**Does it fill a gap?**
- It is **GAME MOMENT** applied at the **end** of an episode: which follow-up moments players meet (GM-2).
- **GI-2** covers the recognition case.
- **Gap:** small. GM-2 says coaches choose which moments players meet; it does not explicitly say that **ending or continuing an episode is one of those choices**. That is a **clarification**, not a new check.
- **Recommendation:** fold into a **GAME MOMENT clarification**; do **not** create a separate check or coach-facing question.

## 29. GAME MOMENT boundary and sequence

**Is "the post-turnover moment isn't sampled" sufficient?**
- **Mostly.** Stopping means the transition isn't met; continuing means it is.
- **But sequence matters to practitioners:** McKay (K1) built practice around **how possession arises** (from kick receipts, errors, quick taps), and K2 tries to keep turnovers **sudden**. Players meet B **because A just happened**, with both teams in the state A left them in.

**Compare:**
- **Start** in a 5v4 counterattack vs
- players **enter** a 5v4 immediately after winning possession.

| Difference | Already captured by |
|---|---|
| Whether players must **recognise** the moment | **GI-2** |
| The **disorganised state** both teams are in (bodies facing wrong way, distances) | **GAME MOMENT** configuration (how the moment arises), plus SPACE / TIME |
| That the moment **emerges** from play rather than being selected | **GAME MOMENT** generation (supplied vs emergent; already in the GAME MOMENT gate) |

**Finding.** Sequence matters, and GAME MOMENT **already contains** the idea of *how a moment arises* (supplied vs emergent). What needs **clarifying** is that GAME MOMENT includes **whether and how follow-up moments arise within an episode**. **No new lever.**

## 33. U12 volunteer translation

> **When they lose the ball, score or miss, ask: is what happens next part of what I'm working on today?**
>
> **If it is** (say you want them to react fast when they lose it), **keep the game going.** Don't whistle.
>
> **If it isn't** (say you're working on getting a shot off), **stop and start again.** More goes at the thing you care about is fine.
>
> **Stopping to help is fine too**, as long as you let them play long enough to see what's happening first.
>
> **No laps or push-ups for mistakes.** Let them go again instead.

## 35. Taxonomy recommendation (provisional; the final gate decides)

### **C, with a GAME MOMENT clarification (D-lite).**

1. **CONSEQUENCES dissolves.** Practitioners don't treat it as a design category; their follow-ups classify under existing levers (§14, §30).
2. **Event-contingent survives as internal notation** (C): an existing manipulation that begins, ends or changes when a defined event occurs. Practitioners already reason this way (CP-5).
3. **GAME MOMENT is clarified, not expanded** (D-lite): it covers **whether and how follow-up moments arise within an episode** (continue, reset, feed, manufacture), alongside its existing *which moments / how often / supplied vs emergent*.
4. **No separate continuity check** (not E): the gap is small and GI-2 + the clarified GAME MOMENT carry it.
5. **Sanctions, external rewards and winner-stays-on** sit outside practice design (competition structure / motivation / discipline).

**Why not A / B:** no practitioner treats consequences or continuity as a property; both are ways existing properties combine over time.
**Why not full D (expansion):** GAME MOMENT already has "how the moment arises"; it needs a sentence, not a new scope.
**Why not E:** a separate check would duplicate GM-2 and GI-2.

## 36. Direct answers

### Why do good coaches let play continue after turnovers?

| Layer | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Evidence** | Transitions are a large share of possessions (Gaelic, rugby); no learning study |
| **Practitioner** | To keep the sudden, unscripted change and both teams' disorganisation; to practise what most match possessions look like; to avoid over-coaching |
| **PáircVision synthesis** | Because the follow-up is part of what they want players to practise |

### Why do good coaches deliberately stop it?

| Layer | Answer |
|---|---|
| **Evidence** | None comparing stop vs continue |
| **Practitioner** | To repeat a picture, correct, ask what was seen, get volume, control time |
| **PáircVision synthesis** | Because today's objective is something other than the follow-up, or because coaching the moment needs a pause |

### Is experiencing the natural follow-up itself a learning mechanism?

**Practitioner belief** (CP-9). Scientifically **UNKNOWN** for tactical learning.

### Does event-contingent deserve framework status or merely internal notation?

**Internal notation.** It classifies nothing new; it describes **when** an existing lever applies.

### Does sequence matter beyond GAME MOMENT?

**It matters, but GAME MOMENT already carries it** once clarified: how a moment arises (supplied vs emergent) and whether follow-up moments arise within an episode. Recognition of the moment is **GI-2**.

### Does Candidate 10 reveal a genuine missing concept or another way existing levers combine?

**Another way existing levers combine** (over time, triggered by events). The only real gap is a **clarification** of GAME MOMENT's scope.

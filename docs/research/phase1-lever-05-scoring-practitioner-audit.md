# PáircVision Phase 1 — Lever 05: SCORING — Practitioner & Cross-Sport Audit

**Type:** Practitioner and coaching-intelligence audit. Research only: no drills, sessions, UI, product features or production code.
**Companion to:** [SCORING scientific audit](./phase1-lever-05-scoring.md). Candidates there are SCO-1 to SCO-9.
**Frozen context:** [SPACE gate](./phase1-lever-01-space-final-gate.md) · [NUMBERS gate](./phase1-lever-02-numbers-final-gate.md) · [TIME gate](./phase1-lever-03-time-final-gate.md) · [DIRECTION gate](./phase1-lever-04-direction-final-gate.md)
**Status:** v1 practitioner audit, September 2026. See the [SCORING Final Evidence & Falsification Gate](./phase1-lever-05-scoring-final-gate.md). **Where either audit and the final gate differ, the final gate governs PáircVision V1.**

> **What this audit is not.** It does not fill the scientific gap. No invasion-sport study rewards a behaviour, removes the reward, and tests whether the behaviour persists. Nothing a practitioner says changes that.
>
> **What it asks:**
> - Why do good coaches change the score?
> - What do they believe it changes?
> - How do they choose what to reward, how much, and when to stop?
> - Do they distinguish *making a behaviour appear* from *teaching when to use it*?
>
> **The sharpest question:** when a good coach changes the score, are they trying to **teach a behaviour**, or to **make a game problem impossible to ignore**?

## 0. Method, access and evidence status

### 0.1 How this audit was done

- **Searches (September 2026)** covered:
  - GAA Tobar / Games-Based Approach material; Gaelic high-performance coach research; two-point discussion;
  - TGfU ("modification by exaggeration");
  - England Football Learning; US Soccer Play-Practice-Play;
  - USA Hockey and ice-hockey coaching sites on the constraints-led approach;
  - basketball expected-value and defensive-stop practices;
  - rugby and competition bonus-point rationales;
  - practitioner criticism of conditions and scoring;
  - score-scenario practice.
- **Access.**
  - The proxy blocked learning.gaa.ie PDFs (including Éamonn Ryan's *Organising Purposeful Games Based Gaelic Football*), Taylor & Francis, SAGE and most coaching platforms.
  - **Nothing was read in FULL TEXT.**
  - Practitioner material is **SEARCH-INDEX ONLY (SIO)**: we saw the search engine's summary, not the page.
  - Research is **ABSTRACT ONLY (AO)** at best.
  - **UNR** marks an unconfirmed author, date, quote or detail.
- **Labels:** RESEARCH-BACKED · GOVERNING-BODY GUIDANCE · ELITE PRACTITIONER METHOD · COACHING CONVENTION · PRACTITIONER OPINION · PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION.

### 0.2 Honest headline

1. **The reasoning behind bonus scoring is thin in accessible practitioner material.** Most sources state *what* is rewarded. Few say *why*. Fewer still say what happened when the bonus was removed.
2. **The clearest practitioner rationales:**
   - "reward without forcing" (bonus preserves choice);
   - "draw attention to the focus" (TGfU exaggeration);
   - "incentivise / discourage" (USA Hockey CLA);
   - competition and intensity.
3. **We found no practitioner source explaining how a bonus's size is calibrated**, and none describing removing a *bonus* and checking whether the behaviour persisted.
4. **Governing bodies are now the largest "scoring designers" in Gaelic games.** The two-point score and the trial two-point sideline cut in hurling are value manipulations at competition level, justified in the same incentive language coaches use.
5. **Kinnerk:** nothing attributable on scoring manipulation.

## 5. Score vs rule: practitioner test

- **Do practitioners distinguish "must switch" from "switch = double"?**
  - **Some do, explicitly** (S3).
  - USA Hockey frames CLA as incentivising **or** discouraging (S5).
  - TGfU treats scoring as one of several exaggeration tools (S2).
- **Explicit preference for incentives over "must" rules:**
  - found in **one youth-coaching source** (S3);
  - consistent with S7 ("more natural").
  - **No consensus manufactured.** We did not find broad elite practitioner statements either way.
- **Criticism of "must" rules:**
  - S8 (pass-count conditions produce purposeless passing);
  - the DIRECTION practitioner audit found no sourced elite criticism of forward-only rules.
- **Do practitioners believe bonuses preserve decision-making because the alternative stays legal?**
  - S3 says so directly.
  - **That belief is untested** (scientific audit §3, §6).
- **Dominance problem.**
  - If a bonus is so large that no rational player ignores it (e.g., a Royal Road goal worth 3× normal, S5), choice is **nominal**. The unrewarded option is still legal but rarely worth taking.
  - No practitioner source addressed this.
  - **PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION:** "choice is preserved" holds only while the unrewarded option remains competitive in value.

## 8. Process vs outcome scoring

**Why reward before the actual score?** Practitioner reasons found:
- make a principle visible (S2);
- apply a newly learned skill in the game (S6);
- value an action that creates chances but is not itself on the scoreboard: the cross-ice pass (S5), a regain (S4, S9), building through thirds (S3);
- show players the link to match success (S7).

**The danger: does the process become the objective?**
- Practitioner acknowledgement: **only S8** (passes without purpose).
- S3's "don't say MUST" shows awareness that the process is not always right.
- Otherwise not discussed.
- **Scientific status:** process scoring changes acute behaviour (scientific audit §8). Its benefits and risks are **PÁIRCVISION HYPOTHESIS**.
- **Cross-link:** DIRECTION DIR-3. Progress is judged by effect, not action. A process bonus pays for the action.

## 9. The scoreboard as attention

Practitioners describe scoring as several **different** mechanisms, often mixed together:

| Mechanism | Practitioner language | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Attention cue / tactical emphasis** | "draws players' attention to the teaching focus" | S2 |
| **Incentive / reward** | "incentivise or discourage behaviours" | S5 |
| **Feedback / meaning** | "see how being better in that area will help them succeed" | S7 |
| **Motivation / competition** | "competitive … game-like intensity" | S9 |
| **Skill application** | "apply newly learned skills" | S6 |
| **Measurement** | Consecutive-stop tallies | S9 (implicit) |

- These are **not interchangeable**. An attention cue can work without any value change (a coach simply saying "look for the Royal Road"). An incentive works even if unexplained.
- **No source tests which mechanism does the work.**
- **Flag for the INFORMATION audit.** If scoring works mainly as an attention cue, part of SCORING may belong to INFORMATION. The scientific audit's U-20 study already found an *explicit* regain rule changed behaviour more than free play (scientific audit §4 #2; §19 parked).

- **Rarely made explicit.**

- **PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION:** the distinction exists in practitioner thinking but is not operationalised into what coaches watch.

## 13. Coach intervention

| Intervention | Practitioner evidence |
|---|---|
| Questioning / guided discovery | GAA GBA (S1). Players disliked huddles in one Gaelic study (*RQES* 2025) |
| Instruction | *RQES* 2024 youth soccer (science): instruction was associated with more cooperative networks than constraints alone |
| Freeze play | GAA Freeze and Fix |
| Let the game continue / silent observation | US Soccer (S12) |
| Change the scenario (score / time) | High-performance Gaelic coaches (S11) |
| Change bonus value / remove bonus / change scoring criteria | **Not described** |
| Change target / space / numbers | General CLA language (S5) |

**Do good coaches change what they SAY or what the GAME VALUES?** Both appear.
- The **scenario** practice (S11) changes what the game means (score state) through what the coach **says**.
- Bonus systems change values **without** necessarily saying why.
- **Science note:** *RQES* 2024 suggests that constraint without instruction pushed youth players toward individual actions (AO). Changing only the values may not be enough.

## 14. Basketball (priority cross-sport)

- **Value is embedded in the sport** (2 vs 3).
- The practitioner concept of shot quality is **value × probability** (S10):
  - "take the same percentages from more valuable spots";
  - a 35 % three > a 52 % two;
  - rim and corner-three preference.
- **Higher value ≠ better decision** is already built into expected value. A contested three by a poor shooter can be worse than an open layup.
  - The practitioner phrase is "good shot / bad shot", not "shoot more threes".
- Practice scoring:
  - consecutive-stop and reset scoring (S9);
  - possession-reward and pass-graded scoring (science, scientific audit §4);
  - "must touch paint" (a RULE, DIRECTION audit).
- **Translation to Gaelic (PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION):**
  - The two-pointer makes Gaelic shot selection an expected-value problem, as S13's "twice the reward, slightly higher risk" reasoning shows.
  - Basketball's lesson is to teach **probability** (distance, pressure, shooter), not just value.
  - **Shot probabilities do not transfer**; the concept does.

## 16. Score state

- **How coaches train it:** high-performance Gaelic coaches **stop games and impose scenarios** ("up by four points, three minutes to go"), and mimic opponents (S11). Youth-coaching writing describes similar scenarios (1–0 up, five minutes left, a player sent off).
- **What they manipulate:**
  - mainly **INFORMATION** (the stated score and time), which changes what winning requires;
  - the scoring **system** is unchanged.
  - Where a scenario adds "a draw is enough" or "you need a goal", it changes the **win condition**. That sits between SCORING and CONSEQUENCES.
- **Boundary kept OPEN for the INFORMATION audit** (scientific audit §18).

## 19. CONSEQUENCES boundary

**Provisional distinction:**
- **SCORING** changes what an outcome is **worth**.
- **CONSEQUENCES** changes what **happens next**.

| Example | Classification | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Regain = 2 points | SCORING | — |
| Regain = immediate 5v3 attack | CONSEQUENCES | Also NUMBERS (overload) |
| Score = retain possession | CONSEQUENCES | Basketball possession-reward study (science) |
| Turnover = opposition restarts beside your goal | CONSEQUENCES | Also SPACE / DIRECTION |
| Kill drill: stay on defence until three stops | CONSEQUENCES | Termination condition |
| Kill drill: score resets to zero | **SCORING** (negative / reset) | Edge: resets change value history, not the next event |
| Losers do push-ups | **Post-game consequence** | Outside the game; may be neither lever (a motivational stake) |
| "Winner stays on" | CONSEQUENCES | Affects who plays next |
| Bonus that *grants* possession ("score after switch = 1 point + ball back") | **Both** | Designs often bundle both, so their effects are confounded |
| Scenario "a draw is enough" | Win condition: SCORING × CONSEQUENCES × INFORMATION | Edge case |

**Verdict: the distinction is useful**, and the edge cases are real: bundled rewards, win conditions, post-game stakes. **Not frozen.** CONSEQUENCES needs its own scientific audit.

## 20. Scoring as feedback

- Practitioners do present scoring as feedback about success ("see how being better … helps", S7).
- **The ambiguity:** a process point may tell players *"the coach values that action"* rather than *"that action was tactically correct"*.
- **Practitioner awareness:** implicit in S3, which avoids "must" because the action is not always right. Otherwise **not found**.
- **PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION.** Process scoring risks teaching **coach preference**, not **game information**. Outcome scoring (real goals and points) is feedback **from the game**. Process scoring is feedback **from the coach, delivered through the game**.
- **Flag for INFORMATION.**

## 22. Scoring × the four frozen levers

Practitioner examples where SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION are identical and only scoring changes:
- the Royal Road goal (S5);
- the one-timer goal (S6);
- the first-time finish (S4);
- regain-and-score by third (S4);
- the through-the-thirds bonus (S3).

| Example | What scoring made more valuable | What physical opportunities changed |
|---|---|---|
| Royal Road = 3 | Cross-ice passes before shooting | **None.** The lane existed before |
| One-timer = 2 | One-touch finishes | None |
| First-time finish = 2 | Finishing without a touch | None |
| Through-the-thirds bonus | Building play | None |
| Regain weighted by third | Where regains happen | None |

**PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION (candidate central distinction):**
- SPACE, NUMBERS, TIME and DIRECTION change **which opportunities exist**.
- SCORING changes **how much existing opportunities are worth**.
- A bonus cannot create a cross-ice lane. It can only make players more likely to look for and take one.
- Whether they then learn to **see** the lane without the bonus is the unanswered question.

## 29. Volunteer coach translation (U12 Gaelic, wet Tuesday)

> **A bonus can be handy.** If you give an extra point for a score after switching the play, players will start looking for the switch. It shines a light on something they'd otherwise miss.
>
> **It can also fool you.** You'll see loads of switches, and it'll look like they've learned it. They may just be chasing your point. Some of those switches will be the wrong play.
>
> **Bonus vs rule.** "You must switch before you score" takes away every other option. "A switch-score is worth an extra point" leaves them free to go straight through when that's on. That's the idea anyway. If the bonus is huge, they'll switch every time, and you're back to a rule.
>
> **Keep the bonus small enough** that going straight at goal is still sometimes the smart play.
>
> **Seeing more of it doesn't prove they've learned it.** The real question is whether they switch at the right times when there's no bonus.
>
> **So go back to normal scoring.** Play the real game: goal, point, two-pointer. Watch whether they still find the switch when it's on and skip it when it isn't. Look again next week. That tells you more than the bonus game did.
>
> **Watch more than the scoreboard.** Did the bonus action actually help? Did anything else go missing: shots, runs, patience?

## 30. Final output

### A. Why practitioners manipulate scoring

- To **draw attention** to a principle (TGfU).
- To **incentivise** without banning (CLA, youth coaching).
- To make practice **more natural** than conditions.
- To get **newly learned skills** used in games.
- To show **why** a behaviour matters.
- To create **competition and intensity**.

The dominant intent appears to be making a game problem **harder to ignore**, more than teaching a behaviour. That reading is PÁIRCVISION INTERPRETATION of stated rationales.

### B. What good practitioners appear to observe

- Frequency of the rewarded action.
- Occasionally appropriateness (S3) and shot quality (S10).
- Application in a final free game (S12).
- Absent from sources: what preceded or followed the action, ignored alternatives, opposition adaptation, bonus dominance.

### C. How practitioners calibrate reward magnitude

- **Not documented.**
- Double and triple look like convention and salience.
- No adjustment accounts; no "too big / too small" reports.

### D. When practitioners remove scoring manipulations

- **Structurally**: at the end of the session (PPP / WPW / GBA return to game).
- **Explicit bonus removal with targeted observation: not found.**

### E. Evidence of players gaming scoring systems

- **One practitioner report:** purposeless passing under pass-count conditions.
- Competition analogues exist (sabotage; defensive adaptation).
- Otherwise not found. **Absence of reports ≠ absence of gaming.**

### F. Process vs outcome scoring

- Practitioners reward process to make hidden, chance-creating actions visible and valued.
- The risk that the process becomes the objective is acknowledged only indirectly (S3, S8).
- Process scoring pays for the **action**, not its **effect** (DIRECTION DIR-3).

### G. Score vs rule: practitioner verdict

- Some practitioners explicitly prefer bonuses to "must" rules **to preserve choice**.
- There is no broad consensus, and the belief is untested.
- Choice is only real while the unrewarded option remains worth taking.

### H. SCORING vs CONSEQUENCES provisional boundary

- **Worth** (SCORING) vs **what happens next** (CONSEQUENCES) is useful.
- Edge cases: bundled rewards, reset scoring, win conditions, post-game stakes.
- **Not frozen.**

### I. Gaelic-specific insights

- Official GBA material supports conditioned games and exaggeration **as focus**, without claiming proven learning.
- High-performance Gaelic coaches manipulate **score state** by scenario rather than point values.
- The two-point era turns real Gaelic scoring into an expected-value problem that players and analysts already reason about.
- Governing bodies are running value experiments (two-pointer; the hurling sideline-cut trial) whose effects are **descriptive and confounded**.
- **No Gaelic practitioner rationale for specific bonus values was found.**

### J. Cross-sport concepts worth translating

- Basketball **value × probability** ("good shot"), teaching probability rather than value alone.
- Soccer / PE **whole-part-whole** (return to the real game).
- CLA's **incentivise vs force** framing.
- Elite **score-time scenarios** (as INFORMATION).
- **Not** translated: sport-specific values, "must touch paint", league-points structures.

### K. Practitioner claims unsupported by learning evidence

- "Reinforces the habit"; "teaches"; "improves decision-making"; "proven"; "competition increases learning";
- "players learn without being told"; "bonus preserves decisions"; "final game shows learning"; "points off improves execution";
- "bonus makes skills transfer".

### L. EXPOSE → EXPLORE → RELEASE → RETAIN comparison

| Phase | Practitioner frequency |
|---|---|
| EXPOSE | Common |
| EXPLORE | Stated intent |
| RELEASE | Common as session structure; explicit bonus removal rare / not found |
| RETAIN | Rare, implicit, confounded |

**No validation claimed.**

### M. Questions for the SCORING final falsification gate

1. Is there **any** invasion-sport study (including PE, handball, netball, rugby) that removes a scoring manipulation and tests the behaviour later? If not, is SCO-2 the only safe frozen statement about learning?
2. Does scoring work mainly as **incentive** or as **attention cue**? If attention, how much of SCORING belongs to INFORMATION?
3. At what bonus size does a "preserved choice" become a de facto rule, and can that ever be defined without player-specific probabilities?
4. Is the SCORE vs RULE distinction behaviourally real, or only descriptively neat? What evidence would falsify it?
5. Should "SCORING changes the value of existing opportunities; the other levers change which opportunities exist" be frozen as the lever's defining distinction, or is it contradicted by cases where value changes create new opportunities (e.g., defences reorganising)?
6. Do process bonuses teach coach preference rather than game information, and how could anyone tell?
7. Is "negative scoring" a SCORING manipulation, a CONSEQUENCE, or a separate motivational-climate issue?
8. Does keeping score (vs not) change practice behaviour enough to count as a manipulation at all, and where does competition itself belong?
9. Should real Gaelic scoring (goal / point / two-pointer) be the default reference, with invented bonuses treated as deviations needing justification? What evidence would support or refute that?
10. Can pre-2025 Gaelic shot probabilities be used for **any** current coaching statement, given the rule changes?
11. Where does score **state** live (INFORMATION, SCORING, CONSEQUENCES), and should score-time scenarios be audited under INFORMATION?
12. Is "make a game problem harder to ignore" a distinct, testable purpose, or indistinguishable from "make the behaviour happen more"?
13. Does the frozen "useful rather than compulsory" principle (SPACE P7) gain or lose support from SCORING, given that bonuses are neither naturally useful nor compulsory?
